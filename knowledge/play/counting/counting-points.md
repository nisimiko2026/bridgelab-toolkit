# Counting High-Card Points

## Overview

Counting high-card points (HCP) is a fundamental bridge skill. By combining information from the auction, opening lead, and defenders' play, players can estimate or determine the location of the missing honors.

Unlike hand evaluation before the auction, counting points is a process of deduction during the play.

## Why Count Points?

- Locate missing honors
- Improve finesse decisions
- Plan the play
- Predict defenders' distributions
- Determine whether an honor is likely to be onside

## Step 1 – Count the Known Points

Add together:

- Dummy's HCP
- Your own HCP

The remaining points belong to the opponents.

## Step 2 – Use the Auction

Estimate each opponent's range.

Examples:

- 1NT opening
- Weak Two
- Preempt
- Overcall
- Takeout Double

Each bid provides information about point ranges.

## Step 3 – Use Revealed Honors

As honors appear during the play, update the count.

Example:

West has shown:

- ♠A
- ♥K
- ♦Q

West has already shown 9 HCP.

## Step 4 – Combine with Distribution

Point count alone is not enough.

Use:

- bidding
- distribution
- signals
- carding

to identify which opponent is likely to hold the remaining honors.

## Typical Examples

### Example 1

You hold 15 HCP.

Dummy has 11 HCP.

Opponents therefore have 14 HCP.

West opened 1♠.

West later shows:

♠AK
♥K

West already has 13 HCP.

Therefore East is unlikely to hold another major honor.

### Example 2

After a 15–17 NT opening,

Responder has 8 HCP.

The defenders therefore have between 15 and 17 HCP.

As honors appear, their exact locations become clear.

## Common Mistakes

- Forgetting revealed honors
- Ignoring the auction
- Counting only one defender
- Not updating after every trick
