# Counting Losers Technique

## Overview

**Counting Losers** is the primary planning method for declarer play in **suit contracts**. Before playing to the first trick, declarer counts the number of **potential losing tricks** and develops a plan to eliminate enough of them to make the contract.

Unlike **Counting Winners**, which is generally used in notrump contracts, Counting Losers focuses on identifying losers and finding ways to remove them through ruffing, discarding, finessing, establishing long suits, or other declarer-play techniques.

Expert declarers begin almost every suit contract by counting losers before playing to Trick One.

---

# Purpose

Counting Losers allows declarer to:

- Determine whether the contract is makeable.
- Identify which losers must be eliminated.
- Choose the best line of play.
- Plan ruffs and discards.
- Decide when to draw trumps.
- Avoid unnecessary risks.

---

# Basic Principle

Before playing from dummy, ask:

> **"How many potential losers do I have?"**

Then compare that number with the number of losers the contract can afford.

Example:

Contract:

```
4♠
```

Required:

```
10 tricks
```

Declarer may lose **3 tricks**.

If declarer counts **5 losers**, two losers must be eliminated.

---

# When to Use

Counting Losers is essential in:

- Suit contracts.
- Slam contracts in a suit.
- Distributional hands.
- Hands involving ruffs or long-suit establishment.

---

# What Is a Loser?

A **loser** is a trick the opponents are likely to win if nothing is done to prevent it.

Typical losers include:

- Missing top honors.
- Small cards without support.
- Unprotected honors.

Potential losers may later disappear through good declarer play.

---

# Counting Losers

Count losers in each suit separately.

### Example

```
♠ AKQ
♥ K42
♦ Q73
♣ J64
```

Losers:

- Spades: 0
- Hearts: 2
- Diamonds: 2
- Clubs: 3

Total:

**7 losers**

---

# Example 1 – Suit Contract

Contract

```
4♥
```

Dummy

```
♠ AQ4
♥ K83
♦ AQ74
♣ K82
```

Declarer

```
♠ K75
♥ AQJ64
♦ J62
♣ A43
```

Potential losers:

- Spades: 0
- Hearts: 0
- Diamonds: 1
- Clubs: 0

Total:

**1 loser**

Contract is easily made.

---

# Example 2 – Need to Eliminate Losers

Contract

```
4♠
```

Losers:

- Two diamonds.
- One club.
- One heart.

Total:

**4 losers**

Declarer must eliminate one loser by:

- Ruffing.
- Discarding on a long suit.
- Taking a finesse.
- Executing an endplay.

---

# Methods of Eliminating Losers

Common methods include:

- Ruffing losers.
- Establishing a long suit.
- Discarding on winners.
- Loser-on-Loser play.
- Cross Ruff.
- Dummy Reversal.
- Ruffing Finesse.
- Endplay.
- Squeeze.

---

# Drawing Trumps

After counting losers, decide:

Should trumps be drawn immediately?

Sometimes:

Yes — to prevent defenders from ruffing.

Sometimes:

No — because declarer first needs to ruff losers.

Counting losers helps answer this question.

---

# Planning the Play

Before Trick One:

1. Count losers.
2. Determine how many must disappear.
3. Identify possible elimination methods.
4. Plan the order of play.
5. Preserve entries.

---

# Example 3 – Ruffing Losers

Contract

```
4♠
```

Declarer has:

Two club losers.

Dummy has:

A singleton club.

Declarer plans to:

Ruff one club loser in dummy.

Losers reduced from two to one.

---

# Example 4 – Discarding Losers

Dummy

```
♦ AKQJ7
```

Declarer

```
♣ 864
```

After diamonds are established:

Discard losing clubs.

Losers disappear.

---

# Recount During the Play

After every important trick:

- Recount remaining losers.
- Reevaluate the plan.
- Adjust to new information.

Expert declarers continuously update the loser count.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Counting potential losers that can easily be discarded.
- Forgetting planned ruffs.
- Drawing trumps too early.
- Ignoring entry requirements.
- Failing to recount after establishing a suit.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Prevent ruffs.
- Attack declarer's entries.
- Lead trumps when appropriate.
- Force declarer to ruff high.
- Count declarer's likely losers.

---

# Advantages

- Provides a clear plan.
- Excellent for suit contracts.
- Helps decide when to draw trumps.
- Integrates with most declarer-play techniques.
- Improves timing.

---

# Disadvantages

- Less useful in notrump contracts.
- Requires accurate planning.
- Loser count changes during the play.
- Entries must also be considered.

# Declarer's Checklist

Before Trick One:

- How many losers do I have?
- How many may I afford?
- Which losers can disappear?
- Should I draw trumps?
- Are my entries sufficient?

---

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Count losers.
2. Decide how many must disappear.
3. Choose the best elimination method.
4. Preserve entries.
5. Recount after every major development.

---

# Memory Aid

**Counting Losers = "Lose Less, Make More."**

Remember:

- **Count potential losers before Trick One.**
- **Identify how each loser can disappear.**
- **Do not draw trumps automatically.**
- **Protect your entries while eliminating losers.**
- **Recount after every important development.**

Counting Losers is the foundation of declarer play in suit contracts. Expert players begin by identifying every potential loser, then create a detailed plan to eliminate enough of them through ruffing, discards, long-suit establishment, endplays, squeezes, and other declarer techniques. Accurate loser counting leads to better planning, improved timing, and more successful contracts.
