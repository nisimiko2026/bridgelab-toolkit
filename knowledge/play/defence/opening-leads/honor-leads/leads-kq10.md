# Opening Leads from KQ10

## Overview

The holding **King-Queen-Ten (KQ10)** is a **strong but incomplete honor sequence**. While the King and Queen form a touching sequence, the missing Jack means the suit is not as powerful as **KQJ** or **KQJ10**. Nevertheless, **KQ10** is treated as a standard sequence for opening leads.

In most bridge systems, the correct opening lead is:

```
King
```

The King promises at least the Queen and usually the Ten, while normally denying the Ace.

The guiding principle is:

> **Lead the top of the touching honor sequence to establish the suit and inform partner of your holding.**

---

# Purpose

Opening leads from KQ10 aim to:

- Establish the suit.
- Force declarer's Ace.
- Promote the Ten into a winner.
- Improve defensive communication.
- Help partner recognize the honor combination.

---

# Why Lead the King?

Holding:

```
♠ K Q 10 7 4
```

The King is the standard lead because:

- It is the top of the touching sequence.
- It identifies a strong honor holding.
- It allows partner to interpret the suit correctly.
- It helps establish the suit efficiently.

---

# Standard Expert Treatment

| Holding | Opening Lead |
|----------|--------------|
| KQ | King |
| KQ10 | King |
| KQJ | King |
| KQJ10 | King |

The King generally promises the Queen and may also include the Ten or Jack.

---

# Against Notrump Contracts

## Objectives

- Establish the long suit.
- Force declarer's Ace.
- Promote the Ten into an additional winner.
- Learn whether partner has the Jack or useful length.

### Example

Holding

```
♣ K Q 10 8 4
```

Contract

```
3NT
```

Lead:

```
♣K
```

If declarer wins the Ace, the remaining honors often establish the suit.

---

# Against Suit Contracts

## Objectives

- Cash immediate winners when appropriate.
- Force declarer's Ace.
- Avoid helping declarer establish side suits.
- Look for ruff opportunities if partner has a doubleton.

Example

Holding

```
♥ K Q 10 5
```

Contract

```
4♠
```

Lead:

```
♥K
```

Partner's signal determines whether the suit should continue.

---

# Partner's Signals

The meaning depends on partnership agreement.

## Attitude

Typical agreement:

- High = Encourage.
- Low = Discourage.

Useful when deciding whether to continue the suit.

---

## Count

Some partnerships prefer count after a King lead.

### Standard Count

```
High-Low = Even

Low-High = Odd
```

### UDCA

```
High-Low = Odd

Low-High = Even
```

---

## Suit Preference

When count and attitude are already known, Suit Preference may recommend the best switch.

---

# Example 1 – Establishing the Suit

Holding

```
♦ K Q 10 6
```

Lead:

```
♦K
```

Declarer wins the Ace.

The Queen and Ten later become winners.

---

# Example 2 – Continue

Holding

```
♠ K Q 10 4
```

Partner encourages.

Continue spades.

The defense develops additional tricks.

---

# Example 3 – Switch

Holding

```
♣ K Q 10 5
```

Partner discourages.

Switch to another suit.

Correct timing prevents declarer from gaining tempo.

---

# Planning the Defense

Before leading KQ10, ask:

- Is this my best attacking suit?
- Am I trying to establish the suit?
- Does the auction suggest another lead?
- Should I continue after Trick One?
- Can the Ten become a winner?

---

# Special Considerations

### Missing the Jack

The missing Jack may affect:

- Suit establishment.
- Number of eventual winners.
- Timing.

Do not assume the suit will automatically produce three tricks.

---

### Missing the Ace

The King lead generally indicates the Ace is elsewhere unless the partnership has special agreements.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Leading the Queen instead of the King.
- Automatically cashing multiple honors.
- Ignoring partner's signal.
- Continuing after a discouraging signal.
- Forgetting the auction.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Before play, discuss:

- Does the King always promise the Queen?
- What signal is expected after a King lead?
- Different methods against Notrump and suit contracts.
- When Suit Preference applies.
- Whether Journalist or Rusinow Leads are used.

---

# Advantages

- Standard treatment recognized by most partnerships.
- Helps establish long suits.
- May promote the Ten.
- Improves defensive communication.
- Flexible continuation.

---

# Disadvantages

- Missing the Jack may reduce the suit's strength.
- Incorrect continuation may help declarer.
- Requires partnership agreement on follow-up signals.

# Defender's Checklist

Before leading KQ10:

- Am I leading from a touching sequence?
- Does our partnership lead the King?
- What information do I need from partner?
- Should I continue after the signal?
- Can the Ten become an additional winner?

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Identify the sequence.
2. Lead the King.
3. Read partner's signal.
4. Continue or switch.
5. Reevaluate after every trick.

# Memory Aid

**KQ10 Leads = "Top of the Sequence, Then Listen to Partner."**

Remember:

- **Lead the King from KQ10 in standard opening-lead methods.**
- **The King promises the Queen and often the Ten.**
- **The missing Jack means the suit may require careful continuation.**
- **Use partner's signal to decide whether to continue or switch.**
- **Do not assume the Ten will become a winner without considering the auction and the play.**

Opening leads from **KQ10** are a standard and effective treatment in bridge. By leading the King from this touching sequence and interpreting partner's signal correctly, defenders can establish long-suit tricks, promote the Ten into a winner, and coordinate the defense while avoiding unnecessary continuation when another line of defense offers a better chance of defeating the contract.
