# Opening Leads from AJ10

## Overview

The holding **Ace-Jack-Ten (AJ10)** is an **interior sequence**. Unlike **AKQ**, **KQJ**, or **QJ10**, it does **not** contain touching top honors. As a result, the correct opening lead depends on the contract type and the partnership's lead agreements.

In standard methods:

- **Against Notrump:** Lead the **fourth-best spot card** from length.
- **Against Suit Contracts:** The **Ace** may be led by agreement when seeking count or a possible ruff.
- **Do not normally lead the Jack** in standard opening lead methods.

In partnerships using **Journalist Leads**, the **Ten** may be led from **AJ10x** to show an interior sequence.

The guiding principle is:

> **AJ10 is an interior sequence—avoid treating it like a touching honor sequence.**

---

# Purpose

Opening leads from AJ10 aim to:

- Develop the suit safely.
- Preserve honor strength.
- Obtain information from partner.
- Avoid giving declarer unnecessary tricks.
- Establish long-suit winners.

---

# Understanding the Holding

Example:

```
♠ A J 10 7 4
```

Strengths:

- One immediate winner.
- Strong intermediates.
- Good long-suit potential.

Weaknesses:

- Missing the King.
- Missing the Queen.
- No touching honors.

---

# Standard Expert Treatment

## Against Notrump

The recommended lead is usually:

```
Fourth-best spot card
```

Example

Holding

```
♣ A J 10 8 5
```

Lead

```
♣5
```

Reason:

- Preserve the Ace.
- Avoid exposing the Jack.
- Give the partnership the best chance to establish the suit.

---

## Against Suit Contracts

Leading the Ace is appropriate when:

- The partnership leads Ace from strong holdings.
- You want count from partner.
- A ruff may be available.

Otherwise, another suit may provide a better opening lead.

---

# Alternative Methods

## Journalist Leads

Some partnerships playing Journalist Leads use:

```
10
```

from

```
AJ10x
```

to show an interior sequence.

This treatment is **not** standard and requires explicit partnership agreement.

# Partner's Signals

When the Ace is led, partner typically gives:

## Count

### Standard Count

```
High-Low = Even

Low-High = Odd
```

### UDCA

```
High-Low = Odd

Low-High = Even
```

---

## Attitude

Some partnerships instead request:

- High = Encourage.
- Low = Discourage.

Lead

Partner recognizes the interior sequence.

# Planning the Defense

Before leading AJ10, ask:

- Are we defending Notrump or a suit contract?
- Do we play standard or Journalist Leads?
- Do I need information from partner?
- Am I trying to establish this suit?
- Is another lead more attractive?

---

# Common Mistakes

- Leading the Jack in standard methods.
- Treating AJ10 like KQJ.
- Cashing the Ace automatically.
- Ignoring the auction.
- Forgetting partnership agreements.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Before play, discuss:

- Does the Ace ask for count or attitude?
- Do we use fourth-best leads from AJ10?
- Do we play Journalist Leads?
- When should the Ace be led?
- How do our agreements differ between Notrump and suit contracts?

---

# Advantages

- Good long-suit potential.
- Flexible treatment.
- Fits naturally into fourth-best lead methods.
- Can show an interior sequence in Journalist systems.

---

# Disadvantages

- No touching sequence.
- Requires accurate partnership agreements.
- Incorrect honor leads may help declarer.
