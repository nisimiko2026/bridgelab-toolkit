# Opening Leads from AKJ

## Overview

The holding **Ace-King-Jack (AKJ)** is a powerful but delicate opening lead combination. Unlike **AKQ**, where three touching honors provide an obvious sequence, **AKJ** contains a **broken sequence**. This makes the choice of opening lead more dependent on partnership agreements, the contract type, and the overall defensive plan.

In most modern methods:

- Against **Notrump**, the **Ace** is usually led if the partnership leads Ace from AK(x) and AKJ(x), asking partner for **attitude** or **count**.
- Against **Suit Contracts**, the **Ace** is also the normal lead when looking for count or a possible ruff.
- The **King** is generally **not** led from AKJ unless the partnership has a specific agreement.

The guiding principle is:

> **Lead to gather information first, then decide whether the suit should be continued.**

---

# Purpose

Opening leads from AKJ aim to:

- Cash immediate winners.
- Discover partner's distribution.
- Determine whether the Jack will become a winner.
- Look for ruff opportunities.
- Prevent declarer from establishing side suits.

---

# Understanding the Holding

Example:

```
♠ A K J 7 4
```

Strengths:

- Two immediate winners.
- Excellent chance to establish additional tricks.

Weakness:

- Missing the Queen means the suit may block or require careful timing.

---

# Standard Expert Treatment

| Contract | Typical Lead | Purpose |
|----------|--------------|----------|
| Notrump | Ace | Ask for attitude or count (partnership agreement) |
| Suit Contract | Ace | Obtain count and evaluate a possible ruff |
| Special Agreements | King (rare) | Only if specifically agreed by the partnership |

Most expert partnerships treat **AKJ** exactly like **AK** for opening-lead purposes.

---

# Against Notrump

## Objectives

- Establish the suit quickly.
- Learn whether partner has the Queen or useful length.
- Decide whether to continue.

### Example

Holding

```
♣ A K J 8 5
```

Contract

```
3NT
```

Lead:

```
♣A
```

Partner signals:

- Encourage with the Queen or useful length.
- Discourage with poor support.

Continue or switch according to the signal.

---

# Against Suit Contracts

## Objectives

- Cash immediate winners.
- Discover whether partner has a doubleton.
- Decide whether continuing creates a ruff.

Example

Holding

```
♥ A K J 6
```

Contract

```
4♠
```

Lead:

```
♥A
```

Partner gives count.

If partner shows a doubleton, continue hearts to score a ruff.

---

# Partner's Signals

## Count

Many partnerships request **count** after an Ace lead.

### Standard Count

```
High-Low = Even

Low-High = Odd
```

### UDCA

```
High-Low = Odd

Low-High = Even
```

---

## Attitude

Other partnerships request:

- High = Encourage.
- Low = Discourage.

This helps determine whether continuing the suit is worthwhile.

---

## Suit Preference

After count is known, partner may use Suit Preference to recommend the best switch or return.

---

# Example 1 – Queen Located

Holding

```
♦ A K J 9
```

Partner encourages.

Later the Queen appears from declarer.

The Jack becomes an established winner.

---

# Example 2 – Partner Shows Doubleton

Holding

```
♠ A K J 5
```

Contract

```
4♥
```

Lead:

```
♠A
```

Partner gives count indicating two spades.

Continue the suit.

Partner scores a ruff.

---

# Example 3 – Switch After the Ace

Holding

```
♣ A K J 4
```

Partner discourages.

Instead of cashing the King automatically, switch to another suit.

Correct timing preserves communication and may defeat the contract.

---

# Planning the Defense

Before leading AKJ, ask:

- Do I need count or attitude?
- Am I hoping to establish the Jack?
- Is a ruff possible?
- Should I continue after the first trick?
- Does the auction suggest another lead?

---

# Common Mistakes

- Treating AKJ like AKQ and automatically cashing multiple honors.
- Ignoring partner's signal.
- Continuing after a discouraging signal.
- Forgetting partnership agreements.
- Blocking the suit unnecessarily.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Before play, discuss:

- Does the Ace or King lead from AKJ?
- Does the Ace ask for count or attitude?
- Different methods against Notrump and suit contracts.
- When Suit Preference applies.
- Whether AKJ is treated exactly like AK.

---

# Advantages

- Two immediate winners.
- Excellent information after partner's signal.
- Often establishes the Jack.
- Flexible continuation decisions.
- Strong opening lead against both contract types.

---

# Disadvantages

- Missing the Queen may block the suit.
- Requires accurate partnership agreements.
- Automatic continuation may help declarer.
- Timing is important.

# Defender's Checklist

Before leading AKJ:

- Am I following our partnership agreement?
- Do I want count or attitude?
- Can partner have a doubleton?
- Should I continue after Trick One?
- Will the Jack become a winner?

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Lead the agreed honor.
2. Read partner's signal.
3. Decide whether to continue.
4. Look for ruff opportunities.
5. Reevaluate after every trick.

---

# Comparison

| Holding | Typical Treatment |
|----------|-------------------|
| AKQ | Usually lead the Ace; three top honors |
| AKJ | Usually lead the Ace; broken sequence requiring careful continuation |
| AK | Usually lead the Ace; seek information |
| KQJ | Lead the King from the sequence |

---

# Typical Defensive Process

```
Lead the Ace
↓

Partner Gives Count or Attitude

↓

Evaluate the Signal

↓

Continue or Switch

↓

Develop the Jack if Possible
```

---

# Memory Aid

**AKJ Leads = "Lead for Information, Then Decide."**

Remember:

- **AKJ is a broken sequence, not a solid sequence like AKQ.**
- **Most expert partnerships treat AKJ the same as AK and lead the Ace.**
- **Partner's signal determines whether the suit should be continued.**
- **Do not assume the Jack is an immediate winner—plan to establish it if possible.**
- **Against suit contracts, always watch for opportunities to give partner a ruff.**

Opening leads from **AKJ** require more judgment than leads from AKQ because the missing Queen may affect the suit's development. By leading the agreed honor, interpreting partner's signal, and carefully planning the continuation, defenders can maximize the value of this strong but slightly imperfect holding.
