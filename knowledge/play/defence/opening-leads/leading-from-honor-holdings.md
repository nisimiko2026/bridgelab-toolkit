# Leading from Honor Holdings

## Overview

Leading from honor holdings is one of the most important aspects of opening lead strategy. An honor lead can help establish defensive tricks and communicate valuable information to partner, but it can also give declarer a free finesse or an extra trick if chosen carelessly.

The correct lead depends on:

- The honor combination.
- The contract (notrump or suit).
- The auction.
- Partnership agreements.
- The overall defensive objective.

Understanding **when to lead an honor, which honor to lead, and when to avoid honor leads altogether** is a fundamental defensive skill.

---

# General Principles

When choosing a lead from an honor holding:

1. Follow your partnership's lead agreements.
2. Let the auction guide your choice.
3. Lead from touching honor sequences whenever possible.
4. Avoid unsupported honor leads unless justified.
5. Consider whether the contract is notrump or a suit contract.

---

# Touching Honor Sequences

Touching honors are the safest and most informative honor leads.

Examples:

| Holding | Standard Lead | Rusinow Lead |
|---------|---------------|--------------|
| A K | A | K |
| A K Q | A | K |
| K Q | K | Q |
| K Q J | K | Q |
| Q J | Q | J |
| Q J 10 | Q | J |
| J 10 9 | J | 10 |

These leads:

- Help establish tricks.
- Clearly describe the holding.
- Give partner valuable information.
- Rarely cost a trick.

---

# Broken Honor Holdings

Broken honors are separated by one or more missing honors.

Examples:

```
A Q 8 5
A J 9 4
K J 8 4
Q 10 8 3
J 9 7 4
```

These holdings usually **do not justify** leading the honor.

Instead, partnerships often lead:

- Fourth-best.
- Third-and-Fifth.
- Another agreed spot-card lead.

The goal is to preserve the honor while developing the suit.

---

# Unsupported Honors

Leading from an unsupported honor is often dangerous.

Examples:

```
K 8 5 3
Q 9 6 2
J 10 7 3
```

An unsupported honor may:

- Establish declarer's missing honor.
- Give away a finesse.
- Create an unnecessary trick for declarer.

Unless the auction strongly suggests otherwise, consider a safer lead.

---

# Interior Sequences

Interior sequences contain touching honors below a missing higher honor.

Examples:

```
K J 10 5
Q 10 9 4
J 9 8 3
```

Partnership agreements vary.

Some partnerships lead:

- The top of the interior sequence.
- The lower touching honor.
- A spot card.

Consistency is more important than the chosen method.

---

# Leading an Ace

An ace lead is often appropriate when:

- Holding AK.
- Defending a slam.
- The partnership uses ace leads to request attitude.
- A quick trick may disappear.

Avoid leading a bare ace unless the auction provides a compelling reason.

---

# Leading a King

Common agreements include:

- King from AK.
- King from KQ.
- King asking for count or unblock.

The meaning depends entirely on partnership agreement.

---

# Honor Leads Against Notrump

Honor leads are generally more attractive against notrump because:

- Long suits can be established.
- Declarer cannot ruff winners.
- Immediate development is important.

Touching honor sequences are particularly effective.

---

# Honor Leads Against Suit Contracts

Against suit contracts, caution is often required.

Consider:

- Will the lead establish declarer's honors?
- Is a trump lead safer?
- Would partner's suit be a better attack?
- Is declarer likely to ruff losers?

Passive leads are often preferable to speculative honor leads.

---

# Honor Leads Against Slams

Against slams:

- Avoid giving declarer a free trick.
- Lead an honor only when supported by the auction or partnership agreements.
- Passive and trump leads are frequently safer than unsupported honor leads.

---

# The Auction Matters

The same holding may require different leads depending on the bidding.

Example:

Holding:

```
♠ K J 10 4
```

Against 3NT after partner overcalls spades:

A spade honor lead is attractive.

Against 6♥ after no suit has been bid:

A passive lead may be superior.

Always combine your holding with the information revealed by the auction.

---

# Practical Example 1

Holding:

```
♣ K Q 8 4
```

Standard lead:

```
♣K
```

A classic touching honor sequence.

---

# Practical Example 2

Holding:

```
♦ A Q 8 5
```

Against 3NT

Lead:

```
♦5
```

Fourth-best is usually preferred because the honors are not touching.

---

# Practical Example 3

Holding:

```
♥ K 8 5 2
```

Against 6♠

A passive small-heart lead is usually safer than leading the unsupported king.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Discuss:

- Standard or Rusinow honor leads.
- Ace and king lead meanings.
- Interior sequence treatments.
- Fourth-best or Third-and-Fifth spot-card leads.
- Top of Nothing.
- Suit versus notrump differences.
- Slam-defense agreements.

Clear agreements ensure that partner interprets honor leads correctly.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Leading unsupported honors without a reason.
- Ignoring partnership lead agreements.
- Forgetting that honor-sequence leads take precedence over spot-card leads.
- Using the wrong honor under Rusinow.
- Allowing the holding to dictate the lead while ignoring the auction.

---

# Checklist Before Leading an Honor

Ask yourself:

1. Do I have a touching honor sequence?
2. Is this a broken honor holding?
3. Am I giving declarer a free finesse?
4. Does the auction support an attacking lead?
5. What do our partnership agreements require?
6. Would a passive lead be safer?

# Key Principles

- Lead from touching honor sequences according to your partnership agreement.
- Preserve broken honor holdings by leading a spot card when appropriate.
- Avoid unsupported honor leads unless the auction clearly justifies them.
- The auction is just as important as the cards you hold.
- Consistent partnership agreements are essential for effective defensive communication.
