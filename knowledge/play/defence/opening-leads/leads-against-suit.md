# Leads Against Suit Contracts

## Overview

The opening lead against a suit contract is often the defenders' best opportunity to establish tricks, attack declarer's communication, or prevent declarer from making the contract. Unlike notrump contracts, suit contracts usually involve ruffing, making the choice of opening lead significantly different.

Successful opening leaders combine information from:

- The auction
- Partner's bidding
- Dummy's expected shape
- Vulnerability
- The contract level
- Standard lead agreements

Good opening leads aim to:

- Establish defensive tricks
- Find partner's strength
- Avoid giving declarer unnecessary tricks
- Create ruffing opportunities
- Attack declarer's communications

---

# General Priorities

Typical order of preference:

1. Lead partner's suit.
2. Lead an unbid suit.
3. Passive lead when holding strength but no clear attack.
4. Aggressive lead when the auction suggests declarer has a source of tricks.
5. Trump lead when appropriate.

---

# Leading Partner's Suit

Normally the safest and strongest choice.

Reasons:

- Partner has already shown values.
- Partner often has length.
- Partnership has already identified a source of tricks.

### Lead Style

Typically:

- Top of touching honors
- Fourth-best from length (depending on partnership methods)
- Low from unsupported honors if agreed

Example:

```
♠ K J 10 5

Partner overcalled spades.

Lead:
♠K
```

---

# Lead Through Strength

When dummy is expected to hold honors.

Example:

```
West              East

♠ Q 9 6          ♠ A J 8 5
```

Leading spades attacks declarer's likely honor holdings.

---

# Avoid Leading Into Strength

If declarer has announced a suit strongly:

```
1♥ — 4♥
```

Avoid leading hearts.

You often help declarer establish the suit.

---

# Lead an Unbid Suit

When neither opponent has bid the suit.

Advantages:

- More likely to find partner's values.
- Less likely to establish declarer's suit.
- Often creates immediate defensive tricks.

---

# Passive Leads

Sometimes avoiding damage is best.

Passive leads include:

- Small trump
- Small card from three small
- Low card from a broken sequence

Example:

```
♠ 9 5 2

Lead:
♠2
```

rather than leading away from an unsupported king.

Passive leads are especially attractive against:

- Slam contracts
- Thin games
- Strong declarers

---

# Aggressive Leads

Attack when:

- Defense needs quick tricks.
- Auction shows declarer has many winners.
- Waiting helps declarer.

Examples:

Lead from:

```
♣ K J 9 5
```

or

```
♦ Q J 10 x
```

to establish tricks before declarer can discard losers.

---

# Trump Leads

A trump lead is one of the most important weapons in suit contracts.

Objectives:

- Reduce dummy's ruffing power.
- Prevent crossruffs.
- Cut communication.
- Eliminate ruff-and-discard opportunities.

Trump leads are especially effective when:

- Dummy is expected to have short trumps.
- Opponents have shown two suits.
- Crossruff appears likely.
- Declarer has a long side suit.

### Example

Auction:

```
1♠  Pass
2♦  Pass
4♠
```

Holding:

```
♠ 7 4
```

A trump lead may eliminate dummy's planned ruffs.

---

# Singleton Leads

Leading a singleton aims to obtain a ruff.

Requirements:

- Side entry to regain the lead.
- Partner likely has strength.
- Declarer unlikely to draw trumps immediately.

Example:

```
♣7
```

against 4♠ after partner has bid clubs.

Singleton leads are attractive against:

- High-level suit contracts
- Sacrifices
- Competitive auctions

Avoid singleton leads if declarer is likely to draw trumps immediately.

---

# Doubleton Leads

Some partnerships lead a doubleton seeking a later ruff.

Example:

```
♥8 4
```

Agreement is essential.

---

# Leading From Honor Sequences

Standard choices:

| Holding | Typical Lead |
|----------|--------------|
| AKQ | A |
| AKJ | A |
| KQJ | K |
| QJ10 | Q |
| J109 | J |

Touching honors are generally safe opening leads.

---

# Unsupported Honors

Avoid leading unsupported honors.

Example:

```
♠ K x x
```

Often a poor lead.

Instead choose:

- Partner's suit
- Passive lead
- Trump

---

# Ace Leads

Lead an ace when:

- Holding AK.
- Planning to look at dummy.
- Seeking partner's signal.
- Defending slam.

Many partnerships use ace asks for attitude.

---

# King Leads

Common agreements:

- From AK.
- From KQ.
- Asking for count or unblock.

Partnership agreement is essential.

---

# Leads Against Slams

Objectives differ.

Avoid:

- Giving away tricks.

Prefer:

- Passive leads.
- Safe trump leads.
- Cashing obvious winners only when appropriate.

Singleton leads become much riskier.

---

# Auction-Based Adjustments

## Opponents Bid One Suit Only

Usually attack another suit.

---

## Opponents Bid Two Suits

Trump lead becomes more attractive.

---

## Strong Artificial Auctions

Passive leads often outperform speculative attacks.

---

## Sacrifice Auctions

Aggressive leads become more attractive.

Defense often needs immediate tricks.

---

# When Not to Lead Trumps

Avoid trump leads when:

- You seek a ruff.
- Partner has bid a short suit.
- Declarer likely needs help drawing trumps anyway.
- You have a natural attacking lead.

---

# Common Errors

- Leading away from unsupported kings.
- Automatically leading trumps.
- Ignoring partner's suit.
- Leading declarer's announced suit.
- Trying for miracle leads at matchpoints.
- Giving declarer a free finesse.
- Leading aces without partnership agreement.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Discuss:

- Ace asks for attitude or count.
- King asks.
- Style from AK.
- Lead of singleton honors.
- Lead from interior sequences.
- Lead of doubletons.
- Trump lead philosophy.
- Third- and fifth-best versus fourth-best.
- Rusinow or standard honor leads.

---

# Practical Checklist

Before selecting the opening lead, ask:

1. What did the auction tell me?
2. Has partner bid a suit?
3. Is declarer likely planning a crossruff?
4. Would a trump lead disrupt declarer?
5. Can I establish tricks immediately?
6. Am I leading away from an unsupported honor?
7. Is a passive lead safer?
8. What are my partnership lead agreements?

---

# Key Principles

- Partner's suit is usually the best lead.
- Attack when the defense must establish tricks quickly.
- Choose passive leads when giving away a trick is costly.
- Trump leads can defeat many contracts by preventing ruffs.
- Avoid unsupported honor leads unless clearly justified.
- Let the auction guide your opening lead more than your cards alone.
