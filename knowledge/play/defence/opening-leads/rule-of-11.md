# Rule of 11

## Overview

The **Rule of 11** is a classic bridge calculation used after a **fourth-best opening lead** against a **Notrump contract**. It allows both the leader and partner to determine **how many cards higher than the opening lead are held by the other three hands**.

By combining this calculation with the visible cards in dummy and their own hand, defenders can often identify exactly how many higher cards declarer holds. This helps determine whether to win a trick immediately, duck, or preserve an honor.

The Rule of 11 is one of the most useful defensive counting techniques and is also valuable for declarer.

The guiding principle is:

> **Subtract the opening lead from 11 to determine how many higher cards remain in the other three hands.**

---

# Purpose

The Rule of 11 helps players to:

- Read the opening lead.
- Locate higher cards.
- Decide whether to win or duck.
- Improve defensive communication.
- Predict declarer's remaining cards.
- Make better play decisions.

---

# Requirements

The Rule of 11 applies only when:

- The contract is **Notrump**.
- The opening lead is agreed to be **fourth-best**.
- The partnership uses fourth-best leads from length.

It **does not normally apply** if:

- Third- and fifth-best leads are used.
- Journalist, Rusinow, or other lead conventions are used.
- The lead is from a short suit or an honor sequence.

---

# The Formula

```
11 − Opening Lead = Number of Higher Cards
held by the other three hands.
```

The leader's remaining higher cards are **not** counted because the lead is known to come from that hand.

---

# Example 1 – Opening Lead of the 7

Opening lead:

```
♠7
```

Calculation:

```
11 − 7 = 4
```

There are **4 cards higher than the 7** in the other three hands.

Suppose dummy and East together can see:

```
♠A
♠Q
♠8
```

Three higher cards are visible.

Therefore declarer has:

```
4 − 3 = 1
```

higher spade remaining.

---

# Example 2 – Opening Lead of the 4

Opening lead:

```
♥4
```

Calculation:

```
11 − 4 = 7
```

Dummy and partner can see:

```
♥A
♥K
♥J
♥9
♥6
```

Five higher cards are visible.

Declarer therefore has:

```
7 − 5 = 2
```

higher hearts remaining.

---

# Example 3 – Deciding Whether to Duck

West leads:

```
♦5
```

Dummy

```
♦A J 9
```

East

```
♦Q 8 2
```

Rule of 11:

```
11 − 5 = 6
```

Visible higher cards:

```
A
Q
J
9
8

= 5 cards
```

Therefore declarer has only **one** higher diamond.

East can judge whether winning immediately or ducking is best.

---

# How Defenders Use the Rule

### Leader

The leader can estimate:

- Declarer's length.
- Partner's honor location.
- Whether to continue the suit.

---

### Third Hand

Partner can determine:

- How many higher cards declarer has.
- Whether an honor should be played.
- Whether ducking is correct.

---

# How Declarer Uses the Rule

Declarer can also benefit if the defenders are known to use fourth-best leads.

Declarer may estimate:

- The leader's original length.
- Remaining honors.
- Defensive communication.
- Whether a finesse is likely to succeed.

---

# Planning the Defense

After the opening lead, ask:

- Is this a genuine fourth-best lead?
- What is the Rule of 11 result?
- How many higher cards are visible?
- How many remain with declarer?
- Should I win or duck?

---

# Common Situations

The Rule of 11 is especially useful:

- Against Notrump contracts.
- On low opening leads.
- When deciding whether to play an honor.
- When reading declarer's holding.
- During the first few tricks.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Applying the Rule when the lead is not fourth-best.
- Forgetting visible spot cards.
- Ignoring the auction.
- Ignoring partnership lead agreements.
- Assuming the calculation proves the exact layout.

---

# Advantages

- Simple and easy to remember.
- Provides immediate information.
- Improves defensive accuracy.
- Helps locate declarer's higher cards.
- Useful for both declarer and defenders.

---

# Disadvantages

- Applies only to fourth-best leads.
- Gives probabilities, not complete certainty.
- Can be defeated by deceptive opening leads.
- Less useful with modern alternative lead methods.

# Memory Aid

**Rule of 11 = "Subtract, Count, Decide."**

Remember:

- **The Rule of 11 works only after a genuine fourth-best opening lead.**
- **Subtract the spot card led from 11.**
- **Count all higher cards visible in dummy and your own hand.**
- **The remainder tells you how many higher cards declarer holds.**
- **Combine the Rule of 11 with the auction, counting, and partnership agreements for the best results.**

The Rule of 11 is one of bridge's classic defensive tools. Although modern partnerships may use different opening lead styles, it remains an elegant and practical method for interpreting fourth-best leads. When used together with distribution counting and High Card Point counting, it helps both defenders and declarer make more informed decisions throughout the play.
