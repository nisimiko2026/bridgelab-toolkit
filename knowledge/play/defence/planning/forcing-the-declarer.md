# Forcing the Declarer

## Overview

**Forcing the declarer** is a defensive technique in trump contracts in which the defenders repeatedly lead a suit that the declarer must ruff. Every forced ruff consumes one of declarer's trump cards, reducing declarer's trump length and often preventing the declarer from using trumps for drawing opponents' trumps, ruffing losers, or establishing long suits.

The technique is most effective when:
- Declarer begins with only a small trump advantage.
- Declarer plans to use dummy for multiple ruffs.
- Declarer has not yet drawn the defenders' trumps.
- Defenders can repeatedly regain the lead.

---

# Objectives

A successful forcing defense aims to:

- Shorten declarer's trump holding.
- Eliminate declarer's ability to ruff losers.
- Reduce declarer's entries.
- Promote defenders' trumps into winners.
- Disrupt declarer's timing.

---

# Basic Principle

Every compulsory ruff costs declarer one trump.

If declarer expected to use those trumps later for:
- ruffing side-suit losers,
- drawing defenders' trumps,
- maintaining trump control,

then each forced ruff makes the contract more difficult.

---

# Typical Situation

Suppose declarer has:

```
Declarer: 6 trumps
Dummy:     3 trumps
Defenders: 4 trumps each
```

Declarer intends to:

1. Draw two rounds of trumps.
2. Ruff two losers in dummy.

If defenders force declarer to ruff twice before trumps are drawn, declarer now has only four remaining trumps and may no longer have sufficient trump control to execute the original plan.

---

# When the Technique Works Best

Forcing is strongest when:

- Declarer has only one extra trump over defenders.
- Dummy is short in the forcing suit.
- Defenders have several winners in the forcing suit.
- Declarer must ruff high.
- Defenders can regain the lead repeatedly.

---

# Classic Example

Contract: **4♠**

```
North (Dummy)
♠ 84
♥ AK72
♦ KJ6
♣ AQ93

South (Declarer)
♠ AKJ763
♥ 5
♦ AQ74
♣ J82
```

West leads ♥Q.

Instead of switching after winning later tricks, East and West continue leading hearts whenever they regain the lead.

Declarer repeatedly ruffs hearts.

Eventually:

- declarer runs short of trumps,
- cannot draw all opposing trumps,
- loses control,
- cannot ruff remaining diamond losers.

The forcing defense defeats the contract.

---

# The Uppercut

One powerful form of forcing is the **uppercut**.

Defenders lead a suit that forces declarer to ruff high.

A defender overruffs or promotes partner's trump into a winner.

Example:

```
Dummy
♠ K84

Declarer
♠ AQJ

West
♠ 1095

East
♠ 762
```

A forcing lead requires declarer to ruff with the queen.

West later scores the 10 because declarer's higher trump has been consumed.

---

# Forcing Dummy Instead

Sometimes defenders force dummy rather than declarer.

This can:

- eliminate dummy's long trumps,
- remove ruffing entries,
- destroy crossruff plans.

Although less common, it is equally effective when dummy is the long-trump hand.

---

# Recognizing Declarer's Plan

Look for clues such as:

- Declarer postpones drawing trumps.
- Dummy contains several short suits.
- Declarer immediately starts ruffing.
- Declarer preserves high trumps.
- Crossruff appears likely.

These usually indicate that forcing defense may succeed.

---

# Requirements for Successful Forcing

The defense usually needs:

- An established side suit to continue leading.
- Communication between defenders.
- Entries to regain the lead.
- Accurate counting of declarer's trumps.

Without entries, forcing often fails because defenders cannot continue attacking.

---

# Counting Trumps

Effective forcing depends on accurate counting.

Track:

- Declarer's original trump length.
- Number of forced ruffs.
- Number of normal trump plays.
- Remaining trump balance.

Often the defense should continue forcing until declarer's trump length equals or falls below the defenders'.

---

# When Not to Force

Avoid forcing when:

- Declarer has overwhelming trump length.
- Declarer is happy to ruff.
- Declarer was going to lose those trumps anyway.
- You have cashing winners that should be taken immediately.
- Switching attacks a more vulnerable suit.

Forcing simply because it is possible is often a mistake.

---

# Common Defensive Errors

### Forcing Too Late

If declarer has already drawn trumps, forcing is impossible.

---

### Switching Too Soon

After beginning the forcing defense, changing suits often allows declarer to recover.

---

### Ignoring Communication

The forcing suit is useless if defenders cannot regain the lead.

---

### Helping Declarer

Occasionally repeated forcing removes declarer's losers while preserving entries.

Always visualize the entire hand before committing.

---

# Defensive Signals

Partner should help identify forcing opportunities by:

- encouraging continuation of the forcing suit,
- giving count where appropriate,
- preserving entries,
- avoiding unnecessary ruffs.

---

# Relationship to Other Techniques

Forcing often combines with:

- Trump Promotion
- Uppercut
- Tap
- Shortening Declarer's Trumps
- Ruff and Overruff
- Crossruff Defense
- Entry Management
- Active Defense

---

# Practical Tips

- Count declarer's trumps from the auction and play.
- Force before declarer draws trumps.
- Continue the forcing suit whenever practical.
- Preserve entries needed for additional forcing.
- Do not abandon the forcing attack prematurely.
- Watch whether declarer's trump advantage is disappearing.
- Consider forcing whenever declarer delays drawing trumps.

---

# Key Takeaways

- Forcing the declarer shortens declarer's trump holding.
- Every forced ruff is a defensive gain if declarer needed those trumps later.
- The technique is strongest before trumps are drawn.
- Success depends on entries, communication, and accurate trump counting.
- Well-executed forcing frequently transforms an otherwise making contract into a defeated one.
