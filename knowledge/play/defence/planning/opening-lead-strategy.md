# Opening Lead Strategy

## Overview

The **opening lead** is often the most important defensive decision in a bridge hand. Before dummy appears, the opening leader has more influence on the outcome of the contract than at any other point during the play.

A successful opening lead can:

- Defeat a contract immediately.
- Establish a long suit.
- Create a ruff.
- Disrupt declarer's communication.
- Prevent a squeeze or endplay.

A poor opening lead may give declarer an extra trick or even the entire contract.

The guiding principle is:

> **Lead actively when the defense must attack. Lead passively when declarer is likely to establish tricks on their own.**

---

# Purpose

A good opening lead aims to:

- Establish defensive winners.
- Attack declarer's weak suits.
- Prevent declarer's plan.
- Give partner information.
- Create communication between defenders.
- Avoid giving away unnecessary tricks.

---

# Information Available

Before making the opening lead, consider:

- The auction.
- Vulnerability.
- Matchpoints or IMPs.
- Partner's bidding.
- Opponents' bidding.
- Your own holding.
- Lead agreements.

The auction usually provides more information than the cards themselves.

---

# General Priorities

A useful order of preference is:

1. Lead partner's suit.
2. Lead from a strong sequence.
3. Lead your longest and strongest suit against Notrump.
4. Look for a ruff against suit contracts.
5. Choose a passive lead when attacking is dangerous.

---

# Opening Leads Against Notrump

## Primary Objectives

- Establish a long suit.
- Force declarer to lose the lead.
- Cash winners before declarer develops theirs.

### Recommended Leads

- Fourth-best from the longest and strongest suit.
- Top of an honor sequence.
- Partner's bid suit.

Avoid:

- Unsupported Aces.
- Unsupported Kings.
- Leading away from unsupported honors.

---

# Opening Leads Against Suit Contracts

## Primary Objectives

- Cash immediate winners.
- Obtain ruffs.
- Force declarer.
- Prevent side-suit establishment.

Recommended leads:

- Singleton (when a ruff is likely).
- Partner's suit.
- Honor sequence.
- Ace from AK (by partnership agreement).

Avoid:

- Leading away from unsupported Aces.
- Leading unsupported Queens or Kings.
- Giving declarer an unnecessary finesse.

---

# Leading Partner's Suit

One of the safest principles.

Advantages:

- Partner has indicated strength.
- Improves partnership communication.
- Frequently establishes tricks quickly.

Exception:

Do not lead partner's suit if:

- Dummy's bidding strongly suggests declarer controls it.
- Another lead is clearly superior.

---

# Leading from Honor Sequences

Standard methods:

| Holding | Typical Lead |
|----------|--------------|
| AKQ | Ace |
| AK | Ace (modern methods) |
| KQJ | King |
| KQ10 | King |
| QJ10 | Queen |
| J109 | Jack |

Lead the **top of a touching sequence**.

---

# Fourth-Best Leads

Against Notrump, the standard lead from a long suit is:

```
Fourth-highest card
```

Example:

```
♠ K 9 7 4 2

Lead:

♠4
```

This helps partner apply the Rule of 11 and estimate the leader's length.

---

# Interior Sequences

Examples:

```
AQ10
AJ10
KJ10
```

Many partnerships lead:

- Fourth-best (standard methods).
- Ten (Journalist methods).

Agreement is essential.

---

# Active vs. Passive Leads

## Active Lead

Purpose:

- Establish tricks immediately.
- Attack declarer's communication.

Examples:

- Honor sequence.
- Long suit.
- Singleton.

Best when:

- Defense must act quickly.

---

## Passive Lead

Purpose:

- Avoid giving declarer a trick.

Examples:

- Small card from a safe suit.
- Avoid leading away from unsupported honors.

Best when:

- Declarer appears to have multiple losers.

---

# Singleton Leads

Often correct against suit contracts.

Objectives:

- Obtain a ruff.
- Shorten the leader's hand.
- Disrupt declarer.

Requirements:

- Partner should be able to gain the lead.

Do **not** lead a singleton against Notrump.

---

# Leading Trumps

A trump lead may be correct when:

- Declarer plans a Cross Ruff.
- Dummy has many side-suit losers.
- Preventing ruffs is critical.

---

# The Auction Matters

Examples:

If declarer has:

```
1NT – 3NT
```

Lead your strongest suit.

If partner overcalled:

```
1♠
```

Lead partner's suit unless another lead is clearly superior.

---

# Planning the Defense

Before leading, ask:

- What is declarer's likely plan?
- What is my partnership agreement?
- Is partner's suit available?
- Can I establish a long suit?
- Should I attack or defend passively?

---

# Common Mistakes

- Leading unsupported honors.
- Ignoring the auction.
- Forgetting partnership agreements.
- Automatically leading the longest suit.
- Choosing an active lead when a passive lead is better.
- Forgetting vulnerability and scoring method.

---

# Opening Lead Checklist

Before placing the first card on the table:

- Review the auction.
- Identify declarer's likely strengths.
- Consider partner's bidding.
- Determine the contract type.
- Apply your partnership's lead agreements.
- Decide between an active or passive lead.

---

# Advantages of Good Opening Leads

- May defeat the contract immediately.
- Gives partner valuable information.
- Establishes defensive communication.
- Prevents declarer's plan.
- Creates opportunities for ruffs.

# Memory Guide

## Seven Questions

1. What did the auction tell me?
2. What contract am I defending?
3. Should I lead partner's suit?
4. Do I have a touching honor sequence?
5. Should I attack or defend passively?
6. What information do I want partner to receive?
7. What is declarer's most likely plan?

---

# Comparison

| Situation | Preferred Lead |
|-----------|----------------|
| Partner bid a suit | Usually partner's suit |
| Notrump | Longest and strongest suit |
| Suit contract | Ruff, sequence, or partner's suit |
| Touching honors | Top of the sequence |
| Dangerous contract | Passive lead |
| Cross Ruff threat | Trump lead |

# Memory Aid

**Opening Lead Strategy = "Think Before You Lead."**

Remember:

- **The auction is your most valuable source of information.**
- **Against Notrump, establish long suits.**
- **Against suit contracts, consider ruffs and trump leads.**
- **Lead the top of touching honor sequences.**
- **Choose between active and passive leads based on declarer's likely plan.**
- **A successful opening lead often determines the outcome of the entire hand.**

Opening Lead Strategy is the foundation of successful defense. By combining information from the auction, sound partnership agreements, and careful analysis of the contract, defenders can select opening leads that maximize their chances of defeating the contract while minimizing the risk of giving declarer an unnecessary advantage.
