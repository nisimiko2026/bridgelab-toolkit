# Counting Declarer's Hand

## Overview

**Counting Declarer's Hand** is one of the most important defensive techniques in bridge. Skilled defenders continuously reconstruct declarer's distribution, high-card holdings, and remaining cards by combining information from the auction, the opening lead, dummy, and every card played.

Rather than defending one trick at a time, expert defenders develop a complete picture of declarer's hand. This enables them to find winning switches, avoid giving away tricks, recognize endplay and squeeze positions, and defeat contracts that would otherwise succeed.

The guiding principle is:

> **Every card played reveals information. Count the entire hand—not just the current trick.**

---

# Purpose

Counting Declarer's Hand allows defenders to:

- Determine declarer's distribution.
- Locate missing honors.
- Predict declarer's remaining cards.
- Recognize declarer's plan.
- Choose the correct defensive switch.
- Prevent squeezes and endplays.
- Coordinate effectively with partner.

---

# Basic Principle

Begin with the information from:

- The auction.
- Dummy.
- The opening lead.

Then update your picture after every trick.

By the end of the hand, defenders should know declarer's exact distribution.

---

# Sources of Information

## The Auction

The bidding may reveal:

- Suit length.
- Balanced or unbalanced shape.
- Strength.
- Fit.
- Short suits.
- Likely honor locations.

Example:

```
1NT – 3NT
```

Strongly suggests a balanced hand.

---

## Dummy

When dummy appears, count:

- Total points.
- Suit lengths.
- Shortages.
- Side suits.
- Entries.

Compare dummy with the auction.

---

## Opening Lead

The opening lead may reveal:

- Length.
- Honor sequences.
- Partnership lead style.
- Defensive plan.

---

## Cards Played

Each card narrows the possibilities.

Observe:

- Suit following.
- Discards.
- Signals.
- Ruffing.
- Honor plays.

---

# Step-by-Step Counting

### Step 1 – Estimate Declarer's Shape

Example:

Auction suggests:

```
5♠
4♥
2♦
2♣
```

As the play continues, confirm or revise the estimate.

---

### Step 2 – Count Each Suit

Suppose declarer has shown:

- Five spades.
- Four hearts.
- Two diamonds.

Declarer must therefore have:

```
2 clubs
```

because every hand contains exactly **13 cards**.

---

### Step 3 – Count High-Card Points

Subtract:

- Dummy's points.
- Your points.
- Partner's known points.

The remainder belongs to declarer.

This helps locate missing honors.

---

### Step 4 – Count Remaining Cards

After several tricks, ask:

- How many trumps remain?
- How many cards remain in each side suit?
- Which defender still guards each suit?

---

# Example 1 – Counting Distribution

Auction

```
1♠ – 2♠ – 4♠
```

Dummy shows:

```
3 spades
```

Declarer later ruffs two clubs.

Now you know declarer began with:

- Five spades.
- Singleton club.

The remaining suit lengths become much easier to determine.

---

# Example 2 – Locating an Honor

Dummy

```
♦ AQJ
```

You hold:

```
♦ K74
```

The auction indicates declarer opened **1NT**.

Declarer is much more likely to hold the missing ♦10 than partner.

Your defense should reflect this information.

---

# Example 3 – Predicting the Endgame

By Trick 9 you know declarer started with:

```
5-3-3-2
```

Only one unknown card remains.

Declarer's final position becomes completely predictable.

You can defend accordingly.

---

# Counting Trumps

Always know:

- Original trump length.
- Remaining trump length.
- Whether declarer is shortening.
- Whether a Trump Coup or Cross Ruff is possible.

Trump counting is essential in suit contracts.

---

# Recognizing Declarer's Plan

Counting helps identify whether declarer intends to:

- Ruff losers.
- Establish a long suit.
- Take a finesse.
- Execute a squeeze.
- Perform an endplay.
- Cross Ruff.
- Dummy Reversal.

Early recognition improves defensive decisions.

---

# Defensive Planning

After each trick, ask:

- What have I learned?
- Which suit lengths are now known?
- Where are the remaining honors?
- What is declarer's likely plan?
- Should I switch suits?

---

# Common Mistakes

- Counting only one suit.
- Ignoring the auction.
- Forgetting previous discards.
- Losing track of trumps.
- Defending trick by trick instead of hand by hand.

---

# Partnership Cooperation

Both defenders should:

- Count declarer's shape.
- Interpret signals consistently.
- Exchange information through legal carding methods.
- Recognize when declarer's plan changes.

---

# Advantages

- Improves defensive accuracy.
- Helps locate missing honors.
- Predicts declarer's remaining cards.
- Prevents squeezes and endplays.
- Leads to better defensive switches.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires concentration.
- Easy to lose count.
- New information may require revising earlier conclusions.
- Demands good memory.

- How many trumps remain?

- What is the best defensive switch?

# Memory Guide

## Six Steps

1. Analyze the auction.
2. Count dummy's distribution.
3. Estimate declarer's shape.
4. Track every suit.
5. Count remaining trumps.
6. Revise your picture after every trick.

# Memory Aid

**Counting Declarer's Hand = "Every Card Tells a Story."**

Remember:

- **Start counting as soon as dummy appears.**
- **Use the auction, opening lead, and every card played.**
- **Keep track of declarer's distribution and remaining trumps.**
- **Update your picture after every trick.**
- **Expert defenders defend the entire hand, not just the current trick.**

Counting Declarer's Hand is one of the defining skills of expert defense. By continuously reconstructing declarer's distribution, honor locations, and overall plan, defenders gain the information needed to make accurate switches, avoid costly errors, and defeat contracts that would otherwise succeed.
