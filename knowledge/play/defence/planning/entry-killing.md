# Entry Killing Technique

## Overview

**Entry Killing** is an advanced declarer or defensive technique in which a player deliberately **removes or destroys an important entry** to another hand. By eliminating that entry, the player prevents the opponent from reaching established winners or executing a planned line of play.

Entry Killing is closely related to **Merrimac Coup**, **Scissors Coup**, **Hold-Up Play**, and **Entry Management**, but its focus is broader: **any play whose primary objective is to eliminate a vital entry.**

The guiding principle is:

> **Destroy the entry before it can be used.**

---

# Purpose

Entry Killing allows a player to:

- Cut communication between hands.
- Strand established winners.
- Prevent the establishment of long suits.
- Stop squeezes or endplays.
- Improve declarer's or defenders' timing.

---

# Basic Principle

A player deliberately forces an important entry to disappear before it can be used.

The destroyed entry may belong to:

- Declarer.
- Dummy.
- One defender.
- The defensive partnership.

Once the entry is gone, valuable winners become inaccessible.

---

# Types of Entry Killing

## 1. Declarer Kills a Defensive Entry

Declarer removes a defender's only entry to partner's established suit.

This is common in:

- Merrimac Coup.
- Scissors Coup.
- Hold-Up Play.

---

## 2. Defense Kills Dummy's Entry

The defenders remove dummy's outside entry before declarer establishes a long suit.

Result:

Dummy's winners become stranded.

---

## 3. Defense Kills Declarer's Entry

The defenders attack declarer's communication by forcing declarer to use an important entry too early.

Later, declarer cannot reach established winners.

---

## 4. Declarer Kills Dummy's Blocking Entry

Declarer intentionally cashes or unblocks an honor that would otherwise block communication.

This often occurs during:

- Vienna Coup.
- Entry Management.
- Long-suit establishment.

---

# Example 1 – Declarer Removes a Defensive Entry

## Contract

```
3NT
```

West has:

```
♣ KQJ10
```

East has:

```
♣ A
```

The ♣A is West's only outside entry.

Declarer forces East to win the Ace early.

West's club winners later become unreachable.

---

# Example 2 – Defense Removes Dummy's Entry

Dummy

```
♦ AKQ
```

The only outside entry is:

```
♥A
```

The defenders immediately force out the ♥A.

Later, dummy's diamonds are established but cannot be reached.

---

# Example 3 – Killing Declarer's Entry

Declarer plans to establish clubs.

The defenders recognize that dummy has only one outside entry.

They immediately attack that entry.

When the clubs are established, dummy is isolated.

---

# Planning the Play

Before attempting Entry Killing, ask:

- Which hand has the dangerous winners?
- What is the critical entry?
- Can I eliminate it?
- Is the sacrifice worthwhile?
- Will communication be permanently broken?

---

# Timing

Timing is essential.

Typical sequence:

1. Identify the critical entry.
2. Force it to be used.
3. Preserve your own communication.
4. Establish your winners.
5. Exploit the isolated hand.

Waiting too long often allows the opponents to use the entry first.

---

# Why It Works

Bridge hands often depend on:

- One vital entry.
- One outside honor.
- One carefully preserved card.

Destroying that single entry can completely change the outcome of the hand.

---

# Common Situations

Entry Killing frequently occurs:

- In Notrump contracts.
- During long-suit establishment.
- Before squeeze endings.
- In Merrimac Coup positions.
- During Hold-Up Play.
- In defensive attacks on dummy.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Misidentifying the key entry.
- Destroying your own communication.
- Waiting too long.
- Failing to count remaining entries.
- Ignoring alternative winning lines.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Against Entry Killing, players should:

- Preserve multiple entries.
- Unblock honors early when necessary.
- Recognize attacks on communication.
- Establish winners before entries disappear.
- Avoid depending on a single outside entry.

---

# Advantages

- Neutralizes established winners.
- Breaks communication.
- Prevents long-suit cashing.
- Supports many advanced techniques.
- Effective for both declarer and defenders.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires accurate counting.
- Timing is critical.
- Often depends on precise visualization.
- Missing the correct entry may waste the opportunity.

# Memory Aid

**Entry Killing = "Destroy the Bridge Before They Cross It."**

Remember:

- **Every long suit needs an entry.**
- **Identify the single card that provides communication.**
- **Remove that entry before the winners are established or cashed.**
- **Protect your own entries while attacking your opponents'.**
- **Many famous coups, including the Merrimac Coup and Scissors Coup, are specialized forms of Entry Killing.**

Entry Killing is one of the fundamental concepts of expert bridge. Whether used by declarer to isolate the defenders' long suit or by defenders to strand dummy's winners, eliminating a critical entry can completely change the course of a hand. Players who recognize entry positions early gain a significant strategic advantage in both declarer play and defense.
