# Defensive Planning

## Overview

Successful defence begins long before the opening lead.

Expert defenders continually develop, execute, and revise a defensive plan
throughout the play. Every bid, every card played, every signal, and every trick
provides new information that influences the best defensive strategy.

Unlike declarer, defenders possess incomplete information and must cooperate
with partner through legal communication, logical inference, and sound
judgement.

Defensive planning combines

- auction analysis,
- opening lead selection,
- counting,
- signalling,
- communication,
- timing,
- inference,
- partnership cooperation.

---

# Objectives

After studying this section, the reader should be able to

- formulate a defensive plan before the opening lead,
- recognize declarer's likely plan,
- identify defensive priorities,
- count declarer's tricks,
- determine when to attack or defend passively,
- revise the plan as new information appears,
- coordinate effectively with partner.

# Design Principles

## Build a Plan Before Trick One

The best defenders begin planning before making the opening lead.

---

## Count Constantly

Every defensive plan depends upon

- counting,
- inference,
- communication,
- partnership agreements.

---

## Think Like Declarer

The easiest way to defeat declarer is first to understand declarer's intended
line of play.

---

## Adapt Continuously

A defensive plan should evolve as new information becomes available.
