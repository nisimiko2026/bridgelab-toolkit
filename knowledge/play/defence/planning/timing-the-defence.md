# Timing the Defence

## Overview

**Timing the defence** is the art of choosing **when** to attack, cash winners, switch suits, force declarer, or hold up. In many contracts, the success of the defense depends less on *what* is played than on *when* it is played.

Strong defenders constantly evaluate the tempo of the hand. They recognize when to:

- attack immediately,
- wait for more information,
- preserve entries,
- delay cashing winners,
- or force declarer into acting first.

Good timing transforms individual defensive techniques into a coordinated plan.

---

# Objectives

Effective defensive timing aims to:

- Develop enough defensive tricks before declarer establishes winners.
- Preserve communication between defenders.
- Prevent declarer from gaining extra entries.
- Attack at the moment of greatest impact.
- Avoid wasting winners or entries.

---

# Basic Principle

Every defensive play should support an overall plan.

Before playing each card, ask:

- **What is declarer trying to accomplish?**
- **What must the defense do first?**
- **Can this wait, or must it be done now?**

The correct timing often determines whether the defense succeeds.

---

# Establishing a Defensive Plan

After the opening lead and dummy's appearance:

1. Count declarer's likely winners.
2. Count defensive winners.
3. Identify declarer's probable plan.
4. Determine whether the defense must attack immediately or can defend passively.
5. Preserve the entries needed to execute the plan.

Without a clear plan, defenders often lose control of the hand.

---

# When to Attack Immediately

Immediate action is required when declarer threatens to establish additional tricks.

Examples include:

- attacking declarer's vulnerable suit,
- knocking out a key stopper,
- forcing declarer before trumps are drawn,
- promoting trump tricks,
- cashing winners before they disappear.

Delaying these actions may allow declarer to take control.

---

# When to Wait

Patience is often the winning strategy when:

- declarer must guess correctly,
- the defense already has enough tricks,
- an attacking switch may give away a trick,
- additional information is needed,
- declarer has entry problems.

Waiting forces declarer to solve the difficult problems.

---

# Timing Winners

A common mistake is cashing winners too early.

Instead, consider whether a winner should be preserved:

- as an entry,
- to interrupt declarer's communications,
- to force declarer later,
- or to maintain flexibility.

A delayed winner is often more valuable than an immediate one.

---

# Preserving Communication

Defensive timing depends heavily on entries.

Before cashing a winner, ask:

- Will partner still be able to reach my hand?
- Am I using my only entry?
- Can I regain the lead later?

Many defensive opportunities disappear because entries are exhausted too soon.

---

# Timing Suit Switches

Changing suits is often the most important defensive decision.

A switch should occur when:

- the current suit has achieved its objective,
- another attack is clearly stronger,
- declarer has revealed useful information,
- communication permits the change.

Avoid switching merely because the original suit appears unproductive.

---

# Timing Against Trump Contracts

In suit contracts, timing often revolves around trump control.

The defense must decide whether to:

- continue forcing declarer,
- switch to trumps,
- promote trump tricks,
- cash side winners,
- or attack declarer's entries.

The correct sequence is often critical.

---

# Timing Against Notrump Contracts

Against notrump, timing usually focuses on:

- establishing long suits,
- knocking out stoppers,
- preserving entries,
- avoiding premature cashing,
- attacking before declarer establishes winners.

Often the defense has only one opportunity to establish a suit before declarer gains control.

---

# Hold-Up Timing

Sometimes defenders deliberately delay winning a trick.

Reasons include:

- breaking declarer's communications,
- removing entries,
- preventing blocked suits,
- disrupting declarer's timing.

Correctly timed hold-up plays are among the strongest defensive weapons.

---

# Timing Trump Promotion

Trump promotion frequently depends entirely on timing.

The defense must:

1. retain high trumps,
2. regain the lead,
3. force declarer appropriately,
4. promote partner's trump at exactly the right moment.

Too early or too late, and the opportunity disappears.

---

# Reading Declarer's Timing

Declarer's actions often reveal the intended plan.

Watch for:

- delaying the trump suit,
- preserving entries,
- ducking tricks,
- attacking a particular suit repeatedly,
- unusual pauses before key plays.

These clues help defenders anticipate future play.

---

# Common Timing Errors

## Cashing Winners Too Early

Early winners may destroy defensive communication.

---

## Waiting Too Long

Delaying an attack may allow declarer to establish the needed tricks.

---

## Using the Wrong Entry

An unnecessary winner may consume the only entry needed later.

---

## Switching Prematurely

Abandoning an attack before it succeeds often helps declarer.

---

## Ignoring Tempo

Every unnecessary defensive trick may give declarer the extra tempo needed to complete the contract.

---

# Practical Questions

Before every important defensive play, ask:

- Must I attack now?
- Can this winner serve as a later entry?
- Does partner still have communication?
- Is declarer running out of entries?
- Should I force declarer now or later?
- Will waiting create a better opportunity?

These questions help maintain the correct defensive tempo.

# Practical Tips

- Develop a complete defensive plan before trick two whenever possible.
- Count both defensive entries and declarer's entries.
- Cash winners only when they are at their maximum value.
- Attack immediately only when delay benefits declarer.
- Use passive defense when declarer must solve the remaining problems.
- Continuously update your plan as new information becomes available.
- Remember that excellent defense depends on timing as much as card technique.

---

# Key Takeaways

- Timing is one of the most important aspects of successful defense.
- The best defensive play is often determined by **when** it is made rather than **which** card is played.
- Preserve entries and communication whenever possible.
- Balance patience with timely aggression based on declarer's plan.
- Well-timed defense consistently defeats contracts that would otherwise succeed.
