# Preventing Endplays

## Overview

An **endplay** (also called a **throw-in**) occurs when declarer deliberately forces a defender to win a trick and then make a lead that benefits declarer. The defender may be compelled to:

- lead away from an honor,
- concede a ruff-and-sluff,
- break a critical suit,
- or provide a free finesse.

**Preventing an endplay** requires defenders to recognize declarer's intentions early and take actions that deny the favorable ending before it develops.

Unlike defending against immediate threats, preventing an endplay often involves planning **several tricks in advance**.

---

# Objectives

The defense seeks to:

- Avoid being forced into an unfavorable lead.
- Preserve safe exit cards.
- Interrupt declarer's elimination play.
- Maintain communication between defenders.
- Force declarer to solve suit combinations independently.

---

# Basic Principle

An endplay succeeds only if three conditions are met:

1. Declarer removes the defenders' safe exit cards.
2. Declarer forces a defender to win a trick.
3. That defender has no harmless return.

Prevent the endplay by disrupting **any one** of these conditions.

---

# Recognizing the Warning Signs

Declarer may be preparing an endplay when they:

- Draw trumps early.
- Cash several side suits without obvious purpose.
- Eliminate one or more suits from both hands.
- Delay taking certain winners.
- Duck tricks unexpectedly.
- Preserve entries for the late play.

These actions often indicate that declarer is building toward an endgame position.

---

# Preserve Safe Exit Cards

A **safe exit card** allows a defender to return the lead without helping declarer.

Examples include:

- Returning declarer's established suit.
- Leading a suit where declarer already controls all honors.
- Leading a completely exhausted suit.

Avoid wasting these cards too early.

---

# Cash Winners at the Right Time

Holding up a winner is not always correct.

Sometimes a defender should immediately cash an ace or king before declarer can:

- eliminate the suit,
- remove partner's entries,
- or convert the winner into a dangerous entry.

Correct timing often prevents the entire endplay.

---

# Exit Before Declarer Is Ready

If you expect to lose the lead later, consider making a harmless exit while it is still available.

Typical safe exits include:

- Returning declarer's suit.
- Continuing partner's established suit.
- Switching to trumps before declarer finishes a crossruff.
- Leading a completely safe side suit.

A timely exit may destroy declarer's elimination plan.

---

# Preserve Communication

Communication between defenders is essential.

Avoid:

- using the only entry unnecessarily,
- blocking partner's suit,
- cashing winners that eliminate later entries.

Without communication, defenders often become easy endplay targets.

---

# Avoid Becoming the Target Defender

Declarer usually chooses the defender with the weakest exit cards.

To avoid this:

- keep flexible low cards,
- preserve at least one harmless return,
- avoid shortening every safe suit,
- recognize which defender declarer is trying to isolate.

Sometimes retaining an insignificant low card prevents the entire endplay.

---

# Prevent Elimination Play

Most endplays begin with an elimination.

Declarer attempts to remove every safe exit by:

1. Drawing trumps.
2. Cashing side suits.
3. Eliminating remaining side-suit cards.
4. Throwing in a defender.

The defense should interrupt this sequence whenever possible by:

- cashing winners,
- exiting safely,
- maintaining communication,
- avoiding unnecessary discards.

---

# Typical Example

Suppose West holds:

```
♠ Kx
♥ Jx
♣ xx
```

Dummy holds:

```
♠ AQ
♥ AQ
♣ —
```

Declarer strips clubs and diamonds, then exits with a club.

West wins and must lead:

- a spade into **AQ**, or
- a heart into **AQ**.

Either return gives declarer an additional trick.

Had West preserved a safe club exit earlier—or exited safely before the elimination was complete—the endplay would have failed.

---

# Endplays in Trump Contracts

In suit contracts, endplays frequently produce:

- Ruff-and-sluff positions.
- Leads into tenaces.
- Trump promotions.
- Entry destruction.
- Forced leads from guarded honors.

The defense should anticipate these possibilities whenever declarer delays certain plays after drawing trumps.

---

# Endplays in Notrump Contracts

In notrump, endplays often involve:

- forcing a lead into an honor combination,
- establishing an extra finesse,
- exposing guarded kings or queens,
- eliminating safe suits.

Passive defense is usually preferable once the danger is recognized.

---

# Common Defensive Errors

## Using Every Safe Exit

Leading harmless suits too early leaves only dangerous returns later.

---

## Delaying Winners

Waiting too long to cash winners may allow declarer to strip the suit completely.

---

## Ignoring Declarer's Elimination

When declarer repeatedly cashes side suits without taking obvious winners, suspect an endplay.

---

## Opening New Suits Late

Breaking a new suit near the end often gives declarer the contract.

---

## Poor Communication

Losing partner's entries frequently makes the endplay unavoidable.

---

# Practical Questions

Before giving up the lead, ask:

- Can declarer throw me in later?
- Do I still have a safe exit?
- Should I cash my winner now?
- Has declarer been eliminating suits?
- Which defender is the likely target?
- Can I safely exit before declarer completes the elimination?

These questions often reveal the winning defense.

# Practical Tips

- Recognize elimination play as early as possible.
- Preserve at least one safe exit card whenever practical.
- Cash winners before they become liabilities.
- Maintain communication with partner throughout the hand.
- Avoid opening new suits in the endgame unless necessary.
- Return declarer's suit when it provides the safest exit.
- Always think about the final three or four tricks, not just the current one.

---

# Key Takeaways

- An endplay succeeds only when declarer forces a defender to make a damaging return.
- The best defense is to prevent the position from arising in the first place.
- Preserve safe exits, maintain communication, and time your winners carefully.
- Watch for declarer's elimination play and interrupt it whenever possible.
- Successful defenders think ahead and plan for the endgame long before it arrives.
