# Revolving Discards

## Overview

**Revolving Discards** are a **suit-preference discard method** in which the card discarded indicates which suit the defender prefers partner to play. Unlike Lavinthal, which uses the two remaining suits directly, Revolving Discards assign the preferred suit according to a rotating relationship among the suits.

The method is designed to allow all card ranks to carry suit-preference information while preserving useful high and low spot cards.

---

# Purpose

Revolving Discards help defenders:

- Indicate a preferred suit after becoming void.
- Improve defensive communication.
- Reduce ambiguity in discard situations.
- Provide more informative signals than standard suit-preference methods.

---

# Basic Principle

When discarding, the **rank** of the discarded card determines which suit is preferred.

A commonly used agreement is:

- **High discard** → Higher-ranking adjacent suit.
- **Low discard** → Lower-ranking adjacent suit.

The partnership must agree on the exact rotation before play.

---

# Example Rotation

One common clockwise rotation is:

```
Clubs → Diamonds → Hearts → Spades → Clubs
```

Example:

- Discarding a **high club** asks for **diamonds**.
- Discarding a **low club** asks for **spades**.

Different partnerships may use different rotations.

---

# When Is It Used?

Revolving Discards are typically used:

- On the first discard.
- When attitude and count are no longer required.
- In suit-preference situations.
- During notrump or suit contracts after the original signal has been given.

---

# Advantages

- Provides clear suit-preference information.
- Makes efficient use of spot cards.
- Reduces conflicts between discard methods.
- Can be more informative than standard Lavinthal in long defensive sequences.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires careful partnership agreement.
- More complex than Lavinthal.
- Easy to forget under pressure.
- Rarely played outside experienced partnerships.

---

# Example

South declares **3NT**.

West has no clubs remaining when declarer cashes the club suit.

West discards a **high diamond**.

According to the partnership agreement, this requests a switch to **hearts**.

East gains the lead later and returns a heart, defeating the contract.

---

# Comparison with Other Methods

| Method | Signal Type | Information Conveyed |
|---------|-------------|----------------------|
| Lavinthal | Suit preference | Directly indicates one of the two remaining suits. |
| Italian Discards | Combined attitude and suit preference | Card parity and rank determine meaning. |
| Odd-Even Discards | Suit preference and attitude | Odd and even cards carry different messages. |
| Revolving Discards | Suit preference | Uses a rotating suit assignment based on card rank. |

---

# Partnership Agreements

Before adopting Revolving Discards, partners should agree on:

- The suit rotation.
- Whether high and low cards indicate clockwise or counter-clockwise rotation.
- When Revolving Discards apply.
- Priority relative to attitude and count signals.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Forgetting the agreed rotation.
- Giving a revolving discard when attitude or count has priority.
- Misinterpreting the discard due to partnership misunderstanding.
- Using the method in situations where another signaling agreement applies.

---

# Best Practices

- Use only after establishing clear partnership agreements.
- Review the rotation regularly.
- Follow agreed signaling priorities.
- Confirm applicability on first and subsequent discards.
