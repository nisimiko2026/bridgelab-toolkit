# Forcing Defence

## Overview

**Forcing defence** is an active defensive strategy in trump contracts that seeks to **shorten declarer's trump holding** by repeatedly leading a suit that declarer must ruff. Each forced ruff reduces declarer's trump resources, making it harder to draw trumps, control the hand, or ruff losers.

Unlike a single forcing play, forcing defence is a **coordinated defensive plan** that continues as long as the defenders can regain the lead and keep forcing declarer.

It is one of the most effective ways to defeat contracts where declarer relies on superior trump length.

---

# Objectives

A forcing defence aims to:

- Shorten declarer's trump holding.
- Reduce declarer's control of the hand.
- Prevent dummy from providing multiple ruffs.
- Promote defenders' trumps into winners.
- Disrupt declarer's planned timing.
- Force declarer to abandon the original line of play.

---

# Basic Principle

Every forced ruff costs declarer a trump.

If declarer intended to use those trumps later for:

- drawing defenders' trumps,
- ruffing losers,
- controlling the hand,
- or executing a crossruff,

then each forced ruff weakens declarer's position.

The defense should continue forcing whenever the gain outweighs the tempo given to declarer.

---

# When to Adopt a Forcing Defence

A forcing defence is most effective when:

- Declarer has only a small trump advantage.
- Declarer delays drawing trumps.
- Dummy contains short suits for ruffing.
- Declarer plans a crossruff.
- Defenders can regain the lead multiple times.
- A long defensive suit is available for repeated forcing.

---

# Recognizing Declarer's Plan

Signs that forcing defence may succeed include:

- Declarer immediately starts ruffing in dummy.
- Trumps are not drawn early.
- Dummy has several singleton or void suits.
- Declarer preserves high trumps.
- Declarer appears to need extra trump tricks.

The earlier the defense recognizes these clues, the stronger the forcing attack becomes.

---

# Typical Example

Contract: **4♠**

Declarer begins with:

- Six trumps.
- Several losers that must be ruffed in dummy.

The defenders establish hearts.

Whenever they regain the lead, they continue leading hearts.

Declarer repeatedly ruffs.

Eventually:

- declarer has too few trumps,
- defenders retain trump control,
- declarer cannot complete the planned ruffs,
- the contract fails.

The repeated forcing—not the first forcing lead—is what defeats the contract.

---

# The Importance of Entries

A forcing defence succeeds only if the defenders can continue the attack.

Therefore, defenders should preserve entries that allow them to:

- regain the lead,
- continue the forcing suit,
- avoid blocking communications.

Without entries, the forcing defence usually ends prematurely.

---

# Timing the Forcing Attack

Begin forcing:

- before declarer draws trumps,
- before declarer establishes side suits,
- while the forcing suit remains available.

Continue forcing until:

- declarer's trump length is no longer superior,
- another attack becomes stronger,
- or the forcing suit is exhausted.

---

# Forcing Declarer vs. Forcing Dummy

Most forcing defences attack declarer directly.

Occasionally, the better strategy is to force dummy to ruff, especially when:

- dummy holds the long trump suit,
- declarer plans a crossruff,
- dummy provides repeated entries.

Either approach reduces overall trump control.

---

# Combining with Trump Promotion

Forcing defence often creates opportunities for **trump promotion**.

Repeated high ruffs may:

- exhaust declarer's honors,
- promote partner's intermediate trumps,
- create uppercut positions,
- generate unexpected trump tricks.

Many successful trump promotions begin with a forcing defence.

---

# Active or Passive?

Forcing defence is an example of **active defense**.

It is appropriate when:

- immediate action is required,
- declarer threatens to establish additional tricks,
- waiting benefits declarer.

If forcing no longer improves the position, the defense should switch to a more passive strategy.

---

# Common Defensive Errors

## Starting Too Late

Once declarer has drawn trumps, forcing opportunities often disappear.

---

## Abandoning the Plan

Changing suits after only one forcing lead may allow declarer to recover.

A forcing defence requires persistence.

---

## Ignoring Entries

Winning tricks without preserving communication often ends the forcing attack prematurely.

---

## Forcing Needlessly

If declarer already has overwhelming trump length, forcing merely helps declarer shorten losers without reducing control.

---

## Forgetting the Overall Plan

Forcing is a means to an end—not an objective by itself.

The defense should continually reassess whether forcing remains the best strategy.

---

# Practical Questions

Before beginning a forcing defence, ask:

- Does declarer need trumps for ruffing later?
- Can we regain the lead repeatedly?
- Do we have an established forcing suit?
- Will shortening declarer improve our position?
- Are our entries adequate?
- Should we continue forcing or switch to another attack?

# Practical Tips

- Count declarer's trumps from the auction and early play.
- Begin forcing before declarer draws trumps.
- Preserve entries needed to continue the forcing suit.
- Continue forcing while declarer's trump advantage is shrinking.
- Watch for opportunities to promote partner's trumps.
- Reevaluate the position after every forcing trick.
- Remember that forcing is most effective against contracts relying on trump control.

---

# Key Takeaways

- Forcing defence is a sustained strategy of repeatedly making declarer ruff.
- Its purpose is to shorten declarer's trump holding and destroy trump control.
- Success depends on timing, communication, and preserved entries.
- The technique is especially effective against crossruff and delayed-trump plans.
- A well-executed forcing defence often turns declarer's greatest asset—extra trumps—into a liability.
