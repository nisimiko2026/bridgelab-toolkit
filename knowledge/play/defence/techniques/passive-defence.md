# Passive Defence

## Overview

**Passive defence** is a defensive strategy that focuses on **avoiding mistakes rather than creating tricks**. Instead of attacking aggressively, defenders make safe, low-risk plays that force declarer to solve the remaining problems without assistance.

Passive defence is particularly effective when:

- the defenders already appear to have enough tricks,
- declarer still has work to do,
- any aggressive switch might establish declarer's winners,
- or the location of key honors is uncertain.

One of the most important defensive principles is:

> **Never give declarer a trick they cannot take themselves.**

---

# Objectives

A passive defence aims to:

- Avoid giving away tricks.
- Preserve existing defensive winners.
- Force declarer to take risks.
- Prevent unnecessary establishment of declarer's suits.
- Wait for additional information before committing to an attack.

---

# Basic Principle

When there is no clear attacking opportunity, choose the line that is **least likely to help declarer**.

Rather than trying to defeat the contract immediately, passive defence allows declarer to make the difficult decisions.

Many contracts fail because declarer eventually guesses wrong.

---

# When to Choose Passive Defence

Passive defence is usually correct when:

- Declarer must still establish tricks.
- Defenders already appear to have sufficient winners.
- Breaking a new suit would be dangerous.
- Declarer has hidden tenaces.
- The auction gives little information about honor locations.
- Waiting is unlikely to cost the defense a trick.

---

# Characteristics of Passive Defence

Passive defenders typically:

- avoid leading away from unsupported honors,
- return declarer's suit when safe,
- preserve communication,
- delay risky switches,
- keep exits available,
- avoid opening new suits.

Every play seeks to minimize risk.

---

# Typical Passive Plays

## Returning Declarer's Suit

Returning the suit declarer has already played is often the safest continuation because declarer has already begun solving that suit.

---

## Leading Trumps

Against contracts based on crossruffs or repeated ruffs, a trump continuation may safely reduce declarer's opportunities without exposing side-suit honors.

---

## Leading Low from Weak Holdings

A low card from a weak suit usually reveals little information and rarely gifts declarer an extra trick.

---

## Waiting for More Information

Sometimes the best defense is simply to postpone a difficult decision until declarer's play reveals additional information.

---

# Passive vs. Active Defence

## Passive Defence

Goals include:

- avoiding gifts,
- preserving winners,
- forcing declarer to guess,
- minimizing risk.

Best when:

- declarer has unresolved problems,
- defenders already have sufficient tricks,
- aggressive action is dangerous.

---

## Active Defence

Goals include:

- establishing winners,
- attacking declarer's weak suits,
- promoting trump tricks,
- forcing declarer immediately.

Best when:

- the defense must create tricks quickly,
- declarer has many immediate winners,
- waiting benefits declarer.

Choosing correctly between passive and active defence is one of the hallmarks of expert defenders.

---

# Auction Clues

The auction often suggests whether passive defence is appropriate.

Passive defence becomes more attractive when:

- opponents have exchanged extensive descriptive bidding,
- declarer's distribution appears balanced,
- partner has not indicated a clear attacking suit,
- declarer still needs to establish extra tricks.

---

# Passive Defence in Notrump Contracts

In notrump contracts, passive defence often means:

- avoiding new suits,
- not leading away from kings or queens,
- returning declarer's suit,
- preserving entries,
- forcing declarer to establish long suits personally.

---

# Passive Defence in Trump Contracts

In suit contracts, passive defence may involve:

- avoiding side-suit attacks,
- leading trumps against crossruff plans,
- preventing unnecessary ruffs,
- preserving trump control,
- avoiding leads into declarer's tenaces.

---

# Endgame Considerations

Passive defence becomes increasingly important in the late stages of a hand.

Poor endgame switches frequently:

- concede a finesse,
- create a ruff-and-sluff,
- allow a throw-in,
- establish declarer's last winner.

When the endgame approaches, defenders should carefully preserve safe exits.

---

# Common Defensive Errors

## Becoming Too Passive

Passive defence should not become inactivity.

If declarer is about to establish a long suit, immediate action may be essential.

---

## Breaking Honors Needlessly

Leading away from unsupported honors often gives declarer an undeserved trick.

---

## Opening New Suits

Many contracts succeed because defenders attack a new suit without necessity.

---

## Ignoring Declarer's Plan

Passive defence is effective only if it still disrupts declarer's overall strategy.

Waiting must never allow declarer to develop winners freely.

---

# Practical Questions

Before making a switch, ask:

- Will this lead give declarer a trick?
- Can declarer establish this suit anyway?
- Does partner's bidding suggest an attack?
- Is waiting likely to improve the defense?
- Do we already have enough defensive winners?

These questions help determine whether patience is preferable.

# Practical Tips

- When uncertain, choose the least committal lead.
- Avoid breaking unsupported honor combinations.
- Return declarer's suit if it is harmless.
- Preserve communication with partner.
- Reassess after every trick—passive defence may later become active defence.
- Count declarer's winners before deciding to attack.
- Remember that many contracts are defeated because declarer is left to solve difficult problems alone.

---

# Key Takeaways

- Passive defence emphasizes **not giving declarer unnecessary help**.
- It is most effective when the defense already has sufficient potential tricks.
- Safe exits, careful timing, and patience are its defining characteristics.
- Good defenders know when to wait and when to attack.
- Passive defence forces declarer to earn every trick without assistance from the defense.
