# Backward Finesse

## Overview

A **Backward Finesse** is an unusual type of finesse in which declarer finesses **against the normal direction**. Instead of finessing a lower honor through a higher missing honor, declarer intentionally plays for an honor to be **behind** the touching honors.

Backward finesses are relatively rare because they generally succeed only when the missing honors occupy a specific position. They are usually chosen only when the normal finesse is impossible or when information from the auction or play strongly favors the unusual layout.

---

# Objectives

A backward finesse aims to:

- Win an extra trick by exploiting an unusual honor position.
- Take advantage of information revealed during the auction or play.
- Maximize the probability when standard lines are unavailable.
- Avoid a dangerous opponent when appropriate.

---

# Basic Principle

Suppose declarer holds:

```
Dummy
♠ A Q 10

Declarer
♠ J 8 7
```

Missing:

```
♠ K 9
```

Instead of taking the normal finesse toward the queen, declarer may lead toward the **jack**, hoping that the king lies behind the queen.

This is the opposite of the usual finesse direction.

---

# When Is It Used?

A backward finesse is considered when:

- A normal finesse is impossible.
- The auction strongly suggests an unusual honor location.
- Distribution favors one defender.
- The dangerous opponent should be avoided.
- Standard percentage plays have failed or are inferior.

---

# Information Is Essential

Backward finesses are rarely based on pure probability.

Declarer should consider:

- The auction.
- The opening lead.
- Revealed distribution.
- Vacant Spaces.
- Restricted Choice.
- Previous card play.

Without supporting evidence, the normal finesse is usually superior.

---

# Example 1 – Auction Clue

West opens:

```
1♠
```

Later play suggests that West is unlikely to hold additional high-card points.

Declarer therefore places the missing king with East and chooses the backward finesse.

---

# Example 2 – Distribution

West has shown:

- Six clubs.
- Five hearts.

East has significantly more vacant spaces.

Declarer concludes that East is more likely to hold the missing honor and chooses the backward finesse.

---

# Example 3 – Dangerous Opponent

West has an established long suit.

If West gains the lead, the contract will fail.

Although the backward finesse has a slightly lower mathematical expectation, it loses only to East.

Keeping West off lead makes the backward finesse the better practical choice.

---

# Backward Finesse vs. Standard Finesse

| Standard Finesse | Backward Finesse |
|------------------|------------------|
| Normal direction | Reverse direction |
| Percentage play | Usually inference-based |
| Common technique | Rare technique |
| Based primarily on card position | Based primarily on information |

---

# Planning the Play

Before attempting a backward finesse, ask:

- Is the normal finesse available?
- What has the auction revealed?
- What does the opening lead suggest?
- Which defender is more likely to hold the missing honor?
- Is one defender dangerous?
- Does the available information justify the unusual line?

---

# Common Mistakes

- Attempting a backward finesse without supporting evidence.
- Ignoring the normal percentage play.
- Overlooking clues from the auction or card play.
- Confusing a backward finesse with a two-way finesse.
- Taking the finesse too early instead of gathering additional information.

# Key Principles

- A backward finesse takes the finesse in the opposite direction from the normal line.
- It is usually justified by inference rather than by raw probability.
- Information from the auction and play is often more important than the mathematical odds.
- Consider the dangerous opponent before choosing the direction of the finesse.
- Backward finesses are uncommon but can be the winning line when the evidence supports them.
