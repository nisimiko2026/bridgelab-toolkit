# Double Finesse Technique

## Overview

A **Double Finesse** is a declarer-play technique in which declarer attempts to win tricks against **two missing honors** by taking two successive finesses. Instead of relying on a single favorable card position, declarer gains additional tricks if one or both missing honors are positioned advantageously.

The Double Finesse is a natural extension of the ordinary finesse and is commonly used in both suit and notrump contracts.

---

# Purpose

The Double Finesse allows declarer to:

- Win tricks against two missing honors.
- Maximize tricks from a suit.
- Improve the chances of making the contract.
- Establish long-suit winners.
- Combine with other declarer-play techniques.

---

# Basic Principle

A Double Finesse attempts to:

> **Win tricks against two missing honors by taking two separate finesses.**

Typically, declarer leads toward one honor and later repeats the process against the second missing honor.

The technique usually requires multiple entries.

---

# When to Use

A Double Finesse is appropriate when:

- Two important honors are missing.
- Multiple entries are available.
- No superior percentage line exists.
- The contract requires additional tricks from one suit.

---

# Requirements

A successful Double Finesse usually requires:

- Two missing honors.
- At least two lower honors.
- Multiple entries.
- Proper timing.
- Favorable honor locations.

---

# Example 1 – Classic Double Finesse

Holding

```
Dummy
♠ AQ10

Declarer
♠ 543
```

Missing:

```
♠ KJ
```

Lead a small spade toward dummy.

- If East plays low, play the 10.
- Later, lead another spade toward the Queen.

If both honors are favorably placed, declarer gains three tricks.

---

# Example 2 – Repeating Entries

Holding

```
Dummy
♦ AQ109

Declarer
♦ 43
```

Missing:

```
♦ KJ
```

With sufficient entries, declarer may take successive finesses against both honors.

---

# Example 3 – Slam Contract

Contract

```
6NT
```

Dummy

```
♣ AQ10
```

Declarer

```
♣ 654
```

Missing:

```
♣ KJ
```

Declarer takes two club finesses to establish enough tricks for the slam.

---

# Probability

A Double Finesse is more complex than a simple finesse.

Typical outcomes:

- One finesse succeeds.
- Both succeed.
- Both fail.

The exact probability depends on:

- The missing honors.
- Their locations.
- Additional information from the auction and play.

As always, the bidding and defenders' signals may significantly affect the odds.

---

# Planning the Play

Before attempting a Double Finesse, ask:

- Which honors are missing?
- How many entries are available?
- Can both finesses be repeated?
- Is there a safer alternative?
- Is one finesse sufficient?

---

# Timing

Timing is essential.

Generally:

1. Preserve entries.
2. Draw trumps if appropriate.
3. Delay the finesse if additional information may become available.
4. Avoid blocking the suit.

---

# Common Situations

Double Finesses frequently occur:

- In notrump contracts.
- In slam contracts.
- While establishing long suits.
- In combination with entry management.
- During squeeze endings.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Taking the finesse from the wrong hand.
- Losing the second entry.
- Forgetting a better percentage play.
- Blocking the suit.
- Taking an unnecessary second finesse.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Conceal honor locations.
- Falsecard when appropriate.
- Preserve communication.
- Avoid revealing suit distribution.
- Force declarer into guessing.

---

# Advantages

- Produces extra tricks.
- Maximizes suit potential.
- Frequently essential in slam contracts.
- Combines naturally with entry management.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires multiple entries.
- Depends on favorable honor positions.
- Timing is critical.
- Often inferior to a safety play.

# Declarer's Checklist

Before taking a Double Finesse:

- Which two honors are missing?
- Do I have enough entries?
- Which finesse should I take first?
- Can I repeat the finesse?
- Is there a safer line?

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Identify the missing honors.
2. Preserve entries.
3. Take the first finesse.
4. Return to repeat the finesse.
5. Cash the established winners.

# Memory Aid

**Double Finesse = "Two Missing Honors, Two Chances to Win."**

Remember:

- **Requires two missing honors.**
- **Usually needs multiple entries.**
- **Preserve communication carefully.**
- **Compare with safety and percentage plays before committing.**
- **Use information from the auction and defenders' play to improve your chances.**

The Double Finesse is one of the classic declarer-play techniques in bridge. By carefully managing entries and timing, declarer can attack two missing honors and often establish enough tricks to make otherwise difficult contracts. Expert players always compare the Double Finesse with alternative lines before deciding which offers the greatest chance of success.
