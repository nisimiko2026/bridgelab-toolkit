# Finesse Technique

## Overview

A **Finesse** is one of the most fundamental and frequently used declarer-play techniques in bridge. It is an attempt to win a trick with a lower honor by taking advantage of the favorable position of a missing higher honor.

A successful finesse allows declarer to win tricks that would otherwise be lost.

The finesse is based on **card position**, not card strength. It usually succeeds when the missing honor lies **in front of** the card being finessed.

---

# Purpose

The Finesse allows declarer to:

- Win tricks against missing honors.
- Establish additional winners.
- Maximize trick-taking potential.
- Improve the chances of making the contract.
- Combine with other declarer-play techniques.

---

# Basic Principle

A finesse attempts to:

> **Win a lower honor because the missing higher honor is favorably placed.**

The classic example is:

```
Dummy
♠ AQ

Declarer
♠ 32
```

Missing:

```
♠ K
```

Lead a small spade from declarer toward dummy.

If West plays low, play the Queen.

If East holds the King, the Queen wins.

---

# When to Use

A finesse is appropriate when:

- A higher honor is missing.
- The missing honor is likely to be favorably placed.
- No safer line exists.
- The contract depends on gaining an additional trick.

---

# Requirements

A successful finesse usually requires:

- A missing honor.
- A lower honor that can be played.
- An entry to the correct hand.
- Proper timing.
- Favorable card position.

---

# Types of Finesses

## Simple Finesse

Against one missing honor.

Example:

```
AQ
opposite
32
```

Missing:

```
K
```

---

## Double Finesse

Against two missing honors.

Example:

```
AQ10
opposite
43
```

Missing:

```
KJ
```

Declarer may finesse twice.

---

## Deep Finesse

Against a lower missing honor.

Example:

```
AQ10
opposite
54
```

Finessing the 10 against the Jack.

---

## Repeating Finesse

A finesse that can be taken more than once because sufficient entries exist.

---

## Ruffing Finesse

A finesse in which declarer ruffs if the missing honor appears.

Covered separately in the **Ruffing Finesse** technique.

---

# Example 1 – Simple Finesse

Holding

```
Dummy
♠ AQ

Declarer
♠ 43
```

Missing:

```
♠ K
```

Lead a small spade toward dummy.

If East has the King:

The Queen wins.

---

# Example 2 – Repeating Finesse

Holding

```
Dummy
♦ AQJ

Declarer
♦ 54
```

Missing:

```
♦ K
```

With multiple entries to dummy, declarer can finesse twice if necessary.

---

# Example 3 – Deep Finesse

Holding

```
Dummy
♣ AQ10

Declarer
♣ 54
```

Missing:

```
♣ J
```

Lead toward the 10.

This is a Deep Finesse.

---

# Probability

A simple finesse succeeds approximately:

**50%**

when no additional information is available.

The odds improve when:

- The bidding suggests the location of the missing honor.
- Defenders reveal their distribution.
- Signals provide useful information.

---

# Planning the Play

Before taking a finesse, ask:

- Which honor is missing?
- Which opponent is likely to hold it?
- Do I have enough entries?
- Is there a safer alternative?
- Can I repeat the finesse if necessary?

---

# Timing

Timing is important.

Often declarer should:

- Draw trumps first.
- Preserve entries.
- Delay the finesse until additional information is available.
- Avoid blocking the suit.

---

# Common Situations

Finesses occur:

- In suit contracts.
- In notrump contracts.
- During slam play.
- In combination with squeezes.
- After elimination plays.
- Before endplays.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Taking the finesse from the wrong hand.
- Losing the necessary entry.
- Forgetting a better percentage play.
- Finessing unnecessarily.
- Ignoring information from the bidding.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Conceal honor locations.
- Falsecard when appropriate.
- Preserve communication.
- Avoid revealing suit distribution.
- Force declarer into repeated guesses.

---

# Advantages

- Creates extra tricks.
- Fundamental declarer technique.
- Often provides the only chance to make the contract.
- Works in all forms of bridge.

---

# Disadvantages

- Usually depends on a 50% chance.
- Requires correct timing.
- Entry problems may prevent repetition.
- Can be inferior to a safety play.

# Declarer's Checklist

Before taking a finesse:

- Which honor is missing?
- Which opponent is more likely to hold it?
- Do I have sufficient entries?
- Is there a safer line?
- What happens if the finesse loses?

---

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Identify the missing honor.
2. Lead toward the lower honor.
3. Play the lower honor if appropriate.
4. Preserve entries.
5. Repeat if possible.

---

---

# Memory Aid

**Finesse = "Win with the Lower Honor."**

Remember:

- **The missing honor must be favorably placed.**
- **Lead toward the honor you intend to play.**
- **Preserve entries for repeated finesses.**
- **Use information from the bidding and play.**
- **Always compare the finesse with available safety or percentage plays.**

The Finesse is the cornerstone of declarer play in bridge. Mastering when to finesse, when to delay, and when to choose a safer alternative is one of the defining skills of expert players. Combined with sound planning and accurate card reading, the finesse becomes one of the most powerful weapons available to declarer.
