# Overtaking

## Overview

**Overtaking** is the technique of playing a higher card than partner's or your own winning card in order to gain the lead. Although it appears wasteful, overtaking is often essential for maintaining communication, preserving entries, unblocking a suit, or executing the overall declarer or defensive plan.

Overtaking is used by both **declarer** and **defenders** and is an important element of advanced card play.

---

# Objectives

Overtaking allows a player to:

- Preserve communication.
- Reach established winners.
- Prevent a suit from becoming blocked.
- Keep the correct hand on lead.
- Continue an attack.
- Execute squeezes, endplays, or other advanced techniques.

---

# Basic Principle

Instead of allowing a lower winning card to take the trick, a higher card is deliberately played.

The purpose is not to win the trick itself—it was already going to be won—but to ensure that **the correct hand gains the lead**.

---

# When Declarer Should Overtake

Declarer may overtake to:

- Preserve an important entry.
- Reach an established long suit.
- Unblock a suit.
- Prepare for a squeeze.
- Maintain the planned sequence of play.

The decision is based on communication rather than immediate trick-taking.

---

# Example 1 – Preserving Communication

Dummy:

```
♦ A J 10 9
```

Declarer:

```
♦ K Q
```

Declarer overtakes one honor with another so that the remaining winners can be reached smoothly.

Without the overtake, the suit may become blocked.

---

# Example 2 – Reaching the Long Suit

Contract:

```
3NT
```

Dummy contains an established club suit.

Declarer overtakes a diamond winner to remain in the correct hand and reach the clubs.

The overtake preserves communication.

---

# When Defenders Should Overtake

Defenders may overtake partner's winner to:

- Continue attacking a dangerous suit.
- Preserve communication.
- Prevent declarer from gaining control.
- Reach partner's established winners.
- Execute a defensive coup.

---

# Example 3 – Defensive Overtake

West leads:

```
♥Q
```

Dummy plays low.

East holds:

```
♥A
```

East overtakes the queen with the ace because only East can continue the attack through declarer's king.

Although the queen would have won the trick, overtaking keeps the correct defender on lead.

---

# Overtaking vs. Unblocking

Although related, they are different concepts.

| Overtaking | Unblocking |
|------------|------------|
| Uses a higher card to take a trick already being won | Plays a high card early to prevent a suit from blocking |
| Keeps the correct hand on lead | Maintains communication within a suit |
| May or may not unblock a suit | Does not necessarily involve overtaking |

Many overtakes also serve as unblocking plays, but the objectives are different.

---

# Overtaking vs. Crocodile Coup

A **Crocodile Coup** is a specialized defensive overtake.

Every Crocodile Coup involves overtaking, but not every overtake is a Crocodile Coup.

The Crocodile Coup has the specific objective of preserving defensive communication or keeping the correct defender on lead.

---

# Planning the Overtake

Before overtaking, ask:

- Which hand should remain on lead?
- Will the overtake preserve communication?
- Does it unblock a suit?
- Will it preserve an important entry?
- Is there a simpler line of play?

Overtaking should always support the overall plan.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Automatically allowing the lower winner to take the trick.
- Overtaking without a clear purpose.
- Failing to recognize a blocked suit.
- Destroying an important entry by overtaking unnecessarily.
- Ignoring communication between the hands.

# Key Principles

- Overtaking changes **which hand** wins the trick, not **whether** the trick is won.
- Use overtaking to preserve communication, entries, and the correct lead.
- Many overtakes are made to unblock suits or reach established winners.
- Both declarer and defenders use overtaking as part of advanced card-play planning.
- Never overtake automatically—every overtake should support the overall strategy of the hand.
