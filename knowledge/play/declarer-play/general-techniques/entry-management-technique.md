# Entry Management Technique

## Overview

**Entry Management** is the skill of preserving, creating, and using entries between declarer's hand and dummy. Successful declarer play often depends not on high-card strength, but on the ability to reach the correct hand at the right time.

Poor entry management is one of the most common causes of failed contracts, while expert declarers carefully plan their entries before playing to the first trick.

---

# Purpose

Entry Management allows declarer to:

- Reach established winners.
- Repeat finesses.
- Execute squeezes and endplays.
- Perform cross-ruffs and dummy reversals.
- Maintain communication between the two hands.
- Maximize the value of long suits.

---

# What Is an Entry?

An **entry** is any card or combination of cards that allows declarer to gain the lead in a particular hand.

Typical entries include:

- Aces.
- Kings.
- High trumps.
- Established winners.
- Ruffing entries.

---

# Basic Principle

Before playing from dummy, declarer should ask:

> **"How will I get back there later?"**

Likewise, before cashing an entry:

> **"Do I need this entry later?"**

Planning entries before trick one is one of the hallmarks of expert declarer play.

---

# Types of Entries

## Natural Entries

High cards that are immediate winners.

Examples:

```
♣A
♦K
♠Q (after honors are removed)
```

---

## Trump Entries

High trumps used to reach a hand.

Example:

```
Dummy
♥QJ10

Declarer
♥AK98
```

The ♥Q may serve as an entry after trumps are drawn.

---

## Ruffing Entries

A short suit is ruffed to gain the lead.

Example:

Dummy

```
♣ —
```

Declarer

```
♣ A74
```

Declarer ruffs a club in dummy to enter dummy.

---

## Established Entries

A long suit becomes an entry after opponents' cards are exhausted.

Example:

```
♠ 8765
```

becomes an entry after the defenders' higher spades have been played.

---

# Example 1 – Preserving an Entry

Dummy

```
♣ A
♦ KQJ
```

Declarer

```
♦ A764
```

Do **not** cash the ♣A immediately if it is the only remaining entry to dummy.

---

# Example 2 – Repeating a Finesse

Dummy

```
♠ AQ10
```

Declarer

```
♠ J43
```

After the first finesse succeeds, declarer needs another entry to dummy to repeat the finesse.

Without proper entry management, the second finesse is impossible.

---

# Example 3 – Establishing a Long Suit

Dummy

```
♣ AKQJ6
```

Declarer

```
♣ 32
```

After clubs are established, declarer still needs an entry to dummy to cash the remaining club winners.

---

# Planning the Play

Before Trick One:

1. Count winners.
2. Count losers.
3. Identify every entry.
4. Decide which entries must be preserved.
5. Plan the order of play.

Expert declarers often count entries before counting winners.

---

# Preserving Entries

General rules:

- Do not waste an Ace unnecessarily.
- Avoid blocking long suits.
- Save high cards that provide communication.
- Delay using valuable entries until needed.

---

# Creating Entries

Sometimes declarer can create entries by:

- Ducking.
- Ruffing.
- Unblocking honors.
- Establishing long suits.
- Executing a Vienna Coup.

---

# Blocking Problems

A blocked suit destroys communication.

Example:

Dummy

```
♣ KQJ
```

Declarer

```
♣ A10
```

If the Ace is played too late, the suit may become blocked.

The solution is often to **unblock** the Ace early.

---

# Entry Management in Common Techniques

Entry Management is essential for:

- Squeeze.
- Double Squeeze.
- Endplay.
- Elimination Play.
- Dummy Reversal.
- Cross Ruff.
- Ruffing Finesse.
- Safety Play.
- Loser-on-Loser.

Many advanced techniques fail because of poor communication.

---

# Timing

Timing is critical.

Generally:

- Preserve entries early.
- Use temporary entries only when necessary.
- Cash established winners while communication remains intact.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Wasting the only entry.
- Blocking long suits.
- Using high cards too early.
- Forgetting to preserve communication.
- Counting winners but not entries.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Attack declarer's entries.
- Force declarer to use entries prematurely.
- Remove dummy's entries.
- Disrupt communication between the hands.
- Count declarer's remaining entries.

---

# Advantages

- Improves declarer's timing.
- Maximizes long-suit winners.
- Enables advanced techniques.
- Reduces communication problems.
- Increases contract success.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires careful planning.
- Easy to overlook during play.
- Poor timing may waste valuable entries.
- Blocking can be difficult to repair.

---

# Related Techniques

| Technique | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|
| Ducking | Preserve entries and timing |
| Vienna Coup | Unblock before a squeeze |
| Dummy Reversal | Preserve entries to dummy |
| Cross Ruff | Maintain communication for repeated ruffs |
| Squeeze | Requires precise entry management |
| Endplay | Preserve entries before throwing in a defender |

---

# Declarer's Checklist

Before playing to Trick One:

- Where are all my entries?
- Which entry is irreplaceable?
- Will this winner also serve as an entry?
- Can I establish additional entries?
- Am I blocking any suit?

---

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Count entries.
2. Preserve communication.
3. Avoid blocking.
4. Create new entries when possible.
5. Use entries only when needed.

---

# Comparison

| Technique | Main Idea |
|-----------|-----------|
| Entry Management | Preserve communication between the hands |
| Ducking | Delay winning to improve timing |
| Vienna Coup | Unblock honors to create communication |
| Dummy Reversal | Use entries to support repeated ruffs |
| Cross Ruff | Preserve entries for alternating ruffs |

---

# Typical Entry Plan

```
Identify Entries
↓

Preserve Key Entry

↓

Establish Winners

↓

Use Entry

↓

Cash Established Tricks
```

---

# Memory Aid

**Entry Management = "Never Use Your Last Entry Too Soon."**

Remember:

- **Count entries before counting tricks.**
- **Preserve communication between declarer and dummy.**
- **Avoid blocking long suits.**
- **Create new entries whenever possible.**
- **Many expert techniques succeed or fail because of entry management.**

Entry Management is one of the foundations of expert declarer play. More contracts are lost through poor communication than through poor card combinations. By carefully preserving and creating entries, declarer gains the flexibility needed to execute advanced techniques such as squeezes, endplays, cross-ruffs, and dummy reversals.
