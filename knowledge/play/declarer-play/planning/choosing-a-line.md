# Choosing a Line of Play

## Overview

After counting winners or losers and identifying possible sources of extra tricks, declarer must **choose the best line of play**.

Rarely is there only one possible plan. Most contracts offer several alternatives, such as taking a finesse, establishing a long suit, ruffing losers, or executing an endplay. Expert declarers compare these alternatives before playing to the first trick.

Choosing the correct line involves balancing **probability, safety, timing, communication, and the information available from the auction and play**.

---

# Objectives

Choosing the right line allows declarer to:

- Maximize the probability of making the contract.
- Minimize unnecessary risk.
- Take advantage of information.
- Preserve flexibility.
- Adapt to changing circumstances during the play.

---

# Step 1 – Count Winners or Losers

Before selecting a line, determine:

### In Notrump

- Count sure winners.
- Identify additional tricks needed.

### In Suit Contracts

- Count likely losers.
- Decide how each loser will be handled.

Without this analysis, choosing a line is impossible.

---

# Step 2 – Identify All Possible Plans

Possible sources of extra tricks include:

- Establishing a long suit.
- Finesses.
- Ruffing losers.
- Crossruff.
- Throw-in and endplay.
- Squeeze.
- Promotion of small cards.
- Defensive errors.

Avoid committing to the first idea that comes to mind.

---

# Step 3 – Compare the Alternatives

For each possible line, ask:

- What is the probability of success?
- How many assumptions does it require?
- What can go wrong?
- Does it preserve communication?
- Does it preserve entries?
- Which defender is dangerous?

The best line is often the one with the fewest risks rather than the highest potential reward.

---

# Consider the Auction

The auction frequently influences the correct line.

Examples:

- A preempt suggests a long suit.
- An overcall suggests length and honors.
- A takeout double suggests shortage.
- A notrump opening suggests balanced distribution.

Use this information when evaluating finesses and distributions.

---

# Gather Information First

Do not rush into a finesse or guess.

Often declarer should first:

- Cash certain winners.
- Count the defenders' distribution.
- Watch defenders' discards.
- Identify missing honors.
- Preserve communication.

Additional information may completely change the best line.

---

# Timing Matters

Some lines must be played immediately.

Others improve if delayed.

Consider:

- Should I lose the lead now?
- Should I establish a long suit first?
- Should I draw trumps immediately?
- Should I preserve an entry?

The correct timing is often as important as the chosen line.

---

# Safety vs. Maximum Tricks

Duplicate bridge usually rewards making the contract.

If two lines exist:

- One gives an overtrick but risks defeat.
- One guarantees the contract.

The safer line is often correct.

In matchpoint scoring, however, the value of an overtrick may justify additional risk.

Always consider the form of scoring.

---

# Example 1 – Two Possible Finesses

Contract:

```
3NT
```

Declarer needs one additional trick.

Options:

- Club finesse.
- Diamond finesse.

West has shown club length during the auction.

Declarer chooses the diamond finesse.

The decision is based on inference rather than guesswork.

---

# Example 2 – Finesse or Endplay

Contract:

```
4♠
```

Declarer needs one extra trick.

Options:

- 50% heart finesse.
- Strip and endplay.

After counting the defenders' distribution, declarer realizes the endplay is certain.

The finesse is unnecessary.

---

# Example 3 – Draw Trumps or Ruff First

Contract:

```
4♥
```

Declarer has:

- One club loser.
- One diamond loser.

Dummy has a singleton diamond.

Drawing trumps immediately loses the opportunity to ruff the diamond.

The correct line is:

1. Ruff the diamond.
2. Draw trumps.
3. Cash winners.

---

# Reassess During the Play

The original plan is based on incomplete information.

As the play develops:

- Recount the defenders' distribution.
- Watch every signal and discard.
- Update the location of missing honors.
- Be prepared to change your plan.

Expert declarers continually revise their analysis.

---

# Planning Checklist

Before playing from dummy, ask:

1. How many tricks do I need?
2. What are all my possible sources of extra tricks?
3. Which line has the highest percentage?
4. What information does the auction provide?
5. Can I gather more information before committing?
6. Which defender is dangerous?
7. Does my line preserve entries and communication?
8. Is there a safer alternative?

---

# Common Mistakes

- Choosing the first reasonable-looking line without comparing alternatives.
- Taking an early finesse before gathering information.
- Ignoring entries and communication.
- Playing for an overtrick when making the contract should be the priority.
- Failing to adjust the plan after new information becomes available.
- Overlooking a guaranteed line in favor of a speculative one.

# Key Principles

- Always compare all reasonable lines before playing to the first trick.
- Base your decision on probability, inference, communication, and timing—not intuition alone.
- Gather as much information as possible before committing to a finesse or guess.
- Prefer the line that makes the contract most often, not necessarily the one with the greatest upside.
- Review and adjust your plan as new information becomes available during the play.
