# Preserving Entries

## Overview

**Preserving Entries** is one of the most fundamental principles of declarer
play. An **entry** is a card that allows declarer to transfer the lead from one
hand to the other. Without sufficient entries, established winners may become
unreachable, long suits may never be enjoyed, and otherwise successful contracts
may fail.

Expert declarers do not simply count winners—they count **entries** before
playing to trick one. Every finesse, duck, unblock, ruff, or cashing sequence
must be planned with communication between the two hands in mind.

The principle applies in **both notrump and trump contracts**, although the
nature of entries differs significantly between them.

---

# Objectives

Preserving entries enables declarer to

- reach established winners,
- maintain communication,
- enjoy long suits,
- execute finesses,
- perform squeezes and endplays,
- avoid blocking the hands,
- maximize trick potential.

---

# What Is an Entry?

An **entry** is any card or technique that transfers the lead to the opposite
hand.

Examples include

- high cards,
- established winners,
- ruffs,
- overtakes,
- trump entries,
- throw-ins.

---

# Communication

The two hands should be viewed as two connected locations.

Without communication,

they effectively become two separate hands.

One hand may hold

```
AKQJ
```

while the other holds

all the entries.

Poor communication prevents access to those winners.

---

# Planning Before Trick One

Expert declarers ask

1. Where are my winners?
2. Which hand must reach them?
3. How many entries exist?
4. Which entries are essential?
5. Which entries are expendable?

Only after answering these questions should play begin.

---

# Types of Entries

## Natural Entries

High cards already available.

Example

```
Dummy

Axx

Declarer

KQJ
```

The ace serves as an entry.

---

## Established Entries

Cards that become winners later.

Example

```
Dummy

QJ10

Declarer

AK
```

After driving out the ace,

the queen becomes an entry.

---

## Ruffing Entries

Unique to trump contracts.

Example

Dummy

```
♠ 6

♥ AKQ

♦ AQJ

♣ KQJ
```

Declarer

```
♠ AKQJ

♥ 64

♦ K

♣ A109
```

A small spade ruff in dummy creates an additional entry.

---

## Trump Entries

Small trumps retained specifically to regain dummy.

---

## Throw-in Entries

Occasionally declarer intentionally gives the lead away to regain communication.

---

# Entry Management

Managing entries involves

- preserving,
- creating,
- timing,
- using,
- and sometimes sacrificing entries.

Experts think several tricks ahead.

---

# Preserving Entries in Notrump

In notrump contracts,

entries are precious because

they cannot be created by ruffing.

Every high card matters.

---

## Example

Dummy

```
♠ AQ5

♥ K82

♦ QJ1075

♣ 74
```

Declarer

```
♠ K63

♥ AQ4

♦ 82

♣ AKQ95
```

If declarer cashes

```
♠A

♠Q

```

before establishing diamonds,

dummy may become unreachable.

The proper plan preserves an entry.

---

# Establish Before Cashing

Experts avoid cashing side winners too early.

Instead,

they first establish

- long suits,
- communication,

then enjoy the winners.

---

# Avoid Blocking

Blocking destroys communication.

Example

Dummy

```
KQJ
```

Declarer

```
A109
```

Cashing

```
A

K

Q

J
```

may block the suit.

Proper unblocking preserves entries.

---

# Preserving Entries in Trump Contracts

Trump contracts offer additional flexibility because

ruffs create new entries.

However,

ruffing too early may remove those entries.

---

## Ruff High or Ruff Low?

Sometimes a low trump should be retained solely as an entry.

Example

Dummy

```
♠ 63

♥ AQJ

♦ KQJ

♣ A543
```

Declarer

```
♠ AKQJ

♥ K54

♦ A4

♣ KQ2
```

If dummy ruffs immediately,

later communication disappears.

---

# Using Trump as Transportation

Experts often regard trumps not merely as winners,

but as transportation between hands.

A small trump may be more valuable than a high honor.

---

# Crossruff Planning

Crossruff contracts require careful preservation of

- both trump holdings,
- communication,
- timing.

Using the wrong trump too early often destroys the crossruff.

---

# Entries and Long Suits

Establishing a long suit requires

1. Entries.
2. Timing.
3. Communication.

Without preserved entries,

long-suit winners cannot be reached.

---

# Entries and Finesses

Repeated finesses frequently require multiple entries.

Example

```
AQJ opposite xx
```

Each finesse may consume one entry.

Plan accordingly.

---

# Entries and Ducking

A duck often preserves communication.

Example

Declarer ducks once

to maintain an important entry later.

---

# Entries and Hold-Up Plays

Hold-up plays frequently preserve timing

and entries simultaneously.

---

# Entries and Elimination

Elimination techniques require

careful preservation of

- entries,
- communication,
- trump control.

---

# Entries and Endplays

An endplay often fails

if declarer loses the necessary entry.

---

# Entries and Squeezes

Most squeeze positions collapse without

proper communication.

Entry preservation is therefore one of the first requirements of squeeze play.

---

# Creating Entries

Experts actively create entries.

Methods include

- overtaking,
- ducking,
- preserving honors,
- ruffing,
- unblocking,
- delaying winners.

---

# Destroying Entries

Common mistakes include

- cashing honors prematurely,
- unnecessary overtakes,
- wasting trump entries,
- blocking suits,
- careless discards.

---

# Typical Notrump Example

Dummy

```
♠ A4

♥ K85

♦ QJ1096

♣ 742
```

Declarer

```
♠ KQ3

♥ AQ7

♦ 54

♣ AKQ85
```

Wrong

Cash clubs first.

Right

Establish diamonds while the ace of spades remains as an entry.

---

# Typical Trump Example

Contract

```
4♠
```

Dummy

```
♠ 764

♥ AKJ

♦ AQJ8

♣ 54
```

Declarer

```
♠ AKQJ5

♥ 43

♦ K3

♣ AKQ6
```

Declarer delays ruffing

to preserve dummy's small trump as an entry.

---

# Expert Techniques

Experts routinely

- count entries before winners,
- preserve transportation,
- delay cashing honors,
- unblock early,
- avoid blocking,
- create new entries whenever possible.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Counting Winners Only

Winning tricks are worthless if unreachable.

---

## Cashing Side Aces Too Early

High cards often serve better as entries.

---

## Wasting Trump Entries

Every small trump may become critical.

---

## Blocking Long Suits

Failure to unblock often strands winners.

---

## Ignoring Communication

Many contracts fail because the hands become disconnected.

# Common Expert Advice

A useful saying among expert players is:

> **"Count entries before winners."**

Many contracts that appear to have sufficient tricks fail simply because the
declarer cannot reach those tricks when needed.

Expert declarers therefore evaluate **communication first**, then determine the
best sequence for establishing and cashing winners.

# Summary

**Preserving Entries** is one of the most important planning skills in bridge.
An entry is more than a winner—it is a means of communication between declarer
and dummy. Successful declarer play depends on identifying, preserving,
creating, and timing entries so that established winners remain accessible.

In **notrump contracts**, entries are scarce and must be guarded carefully.
In **trump contracts**, declarer has greater flexibility because ruffs can
create new entries, but careless use of trumps can destroy communication just
as easily.

Mastering entry preservation transforms declarer play from simply taking tricks
to executing a coherent, well-planned strategy, making it an essential skill
for advanced and expert bridge players.
