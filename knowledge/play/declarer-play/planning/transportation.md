# Transportation

## Overview

**Transportation** is the art of moving the lead between declarer and dummy to
execute the planned line of play. While an **entry** is an individual card or
technique that transfers the lead, **transportation** is the broader strategic
management of all available routes between the two hands.

Expert declarers think of declarer and dummy as two separate locations linked by
a limited transportation network. Every successful contract depends upon using
that network efficiently.

Transportation influences nearly every aspect of declarer play, including

- establishing long suits,
- taking finesses,
- drawing trumps,
- crossruffing,
- executing squeezes,
- performing endplays,
- timing the play,
- preserving communication.

Without transportation, winners often become inaccessible.

---

# Objectives

Transportation enables declarer to

- maintain communication,
- reach established winners,
- execute the planned sequence,
- maximize trick potential,
- create additional entries,
- avoid blocked positions,
- preserve flexibility.

---

# Transportation versus Entries

These terms are closely related but not identical.

### Entry

An individual card or play that reaches the opposite hand.

Example

```
Dummy

♠ Axx
```

The ace is an entry.

---

### Transportation

The complete management of every route between the two hands.

Transportation includes

- preserving entries,
- creating entries,
- timing entries,
- choosing which entry to use,
- delaying entry consumption.

---

# The Transportation Network

Think of declarer and dummy as two cities connected by a limited number of
bridges.

Each bridge represents an entry.

Every time a bridge is crossed,

one transportation resource may disappear forever.

Experts constantly ask

> Which bridge should I use now?

and

> Which bridge must I save for later?

---

# Planning Transportation

Before trick one,

expert declarers determine

1. Where are the winners?
2. Which hand must reach them?
3. Which entries are available?
4. Which entries are essential?
5. Which transportation routes can be created?

Transportation planning begins before the first card is played.

---

# Natural Transportation

Natural transportation comes from

- aces,
- kings,
- established winners,
- trump honors.

Example

Dummy

```
♠ A

♥ K

♦ Q

♣ J
```

Each high card may become transportation.

---

# Created Transportation

Transportation can also be created.

Methods include

- ruffing,
- establishing winners,
- overtaking,
- ducking,
- unblocking,
- throw-ins.

Experts frequently manufacture transportation during the play.

---

# Transportation in Notrump

Transportation is at its most fragile in notrump contracts because

- ruffs are unavailable,
- entries are limited,
- blocked suits become critical.

Every high card should be evaluated as

- winner,

or

- transportation.

Often,

transportation is more valuable.

---

# Example

Dummy

```
♠ A4

♥ K82

♦ QJ1097

♣ 763
```

Declarer

```
♠ K63

♥ AQ5

♦ 42

♣ AKQ95
```

The ace of spades may be worth more as transportation than as an immediate
winner.

---

# Transportation in Trump Contracts

Trump contracts provide additional transportation through ruffing.

Small trumps often become

temporary bridges

between the two hands.

Experts avoid wasting these bridges.

---

# Ruffing Transportation

Dummy

```
♠ 63

♥ AKQ

♦ AQJ

♣ KQ54
```

Declarer

```
♠ AKQJ5

♥ 42

♦ K3

♣ A108
```

A small trump may later transport declarer back to dummy after diamonds have
been established.

---

# Transportation by Unblocking

Blocking destroys transportation.

Example

Dummy

```
KQJ
```

Declarer

```
A109
```

Unblocking the ace preserves movement between the hands.

---

# Transportation by Overtaking

Occasionally

an honor should overtake partner's honor solely to preserve future
transportation.

Although counterintuitive,

this is often the expert play.

---

# Transportation by Ducking

Ducking may preserve communication.

Instead of winning immediately,

declarer delays taking the trick,

maintaining a valuable transportation route.

---

# Transportation by Throw-In

Sometimes declarer intentionally loses the lead to regain access later.

This is especially common in

- endplays,
- elimination plays,
- squeeze endings.

---

# Transportation and Timing

Transportation cannot be separated from timing.

Questions include

- Which entry should be used first?
- Which must be preserved?
- When should transportation be created?
- When should it be consumed?

Timing determines transportation efficiency.

---

# Transportation and Long Suits

Long suits require

- establishment,
- communication,
- transportation.

Without transportation,

long-suit winners remain stranded.

---

# Transportation and Finesses

Multiple finesses frequently require repeated transportation between the hands.

Planning these routes is often more important than the finesse itself.

---

# Transportation and Crossruffs

Crossruffs depend entirely upon transportation.

Each ruff transfers the lead.

Destroying transportation prematurely usually destroys the crossruff.

---

# Transportation and Elimination

Transportation determines whether declarer can eliminate side suits in the
proper order.

Poor communication frequently causes elimination plays to fail.

---

# Transportation and Endplays

An endplay often succeeds only if transportation remains available at the
critical moment.

---

# Transportation and Squeezes

Almost every squeeze requires

- transportation,
- communication,
- precise timing.

Many squeeze positions fail because transportation disappears too early.

---

# Transportation Priorities

Experts generally preserve transportation in this order

1. Essential entries
2. Long-suit communication
3. Ruffing entries
4. Side-suit entries
5. Convenience entries

---

# Transportation Planning Questions

Before playing,

ask

- Which hand contains my long suit?
- Which hand contains my transportation?
- Can transportation be created?
- Which entries must survive?
- What happens if one transportation route disappears?

---

# Typical Notrump Example

Dummy

```
♠ A5

♥ KQ4

♦ QJ1086

♣ 72
```

Declarer

```
♠ KQ3

♥ A72

♦ 54

♣ AKQ95
```

Wrong

Cash club winners immediately.

Right

Preserve the ace of spades until diamonds have been established.

---

# Typical Trump Example

Contract

```
4♠
```

Dummy

```
♠ 743

♥ AKQ

♦ AQ86

♣ 53
```

Declarer

```
♠ AKQJ5

♥ 54

♦ K3

♣ AKQ6
```

Drawing all trumps immediately destroys dummy's transportation.

Experts often delay the final trump until communication has been fully utilized.

High cards frequently serve better as entries.

## Blocking Suits

## Ruffing Too Soon

Dummy loses valuable transportation.

## Ignoring Communication

Winning tricks become inaccessible.

## Focusing Only on Winners

Transportation frequently matters more than immediate tricks.

# Summary

**Transportation** is the strategic management of communication between declarer
and dummy. Whereas an **entry** is a single means of reaching the opposite hand,
transportation encompasses the planning, preservation, creation, and efficient
use of every available route throughout the play.

In **notrump contracts**, transportation relies primarily on high cards and
established winners. In **trump contracts**, ruffs and small trumps create
additional transportation opportunities but also introduce new risks if used
carelessly.

Expert declarers recognize that successful contracts are won not merely by
counting winners, but by ensuring those winners remain reachable. Effective
transportation planning is therefore one of the defining characteristics of
advanced declarer play.
