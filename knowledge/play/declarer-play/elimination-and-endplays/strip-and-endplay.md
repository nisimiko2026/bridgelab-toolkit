# Strip and Endplay

## Overview

A **Strip and Endplay** (also called **Strip Squeeze and Endplay** in some literature) is a declarer technique in which declarer first **eliminates ("strips") all safe exit cards from both hands** before deliberately conceding a trick to a defender. With no safe exit remaining, that defender is **endplayed** into making a favorable return.

The technique combines two separate ideas:

1. **Strip** – eliminate the defenders' safe exit suits.
2. **Endplay** – force a defender to give declarer a favorable lead.

Strip and endplay is one of the most powerful declarer techniques in suit contracts and often succeeds when finesses fail.

---

# Objectives

A strip and endplay aims to:

- Eliminate defenders' safe exit cards.
- Force a defender to lead a favorable suit.
- Avoid a finesse.
- Create an extra trick through forced play.
- Turn a defender's winning trick into a liability.

---

# Basic Principle

The play follows three steps:

1. Draw trumps (if appropriate).
2. Eliminate all side suits that provide defenders with safe exits.
3. Lose a trick to the chosen defender.

With no safe return available, the defender must:

- Lead into a tenace.
- Give a ruff and discard.
- Break a favorable suit.
- Concede a trick.

---

# Why It Works

Normally, a defender who wins a trick can safely exit another suit.

After the strip:

- Every safe exit has disappeared.
- Every remaining lead benefits declarer.

The defender is therefore **endplayed**.

---

# Planning the Play

Before beginning the strip, ask:

- Which defender do I want to endplay?
- Which suits must be eliminated?
- Should trumps be drawn first?
- Which exit cards will remain?
- Can the defender safely escape after winning?

The entire ending should be visualized before the first elimination play.

---

# Step 1 – Draw Trumps

In most strip-and-endplay positions, declarer first removes the defenders' trumps.

This prevents:

- Unexpected ruffs.
- Defensive interruptions.
- Additional exit cards.

Some positions deliberately retain a trump, but these are exceptions.

---

# Step 2 – Strip the Side Suits

Declarer cashes all winners in the side suits until neither declarer nor the target defender has a safe exit.

Typical suits stripped include:

- Clubs.
- Diamonds.
- Hearts.

Only the endplay suit remains.

---

# Step 3 – Concede a Trick

Declarer deliberately loses a trick to the chosen defender.

The defender wins—but cannot escape safely.

Every available return now benefits declarer.

---

# Typical Endplay Returns

After the strip, the defender may be forced to:

## Lead into a Tenace

Example:

```
Dummy
♥ A J

Declarer
♥ Q 10
```

The defender must lead hearts, giving declarer an extra trick.

---

## Give a Ruff and Discard

The defender leads a suit that allows declarer to:

- Ruff in one hand.
- Discard a loser from the other.

---

## Break a Suit

The defender is forced to lead away from an honor.

Declarer wins additional tricks without taking a finesse.

---

# Example 1 – Avoiding a Finesse

Contract:

```
4♠
```

Declarer would normally need a diamond finesse.

Instead:

- Draw trumps.
- Cash clubs and hearts.
- Exit with a diamond.

The defender must return a diamond into declarer's tenace.

The finesse becomes unnecessary.

---

# Example 2 – Ruff and Discard

Declarer strips:

- Clubs.
- Diamonds.

West wins a heart.

West's only return is another heart.

Declarer ruffs in dummy and discards a club loser.

---

# Example 3 – Throwing in the Correct Defender

Both defenders can win a trick.

Only East lacks a safe exit.

Declarer therefore throws in East rather than West.

Choosing the correct defender is essential.

---

# Strip and Endplay vs. Throw-In

These terms are closely related but not identical.

| Throw-In | Strip and Endplay |
|----------|-------------------|
| Forces a defender to win a trick | First strips safe exit cards, then throws in a defender |
| May occur without elimination | Elimination is an essential part of the technique |
| Broader concept | Specific type of throw-in |

Every strip-and-endplay includes a **throw-in**, but not every throw-in requires a strip.

---

# Strip and Endplay vs. Elimination

| Elimination | Strip and Endplay |
|-------------|-------------------|
| Removes safe exit suits | Uses elimination to create an endplay |
| Preparation | Complete technique |

Elimination is the preparation.

The endplay is the payoff.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Throwing in the wrong defender.
- Forgetting to eliminate one side suit.
- Failing to draw trumps when necessary.
- Leaving the defender with a safe exit.
- Beginning the strip without planning the ending.

# Key Principles

- Strip the defenders of their safe exit cards before conceding a trick.
- Choose the defender who has no safe return.
- Elimination is the preparation; the endplay is the objective.
- Strip and endplay often replaces an otherwise necessary finesse.
- Success depends on careful planning, accurate counting, and precise timing.
