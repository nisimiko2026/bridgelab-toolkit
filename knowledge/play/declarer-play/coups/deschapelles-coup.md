# Deschapelles Coup

## Overview

The **Deschapelles Coup** is a classic **defensive coup** in which a defender deliberately sacrifices a high honor in order to create an entry for partner and establish or enjoy additional defensive tricks.

The sacrificed honor is not intended to win the current trick. Instead, it forces declarer to win the trick while transferring the lead to the defender's partner at a favorable moment.

Although giving up a high card appears wasteful, the resulting gain in communication and timing often defeats the contract.

Named after the French chess master and card player **Alexandre Deschapelles**, the coup is one of bridge's best-known defensive maneuvers.

---

# Objectives

The Deschapelles Coup aims to:

- Create an entry for partner.
- Preserve defensive communication.
- Establish additional defensive tricks.
- Force declarer into an unfavorable position.
- Defeat the contract through timing.

---

# Basic Principle

A defender intentionally plays a high honor that is certain to lose.

The sacrifice:

- forces declarer to win,
- gives partner the lead later,
- allows partner to cash established winners.

The immediate loss of an honor is more than compensated by the later defensive gain.

---

# Typical Conditions

A Deschapelles Coup is most likely when:

- Partner has an established suit.
- Partner lacks an entry.
- Declarer controls the remaining honors.
- Sacrificing an honor creates the only entry to partner's winners.

---

# Conceptual Example

Suppose West holds:

```
♠ K
```

Partner East has several established spade winners but no entry.

Declarer holds:

```
♠ A Q
```

West leads the ♠K.

Declarer wins with the ace.

Later, East gains the lead and can immediately cash the remaining spade winners.

Without sacrificing the king, East would never have gained access to the suit.

---

# Why It Works

The coup is based on:

- communication,
- timing,
- entry management.

The sacrificed honor serves as a temporary investment that creates a more valuable defensive position.

---

# Deschapelles Coup vs. Merrimac Coup

Although both involve sacrificing a high honor, their objectives differ.

| Deschapelles Coup | Merrimac Coup |
|-------------------|---------------|
| Creates an entry for partner | Removes an opponent's entry |
| Improves defensive communication | Destroys declarer's communication |
| Defensive technique | Usually a defensive technique |
| Sacrifices an honor to help partner | Sacrifices an honor to strand winners |

---

# Planning the Coup

Before sacrificing an honor, ask:

- Does partner have established winners?
- Does partner lack an entry?
- Will the sacrifice create that entry?
- Will declarer be forced to win the trick?
- Does the timing favor the defense?

---

# Common Mistakes

- Sacrificing an honor without a clear objective.
- Failing to recognize partner's communication problem.
- Making the play when partner has no winners to enjoy.
- Giving declarer an unnecessary trick.

# Key Principles

- The Deschapelles Coup deliberately sacrifices an honor for a greater positional gain.
- The objective is usually to create an entry for partner.
- Communication between defenders is more important than preserving an individual honor.
- The coup succeeds because of timing rather than immediate trick-taking.
- It is one of the classic examples of defenders cooperating to defeat a contract.
