# Conditional Probability

## Overview

Conditional probability is one of the most powerful concepts in bridge. It is the process of **updating probabilities when new information becomes available**.

Unlike basic percentage tables, conditional probability recognizes that every bid, opening lead, signal, and played card changes the likelihood of future events.

Expert players continuously revise their expectations throughout the auction and play, making decisions based on **current evidence** rather than on the original odds.

---

# Definition

Conditional probability answers the question:

> **Given what I now know, how likely is this event?**

In bridge, the information may come from:

- The auction.
- The opening lead.
- Cards already played.
- Suit distributions.
- Defensive signals.
- Restricted Choice.
- Vacant Spaces.

Each new piece of information changes the probability of unseen cards.

---

# Mathematical Definition


::contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}


The formal definition is:

> The probability of event **A** occurring, given that **B** has already occurred.

In bridge, the mathematics is usually applied intuitively rather than through explicit calculations.

---

# A Priori vs. Conditional Probability

## A Priori Probability

Before any information is known.

Example:

- Missing queen equally likely to be with either opponent.
- Expected suit breaks.
- Standard percentage plays.

---

## Conditional Probability

After information has been revealed.

Example:

West opens the bidding.

West is now more likely to hold additional high cards.

The probability of missing honors changes immediately.

---

# Sources of New Information

Probability changes after observing:

- Opening bids.
- Overcalls.
- Takeout doubles.
- Opening lead.
- Defender's signals.
- Defender's discards.
- Suit lengths.
- Revealed honors.
- Timing of plays.

Every revealed card provides additional evidence.

---

# Example 1 – The Auction

Suppose you are missing the:

```
♠Q
```

Initially:

- West: 50%
- East: 50%

West opens:

```
1♠
```

West is now more likely to hold the queen because the opening bid suggests spade length and strength.

The probability has changed because of the auction.

---

# Example 2 – Suit Distribution

You discover:

West began with:

- Six hearts
- Three clubs

West therefore has only four cards remaining in the other suits.

East has many more unknown cards.

A missing honor in diamonds is now more likely to be with East.

This is an application of conditional probability through the **Vacant Spaces Principle**.

---

# Example 3 – Restricted Choice

Suppose declarer needs to locate:

```
QJ
```

One defender plays the jack.

Originally:

Either defender might have held both honors.

After seeing the jack, **Restricted Choice** changes the probability.

The defender who played the jack is now less likely to hold the queen as well.

---

# Example 4 – Defender Shows Out

Missing cards:

```
♥Q108
```

West discards on the second round of hearts.

West can no longer hold three hearts.

The probability of East holding the remaining honors increases dramatically.

---

# Conditional Probability During Declarer Play

Declarer continually updates probabilities when deciding:

- Which finesse to take.
- Whether to play for the drop.
- Which defender is dangerous.
- When to draw trumps.
- Which suit to establish.

Good declarers rarely rely solely on memorized percentages.

---

# Conditional Probability in Defense

Defenders also use conditional probability.

Examples include:

- Locating declarer's honors.
- Estimating declarer's shape.
- Determining partner's length.
- Choosing the correct continuation.
- Interpreting signals.

Each trick provides new evidence.

---

# Relationship to Other Probability Principles

Conditional probability is the foundation for many bridge concepts.

### Restricted Choice

Updates probabilities after an honor appears.

### Vacant Spaces

Updates probabilities after suit distributions become known.

### Percentage Plays

Begin with basic probabilities, then adjust them as information accumulates.

### Counting

Provides the information needed to update probabilities accurately.

---

# Why Experts Outperform Tables

Bridge books often list percentage plays.

Experts improve upon these by incorporating:

- Auction inference.
- Card counting.
- Distribution.
- Partnership agreements.
- Defender tendencies.

The result is a more accurate estimate than raw mathematical probability alone.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Ignoring information from the auction.
- Playing according to memorized percentages after new evidence appears.
- Forgetting to count defenders' distributions.
- Ignoring Restricted Choice.
- Ignoring Vacant Spaces.
- Treating probability as fixed throughout the hand.

---

# Practical Example

You hold:

```
♠ A J 8 4
```

Opposite:

```
♠ K 7 6 3
```

Initially, the finesse is approximately a 50% proposition.

During the play you discover:

- West started with six clubs.
- West opened the bidding.
- East showed out in diamonds.

The original 50% estimate is no longer valid.

Conditional probability now favors one defender over the other, and the correct play may change.

# Key Principles

- Conditional probability updates the odds whenever new information becomes available.
- Every bid and every played card changes the probability of unseen cards.
- Expert players continually revise their expectations throughout the hand.
- Counting, Restricted Choice, and Vacant Spaces are all applications of conditional probability.
- Successful bridge depends on combining mathematical probability with observation and inference.
