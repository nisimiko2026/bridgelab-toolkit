# Strip Squeeze Technique

## Overview

A **Strip Squeeze** is an advanced declarer-play technique that combines **Elimination Play (Strip Play)** with a **Squeeze**. Declarer first removes ("strips") the defenders of their safe exit cards and then plays a squeeze card that forces a defender to either unguard a suit or concede a favorable lead.

Unlike a simple squeeze, a Strip Squeeze gives the squeezed defender **no winning option**:

- If the defender discards a guard, declarer gains a winner.
- If the defender keeps all guards, they are thrown in and forced to lead advantageously.

The Strip Squeeze is one of the most elegant and powerful endgame techniques in bridge.

---

# Purpose

The Strip Squeeze allows declarer to:

- Combine elimination and squeeze techniques.
- Force a defender into an impossible choice.
- Create an extra trick.
- Avoid risky finesses.
- Make contracts that appear impossible.

---

# Basic Principle

A Strip Squeeze consists of three phases:

1. **Strip** the defenders of their safe exit cards.
2. **Squeeze** the key defender.
3. **Endplay** the defender if they retain all their guards.

The squeezed defender loses regardless of the choice made.

---

# Requirements

A successful Strip Squeeze usually requires:

- The count to be rectified.
- Elimination of side suits.
- A squeeze card.
- One defender guarding two suits.
- An endplay position if the defender refuses to unguard.
- Proper entries.

---

# Relationship to Other Techniques

A Strip Squeeze combines:

- **Elimination Play**
- **Simple Squeeze**
- **Endplay (Throw-In)**

It is essentially:

> **Strip + Squeeze + Endplay**

---

# Typical Position

West guards:

```
♠
♦
```

Declarer has:

- A threat in spades.
- A threat in diamonds.
- A squeeze card.
- No remaining side suits.

When the squeeze card is played:

- If West discards a spade, declarer wins a spade trick.
- If West discards a diamond, declarer wins a diamond trick.
- If West keeps both guards, declarer throws West in, forcing a favorable lead.

---

# Example 1 – Basic Strip Squeeze

Contract

```
6NT
```

Declarer:

- Cashes all side-suit winners.
- Eliminates clubs and hearts.
- Plays the final club (the squeeze card).

West must:

- Abandon a guard.
- Or be thrown in later.

Either choice gives declarer the contract.

---

# Example 2 – Suit Contract

Trump:

```
♠
```

Declarer:

- Draws trumps.
- Eliminates side suits.
- Plays the final trump.

West is squeezed between:

- Guarding diamonds.
- Guarding clubs.

If West retains both, declarer throws West in with a diamond.

---

# Example 3 – Ruff-and-Discard

After the strip:

West retains:

```
♥
♣
```

If West keeps both suits protected, declarer throws West in.

West must return:

- A heart.
- Or a club.

Either return gives declarer a ruff-and-discard.

---

# Planning the Play

Before Trick One, ask:

- Which defender is the target?
- Which suits must be stripped?
- Where are my threat cards?
- Is the count rectified?
- Do I have the necessary entries?

---

# Rectifying the Count

Most Strip Squeezes require declarer to lose all unavoidable losers before beginning the squeeze.

Failure to rectify the count usually prevents the squeeze from succeeding.

---

# Eliminating Side Suits

The elimination phase usually involves:

- Cashing side-suit winners.
- Ruffing remaining cards.
- Removing defenders' safe exits.

Only after the strip is complete should the squeeze begin.

---

# Timing

Timing is essential.

Typical order:

1. Rectify the count.
2. Strip side suits.
3. Preserve entries.
4. Play the squeeze card.
5. Endplay the defender if necessary.

---

# Common Situations

Strip Squeezes frequently occur:

- In slam contracts.
- In notrump contracts.
- After elimination plays.
- Together with Vienna Coup.
- In end positions with one key defender.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Forgetting to strip one suit.
- Playing the squeeze card too early.
- Misidentifying the squeezed defender.
- Losing an essential entry.
- Failing to rectify the count.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Preserve safe exit cards.
- Exchange guarding responsibilities.
- Maintain communication.
- Recognize developing strip positions.
- Avoid becoming the sole guardian of multiple suits.

---

# Advantages

- Extremely powerful.
- Eliminates guessing.
- Creates unavoidable extra tricks.
- Combines several expert techniques.
- Often wins impossible-looking contracts.

---

# Disadvantages

- Very difficult to recognize.
- Requires precise planning.
- Entry management is critical.
- Timing errors are usually fatal.

Before attempting a Strip Squeeze:

- Have I rectified the count?
- Have I stripped all safe exit suits?
- Which defender is guarding two suits?
- Do I have a squeeze card?
- Can I throw the defender in if necessary?

# Memory Aid

**Strip Squeeze = "Strip First, Squeeze Next, Endplay Last."**

Remember:

- **Remove all safe exit cards before squeezing.**
- **Identify the defender guarding two suits.**
- **Preserve communication and entries.**
- **If the defender keeps all guards, throw them in.**
- **A successful Strip Squeeze combines three techniques: Elimination, Squeeze, and Endplay.**

The Strip Squeeze is one of bridge's most sophisticated declarer-play techniques. By first stripping the defenders of safe exits and then applying squeeze pressure, declarer creates a position in which the defender loses regardless of the choice made. Mastering the Strip Squeeze requires precise counting, excellent timing, and careful entry management, making it a hallmark of expert declarer play.
