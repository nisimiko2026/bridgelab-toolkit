# Double Squeeze Technique

## Overview

A **Double Squeeze** is an advanced declarer-play technique in which **both defenders are squeezed**, either simultaneously or in sequence. Unlike a simple squeeze, which targets one defender guarding two suits, a double squeeze forces **each defender to relinquish a critical guard**, allowing declarer to establish additional winners.

Double squeezes are among the most difficult and elegant plays in bridge and typically occur in end positions after careful planning.

---

# Purpose

The Double Squeeze allows declarer to:

- Force both defenders to discard critical guards.
- Create two or more extra tricks.
- Make contracts that cannot be made by ordinary play.
- Avoid risky guesses or finesses.
- Maximize trick-taking potential in endgames.

---

# Basic Principle

Each defender protects different threat suits.

When declarer cashes the squeeze card:

- One defender must release a guard.
- Later, the other defender must also release a guard.
- Declarer's threat cards become winners.

Both defenders are eventually squeezed.

---

# Requirements

A successful Double Squeeze usually requires:

- The count to be rectified.
- A squeeze card.
- Threat cards against both defenders.
- Proper entries.
- Accurate counting.
- Correct identification of the guarded suits.

---

# Types of Double Squeezes

## Simultaneous Double Squeeze

Both defenders are squeezed on the same trick.

This is the most famous form.

---

## Progressive Double Squeeze

One squeeze creates the position for a second squeeze.

The squeezes occur one after another.

---

## Automatic Double Squeeze

Works regardless of which defender guards which suit.

Relatively rare.

---

## Positional Double Squeeze

Requires specific defenders to guard specific suits.

Most common in practice.

---

# Typical Position

```
West protects:

♠
♦

East protects:

♥
♣
```

Declarer has threats in all four suits.

The squeeze card forces both defenders to discard.

---

# Example 1 – Simultaneous Double Squeeze

Dummy

```
♠ Q
♥ A
♦ K
♣ —
```

Declarer

```
♠ A
♥ —
♦ A
♣ A
```

West

```
♠ K
♦ Q
```

East

```
♥ K
♣ K
```

Declarer cashes the ♣A (the squeeze card).

Both defenders must weaken their holdings.

The remaining threats become winners.

---

# Example 2 – Progressive Double Squeeze

Declarer first squeezes West.

West releases a diamond.

Later declarer squeezes East.

East releases a heart.

Declarer gains two additional tricks.

---

# Planning the Play

Before Trick One, determine:

- Which defender guards which suits.
- Where the threat cards are located.
- Whether the squeeze is positional or automatic.
- Which card will become the squeeze card.
- Whether the count must be rectified.

---

# Rectifying the Count

Most Double Squeezes require declarer to lose all unavoidable losers before the squeeze.

Failing to rectify the count usually prevents the squeeze from working.

---

# Threat Cards

Threat cards are potential winners that become established when a defender abandons a guard.

A Double Squeeze normally contains:

- At least one threat against West.
- At least one threat against East.
- Sometimes a shared threat.

---

# Entries

Entries are critical.

Declarer must preserve communication to enjoy both established threats after the squeeze.

Poor entry management ruins many Double Squeezes.

---

# Common Situations

Double Squeezes frequently occur:

- In slam contracts.
- In notrump contracts.
- After long suits have been established.
- Following elimination plays.
- Together with Vienna Coup.

---

# Example Hand

Dummy

```
♠ Q
♥ A
♦ K
♣ 7
```

Declarer

```
♠ A
♥ Q
♦ A
♣ A
```

West

```
♠ K
♦ Q
```

East

```
♥ K
♣ K
```

The ♣A becomes the squeeze card.

Neither defender can protect both of their critical suits.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Failing to rectify the count.
- Cashing the squeeze card too early.
- Misidentifying the guarded suits.
- Losing an entry.
- Confusing a simple squeeze with a double squeeze.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Exchange guarding responsibilities when possible.
- Preserve communication.
- Count declarer's winners.
- Avoid unnecessary discards.
- Recognize developing squeeze positions.

---

# Advantages

- Produces multiple extra tricks.
- Makes otherwise impossible contracts.
- Extremely powerful in end positions.
- Often avoids difficult guesses.
- Elegant and technically impressive.

---

# Disadvantages

- Very difficult to recognize.
- Requires precise counting.
- Entry management is critical.
- Timing errors usually destroy the squeeze.

# Declarer's Checklist

Before attempting a Double Squeeze:

- Have I rectified the count?
- Which defender guards which suits?
- Where are my threat cards?
- Do I have sufficient entries?
- Which card is my squeeze card?

# Memory Aid

**Double Squeeze = "Both Defenders Must Yield."**

Remember:

- **Both defenders are involved.**
- **Each protects different suits.**
- **Rectify the count first.**
- **Preserve entries carefully.**
- **Cash the squeeze card only when the ending is ready.**

The Double Squeeze is one of the most sophisticated declarer-play techniques in bridge. By simultaneously or progressively forcing both defenders to abandon essential guards, declarer can create multiple extra tricks and make contracts that appear impossible through ordinary play.
