# Progressive Squeeze

## Overview

A **Progressive Squeeze** is a squeeze in which the first squeeze **does not immediately produce the winning trick**. Instead, it forces a defender to relinquish a guard, creating a **second squeeze position** that ultimately wins the extra trick.

Unlike a simple squeeze, where one discard immediately establishes a winner, a progressive squeeze develops in **stages**. Each squeeze advances the position until the defenders can no longer protect all of the required suits.

Progressive squeezes are uncommon but represent an elegant extension of basic squeeze play.

---

# Objectives

A progressive squeeze aims to:

- Gradually remove the defenders' guards.
- Create a second squeeze position.
- Force successive discards.
- Establish an additional winner through cumulative pressure.

---

# Basic Principle

A progressive squeeze works in two stages:

1. The first squeeze forces a defender to weaken their position.
2. The resulting layout creates another squeeze.
3. The second squeeze finally produces the extra trick.

The first squeeze prepares the way for the second rather than producing an immediate winner.

---

# Requirements

A successful progressive squeeze generally requires:

- A squeeze card.
- Multiple threat cards.
- Accurate counting.
- Rectification of the count.
- Careful entry management.
- Correct timing.

Because two squeeze positions must occur in sequence, the communication requirements are often more demanding than for a simple squeeze.

---

# How It Differs from a Simple Squeeze

In a simple squeeze:

- One discard immediately establishes a winner.

In a progressive squeeze:

- The first discard merely changes the position.
- Another squeeze follows.
- Only after the second squeeze does declarer gain the extra trick.

The pressure develops progressively.

---

# Planning the Play

Declarer should identify:

- The initial squeeze card.
- The first defender under pressure.
- The second squeeze position.
- The remaining entries.
- The final established winner.

The entire sequence must be planned before the first squeeze is applied.

---

# Rectifying the Count

As with nearly all squeeze positions:

Declarer must first lose every trick that can safely be lost.

If the count has not been rectified, the defenders may simply win a trick before the progressive squeeze can develop.

---

# Communication

Communication is often the deciding factor.

Declarer must preserve sufficient entries to:

- Execute the first squeeze.
- Reach the second squeeze position.
- Cash the final established winner.

Poor entry management usually destroys the progression.

---

# Conceptual Example

Near the end of the hand:

- West guards hearts and clubs.
- East guards diamonds and clubs.

Declarer cashes the squeeze card.

West is forced to weaken one guard.

That discard creates a new squeeze position against East.

After crossing to the opposite hand, declarer cashes another winner.

East is now squeezed.

Only after both squeezes does declarer establish the final winning trick.

---

# Recognizing Progressive Squeezes

Look for positions where:

- One squeeze does not immediately succeed.
- The first squeeze changes the defensive responsibilities.
- A second squeeze becomes available.
- Entries allow declarer to continue the pressure.

These endings are usually discovered through accurate counting rather than visual pattern recognition.

---

# Progressive Squeeze vs. Double Squeeze

| Progressive Squeeze | Double Squeeze |
|---------------------|----------------|
| Squeezes occur one after another | Both defenders may be squeezed in the same ending |
| First squeeze creates the second | Two squeeze threats exist simultaneously |
| Pressure develops over time | Pressure is applied immediately |

Although both involve multiple squeeze actions, their mechanisms differ.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Expecting the first squeeze to produce an immediate winner.
- Destroying the entries needed for the second squeeze.
- Failing to rectify the count.
- Cashing winners in the wrong order.
- Misidentifying the defender under pressure.

# Key Principles

- A progressive squeeze develops in **stages** rather than producing an immediate winner.
- The first squeeze creates the conditions for a later squeeze.
- Accurate counting, timing, and entry management are essential.
- The count must be rectified before the squeeze sequence begins.
- Progressive squeezes demonstrate how sustained pressure can eventually overwhelm the defenders' communications and guards.
