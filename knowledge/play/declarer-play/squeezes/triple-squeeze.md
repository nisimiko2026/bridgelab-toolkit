# Triple Squeeze

## Overview

A **Triple Squeeze** is one of the rarest and most sophisticated squeeze positions in bridge. It occurs when a single winning card forces a defender to abandon one of **three separate guards**, allowing declarer to establish an additional trick.

Unlike a simple squeeze, which threatens one defender in two suits, or a double squeeze, which combines pressure on both defenders, a triple squeeze creates simultaneous pressure involving **three threats**.

Because of its complexity, the triple squeeze is encountered infrequently, but understanding its principles deepens a player's appreciation of squeeze play.

---

# Objectives

A triple squeeze aims to:

- Create an additional winning trick.
- Exploit a defender's inability to guard multiple suits.
- Force an impossible discard.
- Combine several squeeze threats into a single ending.

---

# Requirements

A successful triple squeeze generally requires:

- A squeeze card.
- Three independent threats.
- Careful preservation of entries.
- Accurate counting of all hands.
- Complete rectification of the count.
- Precise timing.

Missing any of these elements usually causes the squeeze to fail.

---

# The Basic Idea

Near the end of the hand:

- Declarer has one master card (the squeeze card).
- Several suits contain potential winners.
- One defender is responsible for guarding three of them.

When declarer cashes the squeeze card, that defender must discard.

No matter which suit is abandoned, declarer gains an extra trick.

---

# Relationship to Other Squeezes

A triple squeeze extends the principles of simpler squeezes.

| Technique | Pressure Applied |
|------------|------------------|
| Simple Squeeze | One defender, two suits |
| Double Squeeze | Two defenders and multiple threats |
| Triple Squeeze | Three simultaneous threats involving one or both defenders |

Triple squeezes often combine ideas found in both simple and double squeezes.

---

# Planning the Triple Squeeze

Declarer should identify:

- The squeeze card.
- The threat cards.
- Which defender protects each suit.
- The necessary entries.
- Whether the count has been rectified.

Planning begins long before the ending is reached.

---

# Rectifying the Count

As with nearly every squeeze:

Declarer must first lose every trick that can safely be lost.

If declarer still has unnecessary losers when the squeeze card is played, the defenders simply win the next trick and the squeeze disappears.

---

# Preserving Entries

Entries are especially critical.

Declarer must be able to:

- Reach each threat card.
- Cash the squeeze card from the correct hand.
- Collect the newly established winner after the discard.

Without proper communication, the squeeze cannot succeed.

---

# Recognizing a Triple Squeeze

Look for situations where:

- Three suits contain potential threats.
- One defender appears overburdened.
- The remaining tricks are nearly all winners.
- Only one discard can solve declarer's problem.

These positions are rare and often become apparent only after careful counting.

---

# Example (Conceptual)

Suppose declarer has threats in:

- Hearts
- Diamonds
- Clubs

West must protect all three suits.

Declarer cashes the final spade winner.

West must discard.

- If West releases a heart, the heart threat is established.
- If West releases a diamond, the diamond threat wins.
- If West releases a club, the club threat becomes good.

Declarer gains the extra trick regardless of West's choice.

---

# Practical Considerations

Triple squeezes are rarely planned from the start of the hand.

More often they arise naturally after:

- Accurate counting.
- Careful entry preservation.
- Successful elimination of losers.
- Proper timing.

Expert declarers recognize the possibility as the ending develops.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Failing to rectify the count.
- Destroying a vital entry.
- Cashing winners in the wrong order.
- Misidentifying the defender under pressure.
- Overlooking one of the required threat cards.

# Key Principles

- A triple squeeze is one of the most advanced forms of squeeze play.
- It depends on simultaneous pressure created by three threats.
- Accurate counting, precise timing, and careful entry management are essential.
- The count must be rectified before the squeeze card is played.
- Triple squeezes are uncommon, but they illustrate the highest level of declarer technique.
