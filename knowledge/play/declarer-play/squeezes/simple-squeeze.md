# Squeeze Technique

## Overview

A **squeeze** is an advanced declarer-play technique in which a defender is forced to discard a card that protects one suit in order to continue protecting another. By eliminating all safe discards, declarer creates an extra trick that would otherwise be unavailable.

A successful squeeze generally requires:

- One defender guarding two suits.
- Declarer having threats in both suits.
- The count to be rectified (losing the necessary tricks before the squeeze).
- All idle cards eliminated before the squeeze card is played.

Squeezes are among the most powerful techniques in contract bridge and frequently occur in both suit and notrump contracts.

---

# Basic Principle

A squeeze works by forcing a defender to choose between two equally important guards.

When the squeeze card is played:

- If the defender discards from Suit A, declarer wins an extra trick in Suit A.
- If the defender discards from Suit B, declarer wins an extra trick in Suit B.

Either discard gives declarer an additional winner.

---

# Requirements for a Successful Squeeze

A classic squeeze normally requires:

1. **Rectified Count**
2. **A Squeeze Card**
3. **One or More Threat Cards**
4. **A Defender Guarding Multiple Suits**
5. **No Safe Exit Cards Remaining**

Missing any of these elements usually prevents the squeeze from succeeding.

---

# Rectifying the Count

Before executing a squeeze, declarer must usually lose all unavoidable losers.

This is called **rectifying the count**.

Example:

If declarer can afford one loser, that loser should usually be taken before attempting the squeeze.

---

# Threat Cards

A **threat** is a card that can become a winner if the defender abandons a guard.

Example:

```
North
♠ Q
♥ —
♦ K
♣ —

South
♠ A
♥ A
♦ —
♣ A
```

If West guards both spades and diamonds, declarer can squeeze West by cashing the ♣A (the squeeze card).

---

# The Squeeze Card

The squeeze card is the winner that forces the defender to discard.

Usually it is:

- The last winner in a side suit.
- A long established suit.
- The final trump (in suit contracts).

---

# Types of Squeezes

## Simple Squeeze

Only one defender is squeezed.

Most common type.

---

## Double Squeeze

Both defenders are squeezed simultaneously.

Requires careful planning.

---

## Progressive Squeeze

One squeeze creates another squeeze later.

---

## Triple Squeeze

Three suits are involved.

Very rare.

---

## Automatic Squeeze

Works regardless of which defender guards the suits.

---

## Positional Squeeze

Depends on the correct defender holding the guards.

---

# Simple Squeeze Example

## Position

```
Dummy
♠ Q
♥ A
♦ K

Declarer
♠ A
♥ —
♦ A
```

West holds:

```
♠ K
♦ Q
```

Declarer cashes the ♥A.

West must discard:

- If ♠K is discarded → ♠Q wins.
- If ♦Q is discarded → ♦K wins.

Declarer gains an extra trick.

---

# Notrump Squeeze Example

Contract:

```
3NT
```

Declarer has:

- Established clubs.
- Threats in hearts and spades.

West guards both suits.

After cashing clubs, West must release one guard.

Declarer scores the remaining threat.

---

# Suit Contract Squeeze

Squeezes also occur in suit contracts.

Frequently:

- The last trump becomes the squeeze card.
- Defenders must keep guarding side suits while following to trumps.

---

# Vienna Coup

Sometimes a squeeze requires unblocking an honor before the ending.

Example:

Cashing an ace early to unblock a king later.

This preparation is called the **Vienna Coup**.

---

# Entry Management

Good squeezes require careful entries.

Common mistakes:

- Cashing winners too early.
- Blocking threat suits.
- Losing communication between hands.

---

# Identifying a Squeeze

Look for:

- One defender guarding two suits.
- Long established winners.
- Few cards remaining.
- No safe discards.

If all exist, a squeeze may be possible.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Not rectifying the count.
- Cashing the squeeze card too early.
- Blocking the threat suit.
- Miscounting defender's cards.
- Overlooking entries.

---

---

# Defensive Techniques

Defenders can avoid squeezes by:

- Maintaining communications.
- Discarding correctly.
- Reading declarer's threats.
- Exchanging guarding duties when possible.
- Eliminating one threat before the ending.

---

# Advantages

- Creates extra tricks without establishing a suit.
- Wins contracts that appear impossible.
- Effective in both suit and notrump contracts.
- Frequently decisive in slam contracts.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires accurate counting.
- Demands careful planning from trick one.
- Entry errors often ruin the squeeze.
- Some squeezes depend on precise defender distribution.

# Memory Guide

## Four Requirements

- Rectify the count.
- Identify the threat cards.
- Preserve entries.
- Play the squeeze card at the correct moment.

---

## Declarer's Checklist

Before attempting a squeeze, ask:

- Have I lost all necessary losers?
- Which defender guards two suits?
- Where are my threat cards?
- Do I have the right entries?
- Which card is my squeeze card?

---

# Memory Aid

**SQUEEZE**

- **S**ee the threats.
- **Q**uit losing tricks first (rectify the count).
- **U**nblock honors if necessary.
- **E**ntries must be preserved.
- **E**liminate safe exits.
- **Z**ero safe discards for the defender.
- **E**xecute the squeeze card last.

A squeeze is one of bridge's most elegant techniques. By forcing a defender to abandon one of two essential guards, declarer creates an otherwise impossible extra trick. Mastering the concepts of rectifying the count, preserving entries, and recognizing threat cards is the key to recognizing and executing successful squeezes.
