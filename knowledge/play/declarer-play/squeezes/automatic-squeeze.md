# Automatic Squeeze

## Overview

An **Automatic Squeeze** is a squeeze in which the identity of the defender under pressure is **irrelevant**. Regardless of which defender guards the threat suits, the squeeze succeeds because **every possible distribution of the critical guards results in a successful squeeze**.

Unlike a **Positional Squeeze**, which depends on one particular defender holding the guarded suits, an automatic squeeze works automatically once the necessary technical conditions are present.

Automatic squeezes are among the most reliable squeeze positions because declarer does not need to locate the critical defender.

---

# Objectives

An automatic squeeze aims to:

- Force a defender to abandon a necessary guard.
- Establish an additional winner.
- Avoid guessing which defender holds the critical cards.
- Produce an extra trick regardless of the defenders' exact honor locations.

---

# Basic Principle

Suppose declarer has threats in:

- Hearts.
- Clubs.

Either defender may guard:

- Hearts.
- Clubs.

When declarer cashes the squeeze card:

- Whoever guards both suits is squeezed.
- If the guards are divided, the squeeze still succeeds because each defender is forced to protect only one suit while the other defender becomes responsible for the second threat.

The exact location of the guards is unimportant.

---

# Requirements

An automatic squeeze generally requires:

- A squeeze card.
- Two or more threat cards.
- Adequate communication.
- Correct timing.
- Rectification of the count.
- A layout in which every possible guard position is favorable.

---

# Why It Is "Automatic"

The squeeze succeeds because declarer does **not** need to know:

- Which defender holds the missing honor.
- Which defender guards each threat.
- How the defenders divide their responsibilities.

Any legal distribution of the critical guards results in success.

---

# Planning the Play

Declarer should identify:

- The squeeze card.
- The threat suits.
- The entries.
- The remaining winners.
- Whether the count has been rectified.

No attempt is needed to identify a particular defender.

---

# Rectifying the Count

As with most squeeze positions:

Declarer must first lose every unavoidable loser.

Only after the count has been rectified can the squeeze force a meaningful discard.

---

# Communication

Communication remains essential.

Declarer must still be able to:

- Reach both threat cards.
- Cash the squeeze card.
- Enjoy the established winner.

An automatic squeeze is not automatic with respect to communication.

---

# Conceptual Example

Near the end of the hand:

```
Dummy
♥ A
♣ K

Declarer
♦ A  (squeeze card)
♣ A
```

The defenders guard hearts and clubs.

Declarer cashes the ♦A.

Regardless of which defender protects which suit, one of the threats becomes established after the forced discard.

Declarer wins the remaining tricks.

---

# Automatic vs. Positional Squeeze

| Automatic Squeeze | Positional Squeeze |
|-------------------|--------------------|
| Works regardless of which defender holds the guards | Requires one specific defender to hold the guards |
| No guesswork | Depends on correctly identifying the defender |
| More reliable | More dependent on card location |

This distinction is fundamental in squeeze theory.

---

# Automatic vs. Simple Squeeze

Every automatic squeeze is a type of simple squeeze, but not every simple squeeze is automatic.

Some simple squeezes require the guarded suits to lie behind a particular defender.

Automatic squeezes remove that requirement.

---

# Recognizing the Position

Ask yourself:

- Do I have a squeeze card?
- Have I rectified the count?
- Are communication and entries intact?
- Will the squeeze succeed regardless of which defender guards the threats?

If the answer is yes, the squeeze is automatic.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Assuming every simple squeeze is automatic.
- Failing to preserve communication.
- Not rectifying the count.
- Misidentifying the threat cards.
- Cashing the squeeze card too early.

# Key Principles

- An automatic squeeze succeeds regardless of which defender holds the critical guards.
- Declarer does not need to identify the defender under pressure.
- Communication, timing, and rectifying the count remain essential.
- Automatic squeezes are generally more reliable than positional squeezes.
- Before assuming a squeeze is automatic, verify that every possible guard distribution leads to success.
