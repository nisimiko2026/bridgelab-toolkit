# Bridge Glossary

## Overview

This glossary contains commonly used terms in **Contract Bridge** and **Duplicate Bridge**. Terms are listed alphabetically with concise definitions. Where appropriate, the glossary refers to concepts that are explained in greater detail elsewhere in the Bridge Knowledge Library.

---

# A

### Adjusted Score
A score assigned by the tournament director instead of the table result because of an irregularity.

### Alert
A notification that a bid has an artificial or unexpected meaning and may require an explanation.

### Artificial Bid
A bid whose meaning is not the natural suit or level bid.

### Attitude Signal
A defensive signal indicating whether partner should continue the suit.

---

# B

### Balanced Hand
A hand with no voids or singletons and at most one doubleton.

Typical patterns include:

- 4-3-3-3
- 4-4-3-2
- 5-3-3-2

### Bergen Raises
A convention used after a major-suit opening to describe support and strength.

### Blackwood
A slam convention used to ask for Aces (or Key Cards in RKCB).

### Board
A duplicate bridge deal that is played at multiple tables.

### Break
The distribution of missing cards in a suit.

Example:

```
3-2 Break
```

---

# C

### Cappelletti
A defense against a 1NT opening.

### Cash
To take immediate winners.

### Control
An Ace or King that prevents the opponents from taking the first two tricks in a suit.

### Count Signal
A defensive signal showing the number of cards held in a suit.

### Cross Ruff
A declarer play where both hands alternately ruff winners.

### Cue Bid
A bid of the opponents' suit, usually showing strength or a specific convention.

---

# D

### Declarer
The player who first bid the denomination of the final contract.

### Deep Finesse
A finesse against an intermediate honor rather than the highest missing honor.

### Defense
The partnership attempting to defeat the contract.

### Director
The tournament official responsible for applying the Laws of Duplicate Bridge.

### Double
A call that increases the score if the contract succeeds and the penalty if it fails.

### Double Dummy
Analysis assuming all four hands are visible.

### Dummy
Declarer's partner, whose hand is placed face-up after the opening lead.

### Duck
To intentionally lose a trick.

---

# E

### Endplay
A play forcing an opponent to give declarer a favorable result.

### Entry
A card that allows access to a hand.

### Entry Management
Planning how to move between the two hands.

---

# F

### False Card
A deceptive card played to mislead declarer or the defenders.

### Finesse
A technique that attempts to win a trick with a lower honor by assuming the location of a missing higher honor.

### Fit
A partnership holding at least eight cards in the same suit.

### Forcing Bid
A bid that requires partner to bid again.

### Fourth Best
The standard opening lead of the fourth-highest card from the longest and strongest suit.

---

# G

### Game
A contract worth at least 100 trick points before bonuses.

### Gerber
A slam convention using 4♣ to ask for Aces after a Notrump auction.

### Grand Slam
A contract at the seven level requiring all 13 tricks.

---

# H

### Hand Evaluation
The process of estimating the playing strength of a hand.

### Hold-Up Play
A declarer technique that delays winning a trick to cut defenders' communication.

### Honor
An Ace, King, Queen, Jack, or Ten.

### High Card Points (HCP)
A point-count method:

- Ace = 4
- King = 3
- Queen = 2
- Jack = 1

---

# I

### IMP
International Match Point used in team scoring.

### Inference
A logical conclusion based on the auction and play.

### Interior Sequence
An honor holding such as AQ10 or KJ10.

### Intra-Finesse
A finesse that depends on the position of intermediate honors.

---

# J

### Jacoby 2NT
A game-forcing raise of partner's major.

### Jacoby Transfer
A transfer bid after a Notrump opening.

### Journalist Leads
An opening lead system using honor leads to describe specific holdings.

---

# K

### Kaplan-Rubens
An advanced hand evaluation method emphasizing playing strength rather than HCP alone.

### Key Card
An Ace or the King of trumps in Roman Key Card Blackwood.

---

# L

### Lavinthal
A suit-preference signaling method.

### Lead
The first card played to a trick.

### Lebensohl
A convention used after interference over a Notrump opening.

### Losing Trick Count (LTC)
A method for evaluating suit-contract potential.

---

# M

### Major Suit
Hearts or Spades.

### Matchpoints
A duplicate scoring method where each board is compared with every other pair.

### Merrimac Coup
A play that sacrifices a high card to remove an opponent's entry.

### Michaels Cue Bid
A convention showing a two-suited hand after an opponent opens.

### Minor Suit
Clubs or Diamonds.

---

# N

### Negative Double
A conventional double showing values and usually an unbid major.

### Notrump
A denomination with no trump suit.

---

# O

### Opening Bid
The first bid other than Pass.

### Opening Lead
The first card led against the contract.

### Overcall
A bid made after an opponent has opened.

### Overtrick
Any trick taken beyond those required by the contract.

---

# P

### Pair
The two players who play together throughout a session.

### Partscore
A contract worth fewer than 100 trick points before bonuses.

### Pass
A call that neither bids nor doubles.

### Penalty Double
A double intended to defeat the contract for a larger score.

### Percentage Play
A line of play based on the highest mathematical probability.

### Precision
A strong-club bidding system.

### Promotion
Creating a winner by forcing out higher cards.

---

# Q

### Quick Trick
An immediate defensive winner, usually an Ace or AK combination.

---

# R

### Rectify the Count
Lose the necessary tricks before executing a squeeze.

### Redouble
A call that doubles the doubled score.

### Relay Bid
An artificial bid asking partner for more information.

### Restricted Choice
A probability principle used in card play.

### Reverse
A rebid showing extra strength and a higher-ranking second suit.

### Roman Key Card Blackwood (RKCB)
A version of Blackwood that counts the trump King as a Key Card.

### Ruff
To win a trick by playing a trump.

### Rusinow Leads
An opening lead method in which the second-highest honor of a sequence is led.

---

# S

### Safety Play
A line of play designed to maximize the chance of making the contract.

### Sacrifice
A deliberate bid expected to go down for a smaller loss than the opponents' likely score.

### SAYC
Standard American Yellow Card bidding system.

### Short Suit
A singleton or void.

### Sign-Off
A bid indicating no further interest in the auction.

### Singleton
A suit containing one card.

### Slam
A contract at the six or seven level.

### Smolen
A convention after Stayman showing five cards in the other major.

### Squeeze
A play that forces an opponent to abandon a guard.

### Stayman
A convention asking for a four-card major after a Notrump opening.

### Strip
Removing safe exit cards before an endplay.

### Support Double
A double showing exactly three-card support for partner.

### Suit Preference Signal
A signal requesting the higher or lower-ranking side suit.

---

# T

### Takeout Double
A double asking partner to bid an unbid suit.

### Tempo
The pace at which a player bids or plays.

### Texas Transfer
A transfer to a major at the four level.

### Third Hand High
A defensive guideline recommending that third hand usually plays high.

### Throw-In
An endplay forcing an opponent to lead favorably.

### Timing
The order in which declarer or defenders should execute their plan.

### Touching Honors
Consecutive honors such as KQJ or QJ10.

### Transfer
An artificial bid asking partner to bid another suit.

### Trump
The suit that outranks the others during play.

### Trump Coup
A coup involving control of the trump suit.

---

# U

### Unauthorized Information (UI)
Information obtained from partner's tempo, mannerisms, or other illegal sources.

### Uppercut
A defensive play in which an overruff promotes partner's trump.

### Unusual Notrump
A convention showing two lower unbid suits.

---

# V

### Vacant Places
A probability principle based on the number of unknown card positions.

### Vienna Coup
A declarer play that unblocks a high card before a squeeze.

### Void
A suit containing no cards.

### Vulnerability
A condition affecting bonuses and penalties.

---

# W

### Weak Two
A preemptive opening bid at the two level in a six-card suit.

### Winner
A card that is certain to take a trick.
