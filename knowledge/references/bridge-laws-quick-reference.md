# Bridge Laws Quick Reference

## Overview

This guide provides a concise reference to the most important **Laws of Duplicate Bridge** that players encounter during normal tournament play. It is **not** a substitute for the official Laws published by the **World Bridge Federation (WBF)**, but rather a practical summary of the situations most commonly faced at the table.

The guiding principle is:

> **When an irregularity occurs, call the Tournament Director immediately.**

---

# Before Play

## Board

- Verify the correct board number.
- Check vulnerability.
- Check dealer.
- Count your cards (13).

Never remove cards from the board before all players are present.

---

## Convention Card

Players should have an accurate convention card describing their partnership agreements.

Opponents are entitled to review it.

---

# During the Auction

## Legal Calls

The only legal calls are:

- Pass
- Bid
- Double
- Redouble

Calls are made in rotation.

---

## Alerting

Alert artificial or unexpected partnership agreements according to the regulations of the sponsoring organization.

Explain **agreements**, not opinions.

---

## Hesitations

Players may think.

However:

- Hesitations create **Unauthorized Information (UI)**.
- Partner must ignore the UI.
- If UI may affect the result, call the Director.

---

## Insufficient Bid

An insufficient bid is lower than the previous bid.

Example:

```
1♥

1♦
```

The Director should be called.

Depending on the circumstances, the bid may be corrected or accepted under the Laws.

---

## Out-of-Turn Call

A bid, pass, double, or redouble made by the wrong player.

Do not continue the auction.

Call the Director.

---

# During Play

## Opening Lead

The opening lead is made after the auction ends.

Dummy is placed on the table only after the opening lead has been faced.

---

## Dummy's Rights

Dummy may:

- Arrange the hand.
- Play cards as directed by declarer.
- Prevent declarer from revoking (when permitted by the Laws).
- Attempt to prevent irregularities.

Dummy may **not**:

- Suggest plays.
- Participate in the defense.
- Call attention to most irregularities during play (except as permitted by the Laws).

---

## Declarer's Rights

Declarer chooses:

- Which card dummy plays.
- The order of play.
- Claims and concessions.

---

## Defender's Rights

Defenders may:

- Signal legally.
- Ask for explanations of agreements.
- Protect their own rights.
- Call the Director whenever appropriate.

---

# Revokes

A revoke occurs when a player:

- Fails to follow suit while holding a card of that suit.

If a revoke is discovered:

- Stop play if appropriate.
- Call the Director.

The Director determines any adjustment or penalty.

---

# Exposed Cards

A card accidentally exposed may become:

- A penalty card.
- Information available to the opponents.

Do not attempt to correct the situation without the Director.

---

# Incorrect Explanation

If an incorrect explanation of a partnership agreement has been given:

- Call the Director as soon as appropriate.
- The Director will determine whether damage occurred.

Players are expected to explain **their partnership agreements**, even if those agreements were misunderstood by partner.

---

# Claims

A player may claim remaining tricks.

When claiming:

- State the intended line of play if it is not obvious.
- Opponents may accept or dispute the claim.
- If disputed, call the Director.

---

# Concessions

A player may concede one or more tricks.

Once accepted, a concession is generally binding.

---

# Unauthorized Information

Examples include:

- Hesitations.
- Facial expressions.
- Tone of voice.
- Gestures.
- Unusual tempo.

Partner must ignore unauthorized information.

---

# Authorized Information

Players may use:

- The auction.
- Cards played.
- Dummy.
- Opponents' explanations.
- Convention cards.
- Legal inferences.

---

# Calling the Director

Call the Director immediately for:

- Revokes.
- Insufficient bids.
- Out-of-turn calls.
- Exposed cards.
- Incorrect explanations.
- Unauthorized information concerns.
- Disputed claims.
- Any irregularity.

Calling the Director is a normal part of duplicate bridge.

---

# Ethics

Players must:

- Compete fairly.
- Provide complete disclosure.
- Maintain consistent tempo.
- Respect opponents.
- Avoid unauthorized communication.

---

# Slow Play

Players should maintain a reasonable pace.

Repeated slow play may result in procedural penalties.

---

# Mobile Phones

Most tournaments require:

- Phones to be switched off or placed in airplane mode.
- No use during play.

Violations may incur penalties.

---

# Duplicate Etiquette

Good etiquette includes:

- Greeting opponents.
- Keeping the table quiet.
- Avoiding criticism.
- Avoiding celebration or visible disappointment.
- Thanking opponents after the round.

---

# Common Tournament Penalties

Possible Director actions include:

- Adjusted score.
- Procedural penalty.
- Lead restrictions.
- Penalty card procedures.
- Warning.
- Time penalty.

The exact remedy depends on the applicable Laws and regulations.

---

# Frequently Encountered Situations

| Situation | Recommended Action |
|-----------|--------------------|
| Revoke | Call Director |
| Hesitation | Continue normally; Director if necessary |
| Insufficient bid | Call Director |
| Wrong explanation | Call Director |
| Exposed card | Call Director |
| Out-of-turn call | Call Director |
| Disputed claim | Call Director |
| Wrong board | Stop immediately and call Director |

---

# What Players Should Never Do

- Attempt to apply penalties themselves.
- Argue about the Laws.
- Continue after a serious irregularity without guidance.
- Use partner's hesitation.
- Explain opinions instead of agreements.

---

# Essential Player Responsibilities

Every player should:

- Count 13 cards before play.
- Follow suit whenever possible.
- Explain partnership agreements accurately.
- Protect both sides' rights.
- Call the Director whenever necessary.

---

# Quick Checklist

Before play:

- ✓ Correct board
- ✓ Correct dealer
- ✓ Correct vulnerability
- ✓ Thirteen cards

During bidding:

- ✓ Bid in turn
- ✓ Alert when required
- ✓ Explain agreements

During play:

- ✓ Follow suit
- ✓ Maintain tempo
- ✓ Signal legally
- ✓ Respect dummy's rights

If something goes wrong:

- ✓ Stop
- ✓ Call the Director
- ✓ Let the Director apply the Laws

# Memory Guide

## Five Golden Rules

1. Count your cards.
2. Explain agreements honestly.
3. Ignore unauthorized information.
4. Call the Director whenever an irregularity occurs.
5. Treat opponents with courtesy and respect.

---

# Memory Aid

**Bridge Laws = "When in Doubt, Call the Director."**

Remember:

- **The Tournament Director is responsible for applying the Laws—not the players.**
- **Always explain partnership agreements, never your opinions or assumptions.**
- **Unauthorized information must never influence your decisions.**
- **Following suit, maintaining proper tempo, and respecting etiquette are essential parts of ethical play.**
- **The purpose of the Laws is not to punish mistakes, but to restore equity and ensure fair competition.**

This quick reference summarizes the most frequently encountered Laws and procedures in duplicate bridge. While it cannot replace the official Laws of Duplicate Bridge, it provides a practical guide for players at every level and reinforces the ethical principles that make duplicate bridge one of the world's premier mind sports.
