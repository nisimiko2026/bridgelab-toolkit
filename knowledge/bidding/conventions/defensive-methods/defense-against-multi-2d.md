# Defense Against Multi 2♦

## Overview

The **Multi 2♦** opening is one of the most popular artificial weak-two
conventions in modern bridge. It typically shows one of several hand types,
most commonly

- a weak two in either major,
- occasionally a strong balanced hand,
- or another strong hand depending on partnership style.

Because the exact hand is initially concealed, defending against Multi 2♦
requires specialized agreements.

Unlike defenses against natural weak twos, defenders must cope with

- uncertainty regarding opener's suit,
- uncertainty regarding strength,
- possible strong options,
- limited bidding space.

Modern expert partnerships therefore employ dedicated defenses designed to

- compete immediately,
- identify major-suit fits,
- penalize strong variants,
- minimize ambiguity.

---

# Objectives

A sound defense should

- compete effectively opposite weak majors,
- avoid entering against strong variants,
- locate major-suit fits,
- distinguish one-suited and two-suited hands,
- preserve penalty opportunities,
- minimize guesswork.

# Fundamental Defensive Philosophy

The defense should

1. Handle both weak majors.
2. Handle unknown suits.
3. Avoid helping strong options.
4. Penalize when appropriate.

# Modern Expert Treatments

There is **no universal defense** to Multi 2♦. The three most common expert
approaches are:

## Method 1 — Natural (Most Common)

- Double = 15–18 balanced or strong hand
- Suit bids = Natural
- 2NT = Balanced
- 3-level bids = Natural

This is the most widely played treatment.

---

## Method 2 — Multi-Landy

A conventional defense based on Landy principles:

- Double = Values
- 2♥ = Natural
- 2♠ = Natural
- 2NT = Both minors
- Higher bids = Two-suited hands

Popular in European tournament play.

---

## Method 3 — Transfer-Based Defense

Some world-class partnerships employ transfer overcalls after Multi, allowing
more efficient hand description and preserving bidding space. These methods are
powerful but require extensive partnership agreements and are rarely suitable
for casual partnerships.

---

# Expert Partnership Agreements

A partnership should explicitly agree on:

- Meaning of Double
- Whether Double is penalty, takeout, or values
- 2NT treatment
- Two-suited overcalls
- Responses after opener identifies the major
- Continuations after strong Multi options
- Balancing methods
- Lebensohl or Rubensohl continuations
- Actions after further interference

# Historical Perspective

The Multi 2♦ convention became popular in Europe during the 1970s because it
combined weak major openings into a single artificial bid while leaving other
strong hand types available as optional additions. As its popularity grew,
specialized defenses evolved to cope with the uncertainty created by the hidden
major. Today, most expert partnerships adopt a dedicated defense, often based
on values doubles, natural overcalls, and structured continuations after the
opener clarifies the suit.

---

# Summary

Defending against **Multi 2♦** requires methods that accommodate both the
uncertainty of opener's major suit and the possibility of additional strong
hand types. While simple natural defenses remain common, expert partnerships
often employ **values doubles**, **Multi-Landy**, **Lebensohl continuations**,
or transfer-based methods to compete effectively.

Because Multi 2♦ itself is not standardized, the defense must always be adapted
to the opponents' specific version. Detailed partnership agreements are
essential for consistent and accurate competitive bidding.
