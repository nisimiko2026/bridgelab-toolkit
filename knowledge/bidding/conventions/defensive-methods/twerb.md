# TWERB

## Overview

**TWERB** (**Transfer WERB**) is an advanced defensive convention that uses
**transfer overcalls** instead of natural overcalls after opponents open the
bidding. The acronym is commonly interpreted as **Transfer WERB** (Transfer
Weak Entry Response Bids), although usage varies among authors and
partnerships.

Rather than bidding the suit actually held, the defender bids the suit
**immediately below** the intended suit, allowing partner to become declarer
more often and preserving bidding space.

TWERB can be used against

- natural one-level openings,
- weak two openings,
- Multi 2♦,
- strong club systems,
- artificial openings,
- and other conventional methods.

Modern expert partnerships frequently adapt TWERB to their preferred defensive
structure.

---

# Objectives

TWERB aims to

- improve hand description,
- preserve bidding space,
- conceal suit ownership,
- place the stronger hand as declarer,
- increase constructive bidding accuracy,
- interfere efficiently.

---

# Basic Principle

A bid transfers to the **next higher suit**.

Example

```
Overcall     Shows

1♦           Hearts

1♥           Spades

1♠           Clubs

2♣           Diamonds
```

The overcall itself is artificial.

Partner normally completes the transfer.

---

# Why Play Transfers?

Natural overcalls consume valuable bidding space.

Transfers allow

- stronger descriptive auctions,
- lower-level competition,
- hidden honor location,
- flexible continuations.

---

# Basic Structure

The most common structure is

| Overcall | Shows |
|----------|-------|
| Double | Values or takeout |
| 1♦ | Hearts |
| 1♥ | Spades |
| 1♠ | Clubs |
| 2♣ | Diamonds |
| 2♦ | Natural or partnership agreement |
| 2NT | Two-suiter or conventional |

Several expert variations exist.

# Historical Perspective

TWERB emerged during the expansion of transfer-based bidding methods in the late
twentieth century. Inspired by the success of Jacoby Transfers and transfer
responses over strong notrump openings, expert partnerships began applying the
same philosophy to competitive auctions. By replacing natural overcalls with
transfers, they gained bidding space, concealed the location of high cards, and
often placed the stronger hand as declarer.

Although never standardized to the extent of conventions such as Stayman or
Michaels, TWERB has become an effective specialist method for partnerships that
regularly encounter artificial systems and complex competitive auctions.

---

# Expert Notes

Unlike many defensive conventions, **TWERB is a family of methods rather than a
single standardized convention**. Different partnerships assign different
transfer meanings depending on the opening bid being defended and the overall
defensive system.

For this reason, expert partnerships should always document their complete
TWERB structure on their convention card and discuss continuations in detail
before adopting the method.

---

# Summary

**TWERB** is an advanced transfer-overcall system that replaces natural
competitive bids with artificial transfer calls. It provides efficient hand
description, conserves bidding space, conceals honor location, and frequently
places the stronger defender in the declarer's seat.

Because several expert variants exist, successful use of TWERB depends on
thorough partnership agreements. In experienced hands, however, it is one of
the most flexible and powerful methods available for defending against both
natural and artificial opening systems.
