# Suction Convention

## Overview

The **Suction Convention** is an advanced artificial defense against **Strong
Notrump** and **Strong Club** systems. Developed by **Lionel Wright**, it uses
artificial overcalls to describe one- and two-suited hands while making it
difficult for the opponents to locate their best contract.

Unlike natural defenses such as **Cappelletti** or **DONT**, Suction frequently
requires responder to guess whether the overcaller holds

- a one-suited hand, or
- a two-suited hand.

This uncertainty creates significant bidding pressure while allowing the
defenders to compete efficiently with a wide range of distributions.

Suction is primarily an expert convention because it requires extensive
partnership agreements and accurate memory.

---

# Objectives

The Suction Convention aims to

- disrupt strong notrump auctions,
- interfere with relay systems,
- describe one- and two-suited hands,
- preserve bidding space,
- create difficult decisions for responder,
- conceal the exact distribution.

---

# Historical Background

The convention was developed by **Lionel Wright** during the late twentieth
century as an aggressive alternative to natural defenses against strong
notrump openings.

Since then, numerous modified versions have appeared.

Today, "Suction" refers to a **family of related methods**, rather than one
universally standardized convention.

---

# Basic Philosophy

Instead of bidding the suit actually held,

the overcaller frequently bids

the suit immediately **below** it.

The opponents therefore cannot determine immediately whether the overcaller
holds

- one suit,
- or two suits.

---

# Classical Structure

Against

```
1NT
```

the original Suction structure is

| Overcall | Shows |
|----------|--------|
| Double | Strong hand or partnership agreement |
| 2♣ | Clubs OR Diamonds + another suit |
| 2♦ | Diamonds OR Hearts + another suit |
| 2♥ | Hearts OR Spades + a minor |
| 2♠ | Spades OR Clubs + another suit |
| 2NT | Both minors (optional) |

Several published versions differ substantially.

---

# Fundamental Principle

Every artificial bid may represent

- one long suit,

or

- the next higher suit together with another suit.

Example

```
2♦
```

may show

```
♦

or

♥ + another suit
```

The uncertainty is the essence of Suction.

---

# One-Suited Hands

Some partnerships permit

```
2♥

=

Long Hearts
```

Others require

```
Hearts + another suit.
```

Agreement is essential.

---

# Two-Suited Hands

Most expert partnerships require

at least

```
5-4
```

distribution.

Many prefer

```
5-5
```

particularly at unfavorable vulnerability.

---

# Why the Name "Suction"?

The convention tends to

"suck"

the opponents into difficult decisions.

Responder often cannot distinguish between

- a one-suited hand,
- a two-suited hand,

until much later in the auction.

---

# Typical Auction

```
West      North

1NT        2♦*
```

The

```
2♦
```

may represent

- Diamonds,

or

- Hearts plus another suit.

Responder has little reliable information.

---

# Another Example

```
1NT

2♥*
```

Could mean

```
Hearts

or

Spades + Minor
```

depending upon partnership agreement.

# Advancer's Responsibilities

Partner must

- recognize the artificial call,
- distinguish forcing and non-forcing actions,
- clarify the intended suit,
- support constructively.

The partnership should agree

when to

- pass,
- correct,
- invite,
- force.

---

# Continuations

Once the actual suit becomes known,

normal bidding resumes.

Most partnerships use

- natural raises,
- cue bids,
- game tries,
- slam methods.

---

# Against Strong Club

Many partnerships adapt Suction

against

```
1♣
```

Artificial.

The underlying philosophy remains unchanged,

although the actual bid meanings often differ.

---

# Against Precision

Suction is especially effective because

Precision depends heavily upon

- low-level relays,
- descriptive responses.

Artificial interference complicates these auctions.

---

# Against Polish Club

Some partnerships

extend Suction

to

Polish Club systems.

This requires additional agreements.

---

# Responses

After an artificial overcall,

advancer usually

- chooses the most likely strain,
- allows correction,
- competes naturally thereafter.

---

# Corrective Auctions

whether

- Hearts,

# Modern Expert Variations

There is **no universally accepted Suction Convention**.

Modern partnerships generally play one of the following.

## Original Suction

The original Lionel Wright structure.

Designed primarily against

Strong 1NT.

---

## Modified Suction

More descriptive responses.

Simplified continuations.

Most common today.

---

## Strong Club Suction

Adapted specifically for

- Precision
- Blue Club
- Polish Club
- Strong Club systems

---

## Hybrid Methods

Many partnerships combine Suction with

- CRASH
- Meckwell
- TWERB

# Important Partnership Note

> **Suction is not a standardized convention.**

Different books, bridge organizations, and expert partnerships employ
significantly different bid meanings and continuations. Before adopting
Suction, partners should document the complete structure on their convention
card and review all continuations.

---

# Historical Perspective

Suction was one of the earliest convention defenses to exploit ambiguity as a
strategic weapon. By forcing responder to distinguish between one-suited and
two-suited hands with incomplete information, the convention often disrupted
otherwise precise strong notrump and strong club auctions.

Although many expert partnerships now prefer CRASH, Meckwell, or transfer-based
methods, Suction remains an influential convention whose concepts continue to
appear in numerous modern defensive systems.

---

# Expert Strategy

Successful Suction partnerships emphasize

- excellent memory,
- disciplined suit quality,
- accurate judgment,
- clear correction methods,
- vulnerability awareness.

The convention is strongest against highly structured relay systems where early
ambiguity creates the greatest practical problems.

---

# Summary

The **Suction Convention** is an advanced artificial defense against **Strong
Notrump** and **Strong Club** systems. By combining one-suited and two-suited
hands within the same artificial overcalls, it creates uncertainty that makes
constructive bidding difficult for the opponents while preserving flexibility
for the defenders.

Although numerous versions exist, all Suction methods share the same central
idea: **replace immediate disclosure with controlled ambiguity**. In experienced
partnerships, Suction remains one of the most imaginative and disruptive
defensive conventions in tournament bridge.
