# Relay Principle

## Overview

The **Relay Principle** is one of the fundamental concepts of modern artificial
bidding systems. A **relay** is an artificial bid that asks partner to describe
his hand further, allowing one player to become the **captain** of the auction
while the other simply answers a structured series of questions.

Unlike natural bidding, where both players describe their own hands, relay
bidding concentrates the decision-making in one hand. The captain asks
questions, and the responder provides predefined answers according to an agreed
step structure.

Relay principles form the basis of many expert systems, including

- Precision Relay
- Symmetric Relay
- MOSCITO
- Ultimate Club
- Relay versions of Polish Club
- Kokish Relay
- Various strong club systems

Today, relay bidding represents one of the most accurate methods of describing
distribution, strength, controls, and honor location.

---

# Objectives

The Relay Principle enables the partnership to

- exchange highly detailed information,
- conserve bidding space,
- identify exact distribution,
- identify strength,
- identify controls,
- locate key cards,
- improve slam bidding,
- eliminate guesswork.

---

# Fundamental Philosophy

Traditional bidding

```
Both players describe themselves.
```

Relay bidding

```
One player asks.

One player answers.
```

The asker becomes the **Captain**.

---

# What Is a Relay?

A relay is an **artificial forcing bid** requesting partner to continue
describing the hand according to a predefined structure.

Example

```
1♣

1♦

1♥*

1♠*
```

Neither bid is natural.

Both are relays.

---

# Captaincy

One of the most important relay principles is

**Captaincy**.

The captain

- controls the auction,
- decides the contract,
- determines whether to investigate slam.

The responder

simply answers accurately.

---

# Information Flow

Relay systems exchange information in stages.

Typical order

1. Shape
2. Strength
3. Controls
4. Honor location
5. Suit quality
6. Key cards
7. Queens
8. Kings
9. Specific cards

# Why Relay?

Natural bidding often reaches

```
4NT
```

without knowing

- exact shape,
- controls,
- honor location.

Relay systems frequently know

- exact distribution,
- exact controls,
- approximate HCP,
- specific honors

before game has been bid.

# Historical Perspective

Relay bidding emerged during the development of strong club systems in the
second half of the twentieth century. Early artificial systems demonstrated
that structured questioning could convey far more information than traditional
natural bidding. Pioneering work by experts such as Eric Kokish, Eric Crowhurst,
Paul Marston, and others led to increasingly sophisticated relay methods,
culminating in full relay systems capable of identifying exact distribution,
strength, controls, and honor location before reaching game.

Today, relay principles are central to many of the world's strongest bidding
systems and continue to evolve through expert partnership innovation.

---

# Expert Principles

Experienced relay partnerships follow several guiding principles:

- One player is always captain.
- The responder answers, never volunteers.
- Questions follow a predetermined order.
- Shape is normally resolved before strength.
- Controls are identified before key cards.
- Relay bidding stops as soon as sufficient information has been obtained.
- Artificial structure should simplify—not complicate—the auction.

---

# Summary

The **Relay Principle** is the foundation of modern relay bidding. Rather than
having both players describe their own hands, one player assumes the role of
captain and guides the auction through a structured sequence of artificial
relay bids. This approach allows the partnership to exchange highly accurate
information about shape, strength, controls, and honor location while
preserving valuable bidding space.

Although relay systems require significant partnership investment, they remain
among the most powerful tools available for expert constructive and slam
bidding and form the basis of many of the world's leading artificial bidding
systems.
