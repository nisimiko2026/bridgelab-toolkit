# Relay Bidding

## Overview

**Relay Bidding** is an advanced family of artificial bidding methods in which
one partner, known as the **Captain**, directs the auction while the other
partner, known as the **Describer**, answers a sequence of artificial relay
bids.

Unlike natural bidding, where each bid attempts to describe the bidder's hand,
relay bidding separates the partnership's responsibilities:

- **Captain** asks questions.
- **Describer** answers according to predefined response tables.

This approach enables the partnership to exchange an extraordinary amount of
information while using very little bidding space.

Modern relay methods can describe

- exact distribution,
- strength,
- controls,
- suit quality,
- singleton or void location,
- honor concentration,
- and occasionally the exact thirteen-card pattern.

Relay bidding forms the foundation of many expert systems, including

- Precision Relay,
- Symmetric Relay,
- Moscito,
- Ultimate Club,
- Blue Club,
- Super Precision,
- several Polish Club variants.

---

# Objectives

Relay bidding aims to

- maximize bidding accuracy,
- identify the best strain,
- investigate slam efficiently,
- exchange information economically,
- eliminate unnecessary guessing,
- distinguish similar hand types precisely.

---

# Basic Philosophy

Traditional bidding asks

> "What should I tell partner?"

Relay bidding asks

> "What does partner most need to know?"

Only one player controls the auction.

The other simply answers.

---

# Captain and Describer

## Captain

The Captain

- controls the auction,
- asks relay questions,
- evaluates combined assets,
- decides the final contract.

---

## Describer

The Describer

- never guesses,
- follows response tables,
- answers relay bids,
- reveals the hand step by step.

---

# Relay Principle

A relay bid

- is artificial,
- requests additional information,
- usually has no natural meaning.

Example

```
1♣*
1♦*
```

The

```
1♦
```

response is a relay,

not a diamond suit.

---

# Types of Relay Systems

Modern relay systems vary considerably.

The principal families are

- Shape Relays
- Strength Relays
- Control Relays
- Symmetric Relays
- Full Relay Systems

---

# Shape Relay

The first objective is usually

- exact distribution.

Example sequence

```
1♣*
1♦*
1♥*
```

Each response narrows the possible hand patterns.

Eventually the Captain may know

```
4-4-3-2
```

or

```
6-3-2-2
```

exactly.

---

# Strength Relay

After determining shape,

the partnership may identify

- HCP range,
- controls,
- losing trick count,
- playing tricks.

---

# Control Relay

Many relay systems identify

- Aces,
- Kings,
- controls,

before beginning slam investigation.

This is often more useful than simple HCP.

---

# Symmetric Relay

Symmetric Relay is one of the most powerful relay methods.

Every possible hand follows

the same logical structure.

Advantages include

- consistency,
- minimal memory conflicts,
- extremely accurate descriptions.

---

# Step Responses

Most relay systems use **steps**.

Example

```
2♦*
```

asks.

Possible replies

```
2♥
```

Step 1.

```
2♠
```

Step 2.

```
2NT
```

Step 3.

Each step represents predefined information.

---

# Positive and Negative Responses

Strong club systems frequently begin

```
1♣*
```

Strong artificial.

Responder first shows

- negative,
- positive,
- semi-positive,

before relays begin.

# Shape Discovery

A relay auction frequently discovers

- longest suit,
- second suit,
- singleton,
- void,
- exact pattern.

Example

```
4-4-3-2

5-4-2-2

6-4-2-1

7-3-2-1
```

---

# Honor Location

Advanced relay methods continue by locating

- AK,
- AQ,
- guarded honors,
- honor concentration.

This improves

- notrump contracts,
- slam decisions.

---

# Control Investigation

Relay methods frequently identify

- first-round controls,
- second-round controls,
- key cards.

Many systems combine relay bidding with

- Roman Key Card Blackwood,
- Kickback,
- Redwood,
- Spiral Scan.

---

# Relay after Strong Club

A common auction

```
1♣*
1♦*
```

shows

- waiting response.

Opener continues

```
1♥*
```

Relay begins.

# Relay after Opening Responses

Relays are not limited to strong clubs.

Modern partnerships employ relays after

- strong two clubs,
- Multi 2♦,
- Precision openings,
- artificial responses,
- game forcing auctions.

# Relay Auctions

Example

```
1♣*
1♦*
1♥*
1♠*
2♣*
```

Every bid is artificial.

Each provides additional information.

---

# Relay Priorities

Most relay systems identify

1. Shape
2. Strength
3. Controls
4. Honor location
5. Suit quality
6. Slam suitability

---

# Slam Investigation

Relay bidding excels in slam auctions because

the Captain frequently knows

- exact distribution,
- control count,
- wasted values,
- trump quality,

before committing.

This greatly improves slam accuracy.

---

# Competitive Auctions

Relay systems become much more difficult

after interference.

Partnerships usually define

- relay cancellation,
- doubles,
- replacement relays,
- cue bids,
- forcing passes.

Some systems abandon relays entirely after competition.

---

# Memory Requirements

Relay bidding is among the most memory-intensive methods in bridge.

Partnerships must memorize

- response tables,
- step structures,
- exceptions,
- continuations,
- relay breaks.

Successful relay partnerships devote considerable study to system notes.

# Disadvantages

Relay methods require comprehensive written agreements.

# Modern Expert Perspective

Relay bidding represents the highest level of constructive bidding currently
used in tournament bridge. While natural systems rely on judgment and inference,
relay systems strive for **objective information exchange** through carefully
defined artificial sequences.

Most world-class relay partnerships employ the following philosophy:

1. **Discover shape first.**
2. **Determine strength second.**
3. **Locate controls.**
4. **Evaluate honor concentration.**
5. **Investigate slam only after the hand is fully described.**

This minimizes guesswork and maximizes bidding accuracy, especially in close
game and slam decisions.

However, relay systems require extensive preparation, disciplined partnership
agreements, and regular practice. They are generally recommended only for
advanced and expert partnerships willing to invest significant time in learning
and maintaining detailed system notes.

---

# Summary

**Relay Bidding** is a family of sophisticated artificial bidding methods in
which one partner asks structured questions while the other answers according to
predefined response tables. By separating the roles of **Captain** and
**Describer**, relay systems can identify exact shape, strength, controls, and
distribution with remarkable efficiency.

Although relay methods demand exceptional memory and partnership discipline,
they provide unmatched accuracy in constructive bidding and slam investigation,
making them a cornerstone of many world-class bidding systems.
