# Drury Convention

## Overview

The **Drury Convention** is an artificial response used **only by a passed hand** after partner opens **1♥** or **1♠** in **third or fourth seat**.

Its purpose is to distinguish between:

- A light third-seat opening made primarily for tactical reasons.
- A normal opening with full opening strength.

Without Drury, responder cannot safely make a limit raise because opener may have opened aggressively with fewer than normal opening values.

Typical auction:

```
Pass
Pass
1♠
2♣*
```

The **2♣** response is artificial.

---

# Purpose

Drury enables the partnership to:

- Show a limit raise after passing originally.
- Protect against overbidding opposite light openings.
- Allow opener to clarify opening strength.
- Improve game decisions.
- Distinguish tactical openings from normal openings.

---

# When to Use

Drury applies only when:

- Responder is a passed hand.
- Opener bids **1♥** or **1♠** in third or fourth seat.
- No opponent has entered the auction.

Example:

```
Pass
Pass
1♥
2♣*
```

or

```
Pass
Pass
1♠
2♣*
```

---

# Requirements

Responder normally holds:

- Passed hand.
- Three or more-card support.
- Approximately 10-12 support points.
- Invitational values.
- Interest in game opposite a full opening.

Some partnerships require four-card support.

---

# Standard Drury

The traditional meaning is:

```
Pass
Pass
1♥
2♣*
```

Shows:

- Limit raise.
- Three or more-card support.
- Invitational values.

The bid says:

> "Partner, I have enough for a limit raise if your opening is normal."

---

# Reverse Drury

Many partnerships now play **Reverse Drury**.

The auction begins the same way:

```
Pass
Pass
1♠
2♣*
```

The difference lies in opener's rebids.

Reverse Drury has become the modern standard.

---

# Opener's Rebids

## Minimum or Light Opening

```
2♦*
```

Artificial.

Shows:

- Light third-seat opening.
- Minimum hand.
- No interest in game.

Responder normally signs off.

---

## Full Opening

```
2♥
```

or

```
2♠
```

Natural.

Shows:

- Full opening values.
- Willingness to continue.

Responder may now invite or bid game.

---

## Extra Strength

With extras opener may:

- Jump.
- Cue bid.
- Begin slam investigation.

---

# Responder's Continuations

After opener clarifies strength:

## Minimum Opening

Responder usually:

- Passes.
- Returns to the agreed major.

---

## Full Opening

Responder may:

- Invite game.
- Bid game.
- Begin slam investigation.

---

# Strength Requirements

Typical responder values:

- 10-12 support points.
- Good trump support.
- Passed-hand limitation.

Opener:

- Light opening (approximately 10-12 HCP).

or

- Normal opening (12-14+ HCP).

---

# Advantages

Drury:

- Prevents overbidding opposite light openings.
- Allows aggressive third-seat openings.
- Improves game judgment.
- Preserves bidding space.
- Distinguishes tactical and constructive openings.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Applies only to passed hands.
- Requires partnership agreement.
- Uses an artificial response.
- Is unavailable after interference.

---

# Variations

Popular methods include:

- Standard Drury.
- Reverse Drury.
- Two-Way Drury.
- Drury with four-card support only.
- Drury after fourth-seat openings.

Reverse Drury is by far the most common modern treatment.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should discuss:

- Standard or Reverse Drury.
- Minimum support.
- Strength range.
- Opener's rebids.
- Responses after interference.
- Slam continuations.

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

The sequences above assume an uncontested auction. Once the opponents interfere, many partnerships abandon Drury and use natural competitive methods.

---

## Opponent Overcalls

Example

```
Pass
Pass
1♠
(2♦)
?
```

Most partnerships play:

- Drury Off.
- Negative Double.
- Natural raises.
- Cue raises.

Some retain Drury when bidding space remains.

---

## Opponent Doubles

Example

```
Pass
Pass
1♥
(Double)
?
```

Common treatments include:

- Redouble showing values.
- Natural raises.
- Jordan 2NT.
- Drury Off.

---

## Competition After Drury

Example

```
Pass
Pass
1♠
2♣*
(2♦)
?
```

Opener should continue according to partnership agreement.

Many expert partnerships:

- Ignore the interference if practical.
- Switch to natural methods.
- Use doubles or cue bids to replace unavailable responses.

---

## Competitive Game Decisions

After interference both partners should reconsider:

- Combined strength.
- Trump quality.
- Offensive values.
- Defensive values.
- Vulnerability.

---

## Partnership Agreements

Partners should discuss:

- Whether Drury remains on after interference.
- Responses after doubles.
- Responses after overcalls.
- Cue raises.
- Jordan 2NT.
- Slam continuations.

---

## General Principles

When opponents interfere:

- Preserve constructive bidding whenever possible.
- Do not force Drury if natural bidding becomes clearer.
- Continue describing strength accurately.
- Reevaluate after every competitive action.
- Follow partnership agreements consistently.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Using Drury Without Being a Passed Hand

Drury applies only after responder has already passed.

---

## Forgetting Third-Seat Requirement

The convention is designed primarily for third-seat (and sometimes fourth-seat) openings.

---

## Confusing Standard and Reverse Drury

The rebid structure differs.

Always agree which version you play.

---

## Showing Too Much Strength

Game-forcing hands normally begin with different methods.

Drury is primarily a **limit raise** convention.

---

## Ignoring Competition

Many partnerships turn Drury off after interference.

Know your agreements.

---

# Tips

- Learn Reverse Drury—it is the modern standard.
- Remember that Drury exists to protect against light third-seat openings.
- Agree exactly how opener shows a minimum opening.
- Discuss whether fourth-seat openings are included.
- Practice both uncontested and competitive auctions.

---

# Summary

The Drury Convention is one of the most effective methods for handling passed-hand responses to third- and fourth-seat major-suit openings. By using an artificial **2♣** response to show a limit raise, it allows opener to distinguish between tactical light openings and full opening hands before the partnership commits to game. Reverse Drury has become the standard modern treatment because it provides a simple and efficient rebid structure. Successful use depends on disciplined partnership agreements and a clear understanding of competitive continuations.
