# Responding to Multi 2♦

## Bridge Insight

**Responding to a Multi 2♦ opening is a process of discovery. Unlike natural openings, responder does not initially know opener's exact suit—or even, in some partnerships, opener's exact hand type. The responder's primary task is to identify opener's hand while selecting the best contract with the least amount of information exchanged.**

---

# Overview

This article describes the standard methods for responding to the **Multi 2♦** opening.

Since Multi 2♦ usually promises either:

- a Weak Two in Hearts, or
- a Weak Two in Spades,

the first objective is to discover opener's actual suit.

Some partnerships include additional strong options. These are discussed under **System Variations**.

---

# Objectives

Responder aims to:

- Find opener's major suit.
- Stop safely in a part-score with weak hands.
- Invite or bid game with stronger hands.
- Investigate slam when appropriate.
- Distinguish between weak and strong Multi options.

# Standard Responses

## Pass

With:

- Very weak hand.
- Little expectation of game.

Usually chosen only when partnership style permits.

---

## 2♥ — Pass or Correct

The most common response.

```text
2♦

2♥
```

Requests opener:

- Pass with Hearts.
- Bid 2♠ with Spades.

This is the standard response with weak hands.

---

## 2♠

Natural.

Shows:

- Good Spade suit.
- Interest in playing Spades opposite either weak or strong option.

Partnership agreement required.

---

## 2NT

Artificial inquiry.

Requests opener to describe the hand further.

Typical uses:

- Invitational values.
- Game-going hands.
- Slam interest.

Detailed continuations are covered in:

**2NT Inquiry after Multi 2♦**

---

## Three-Level Suit Bids

Usually natural.

May show:

- Long suit.
- Invitational values.
- Competitive hand.

Treatment varies.

---

## 3NT

Natural.

Shows:

- Strong balanced hand.
- Confidence that Notrump is superior.

Usually based on excellent stoppers.

---

## Raise to Game

Example:

```text
2♦

4♥
```

Shows confidence that opener holds Hearts.

Usually based on partnership agreements.

# Partnership Agreements

The partnership should define:

- Whether Pass is permitted.
- Meaning of 2♠.
- Meaning of 2NT.
- Natural versus artificial three-level bids.
- Responses after interference.
- Continuations over strong Multi options.

These agreements should be recorded on the convention card.

---

# System Variations

Common methods include:

- Standard Pass-or-Correct.
- Feature Ask.
- Ogust responses.
- Strong-option Multi.
- Acol Multi.

Each partnership should clearly define its structure.

---

# Expert Thinking

Responder should not rush to game simply because opener has opened.

The Multi opening deliberately conceals information.

Experts first identify:

- Opener's suit.
- Opener's strength.
- Suit quality.

Only then do they judge the final contract.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Forgetting Pass-or-Correct.
- Bypassing the 2NT inquiry.
- Assuming opener has Hearts.
- Forgetting strong Multi options.
- Ignoring vulnerability.

---

# Best Practices

- Learn Pass-or-Correct first.
- Master the 2NT inquiry.
- Discuss interference thoroughly.
- Keep partnership agreements simple.
- Practice complete auctions.

# Expert Tip

**Think in two stages.**

Stage 1:

> Discover opener's hand.

Stage 2:

> Choose the contract.

Many mistakes occur because responders try to choose the final contract before they know whether opener has Hearts, Spades, or (in some methods) a strong hand. Successful partnerships always identify the opening type first and then evaluate the best contract.

---

# Memory Aid

**Responding to Multi = "Identify First, Decide Second."**

Remember:

- Use **2♥** with weak hands to locate opener's major through the Pass-or-Correct mechanism.
- Use **2NT** or another agreed inquiry with stronger hands when more information is needed.
- Delay the final contract until opener's hand type has been clarified.
- Accurate responses to Multi 2♦ rely on disciplined partnership agreements and a clear understanding of the convention's structure.
