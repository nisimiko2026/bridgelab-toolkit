# New Minor Forcing (NMF)

## Overview

**New Minor Forcing (NMF)** is an artificial convention used after opener rebids **1NT**. Responder bids the **unbid minor suit**—not to show that suit, but to request additional information about opener's hand.

The convention helps the partnership:

- Find hidden major-suit fits.
- Distinguish invitational and game-forcing hands.
- Reach the correct notrump contract.
- Continue describing distribution.

The bid is **artificial** and normally **forcing for one round**.

Example:

```
1♣    Pass
1♠    Pass
1NT   Pass
2♦*
```

The **2♦** bid is New Minor Forcing.

---

# Purpose

New Minor Forcing is used to:

- Discover three-card support for responder's major.
- Clarify opener's distribution.
- Investigate game.
- Choose between notrump and a suit contract.
- Avoid missing an eight-card major fit.

---

# When to Use

The convention applies after:

- Opener opens a minor.
- Responder bids a major.
- Opener rebids **1NT**.
- Responder bids the **unbid minor**.

Typical auctions:

```
1♣
1♠
1NT
2♦*
```

```
1♦
1♥
1NT
2♣*
```

---

# Requirements

Responder typically holds:

- Invitational or stronger values.
- At least five cards in the major.
- Uncertainty about the final contract.

Typical strength:

- 10+ HCP

Some partnerships require invitational values only.

---

# What the Bid Means

The new minor says:

> "Please describe your hand further."

It does **not** promise:

- Length in the new minor.
- Interest in playing the minor.

The bid is completely artificial.

---

# Opener's Priorities

After New Minor Forcing, opener should normally respond in the following order.

---

## 1. Show Three-Card Support

Highest priority.

Example

```
1♣
1♠
1NT
2♦*
2♠
```

Shows:

- Three-card spade support.

This immediately identifies an eight-card fit.

---

## 2. Show Four Cards in the Other Major

Example

```
1♦
1♥
1NT
2♣*
2♠
```

Shows four spades.

---

## 3. Bid Notrump

Example

```
1♣
1♥
1NT
2♦*
2NT
```

Shows:

- Balanced hand.
- No major support.
- Appropriate strength.

---

## 4. Bid the Artificial Minor

Shows:

- Additional information.
- Usually denies higher-priority rebids.

---

# Responder's Continuations

Responder now chooses among:

- Pass.
- Invite game.
- Bid game.
- Play in a major.
- Play in notrump.
- Investigate slam.

---

# Strength Requirements

Most partnerships use:

### Invitational

10–11 HCP

---

### Game Force

12+ HCP

Some partnerships use **Two-Way Checkback** instead to distinguish these ranges.

---

# New Minor Forcing vs Fourth Suit Forcing

| New Minor Forcing | Fourth Suit Forcing |
|-------------------|---------------------|
| After 1NT rebid | Before 1NT rebid |
| Uses unbid minor | Uses fourth suit |
| Looks for major support | Requests further description |
| One-round force | One-round or game force |

---

# New Minor Forcing vs Checkback Stayman

| New Minor Forcing | Checkback Stayman |
|-------------------|-------------------|
| One-way convention | Modern extension |
| Invitational or better | Separate invitational and game-force sequences |
| Older style | More flexible |

---

# New Minor Forcing vs Two-Way Checkback

| New Minor Forcing | Two-Way Checkback |
|-------------------|-------------------|
| Single artificial bid | Two artificial inquiries |
| Simpler | More descriptive |
| Widely played | Modern expert treatment |

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

The examples above assume an uncontested auction. If opponents interfere before responder can bid the new minor, most partnerships modify or abandon New Minor Forcing.

---

## Opponent Overcalls

Example

```
1♣
1♠
1NT
(2♥)
?
```

Possible agreements include:

- New Minor Forcing Off.
- Natural bidding.
- Cue-bid replacements.
- Double as an artificial inquiry.

---

## Opponent Doubles

Example

```
1♦
1♥
1NT
(Double)
?
```

Common partnership treatments include:

- Pass.
- Redouble.
- Artificial continuations.
- Natural methods.

---

## Competition After NMF

Example

```
1♣
1♠
1NT
2♦*
(2♥)
?
```

Partnerships should agree whether opener:

- Continues naturally.
- Prioritizes support.
- Uses doubles as artificial bids.

---

## Slam Investigation

Once New Minor Forcing identifies the correct strain, the partnership may continue with:

- Cue Bids.
- Roman Key Card Blackwood.
- Control Bids.

---

## Partnership Agreements

Partners should define:

- Whether New Minor Forcing remains on after interference.
- Responses after doubles.
- Cue-bid replacements.
- Invitational versus game-force sequences.
- Slam continuations.

---

## General Principles

When opponents interfere:

- Preserve the search for a major fit whenever possible.
- Continue describing the hand naturally.
- Reevaluate after every competitive action.
- Follow partnership agreements consistently.

---

# Advantages

New Minor Forcing:

- Finds hidden major fits.
- Improves game decisions.
- Keeps auctions low.
- Clarifies opener's distribution.
- Easy to learn.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Requires partnership agreement.
- Cannot distinguish invitational and game-forcing hands as efficiently as Two-Way Checkback.
- Is sometimes unavailable after interference.
- Gives information to the opponents.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Treating the New Minor as Natural

The bid is artificial.

---

## Forgetting the Rebid Priorities

Opener should normally:

1. Show three-card support.
2. Show four cards in the other major.
3. Describe strength and shape.

---

## Using NMF With Weak Hands

Weak hands should normally pass 1NT.

---

## Confusing NMF With Fourth Suit Forcing

Fourth Suit Forcing occurs **before** opener rebids 1NT.

NMF occurs **after** opener rebids 1NT.

---

## Ignoring Competition

Many partnerships suspend New Minor Forcing after interference.

---

# Tips

- Think of New Minor Forcing as **Stayman after a natural 1NT rebid**.
- Always look for an eight-card major fit before choosing notrump.
- Learn opener's rebid priorities thoroughly.
- If your partnership later adopts Two-Way Checkback, the transition from NMF is straightforward.
- Practice both invitational and game-forcing examples.

---

# Summary

New Minor Forcing is one of the classic conventions used after opener rebids **1NT**. By bidding the unbid minor artificially, responder asks opener to further describe the hand, particularly with respect to major-suit support and distribution. The convention helps partnerships uncover hidden major fits, improve game decisions, and avoid poor notrump contracts. Although many expert partnerships now use Two-Way Checkback, New Minor Forcing remains a simple, effective, and widely played convention.
