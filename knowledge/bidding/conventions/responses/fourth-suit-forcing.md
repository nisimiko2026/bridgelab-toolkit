# Fourth Suit Forcing (4SF)

## Overview

**Fourth Suit Forcing (4SF)** is an artificial convention in which responder bids the **only unbid suit** after opener and responder have naturally bid the other three suits.

The bid does **not** promise length in the fourth suit. Instead, it is an artificial forcing bid requesting opener to further describe the hand.

Fourth Suit Forcing is one of the most widely played conventions because it allows partnerships to:

- Keep the auction alive.
- Clarify strength.
- Describe shape.
- Locate the best strain.
- Investigate game and slam.

Example:

```
1♣    Pass
1♠    Pass
2♥    Pass
2♦*
```

The **2♦** bid is artificial.

---

# Purpose

Fourth Suit Forcing is used to:

- Create a forcing auction.
- Request additional information.
- Ask for a stopper.
- Clarify opener's distribution.
- Explore notrump.
- Investigate game and slam.

---

# When to Use

Fourth Suit Forcing applies when:

- Three different suits have been bid naturally.
- The fourth suit has not yet been bid.
- Responder needs additional information.

Typical examples:

```
1♣
1♠
2♥
2♦*
```

```
1♦
1♥
1♠
2♣*
```

```
1♥
2♣
2♦
2♠*
```

```
1♠
2♣
2♥
2♦*
```

---

# Requirements

Responder typically holds:

- Invitational or stronger values.
- No clear natural rebid.
- Interest in game.
- Interest in notrump or another strain.

Most partnerships require at least:

- 10+ HCP

Some play Fourth Suit Forcing as **game forcing**, while others play it as **one round forcing**.

---

# What the Bid Means

The fourth-suit bid says:

> "Please describe your hand further."

It does **not** necessarily show:

- Length.
- Strength in the fourth suit.
- Desire to play there.

---

# Opener's Rebids

After Fourth Suit Forcing, opener should describe the hand naturally.

Priority usually follows:

## 1. Show a Stopper

If responder may be interested in notrump:

```
1♣
1♠
2♥
2♦*
2NT
```

Shows:

- Diamond stopper.
- Balanced hand.
- Appropriate strength.

---

## 2. Show Three-Card Support

Example

```
1♦
1♠
2♣
2♥*
2♠
```

Shows three-card spade support.

---

## 3. Repeat a Suit

Shows:

- Extra length.
- Good suit quality.

---

## 4. Bid a New Suit

Shows:

- Additional distribution.
- Extra information.

---

## 5. Bid Notrump

Shows:

- Stopper(s).
- Balanced hand.
- Appropriate strength.

---

# Responder's Continuations

Responder now chooses among:

- Pass.
- Game.
- Notrump.
- Major suit.
- Minor suit.
- Slam investigation.

The convention delays commitment until both hands are fully described.

---

# Fourth Suit Forcing vs New Minor Forcing

| Fourth Suit Forcing | New Minor Forcing |
|----------------------|------------------|
| After three suits bid | After 1NT rebid |
| Artificial | Artificial |
| Asks for clarification | Asks opener to describe further |
| Often explores notrump | Often explores major fits |

---

# Fourth Suit Forcing vs Checkback

| Fourth Suit Forcing | Checkback |
|----------------------|----------|
| Earlier auction | After opener rebids 1NT |
| Fourth suit available | Uses an artificial club bid |
| Describes distribution | Looks for major fits |

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

The sequences above assume an uncontested auction. Once opponents interfere, the fourth suit may no longer be available or practical. Most partnerships modify or abandon Fourth Suit Forcing in competitive auctions.

---

## Opponent Overcalls

Example

```
1♣
(1♦)
1♠
Pass
2♥
(3♦)
?
```

The partnership should evaluate:

- Whether Fourth Suit Forcing is still available.
- Whether natural bidding is preferable.
- Whether cue bids replace Fourth Suit Forcing.

---

## Opponent Doubles

Example

```
1♣
Pass
1♠
(Double)
2♥
?
```

Many partnerships continue using Fourth Suit Forcing if bidding space permits.

Others adopt natural methods.

---

## Cue Bid Replacement

If the fourth suit has already been bid by an opponent:

```
1♣
(1♦)
1♠
Pass
2♥
```

Responder cannot bid diamonds artificially.

Many expert partnerships instead use:

- Cue bids.
- Artificial doubles.
- Natural forcing bids.

---

## Slam Investigation

Fourth Suit Forcing frequently begins auctions that later investigate slam.

After opener's clarification, partnerships may continue with:

- Cue Bids.
- Roman Key Card Blackwood.
- Control Bids.

---

## Partnership Agreements

Partners should define:

- Whether Fourth Suit Forcing remains on after interference.
- Whether cue bids replace it.
- Responses after doubles.
- Responses after overcalls.
- Game-force requirements.
- Slam continuations.

---

## General Principles

When opponents interfere:

- Preserve constructive bidding whenever possible.
- Do not force Fourth Suit Forcing if natural bidding becomes clearer.
- Continue exchanging descriptive information.
- Reevaluate after every competitive action.
- Follow partnership agreements consistently.

---

# Advantages

Fourth Suit Forcing:

- Creates forcing auctions.
- Clarifies distribution.
- Locates stoppers.
- Improves notrump bidding.
- Helps investigate game and slam.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Requires partnership agreement.
- Has several forcing styles.
- Does not guarantee a fit.
- May be unavailable after interference.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Treating the Fourth Suit as Natural

The bid is usually artificial.

---

## Promising Length

Fourth Suit Forcing does not promise cards in the fourth suit.

---

## Forgetting Partnership Style

Know whether your partnership plays:

- One-round force.
- Game force.

---

## Showing the Wrong Feature

Agree whether opener should show:

- Support first.
- Stoppers first.
- Shape first.

---

## Ignoring Competition

Many partnerships modify Fourth Suit Forcing after interference.

---

# Tips

- Think of Fourth Suit Forcing as a **request for more information**, not a suit bid.
- Agree opener's rebid priorities before playing.
- Practice notrump auctions arising from Fourth Suit Forcing.
- Learn the relationship between Fourth Suit Forcing, Checkback, and New Minor Forcing.
- Remember that finding the best contract is more important than describing every card.

---

# Summary

Fourth Suit Forcing is one of the cornerstones of modern natural bidding. By using the only unbid suit as an artificial forcing call, responder requests additional information rather than proposing that suit as trumps. The convention allows partnerships to clarify strength, shape, support, and stoppers before selecting the final contract. Whether played as one-round forcing or game forcing, Fourth Suit Forcing significantly improves game and slam bidding and remains an essential part of virtually every modern bidding system.
