# Soloway Jump Shifts

## Overview

**Soloway Jump Shifts** are an advanced artificial bidding treatment in which a
**jump shift by responder** after partner's natural one-level opening shows a
**strong game-forcing hand** rather than the traditional weak or intermediate
hand.

The convention was developed by **Paul Soloway**, one of the greatest bridge
players of all time, to solve the long-standing problem of describing powerful
one-suited hands without consuming excessive bidding space.

Instead of immediately committing to a high-level contract, the partnership
establishes a **game force** while retaining room to exchange information about

- distribution,
- controls,
- suit quality,
- stoppers,
- and slam potential.

Today, Soloway Jump Shifts are played by many expert partnerships, particularly
those using **Two-over-One Game Force**.

---

# Objectives

The convention enables responder to

- establish an immediate game force,
- show a powerful suit,
- preserve bidding space,
- distinguish strong jump shifts from weak jump shifts,
- improve slam investigation,
- accurately describe highly distributional hands.

---

# Basic Principle

Traditional methods

```
1♣
2♠
```

often showed

- strong hand,
- long spade suit,

but consumed valuable bidding space.

Under Soloway,

```
1♣
2♠*
```

shows

- game-forcing values,
- excellent spade suit,
- strong offensive potential,

while allowing the partnership to continue below game.

---

# Applicability

The convention applies after

- 1♣
- 1♦
- 1♥
- 1♠

natural openings.

The jump shift is artificial.

---

# Requirements

Typical expert requirements include

- 16+ HCP or equivalent playing strength,
- six-card or longer suit,
- excellent suit quality,
- game-forcing values,
- strong offensive hand.

Some partnerships evaluate primarily by

- Losing Trick Count,
- controls,
- playing tricks,

rather than raw HCP.

---

# Suit Quality

The jump-shift suit should normally contain

- two of the top three honors,
- or three of the top five honors,
- excellent intermediates,
- strong trick-taking potential.

Typical examples

```
AKQJxx

AQJ10xx

KQJ10xx
```

Poor suits should not qualify.

# Opener's Responsibilities

After the jump shift,

opener should

- describe strength,
- support responder's suit,
- show controls,
- introduce second suits,
- evaluate slam potential.

The partnership is already committed to game.

# Responder's Continuations

Responder continues by describing

- controls,
- secondary suit,
- singleton,
- slam interest.

The auction often develops naturally.

Partners should define

- opener's rebids,

- continuation methods,
- competitive auctions.

---

# Competitive Auctions

If opponents interfere,

partnership agreements should specify

- doubles,
- cue bids,
- forcing passes,
- continuation structure.

The game force remains in effect.

---

# Vulnerability

Unlike preemptive methods,

vulnerability has relatively little effect.

The convention is constructive.

---

# Expert Style

Modern expert partnerships generally require

- genuine game force,
- excellent suit,
- strong offensive hand.

Weak jump shifts are usually assigned to other conventions such as

- Fit Jump,
- Weak Jump Shift,
- Bergen structures.

---

# Advantages

- Immediate game force
- Excellent slam investigation
- Preserves bidding space
- Describes strong one-suited hands
- Accurate hand evaluation

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires partnership agreement
- More memory than natural methods
- Removes natural strong jump shifts

# Relationship to Two-over-One Game Force

Soloway Jump Shifts integrate naturally with **2/1 Game Force**.

A common structure is

| Response | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| 2/1 Response | Balanced or semi-balanced game force |
| Soloway Jump Shift | Strong one-suited game force |
| Jacoby 2NT | Four-card support game force |
| Splinter | Support with shortness |

Each game-forcing hand type follows a unique descriptive path.

# Modern Expert Treatment

Among expert partnerships, Soloway Jump Shifts are valued because they solve one
of the most difficult constructive bidding problems: **describing a powerful
one-suited hand without wasting bidding space**.

The convention complements modern game-forcing structures by assigning each
strong hand type its own descriptive route, improving both game and slam
investigation while maintaining bidding efficiency.

Partnership agreements should define

- minimum playing strength,
- acceptable suit quality,
- opener's rebid priorities,
- continuation structure after interference.

---

# Historical Perspective

Paul Soloway introduced this treatment as part of a broader movement toward
more descriptive and space-efficient constructive bidding. Although not
universal, Soloway Jump Shifts have influenced many modern expert systems and
remain a respected option for partnerships seeking greater precision than
traditional strong jump shifts.

---

# Summary

**Soloway Jump Shifts** are an advanced expert convention in which responder's
jump shift establishes an immediate **game force** while showing a **powerful,
self-sustaining suit**. By preserving bidding space and accurately describing
strong one-suited hands, the convention greatly improves constructive auctions
and slam exploration.

Although not part of standard methods, it fits naturally into modern
**Two-over-One Game Force**, Precision, and other expert systems, making it an
excellent choice for serious tournament partnerships.
