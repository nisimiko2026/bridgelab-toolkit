# Bergen Raises

## Overview

**Bergen Raises** are an extension of standard major-suit raises that allow responder to describe both **strength** and **trump support** more precisely after partner opens **1♥** or **1♠**.

Instead of using a single jump raise to show all invitational hands, Bergen Raises assign different meanings to the **3♣** and **3♦** responses, distinguishing between weak, constructive, and limit raises.

The convention greatly improves game decisions while making competitive auctions more difficult for the opponents.

---

# Purpose

Bergen Raises are designed to:

- Differentiate constructive raises from limit raises.
- Identify game-going hands early.
- Preempt the opponents.
- Improve game and slam bidding.
- Eliminate ambiguity after a major-suit opening.

---

# When to Use

The convention applies after:

```
1♥   ?
```

or

```
1♠   ?
```

provided:

- No opponent has entered the auction.
- Responder has at least four-card support.

Some partnerships require exactly four-card support for Bergen Raises.

---

# Standard Bergen Structure

Several versions exist.

The most widely played modern structure is:

| Response | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| 2♥ / 2♠ | Simple raise (6–9 points, 3-card support) |
| 3♣ | Limit Raise (10–12 points, 4+ trumps) |
| 3♦ | Preemptive Raise (0–6 points, 4+ trumps) |
| 3♥ / 3♠ | Mixed Raise (7–9 points, 4-card support) |
| 2NT | Jacoby 2NT (Game forcing) |

Older partnerships may interchange the meanings of **3♣** and **3♦**.

Always discuss the structure before play.

---

# Requirements

Responder normally holds:

- Four-card or longer trump support.
- Distribution suitable for raising immediately.
- Values appropriate to the agreed response.

---

# Simple Raise

```
1♥
2♥
```

or

```
1♠
2♠
```

Typically shows:

- 6–9 HCP
- Three-card support

Some partnerships use four-card support.

---

# 3♣ — Limit Raise

```
1♥
3♣*
```

or

```
1♠
3♣*
```

Shows:

- 10–12 HCP
- Four or more trumps
- Invitational values

Opener decides whether to accept the invitation.

---

# 3♦ — Preemptive Raise

```
1♥
3♦*
```

or

```
1♠
3♦*
```

Shows:

- Weak hand
- Four or more trumps
- Distributional values

The primary objective is to consume bidding space.

---

# Mixed Raise

Many partnerships assign:

```
1♥
3♥
```

or

```
1♠
3♠
```

to show:

- 7–9 HCP
- Four-card support
- Good offensive distribution

This hand is too strong for a weak preempt but not strong enough for a limit raise.

---

# Jacoby 2NT

Hands with:

- Game-forcing values
- Four-card support

normally begin with:

```
2NT*
```

rather than Bergen Raises.

---

# Opener's Rebids

After a Bergen Raise, opener evaluates:

- Minimum versus maximum.
- Trump quality.
- Distribution.
- Controls.
- Slam potential.

Possible actions include:

- Pass.
- Sign off in game.
- Cue bid.
- Begin slam investigation.

---

# Responder's Continuations

Responder reevaluates after opener's rebid.

Possible continuations include:

- Pass.
- Bid game.
- Cue bid.
- Roman Key Card Blackwood.
- Slam investigation.

---

# Advantages

Bergen Raises:

- Improve game decisions.
- Differentiate several types of raises.
- Increase preemptive pressure.
- Preserve bidding space.
- Improve competitive bidding.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Requires partnership agreement.
- Uses artificial bids.
- Has multiple versions.
- Can occasionally reveal distribution.

---

# Variations

Popular treatments include:

- Original Bergen Raises.
- Reverse Bergen Raises.
- Modified Bergen Raises.
- Three-way Bergen Raises.
- Modern Bergen structure.

Every partnership should agree on the exact meanings.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should discuss:

- Whether Bergen applies with three or four-card support.
- Meanings of 3♣ and 3♦.
- Mixed Raise treatment.
- Responses to interference.
- Slam continuations.
- Competitive auctions.

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

The sequences above assume an uncontested auction. Once the opponents interfere, many partnerships suspend Bergen Raises and adopt natural competitive methods.

---

## Opponent Overcalls

Example

```
1♥   (1♠)   ?
```

Many partnerships play:

- Bergen Off.
- Cue raises.
- Negative Doubles.
- Natural raises.

Others retain parts of the Bergen structure when bidding space remains.

---

## Opponent Doubles

Example

```
1♠   (Double)   ?
```

Common agreements include:

- Redouble showing values.
- Jordan 2NT (Limit Raise or better).
- Bergen Off.
- Natural raises.

Many expert partnerships replace Bergen Raises with **Jordan 2NT** after a takeout double.

---

## Competition After a Bergen Raise

Example

```
1♥
3♣*
(3♠)
?
```

Both partners should reevaluate:

- Combined strength.
- Trump quality.
- Offensive values.
- Vulnerability.
- Law of Total Tricks.

---

## Slam Investigation

If responder has shown a limit raise and opener has extra values, the partnership may continue with:

- Cue Bids.
- Roman Key Card Blackwood.
- Control Bids.

Competition should not automatically prevent slam exploration.

---

## Partnership Agreements

Partners should define:

- Whether Bergen is "On" after interference.
- Responses after doubles.
- Cue raises.
- Jordan 2NT treatment.
- Mixed raises after competition.
- Slam continuations.

---

## General Principles

When opponents interfere:

- Preserve constructive bidding whenever practical.
- Compete with offensive hands.
- Distinguish invitational hands from preemptive raises.
- Reevaluate after every competitive action.
- Follow partnership agreements consistently.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Confusing Different Bergen Versions

There is no universal structure.

Always agree whether:

- 3♣ is constructive or limit.
- 3♦ is weak or invitational.

---

## Using Bergen With Game-Forcing Hands

Game-forcing raises normally begin with:

```
Jacoby 2NT
```

---

## Forgetting Trump Length

Most partnerships require:

- Four-card support.

---

## Ignoring Competition

Many partnerships turn Bergen Raises off after interference.

Know your agreements.

---

## Forgetting Mixed Raises

The direct jump to **3♥** or **3♠** often has a special meaning.

---

# Tips

- Learn one Bergen structure thoroughly rather than several.
- Agree exactly which hands qualify for each response.
- Discuss Jordan 2NT together with Bergen Raises.
- Practice competitive auctions separately.
- Remember that Bergen Raises are designed to make opener's rebid easier.

---

# Summary

Bergen Raises are one of the most effective conventions for responding to a major-suit opening. By assigning different meanings to jump raises, they allow responder to distinguish weak, constructive, invitational, and game-forcing hands while simultaneously increasing preemptive pressure on the opponents. Although several versions exist, the convention significantly improves competitive judgment and game selection when supported by clear partnership agreements.
