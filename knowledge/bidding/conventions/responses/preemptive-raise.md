# Objectives

A preemptive raise aims to

- consume bidding space,
- make opponents guess,
- describe excellent trump support,
- compete aggressively,
- protect the partnership's part-score,
- occasionally buy the contract.

---

# Basic Principle

A jump raise shows

- excellent trump support,
- weak defensive values,
- offensive distribution,
- little or no interest in game.

Example

```
1♠ — 3♠
```

or

```
1♥ — 3♥
```

The exact meaning depends upon partnership agreements.

---

# Philosophy

Preemptive raises are based upon

- **Law of Total Tricks**
- offensive trick-taking potential
- distribution
- trump length
- vulnerability

rather than

- raw HCP.

---

# Typical Requirements

Modern expert style generally requires

- four-card support (minimum),
- often five-card support,
- shortness preferred,
- approximately 0–7 support points,
- few defensive tricks.

Distribution is more important than point count.

---

# Support Points

Evaluation uses

- High Card Points
- shortness
- trump length
- offensive honors

Example

```
♠ K984
♥ 7
♦ Q1086
♣ J1085
```

may qualify despite modest HCP.

---

## Major Opening

```

```

Weak preemptive raise.

---

## Minor Opening

```

3♦
```

Preemptive in systems using Inverted Minors.

---

## Overcall

```
1♥
(1♠)
3♠
```

Weak raise of partner's overcall.

---

# Trump Length

Typical requirements

| Trump Support | Frequency |
|--------------:|-----------|
| 4 cards | Standard |
| 5 cards | Preferred |
| 6 cards | Aggressive |

Three-card support is rarely sufficient.

---

# Distribution

Ideal shapes include

- 5-4-2-2
- 5-4-3-1
- 6-3-2-2
- 4-4-4-1
- 5-5 patterns

Flat hands reduce offensive value.

---

# Offensive Values

Upgrade for

- singleton,
- void,
- fifth trump,
- side suit length,
- trump honors.

Downgrade for

- flat distribution,
- defensive honors,
- scattered values.

---

# Vulnerability

Vulnerability strongly affects style.

## Favorable Vulnerability

Experts become considerably more aggressive.

Lighter values

- acceptable,
- more distribution,
- greater pressure.

---

## Equal Vulnerability

Normal style.

---

## Unfavorable Vulnerability

Require

- better suit quality,
- stronger trump support,
- greater offensive potential.

Avoid unnecessary penalties.

---

# Seat Position

Aggressiveness varies.

### First Seat

Most disciplined.

---

### Second Seat

Normal.

---

### Third Seat

More aggressive.

---

### Fourth Seat

Most conservative.

Part-score considerations dominate.

---

# Law of Total Tricks

Modern preemptive raises are closely connected with the

**Law of Total Tricks**

The partnership often competes to the level equal to the combined trump length.

Example

Nine-card fit

```
Compete safely to the three level.
```

Ten-card fit

```
Compete aggressively to the four level.
```

This principle guides expert competitive decisions.

---

# Competition

After interference,

preemptive raises become even more valuable.

Example

```
1♥
(1♠)
3♥
```

Consumes valuable bidding space.

---

# Passed Hand

Passed hands frequently employ lighter preemptive raises because

- game is less likely,
- obstruction becomes more important.

---

# Game Interest

Preemptive raises deny

- serious game interest,
- invitational strength,
- slam ambitions.

Constructive hands use

- Mixed Raise,
- Bergen Raises,
- Jacoby 2NT,
- Limit Raise,
- Splinter.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should define

- minimum trump length,
- point range,
- vulnerability adjustments,
- competitive style,
- jump raise meanings,
- passed-hand treatments.

---

# Expert Style

Modern tournament partnerships reserve jump raises exclusively for
preemptive purposes.

Constructive hands are shown by

- Mixed Raise,
- Bergen Raises,
- Inverted Minors,
- Jacoby 2NT,
- Splinter.

This creates a complete and efficient raise structure.

---

# Advantages

- Maximum obstruction
- Excellent competitive pressure
- Accurate weak-hand description
- Simple partnership understanding
- Consumes opponents' bidding space

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires accurate judgment
- Vulnerable to penalties
- Less descriptive than constructive raises
- Poor agreements may create misunderstandings

---

# Common Mistakes

## Too Many High Cards

Hands with invitational values should not preempt.

---

## Too Few Trumps

Experts normally require at least four-card support.

---

## Ignoring Vulnerability

Many expensive penalties result from excessive aggression.

---

## Flat Hands

Balanced hands rarely produce effective preempts.

---

## Confusing Mixed Raise

Mixed Raises contain constructive values.

Preemptive raises do not.

# Expert Recommendations

Modern expert partnerships almost universally assign **exclusive meanings** to
each type of raise:

| Bid | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Single Raise | Constructive |
| Jump Raise | Preemptive |
| Mixed Raise | Intermediate constructive |
| Limit Raise | Invitational |
| Bergen Raises | Artificial range-showing |
| Jacoby 2NT | Game-forcing raise |
| Splinter | Game-forcing with shortness |

This structure ensures that every hand with trump support falls into a clearly
defined category, allowing highly accurate competitive, game, and slam bidding.

---

# Summary

The **Preemptive Raise** is one of the fundamental weapons of modern
competitive bridge. By combining strong trump support with offensive
distribution and a willingness to consume bidding space, it places immediate
pressure on the opponents while accurately describing weak, distributional
hands.

In expert partnerships, preemptive raises work in harmony with **Mixed Raises,
Limit Raises, Bergen Raises, Inverted Minors, Jacoby 2NT, and Splinters** to
create a complete and highly descriptive raising structure that maximizes both
constructive accuracy and competitive effectiveness.
