# Swiss Raise

## Overview

**Swiss Raise** is a conventional response to a **1♥** or **1♠** opening that shows a **game-forcing raise with four-card trump support** and **slam interest**.

The convention uses:

- **4♣** over **1♥**
- **4♦** over **1♠**

These bids consume less bidding space than an immediate jump to game while immediately informing opener that responder has a powerful trump raise.

Swiss Raise is particularly valuable when the partnership wishes to explore slam after finding a major-suit fit.

---

# Purpose

Swiss Raise is designed to:

- Show a game-forcing raise.
- Promise four-card trump support.
- Show opening values or better.
- Preserve bidding space for slam investigation.
- Distinguish strong raises from weaker invitational raises.

---

# When to Use

Swiss Raise applies after:

```
1♥
4♣*
```

or

```
1♠
4♦*
```

The auction should normally be:

- Uncontested.
- Without previous interference.

---

# Requirements

Responder normally holds:

- Four-card or longer trump support.
- Opening strength or better.
- Approximately 13+ HCP (or equivalent playing strength).
- Interest in slam.

Distribution is often more important than raw HCP.

---

# Standard Meaning

## After 1♥

```
1♥
4♣*
```

Shows:

- Four-card heart support.
- Game-forcing values.
- Slam interest.

---

## After 1♠

```
1♠
4♦*
```

Shows:

- Four-card spade support.
- Game-forcing values.
- Slam interest.

---

# Why Use Swiss Raise?

Without Swiss Raise:

```
1♠
4♠
```

gives very little descriptive information.

With Swiss Raise:

```
1♠
4♦*
```

opener immediately knows:

- A fit exists.
- Game is certain.
- Slam is possible.

This preserves room for cue bidding.

---

# Opener's Rebids

After a Swiss Raise, opener evaluates:

- Minimum versus maximum.
- Controls.
- Trump quality.
- Shortness.
- Losing tricks.

Typical continuations include:

- Cue bids.
- Control bids.
- Sign off in game.
- Roman Key Card Blackwood.

---

# Responder's Continuations

Responder continues according to opener's evaluation.

Possible actions include:

- Cue bidding.
- Showing additional controls.
- Roman Key Card Blackwood.
- Signing off in game.

---

# Strength Requirements

Typical partnership style:

Minimum:

- Opening values opposite opener.

Normally:

- 13+ HCP.

More important:

- Playing strength.
- Controls.
- Excellent trump fit.

# Comparison with Jacoby 2NT

| Swiss Raise | Jacoby 2NT |
|-------------|------------|
| Immediate game force | Immediate game force |
| Four-card support | Four-card support |
| Directly shows slam interest | Usually broader range |
| Consumes more bidding space | Preserves more bidding space |
| Simpler structure | More descriptive continuations |

Many expert partnerships now prefer **Jacoby 2NT**, but Swiss Raise remains popular because of its simplicity.

# Comparison with Splinter

| Swiss Raise | Splinter |
|-------------|----------|

| Balanced or unbalanced | Distributional |
| Strong raise | Strong raise |

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

Swiss Raise is primarily designed for uncontested auctions. Once opponents interfere, many partnerships abandon the convention because the jump bid is no longer available or natural competitive methods become more valuable.

---

## Opponent Overcalls

Example

```
1♥
(1♠)
?
```

Most partnerships play:

- Swiss Raise Off.
- Cue Raises.
- Jacoby 2NT (if available).
- Natural raises.

---

## Opponent Doubles

Example

```
1♠
(Double)
?
```

Common treatments include:

- Jordan 2NT.
- Redouble showing strength.
- Natural raises.
- Swiss Raise Off.

Most partnerships do **not** use Swiss Raise after a takeout double.

---

## Competition After Swiss Raise

Example

```
1♥
4♣*
(4♠)
?
```

Both partners should reevaluate:

- Combined strength.
- Controls.
- Offensive potential.
- Defensive potential.
- Vulnerability.

Possible actions include:

- Double (penalty).
- Continue cue bidding.
- Bid slam.
- Sign off in game.

---

## Slam Investigation

Swiss Raise is designed specifically to facilitate slam exploration.

After the raise, partnerships commonly continue with:

- Cue Bids.
- Control Bids.
- Roman Key Card Blackwood.
- Grand Slam Force (if appropriate).

---

## Partnership Agreements

Partners should define:

- Whether Swiss Raise is on after interference.
- Responses after doubles.
- Cue-bid priorities.
- Blackwood structure.
- Control bidding agreements.

---

## General Principles

When using Swiss Raise:

- Promise genuine game-forcing values.
- Hold at least four-card trump support.
- Continue exchanging control information.
- Reevaluate after every competitive action.
- Follow partnership agreements consistently.

---

# Advantages

Swiss Raise:

- Immediately establishes a game-forcing fit.
- Shows slam interest.
- Simplifies auctions.
- Allows efficient cue bidding.
- Easy to remember.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Consumes significant bidding space.
- Is unavailable after most interference.
- Describes less than Jacoby 2NT.
- Is less common in expert partnerships today.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Using Swiss Raise with Invitational Hands

Swiss Raise is **game forcing**.

---

## Using Only Three Trumps

Most partnerships require:

- Four-card support.

---

## Confusing Swiss Raise with Splinter

Swiss Raise does **not** promise shortness.

---

## Skipping Cue Bids

The convention exists primarily to facilitate slam investigation.

---

## Ignoring Partnership Agreements

Some partnerships replace Swiss Raise entirely with Jacoby 2NT.

Know which method you play.

---

# Tips

- Think of Swiss Raise as an immediate announcement: **"We have game, let's investigate slam."**
- Use it only with genuine slam interest.
- Practice cue-bidding continuations thoroughly.
- Discuss how Swiss Raise fits alongside Jacoby 2NT and Splinter.
- Remember that controls often matter more than HCP.

---

# Modern Usage

Today, Swiss Raise is less common than it once was. Many expert partnerships prefer **Jacoby 2NT**, which provides more room to exchange information and allows opener to describe hand type in greater detail.

Nevertheless, Swiss Raise remains an effective convention because it is:

- Simple.
- Easy to remember.
- Highly descriptive.
- Excellent for partnerships that prefer direct methods.

---

# Summary

Swiss Raise is a game-forcing response to a major-suit opening that promises **four-card trump support** and **slam interest**. By using **4♣** after **1♥** or **4♦** after **1♠**, responder immediately establishes a forcing auction while preserving enough space for cue bidding and control-showing. Although many modern partnerships now favor Jacoby 2NT, Swiss Raise remains a practical and effective convention for partnerships seeking a straightforward route to slam exploration.
