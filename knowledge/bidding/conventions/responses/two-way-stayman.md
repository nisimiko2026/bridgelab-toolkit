# Two-Way Stayman

## Bridge Insight

**Two-Way Stayman expands the traditional Stayman convention by allowing responder to distinguish between weak and strong hands at the very beginning of the auction. This enables the partnership to investigate major-suit fits more accurately while preserving bidding space for stronger hands.**

---

# Overview

**Two-Way Stayman** is an extension of the Stayman convention used after a natural **1NT opening**.

Unlike standard Stayman, where a single artificial bid covers a wide range of strengths, Two-Way Stayman uses **two different artificial bids** to distinguish between:

- Invitational or stronger hands.
- Weak or non-forcing hands.

The exact structure varies considerably among partnerships.

---

# Objectives

Two-Way Stayman enables the partnership to:

- Locate major-suit fits.
- Differentiate weak and strong responder hands.
- Improve game and slam investigation.
- Preserve bidding space.
- Reduce ambiguity in responder's strength.

---

# When to Use

After a natural

```text
1NT
```

opening.

Responder selects the appropriate Stayman route according to partnership agreements.

---

# Requirements

Responder typically has:

- At least one four-card major.
- Interest in locating a major-suit fit.
- Either weak values or invitational-plus values, depending on the chosen Stayman sequence.

---

# Convention Structure

Unlike standard Stayman, Two-Way Stayman uses two separate artificial methods.

Typical approaches include:

- One Stayman sequence for weak hands.
- One Stayman sequence for invitational or stronger hands.

Some partnerships use:

```text
2♣
```

for one range.

Another artificial bid (commonly **2♦** in transfer-based systems or another agreed call) distinguishes the other range.

The exact structure depends entirely on partnership agreement.

# Responder's Continuations

After opener's response, responder may:

- Pass.
- Invite game.
- Bid game.
- Continue investigating slam.

The continuation depends on:

- Combined strength.
- Major-suit fit.
- Distribution.
- Partnership agreements.

---

# Partnership Agreements

The partnership should define:

- Which bids initiate each Stayman sequence.
- Strength requirements.
- Opener's responses.
- Continuations.
- Methods after interference.

These agreements should appear on the convention card.

---

# System Variations

Published versions include:

- Two-Way Stayman used with transfer systems.
- Two-Way Stayman combined with Puppet Stayman.
- Two-Way Stayman using relay structures.
- Partnership-specific adaptations.

There is no universal standard.

---

# Advantages

Two-Way Stayman:

- Distinguishes responder strength immediately.
- Improves contract selection.
- Enhances game and slam bidding.
- Preserves bidding space.

---

# Limitations

Two-Way Stayman:

- More complex than standard Stayman.
- Requires detailed partnership agreements.
- Multiple incompatible versions exist.

# Expert Thinking

Two-Way Stayman improves the efficiency of the auction by allowing responder to communicate **strength as well as shape** from the first response.

By separating constructive auctions from weak competitive auctions, later bidding becomes more descriptive and accurate.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing Two-Way Stayman with Puppet Stayman.
- Forgetting which artificial bid shows which strength.
- Mixing partnership methods.
- Assuming all partnerships use the same structure.

---

# Best Practices

- Agree the complete response structure.
- Practice both weak and strong sequences.
- Record agreements on the convention card.
- Review interference auctions.

# Expert Tip

**Two-Way Stayman is a framework rather than a single standardized convention.**

Different partnerships assign different artificial bids and response tables. Before adopting the convention, partners should agree every sequence and continuation. The important idea is not the specific bids, but the ability to distinguish responder's strength immediately.
