# Walsh

## Purpose

**Walsh** is a response style used after partner opens **1♣**. Its primary principle is:

> **With less than game-forcing strength, responder bids a four-card major before bidding a longer diamond suit.**

The style emphasizes finding **major-suit fits** as early as possible, even when responder's diamond suit is longer.

Walsh is one of the most widely used response styles in modern Standard American and Two-Over-One systems.

---

# Basic Principle

After a **1♣** opening:

- Bid a **4-card major** before diamonds.
- Bypass diamonds unless:
  - You have game-forcing values, or
  - You do not hold a four-card major.

Example:

♠ KJ84  
♥ Q763  
♦ AJ975  
♣ 8

Auction:

1♣ — **1♥**

Not 1♦, despite holding a longer diamond suit.

---

# When Walsh Applies

The convention applies only after:

| Opening | Walsh Applies |
|---------|---------------|
|1♣|Yes|
|1♦|No|
|Major openings|No|
|1NT|No|

---

# Typical Responses

## With Less Than Game-Forcing Strength

Bid a four-card major first.

Example:

♠ QJ84  
♥ K872  
♦ AJ965  
♣ 4

Auction:

1♣ — **1♥**

---

## Without a Four-Card Major

Bid diamonds naturally.

Example:

♠ Q72  
♥ K83  
♦ AJ974  
♣ J94

Auction:

1♣ — **1♦**

---

## With Game-Forcing Values

Many Walsh partnerships bid naturally.

Example:

♠ AQ84  
♥ KQ74  
♦ AJ975  
♣ A

Auction:

1♣ — **1♦**

Game-forcing values justify showing the longer suit first because there will be ample room later to show the major.

Partnership agreements vary.

---

# Why Walsh?

Without Walsh:

Responder often begins with 1♦, making it harder to discover a 4-4 major fit.

Walsh immediately identifies possible heart or spade fits while preserving bidding space.

---

# Opener's Rebids

Because responder frequently bypasses diamonds, opener can infer useful information.

For example:

1♣ — 1♥

1NT

Often denies four-card heart support and shows a balanced hand.

Responder's later diamond bid may reveal a longer diamond suit.

# Advantages

- Finds 4-4 major fits quickly
- Simplifies responder's first bid
- Preserves bidding space
- Well suited to modern Standard American and 2/1 systems
- Widely played by expert partnerships

---

# Disadvantages

- Delays showing responder's longer diamond suit
- Requires partnership agreement for game-forcing hands
- May conceal distribution temporarily

# Summary

| Holding | Response |
|---------|----------|
|4-card major + longer diamonds, non-GF|Bid the major|
|No four-card major|Bid diamonds|
|Game-forcing hand with longer diamonds|Usually bid diamonds first (partnership agreement)|

**Primary idea:** After a **1♣ opening**, responder **prioritizes bidding a four-card major over a longer diamond suit**, making it easier for the partnership to uncover major-suit fits while preserving bidding efficiency.
