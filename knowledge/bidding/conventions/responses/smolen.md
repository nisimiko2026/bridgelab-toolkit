# Smolen Convention

## Overview

The **Smolen Convention** is an extension of **Stayman** that is used after a **1NT opening** when responder holds:

- **Game-forcing values**, and
- **Five cards in one major and four cards in the other major.**

After using Stayman and hearing opener deny a four-card major, responder jumps in the **four-card major**, which artificially shows **five cards in the other major**.

The convention allows the partnership to reach a **5-3 major-suit fit** while keeping the **strong notrump opener as declarer**, protecting the opening lead.

---

# Purpose

Smolen is designed to:

- Find hidden 5-3 major-suit fits.
- Keep the 1NT opener as declarer.
- Preserve the advantage of concealed high cards.
- Improve game and slam bidding.
- Avoid unnecessary notrump contracts.

---

# When to Use

Smolen applies after:

- Partner opens **1NT**.
- Responder has **game-forcing values**.
- Responder holds **5-4 in the majors**.
- Stayman is used first.
- Opener denies holding a four-card major.

---

# Requirements

Responder normally holds:

- 13+ HCP (or equivalent game-going strength).
- Five cards in one major.
- Four cards in the other major.

Typical shapes include:

```
5-4-2-2
```

```
5-4-3-1
```

```
5-4-1-3
```

---

# Standard Auction

## Example 1 — Five Hearts, Four Spades

Responder:

```
♠ KQ84
♥ AJ943
♦ AQ4
♣ K7
```

Auction:

```
1NT
2♣*
2♦
3♠*
```

The **3♠** bid shows:

- Four spades.
- **Five hearts.**
- Game-forcing values.

Opener now bids:

```
4♥
```

with three hearts.

---

## Example 2 — Five Spades, Four Hearts

Auction:

```
1NT
2♣*
2♦
3♥*
```

The **3♥** bid shows:

- Four hearts.
- **Five spades.**
- Game force.

---

# Why Bid the Four-Card Major?

This is the key idea of Smolen.

Responder jumps in the **shorter major** so opener can declare the contract in responder's **longer major**.

This keeps the strong 1NT hand hidden from the defenders.

---

# Opener's Rebids

After a Smolen bid opener normally:

## Three-Card Support

Raises responder's five-card major.

Example:

```
1NT
2♣
2♦
3♥*
3♠
```

Shows three-card spade support.

---

## No Three-Card Support

Bids:

- 3NT
- Four-card minor
- Cue bid (rare)

according to partnership agreement.

---

## Maximum Hand

May begin slam investigation.

---

# Responder's Continuations

Responder now decides between:

- 3NT
- Game in the major
- Cue bidding
- Roman Key Card Blackwood
- Slam investigation

---

# Strength Requirements

Smolen is normally:

- Game forcing.

Most partnerships require:

- 13+ HCP.

Some partnerships use controls and playing strength instead of raw HCP.

---

# Comparison with Stayman

| Stayman | Smolen |
|----------|--------|
| Looks for 4-4 fits | Looks for hidden 5-3 fits |
| Invitational or stronger | Game forcing |
| Natural continuations | Artificial jump |
| Earlier in the auction | Extension after Stayman |

---

# Comparison with Jacoby Transfers

| Jacoby Transfers | Smolen |
|------------------|--------|
| Shows five-card major | Shows 5-4 majors after Stayman |
| May be weak | Always game forcing |
| Immediate transfer | Begins with Stayman |

---

# Comparison with Texas Transfers

| Texas | Smolen |
|--------|--------|
| Six-card major | Five-card major |
| Direct transfer to game | Investigates best game contract |
| Usually no slam interest | Often slam interest |

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

Smolen is primarily intended for uncontested auctions. Once opponents interfere after the Stayman sequence, many partnerships modify or abandon Smolen.

---

## Opponent Overcalls

Example

```
1NT
2♣*
(2♦)
?
```

Possible agreements include:

- Natural bidding.
- Double as a game force.
- Cue bids replacing Smolen.
- Smolen Off.

---

## Opponent Doubles

Example

```
1NT
2♣*
(Double)
?
```

Many partnerships continue with:

- Pass.
- Redouble.
- Natural continuations.
- System On.

Others abandon Smolen.

---

## Competition After Stayman

Example

```
1NT
2♣*
2♦
(3♣)
?
```

Responder should evaluate:

- Whether Smolen remains available.
- Whether a natural major bid is preferable.
- Partnership agreements.

---

## Slam Investigation

If a major fit is discovered, the partnership may continue with:

- Cue Bids.
- Control Bids.
- Roman Key Card Blackwood.
- Grand Slam Force.

---

## Partnership Agreements

Partners should define:

- Whether Smolen remains on after interference.
- Responses after doubles.
- Cue-bid replacements.
- Slam continuations.
- Puppet Stayman interaction.

---

## General Principles

When using Smolen:

- Begin with Stayman.
- Promise game-forcing strength.
- Jump in the **shorter** major.
- Let opener declare the contract.
- Continue naturally after opener's clarification.

---

# Advantages

Smolen:

- Finds hidden 5-3 major fits.
- Keeps the strong hand as declarer.
- Improves game selection.
- Enhances slam bidding.
- Works naturally with Stayman.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Applies only after Stayman.
- Requires game-forcing values.
- Is unavailable after many competitive auctions.
- Requires partnership agreement.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Jumping in the Wrong Major

Remember:

You jump in the **four-card major** to show the **five-card major**.

---

## Using Smolen with Invitational Hands

Smolen is normally **game forcing**.

---

## Skipping Stayman

Smolen always begins with a Stayman inquiry.

---

## Confusing Smolen with Transfers

Smolen is **not** a transfer convention.

---

## Forgetting Opener's Priority

With three-card support, opener should normally prefer the responder's five-card major over 3NT.

---

# Tips

- Think of Smolen as **Stayman Plus**.
- Memorize the rule: **Bid the shorter major to show the longer major.**
- Use Smolen only with genuine game-forcing values.
- Practice the common 5-4 major patterns.
- Learn Smolen together with Puppet Stayman and Jacoby Transfers.

---

# Modern Usage

Smolen has become a standard extension of Stayman in expert partnerships. It fits naturally with:

- Five-card major systems.
- Standard American.
- 2/1 Game Force.
- Modern Precision methods.

Most tournament partnerships that play Stayman also play Smolen because it improves both game and slam bidding with very little additional complexity.

---

# Summary

The Smolen Convention is an elegant extension of Stayman that allows responder with **game-forcing values** and **5-4 distribution in the majors** to locate hidden **5-3 major-suit fits** while keeping the strong 1NT opener as declarer. By jumping in the **four-card major** to show the **five-card major**, the partnership reaches superior contracts and protects the notrump opener's concealed high cards. Smolen is now considered a standard component of modern notrump systems.
