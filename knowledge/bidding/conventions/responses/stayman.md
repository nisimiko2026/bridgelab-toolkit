# Stayman

## Overview

**Stayman** is one of the most widely used conventions in bridge. It is an **artificial response** to a **1NT** or **2NT** opening that asks opener whether they hold a **four-card major suit**.

The convention helps partnerships locate an eight-card major-suit fit before committing to notrump. Since major-suit games require only ten tricks and usually score better than notrump with an eight-card fit, Stayman is one of the most valuable conventions in modern bridge.

Typical auctions include:

```
1NT
2♣*
```

or

```
2NT
3♣*
```

where the club bid is entirely artificial.

---

# Purpose

Stayman is used to:

- Locate a 4-4 major-suit fit.
- Improve game selection.
- Avoid missing a major-suit game.
- Begin constructive slam investigation.
- Exchange information while preserving bidding space.

---

# When to Use

Stayman is normally used after:

- 1NT opening.
- 2NT opening.
- Equivalent strong balanced sequences.

It is appropriate whenever responder wishes to determine whether opener holds a four-card major.

---

# Requirements

Responder typically has:

- Invitational values or stronger.
- At least one four-card major.

Many partnerships also use Stayman with:

- Both four-card majors.
- Some weaker hands planning to escape to a major (Garbage Stayman).
- Slam interest.

---

# Typical Hand Types

Examples include:

- 4-4 in the majors.
- 5-4 with one major.
- Balanced invitational hands.
- Balanced game-going hands.
- Slam-oriented hands.

---

# The Stayman Inquiry

The artificial **2♣** (or **3♣** over 2NT) asks:

> "Do you have a four-card major?"

The bid does **not** promise clubs.

---

# Opener's Responses

Standard responses are:

## 2♦

No four-card major.

---

## 2♥

Four hearts.

May also contain four spades depending on partnership agreement.

---

## 2♠

Four spades.

Normally denies four hearts.

---

## 2NT Auctions

After a 2NT opening, responses follow the same principles:

```
2NT
3♣*
3♦
```

No four-card major.

```
2NT
3♣*
3♥
```

Four hearts.

```
2NT
3♣*
3♠
```

Four spades.

---

# Responder's Continuations

After opener replies, responder selects the best contract.

## Major Fit Found

Bid:

- 4♥
- 4♠

or continue with slam investigation.

---

## No Major Fit

Choose among:

- 2NT
- 3NT
- Minor-suit game
- Slam exploration

depending upon strength and distribution.

---

# Invitational Auctions

Example

```
1NT
2♣
2♦
2NT
```

Responder invites game.

---

# Game Auctions

Example

```
1NT
2♣
2♥
4♥
```

A 4-4 heart fit has been located.

---

# Slam Investigation

Once a major fit has been discovered, the partnership may continue with:

- Cue Bidding
- Blackwood
- Roman Key Card Blackwood
- Control Bids

Stayman frequently serves as the first step toward slam.

---

# Garbage Stayman

Some partnerships extend Stayman to include weak hands.

Example

```
♠ J987
♥ 8643
♦ 72
♣ 864
```

Auction

```
1NT
2♣
2♦
2♥
```

Responder escapes to a major instead of remaining in notrump.

This treatment requires partnership agreement.

---

# Advantages

Stayman:

- Finds major-suit fits.
- Improves game selection.
- Enhances slam bidding.
- Preserves bidding space.
- Is easy to learn.
- Applies in many auctions.

---

# Limitations

Stayman:

- Does not locate five-card major fits by itself.
- Requires partnership agreement.
- May not be available after interference.
- Can reveal information to the opponents.

---

# Variations

Popular variations include:

- Garbage Stayman
- Non-Promissory Stayman
- Three-Way Stayman
- Puppet Stayman
- Minor Suit Stayman
- Smolen continuations

Each partnership should agree which methods are used.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should discuss:

- Minimum strength.
- Garbage Stayman.
- Invitational versus game-forcing hands.
- Responses with both majors.
- Continuations after every response.
- Slam continuations.
- Competitive auctions.

# Example Auctions

## Example 1 — Heart Fit

```
1NT
2♣*
2♥
4♥
```

Responder discovers a 4-4 heart fit and places the contract in game.

---

## Example 2 — No Major Fit

```
1NT
2♣*
2♦
3NT
```

No major fit exists, so notrump becomes the best contract.

---

## Example 3 — Spade Fit

```
1NT
2♣*
2♠
4♠
```

A spade game is reached after locating an eight-card fit.

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

The sequences described above assume an uncontested auction. When the opponents interfere over a **1NT** or **2NT** opening, standard Stayman methods may no longer be available. Every partnership should agree in advance how interference affects the convention.

---

## Opponent Overcalls

Example

```
1NT   (2♦)   ?
```

Responder's priorities remain:

- Search for a major-suit fit.
- Reach the best game contract.
- Preserve slam possibilities.

Many partnerships adopt one of two approaches:

### Systems On

Normal Stayman and transfer responses continue whenever bidding space permits.

### Systems Off

After interference, Stayman is suspended and replaced by natural or competitive methods.

---

## Opponent Doubles

Example

```
1NT   (Double)   ?
```

Common partnership treatments include:

- Systems On.
- Systems Off.
- Pass as a forcing or penalty action.
- Redouble.
- Escape methods.

Every partnership should define these agreements before play.

---

## Interference After Stayman

Example

```
1NT
Pass
2♣*
(2♥)
?
```

Possible partnership approaches include:

- Continue the normal response structure.
- Double to show values.
- Natural bidding.
- Pass forcing.

The exact treatment depends entirely on partnership agreement.

---

## Lebensohl and Rubensohl

When interference occurs after a **1NT** opening, many partnerships employ:

- **Lebensohl**
- **Rubensohl**

These conventions help distinguish:

- Weak hands.
- Invitational hands.
- Game-forcing hands.
- Hands with or without a stopper.

Detailed methods are described in their respective articles.

---

## Maintaining the Search

Even after interference, the partnership should continue trying to determine:

- Whether a major fit exists.
- Whether notrump remains the best contract.
- Whether game or slam should be explored.

Do not abandon constructive bidding simply because the opponents have entered the auction.

---

## Partnership Agreements

Partnerships should agree on:

- Systems On versus Systems Off.
- Responses after doubles.
- Responses after overcalls.
- Whether Stayman remains available.
- Lebensohl or Rubensohl methods.
- Slam continuations after interference.

---

## General Principles

When opponents interfere:

- Preserve the search for a major fit whenever practical.
- Continue exchanging useful information.
- Follow partnership agreements rather than improvising.
- Reevaluate the partnership's combined strength after every bid.
- Remember that finding the correct game remains the primary objective.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Using Stayman Without a Reason

Do not bid Stayman simply because you have clubs.

---

## Forgetting Invitational Values

Know whether your partnership permits weak Stayman or Garbage Stayman.

---

## Missing a Major Fit

Always complete the Stayman sequence before committing to notrump.

---

## Forgetting Partnership Agreements

Many continuations differ between partnerships.

---

## Ignoring Interference

Stayman sequences frequently change after doubles or overcalls.

Know your partnership's agreements before playing.

---

# Tips

- Learn the complete Stayman structure, not just the first bid.
- Remember that Stayman asks about majors—not clubs.
- Think ahead before making the inquiry.
- Discuss Garbage Stayman and competitive continuations with your partner.
- Use Stayman to improve both game and slam bidding.

---

# Summary

Stayman is the cornerstone convention for notrump auctions. By asking opener to show a four-card major, it enables partnerships to locate major-suit fits, improve game selection, and begin accurate slam investigation. Although simple in concept, successful use depends on clear partnership agreements regarding continuations, variations, and competitive auctions.
