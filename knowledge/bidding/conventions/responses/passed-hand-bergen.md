# Passed-Hand Bergen Raises

## Overview

**Passed-Hand Bergen Raises** are an advanced partnership treatment that adapts
the **Bergen Raises** convention to auctions in which responder has already
passed. The convention allows responder to distinguish several types of
constructive raises after partner opens **1♥** or **1♠** in third or fourth
seat.

Unlike standard Bergen Raises, which primarily describe responder's strength,
Passed-Hand Bergen Raises recognize that responder has already denied an opening
bid. The emphasis therefore shifts toward

- support quality,
- playing strength,
- distribution,
- game potential,
- and obstructive value.

Because responder has already passed, all raises are limited, allowing opener
to evaluate whether the opening was full strength or a light third-seat action.

---

# Objectives

Passed-Hand Bergen Raises enable the partnership to

- distinguish weak, constructive and invitational raises,
- identify four-card support immediately,
- improve game decisions,
- evaluate light third-seat openings,
- increase competitive accuracy,
- preserve bidding space.

---

# Background

Consider the auction

```
Pass
Pass
1♠
?
```

Responder cannot have opening values.

Without artificial raises, opener often cannot distinguish

- weak support,
- constructive support,
- invitational support,
- highly distributional hands.

Passed-Hand Bergen Raises solve this problem.

---

# Relationship to Standard Bergen Raises

Standard Bergen Raises assume responder has not previously passed.

Passed-Hand Bergen Raises modify the ranges because responder has already shown

- fewer than opening values.

The responses therefore become narrower and more descriptive.

---

# Typical Structure

One expert treatment is

| Response | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| 3♣ | Constructive raise with four-card support |
| 3♦ | Invitational raise with four-card support |
| 3♥ / 3♠ | Preemptive raise |
| 2NT | Passed-Hand Jacoby (optional) |
| Splinter | Passed-Hand Splinter (optional) |

Numerous expert variations exist.

---

# Requirements

Typical requirements

- passed hand,
- four-card trump support,
- constructive playing strength,
- offensive distribution.

Support points generally matter more than HCP.

---

# Hand Evaluation

Evaluate using

- support points,
- Losing Trick Count,
- trump quality,
- controls,
- shortness,
- offensive potential.

Flat hands should be downgraded.

# Preemptive Raise

Jump directly to

```

```

shows

- weak hand,
- excellent support,
- obstructive values.

The exact range depends upon partnership agreement.

# Opener's Evaluation

Opener should evaluate

- opening strength,
- trump quality,
- wasted values,
- controls,
- distribution.

The primary question becomes

> "Is my opening full strength or merely a light third-seat opening?"

# Competition

If opponents interfere,

partnership agreement determines whether

- Bergen remains on,
- cue bids replace raises,
- doubles substitute,
- raises become natural.

Many partnerships abandon Bergen after significant interference.

# Vulnerability

Passed-Hand Bergen Raises become more aggressive

- favorable vulnerability,

and more disciplined

- unfavorable vulnerability.

The style should match the partnership's competitive philosophy.

# Expert Style

Modern expert partnerships often combine

- Drury,
- Reverse Drury,

- Passed-Hand Splinter,
- Passed-Hand Jacoby,

to classify virtually every type of supporting hand.

# Modern Expert Perspective

Unlike **Drury**, **Passed-Hand Bergen Raises are not a universally recognized
standard convention**. They are a **partnership treatment** developed by some
expert pairs who wish to retain the descriptive advantages of Bergen Raises
after responder has passed.

Many world-class partnerships prefer to use

- Drury,
- Reverse Drury,
- Passed-Hand Splinters,

instead of extending Bergen Raises. Others adapt Bergen structures by lowering
the strength ranges to account for responder's original pass.

Accordingly, partnerships adopting Passed-Hand Bergen Raises must define:

- the exact meanings of 3♣ and 3♦,
- minimum support requirements,
- whether the bids are forcing or invitational,
- opener's rebid structure,
- treatment after interference.

---

# Expert Recommendations

For most expert partnerships today, the preferred passed-hand structure is

| Bid | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Simple Raise | Weak constructive |
| Drury | Constructive inquiry |
| Reverse Drury | Stronger constructive raise |
| Passed-Hand Splinter | Invitational with shortness |
| Passed-Hand Jacoby | Invitational balanced raise (if played) |

**Passed-Hand Bergen Raises** are best viewed as an optional refinement for
partnerships that already employ Bergen Raises extensively and want to preserve
their descriptive philosophy in passed-hand auctions.

---

# Summary

Passed-Hand Bergen Raises extend the ideas of Bergen Raises into passed-hand
auctions by allowing responder to distinguish different constructive raises
after a major-suit opening. Although not part of mainstream standard methods,
they can provide excellent descriptive accuracy when supported by detailed
partnership agreements.

Because expert practice varies considerably, partnerships should treat
Passed-Hand Bergen Raises as a customizable agreement rather than a universally
defined convention.
