# Ogust Convention

## Overview

The **Ogust Convention** is an artificial response to a **Weak Two opening** that allows responder to determine whether opener has a **minimum or maximum hand** and whether the opening suit is **good or poor**.

The convention uses an artificial **2NT inquiry**, after which opener describes the quality of both the hand and the suit.

Ogust is particularly valuable when responder is uncertain whether game is possible opposite a weak two opening.

Typical auction:

```
2♥
2NT*
```

or

```
2♠
2NT*
```

The **2NT** bid is artificial.

---

# Purpose

Ogust enables the partnership to:

- Distinguish minimum and maximum weak-two openings.
- Evaluate suit quality.
- Improve game decisions.
- Avoid poor games.
- Reach excellent thin games.
- Improve slam investigation (rare).

---

# When to Use

Ogust applies after:

```
2♥
2NT*
```

or

```
2♠
2NT*
```

Some partnerships also use Ogust after:

- Weak 2♦
- Multi 2♦ (after opener has identified the major)

It is normally **not** used after strong artificial openings.

---

# Requirements

Responder normally holds:

- Invitational or stronger values.
- Uncertainty about game.
- Interest in learning opener's exact strength.

Responder already expects opener to have:

- A Weak Two opening.
- Six-card suit.
- Limited strength.

---

# The 2NT Inquiry

The **2NT** response asks:

> "Tell me about your hand and your suit."

The inquiry requests two pieces of information:

- Minimum or maximum hand.
- Good or poor suit quality.

---

# Standard Response Structure

The most widely played structure is:

| Opener's Rebid | Meaning |
|----------------|---------|
| **3♣** | Minimum hand, poor suit |
| **3♦** | Minimum hand, good suit |
| **3♥ / 3♠** | Maximum hand, poor suit |
| **3NT** | Maximum hand, good suit |

If the opening bid was **2♠**, opener rebids **3♠** instead of **3♥** to show a maximum hand with a poor suit.

This structure is simple and widely used throughout the bridge world.

---

# What Is a Good Suit?

A "good" suit usually contains:

- Two of the top three honors

or

- Three of the top five honors

Examples:

Good:

```
AKQxxx
AQJxxx
KQJxxx
```

Poor:

```
Q109xxx
J109xx
K108xxx
```

Partnership agreement determines the exact definition.

---

# What Is a Maximum?

Typical partnerships define:

Minimum:

- 5-7 HCP

Maximum:

- 8-10 HCP

Some partnerships instead evaluate:

- Playing tricks.
- Offensive potential.

---

# Responder's Continuations

After opener's description responder chooses among:

## Pass

Minimum opposite minimum.

---

## Invite

With borderline values.

---

## Bid Game

When opener shows:

- Maximum.
- Good suit.

---

## Explore Slam

Rare.

Usually only after exceptional distribution.

---

# Opener's Rebids

After the Ogust response opener simply answers the inquiry.

The rebid is descriptive rather than competitive.

Once opener has answered, responder becomes captain.

---

# Advantages

Ogust:

- Improves game decisions.
- Describes both strength and suit quality.
- Reduces guesswork.
- Helps responder evaluate accurately.
- Easy to remember.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Consumes bidding space.
- Requires partnership agreement.
- Is unavailable if opponents interfere immediately.
- Gives information to the opponents.

---

# Variations

Popular versions include:

- Standard Ogust.
- Reverse Ogust.
- Feature-first Ogust.
- Modified Ogust.

Some partnerships reverse the meanings of **3♣** and **3♦**, or use different rebids to distinguish suit quality.

Every partnership should agree on the exact response structure.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should discuss:

- Minimum versus maximum range.
- Definition of a good suit.
- Whether Ogust applies after 2♦.
- Responses after Multi 2♦.
- Competitive methods.
- Slam continuations.

---

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

The auctions above assume an uncontested Weak Two opening. Once opponents interfere, the artificial **2NT inquiry** may no longer be available.

Most partnerships modify or abandon Ogust after interference.

---

## Opponent Overcalls

Example

```
2♥
(2♠)
?
```

Possible partnership treatments include:

- Natural bidding.
- Feature Ask.
- Lebensohl methods.
- Ogust Off.

Many partnerships suspend Ogust entirely.

---

## Opponent Doubles

Example

```
2♠
(Double)
?
```

Common agreements include:

- 2NT remains Ogust.
- 2NT becomes natural.
- Redouble shows values.
- Natural continuations.

Partnership agreement is essential.

---

## Competition After 2NT

Example

```
2♥
2NT*
(Double)
?
```

Typical agreements include:

- Pass.
- Redouble.
- Continue Ogust responses.
- Natural rebids.

Every partnership should define these continuations before play.

---

## Feature Ask vs Ogust

Some partnerships replace Ogust with a **Feature Ask** after interference.

Feature Ask requests:

- Outside ace.
- Outside king.
- Useful side feature.

Unlike Ogust, it does **not** ask about strength and suit quality.

---

## Partnership Agreements

Partners should define:

- Whether Ogust remains on after interference.
- Responses after doubles.
- Responses after overcalls.
- Feature Ask relationship.
- Game invitations.
- Slam continuations.

---

## General Principles

When opponents interfere:

- Preserve constructive bidding whenever practical.
- Do not force Ogust if natural bidding becomes clearer.
- Continue describing your hand accurately.
- Reevaluate after every competitive action.
- Follow partnership agreements consistently.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Forgetting the Response Structure

The four Ogust responses must be completely memorized.

---

## Misjudging Suit Quality

Do not classify every six-card suit as "good."

Honor quality matters.

---

## Confusing Ogust With Feature Ask

Ogust asks about:

- Strength.
- Suit quality.

Feature Ask asks about:

- Outside honors.

They answer different questions.

---

## Using Ogust Without Invitational Values

Hands that already know the final contract often do not need Ogust.

---

## Ignoring Competition

Many partnerships suspend Ogust after interference.

Know your agreements.

---

# Tips

- Learn the four Ogust responses as a single pattern.
- Agree precisely what defines a "good suit."
- Distinguish HCP from playing strength.
- Practice game decisions after every response.
- Discuss Feature Ask together with Ogust.

---

# Summary

The Ogust Convention is the standard inquiry after a Weak Two opening. By using an artificial **2NT** response, responder learns both the strength of opener's hand and the quality of the opening suit, allowing far more accurate game decisions than natural bidding alone. Although several response structures exist, the traditional four-step method remains the most widely played. Successful use depends on disciplined hand evaluation, clear partnership agreements, and careful judgment after competitive interference.
