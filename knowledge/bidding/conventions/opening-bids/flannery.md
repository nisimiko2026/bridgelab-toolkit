# Flannery Convention

## Overview

The **Flannery Convention** is an artificial **2♦ opening** that shows:

- Exactly **5 hearts**
- Exactly **4 spades**
- Opening strength

It was developed by William Flannery to solve one of the most awkward problems in natural bidding: opening **1♥** with a **5-4 major-suit holding** and then having difficulty describing the hand after responder's reply.

Instead of opening **1♥**, the partnership opens **2♦**, immediately describing both major suits and a limited opening hand.

The convention replaces the natural Weak Two Diamond opening.

---

# Purpose

Flannery is designed to:

- Show both major suits immediately.
- Eliminate difficult rebid problems.
- Improve part-score and game bidding.
- Help responder judge the correct major-suit fit.
- Improve competitive bidding.

---

# When to Use

Flannery applies only as an opening bid.

```
2♦*
```

Artificial.

Shows:

- Five hearts
- Four spades
- Opening values

---

# Requirements

Typical requirements are:

## Strength

Usually:

- 11–15 HCP

Some partnerships play:

- 11–16 HCP

The exact range should be agreed.

---

## Distribution

Exactly:

```
5 Hearts
4 Spades
```

No longer major.

No shorter heart suit.

---

## Suit Quality

Heart suit should normally contain:

- Five cards
- Reasonable quality

Spades should be genuine.

---

## Hand Type

Usually:

- Unbalanced
- Semi-balanced
- Occasionally 5-4-2-2

---

# Why Flannery?

Without Flannery:

```
♠ AQ84
♥ KQ974
♦ A6
♣ J82
```

opens

```
1♥
```

If responder bids:

```
1NT
```

opener has no perfect rebid.

Flannery solves this problem by showing the exact shape immediately.

---

# Standard Responses

Partnership agreements vary.

Typical structure:

---

## 2♥

Preference for hearts.

Usually minimum values.

---

## 2♠

Preference for spades.

Responder prefers the four-card spade fit.

---

## 2NT

Artificial.

Often asks opener to further describe the hand.

Many partnerships use it as an inquiry.

---

## 3♣ / 3♦

Natural.

Game interest.

---

## Jump to Game

Shows sufficient strength.

Example:

```
2♦*
4♥
```

---

# Opener's Rebids

After responder's inquiry, opener may describe:

- Minimum versus maximum.
- Shortness.
- Suit quality.
- Controls.

Exact meanings depend upon partnership agreement.

---

# Responder Rebids

Responder evaluates:

- Major-suit fit.
- Combined strength.
- Distribution.
- Slam potential.

Possible actions include:

- Pass.
- Invite game.
- Bid game.
- Begin slam investigation.

---

# Slam Investigation

Although Flannery usually describes limited opening hands, slam occasionally becomes possible.

Methods include:

- Cue Bids.
- Blackwood.
- Roman Key Card Blackwood.

---

# Advantages

Flannery:

- Describes both majors immediately.
- Eliminates awkward rebids.
- Improves major-suit game bidding.
- Helps responder judge quickly.
- Simplifies competitive auctions.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Gives up the natural Weak Two Diamond opening.
- Requires detailed partnership agreements.
- Is less useful with modern Two-over-One methods.
- Is not universally played.

---

# Variations

Common treatments include:

- Standard Flannery
- Reverse Flannery
- Flannery with 2NT inquiry
- Modified strength ranges

Every partnership should agree on its methods.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Discuss:

- HCP range.

- Invitational sequences.

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

The auctions described above assume an uncontested opening. When the opponents interfere, the partnership should continue to use the information already exchanged—namely opener's exact **5-4 major-suit pattern**—to judge the level of competition.

Because Flannery consumes the **2♦** opening, bidding space is limited and partnership agreements become especially important.

---

## Opponent Overcalls

Example

```
2♦*   (2♠)   ?
```

Responder should evaluate:

- Heart fit.
- Spade fit.
- Combined strength.
- Offensive versus defensive values.
- Vulnerability.

Possible actions include:

- Pass.
- Double (partnership agreement).
- Raise hearts.
- Bid notrump.
- Cue bid.

---

## Opponent Doubles

Example

```
2♦*   (Double)   ?
```

Typical agreements include:

- Pass.
- Redouble showing values.
- 2♥ preference.
- 2♠ preference.
- Natural bids.

Every partnership should define these treatments before play.

---

## Competition After the Response

Example

```
2♦*
Pass
2♥
(3♣)
?
```

Both partners should now reevaluate:

- Combined strength.
- Trump fit.
- Distribution.
- Offensive potential.
- Vulnerability.

Once a major-suit fit has been established, competing further often depends more on playing strength than on high-card points.

---

## Balancing and Reopening

If the opponents compete aggressively after the Flannery auction, either partner may have opportunities to:

- Balance.
- Double.
- Compete further.
- Defend.

Judgment should be based on partnership style and vulnerability.

---

## Partnership Agreements

Partnerships should discuss:

- Responses after doubles.
- Responses after overcalls.
- Whether the 2NT inquiry remains available.
- Competitive raises.
- Cue bids.
- Balancing style.

---

## General Principles

When opponents interfere after a Flannery opening:

- Continue using opener's known 5-4 major distribution.
- Protect the known major-suit fit whenever practical.
- Compete with offensive hands.
- Reevaluate after every competitive bid.
- Follow partnership agreements rather than improvising.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Opening With the Wrong Shape

Flannery promises exactly:

- Five hearts
- Four spades

Do not stretch the requirements.

---

## Forgetting the Strength Range

Do not use Flannery with minimum weak-two hands or very strong opening hands unless your partnership specifically agrees.

---

## Ignoring Major Fits

The principal objective is to identify the correct major-suit contract quickly.

---

## Forgetting the Inquiry Structure

If playing a 2NT inquiry, both partners must know the response structure.

---

## Ignoring Competitive Agreements

Because the auction starts at the two level, partnership misunderstandings can become expensive.

---

# Tips

- Think of Flannery as a **shape-showing** convention first and a strength-showing convention second.
- Use it only with the agreed 5-4 major pattern.
- Practice the 2NT inquiry responses.
- Remember that responder already knows opener's exact major-suit distribution.
- Discuss competitive auctions before using the convention regularly.

---

# Summary

Flannery is an artificial **2♦ opening** that shows exactly **five hearts, four spades, and opening values**. It solves difficult rebid problems that arise after a natural **1♥** opening by describing both major suits immediately. The convention improves game and competitive bidding by allowing responder to evaluate major-suit fits early in the auction. Because it replaces the natural Weak Two Diamond opening, successful use depends on detailed partnership agreements regarding responses, continuations, and competitive auctions.
