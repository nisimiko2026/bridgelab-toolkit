# Ekren Convention

## Overview

The **Ekren Convention** is an artificial **2♦ opening** that shows a **weak hand containing both major suits**. Unlike the Multi 2♦ convention, Ekren immediately identifies the partnership's two-suited nature rather than concealing the long suit.

The convention is designed to:

- Show both major suits.
- Consume bidding space.
- Make it difficult for opponents to enter the auction.
- Help the partnership locate a major-suit fit quickly.

Several versions of Ekren exist, but the most common treatment shows:

- Both major suits.
- Weak opening values.
- Usually at least 4-4 distribution.

The convention replaces the natural Weak Two Diamond opening.

---

# Purpose

Ekren enables the partnership to:

- Show both major suits with one bid.
- Preempt the opponents.
- Locate major-suit fits immediately.
- Improve competitive bidding.
- Make constructive decisions despite limited strength.

---

# When to Use

Ekren is used only as an opening bid.

```
2♦*
```

Artificial.

Shows:

- Hearts and spades.
- Weak opening values.

---

# Requirements

## Strength

Typical range:

- 6-11 HCP

Some partnerships use:

- 5-10 HCP
- 6-10 HCP
- 6-11 HCP

Playing strength is usually more important than raw HCP.

---

## Distribution

Most common shapes include:

```
4-4
```

```
5-4
```

```
4-5
```

Some partnerships also include:

```
5-5
```

The exact agreement should be discussed before play.

---

## Suit Quality

Neither major needs to be exceptionally strong.

The convention emphasizes:

- Distribution.
- Offensive potential.
- Preemptive value.

---

## Vulnerability

Ekren is generally:

- More aggressive when favorable.
- More disciplined when vulnerable.

Suit quality should improve as vulnerability increases.

---

# Typical Hand Types

Examples include:

```

♥ Q1085
♦ 74
♣ J964
```

or

```

♥ J965
♦ 84
♣ 75
```

Both hands are unsuitable for a natural weak two but ideal for Ekren.

# Standard Responses

Partnership agreements vary.

Typical structure includes:

---

## 2♥

Preference for hearts.

Usually constructive.

---

## 2♠

Preference for spades.

---

## 2NT

Artificial inquiry.

Requests additional information.

Commonly asks opener to clarify:

- Strength.
- Shape.
- Suit lengths.

---

## 3♣

Artificial inquiry in some partnerships.

---

## New Suit

Natural.

Game interest.

---

## Jump to Game

Shows sufficient strength and support.

---

# Opener's Rebids

After an inquiry, opener describes:

- Minimum or maximum.
- Better major.
- Exact distribution.
- Suit quality.

Response structures vary widely.

---

# Responder Rebids

Responder evaluates:

- Major fit.
- Combined playing strength.
- Distribution.
- Offensive values.

Possible actions include:

- Pass.
- Invite game.
- Bid game.
- Continue investigating slam (rare).

---

# Game Contracts

Responder normally selects:

```
4♥
```

or

```
4♠
```

after identifying the superior fit.

---

# Advantages

Ekren:

- Shows both majors immediately.
- Improves competitive bidding.
- Consumes bidding space.
- Helps locate major fits.
- Makes life difficult for opponents.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Gives up the natural Weak Two Diamond opening.
- Requires partnership agreements.
- Has many different response structures.
- Is less common than Multi 2♦.

---

# Variations

Popular treatments include:

- Standard Ekren.
- Extended Ekren.
- Ekren with 2NT inquiry.
- Ekren including 5-5 majors.
- Modified strength ranges.

Every partnership should agree on its exact methods.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should discuss:

- HCP range.
- Minimum major length.
- Whether 5-5 hands qualify.
- 2NT inquiry responses.
- Invitational sequences.
- Competitive methods.

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

The auctions described above assume an uncontested opening. Because Ekren already consumes bidding space while showing both major suits, opponents frequently compete immediately.

The partnership should use the known two-suited distribution to judge whether further competition is appropriate.

---

## Opponent Overcalls

Example

```
2♦*   (2♠)   ?
```

Responder evaluates:

- Heart support.
- Spade support.
- Combined offensive values.
- Vulnerability.
- Distribution.

Possible actions include:

- Pass.
- Double (partnership agreement).
- Raise hearts.
- Raise spades.
- Bid notrump.
- Cue bid.

---

## Opponent Doubles

Example

```
2♦*   (Double)   ?
```

Typical agreements include:

- Pass.
- Redouble showing values.
- 2♥ preference.
- 2♠ preference.
- Natural bids.

Every partnership should define these methods before play.

---

## Competition After the Response

Example

```
2♦*
Pass
2♥
(3♣)
?
```

Both partners should now reevaluate:

- Trump fit.
- Playing strength.
- Offensive versus defensive values.
- Distribution.
- Vulnerability.

Distribution often becomes more important than high-card points.

---

## Balancing and Reopening

If the opponents compete successfully, either partner may have opportunities to:

- Balance.
- Double.
- Compete further.
- Defend.

Judgment should be based on:

- Playing tricks.
- Distribution.
- Partnership style.

---

## Sacrifice Decisions

Because Ekren frequently identifies a major-suit fit, sacrifice decisions occur often.

Before competing further, consider:

- Vulnerability.
- Total tricks.
- Offensive potential.
- Likely penalty.

---

## Partnership Agreements

Partnerships should discuss:

- Responses after doubles.
- Responses after overcalls.
- Whether 2NT inquiry remains available.
- Competitive raises.
- Cue bids.
- Balancing style.

---

## General Principles

When opponents interfere after an Ekren opening:

- Continue using the known two-suited shape.
- Protect the best major-suit fit.
- Compete with offensive hands rather than defensive hands.
- Consider the Law of Total Tricks.
- Reevaluate after every competitive bid.
- Follow partnership agreements rather than improvising.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Opening With the Wrong Shape

Ekren promises both major suits.

Do not use it with only one major.

---

## Ignoring Vulnerability

Aggressive vulnerable preempts can become expensive.

---

## Forgetting Partnership Agreements

Different versions of Ekren have very different response structures.

---

## Overvaluing High Cards

Ekren is based primarily on:

- Distribution.
- Playing tricks.
- Offensive values.

---

## Competing Automatically

After interference, reevaluate before continuing the auction.

---

# Tips

- Think of Ekren as a distributional convention rather than an HCP convention.
- Agree precisely which shapes qualify.
- Practice the 2NT inquiry responses.
- Let vulnerability influence your style.
- Remember that both partners already know the two-suited nature of the hand.

---

# Summary

Ekren is an artificial **2♦ opening** that shows a weak hand with both major suits, typically 4-4, 5-4, or 4-5 distribution. It combines constructive bidding with a strong preemptive effect by immediately identifying the partnership's major-suit holdings while consuming valuable bidding space. Although several response structures exist, successful use depends on disciplined hand evaluation, clear partnership agreements, and sound competitive judgment.
