# Multi 2♦ Convention

## Overview

The **Multi 2♦** convention is an artificial opening bid in which **2♦** does not have a single fixed meaning. Instead, it shows one of several predefined hand types, the most common being **a weak two opening in either hearts or spades**.

Unlike natural weak-two openings, Multi 2♦ conceals opener's long suit, making it more difficult for the opponents to enter the auction accurately.

Although many variations exist, the standard version promises one of:

- Weak Two in Hearts
- Weak Two in Spades

Some partnerships include additional strong hand types, but these are optional partnership agreements.

---

# Purpose

Multi 2♦ is designed to:

- Conceal the long major suit.
- Increase preemptive pressure.
- Make competitive bidding more difficult.
- Preserve bidding space.
- Improve both offensive and defensive decisions.

---

# Standard Meanings

The most common version shows one of:

```
Weak Two in Hearts
```

or

```
Weak Two in Spades
```

The exact major is revealed later.

---

# Optional Additional Meanings

Some partnerships extend Multi 2♦ to include:

- Strong balanced hand
- Strong minor-suited hand
- Strong two-suiter
- Gambling hand

These treatments vary considerably and should always be agreed beforehand.

---

# Requirements

## Strength

Typical range:

- 6–10 HCP

Some partnerships use:

- 5–10 HCP
- 6–11 HCP

Playing tricks are more important than HCP.

---

## Distribution

Normally:

- Six-card heart suit

or

- Six-card spade suit

Some partnerships permit excellent five-card suits.

---

## Suit Quality

Suit should normally contain:

- Two of the top three honors

or

- Three of the top five honors

Suit quality depends upon vulnerability.

---

## Vulnerability

The convention is:

- Aggressive when favorable.
- Disciplined when vulnerable.

Suit quality should improve as vulnerability becomes unfavorable.

# Standard Responses

Responder first determines whether the partnership should:

- Play part-score.
- Compete.
- Investigate game.

---

## 2♥

Pass or correct.

Opener:

- Passes with hearts.
- Corrects to 2♠ with spades.

This is the most common relay.

---

## 2NT

Artificial inquiry.

Requests additional information.

Typical questions include:

- Which major?
- Minimum or maximum?
- Suit quality.
- Feature.

---

## 3♣

Natural.

Constructive.

---

## 3♦

Artificial or natural depending upon partnership agreement.

---

## Jump to Game

Responder places the contract.

---

# Opener's Rebids

After a relay, opener clarifies:

- Hearts or spades.
- Minimum or maximum.
- Suit quality.
- Feature (if asked).

Every partnership should agree on the exact structure.

---

# 2NT Inquiry

Many partnerships use **2NT** as a feature ask.

Typical responses describe:

- Major suit.
- Outside ace or king.
- Minimum or maximum.
- Suit quality.

Several popular structures exist.

---

# Responder Rebids

Responder reevaluates:

- Combined strength.
- Trump fit.
- Playing tricks.
- Vulnerability.

Possible continuations include:

- Pass.
- Invite game.
- Bid game.
- Slam investigation (rare).

---

# Advantages

Multi 2♦:

- Conceals the long suit.
- Makes defense more difficult.
- Creates preemptive pressure.
- Preserves flexibility.
- Complicates opponent overcalls.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Requires detailed partnership agreements.
- Has many different versions.
- Is artificial.
- May require alerting or disclosure under local regulations.
- Is restricted in some bridge organizations and events.

Players should verify whether Multi 2♦ is permitted under the regulations governing the event they are playing.

---

# Variations

Popular versions include:

- Standard Multi
- Multi with strong balanced option
- Multi with strong minor option
- Multi with two-suited options
- Modern Multi

Every partnership should agree on the meanings before play.

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

Because Multi 2♦ conceals opener's actual suit, opponents often find competitive decisions more difficult than against a natural weak two. At the same time, the partnership has less room to exchange information once interference occurs.

After competition, both partners should continue describing their hands while preserving the secrecy of opener's distribution whenever practical.

---

## Opponent Overcalls

Example

```
2♦*   (2♠)   ?
```

Responder evaluates:

- Combined strength.
- Likelihood of a heart or spade fit.
- Offensive values.
- Vulnerability.

Possible actions include:

- Pass.
- Double (partnership agreement).
- 2NT inquiry (if available).
- Raise after opener clarifies.
- Cue bid.

Partnerships should agree whether the normal relay structure remains available after interference.

---

## Opponent Doubles

Example

```
2♦*   (Double)   ?
```

Typical agreements include:

- Pass.
- Redouble showing values.
- 2♥ relay.
- Natural suit bids.
- 2NT inquiry.

Every partnership should define these treatments before play.

---

## Competition After the Relay

Example

```
2♦*
Pass
2♥
(3♣)
?
```

Opener should continue according to partnership agreement.

Both partners should reevaluate:

- Major-suit fit.
- Combined strength.
- Playing tricks.
- Offensive versus defensive values.
- Vulnerability.

---

## Feature Ask After Competition

If the partnership uses a **2NT Feature Ask**, interference may alter the available responses.

Possible agreements include:

- Continue the inquiry whenever possible.
- Replace conventional methods with natural bidding.
- Use doubles or cue bids to replace unavailable responses.

Because methods vary, these continuations should be discussed before play.

---

## Balancing and Reopening

If the opponents win the auction at a low level, either partner may have an opportunity to:

- Balance.
- Double.
- Compete further.
- Defend.

Judgment should be based primarily on:

- Playing tricks.
- Distribution.
- Vulnerability.
- Partnership style.

---

## General Principles

When opponents interfere after a Multi 2♦ opening:

- Continue describing the hand whenever practical.
- Preserve the uncertainty about opener's major suit if possible.
- Compete with offensive hands rather than defensive hands.
- Consider the Law of Total Tricks before sacrificing.
- Follow partnership agreements rather than improvising.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Opening Outside the Agreed Range

Multi promises a limited hand unless the partnership has agreed additional strong options.

---

## Forgetting the Relay Structure

Both partners must know the meanings of:

- 2♥
- 2NT
- Relay continuations

---

## Ignoring Vulnerability

Aggressive vulnerable preempts can become expensive.

---

## Mixing Different Versions

Many versions of Multi exist.

Always confirm which version the partnership is playing.

---

## Ignoring Competition

Interference often removes conventional responses.

Know your partnership's competitive methods.

---

# Tips

- Keep the convention as simple as possible.
- Practice relay auctions before adding advanced options.
- Evaluate playing tricks rather than HCP alone.
- Let vulnerability influence your style.
- Ensure both partners know the complete response structure.

---

# Summary

Multi 2♦ is one of the most effective artificial preemptive conventions in modern bridge. By concealing whether opener holds hearts or spades, it creates uncertainty for the opponents while allowing the partnership to compete constructively. Although many variations exist, the standard version shows a weak two in either major and relies on well-defined relay responses to clarify opener's hand. Successful use depends on disciplined partnership agreements, careful hand evaluation, and sound competitive judgment.
