# Invitational Bidding

> *Inviting partner to bid one level higher.*

Invitational bidding occupies the important middle ground between signing off
and forcing to game or slam. An invitational bid asks partner to evaluate the
combined strength of the partnership and decide whether to continue.

Successful invitational bidding requires sound hand evaluation, partnership
agreements, and practical judgment rather than simply counting high-card
points.

This section of **BridgeLab** covers every major invitational method, from
simple limit raises to advanced game tries and quantitative slam invitations.

---

# Introduction

An **invitational bid** expresses interest in bidding higher but leaves the
final decision to partner.

Partner is expected to

- accept,
- decline,
- occasionally improve the contract,

based upon

- strength,
- distribution,
- controls,
- fit,
- playing potential.

Invitations are among the most judgment-intensive areas of bridge.

---

# Objectives

Invitational bidding aims to

- reach close games,
- avoid poor games,
- investigate slam efficiently,
- exchange additional information,
- improve contract selection,
- maximize expected score.

# Hand Evaluation

Invitational decisions should consider

- High-card points
- Distribution
- Controls
- Fit quality
- Suit texture
- Working honors
- Playing tricks
- Vulnerability
- Scoring method

Modern bridge emphasizes **playing strength** over point count alone.

---

# Partnership Judgment

Partner should evaluate

- maximum versus minimum,
- fit,
- controls,
- offensive potential,
- duplication of values,
- location of honors.

The acceptance of an invitation is often more important than the invitation
itself.

---

# Matchpoints versus IMPs

## Matchpoints

- Thin games are less attractive.
- Accurate partscore decisions are critical.
- Overtricks have greater value.

---

## IMPs

- Accept close game invitations more readily.
- Vulnerable games are especially valuable.
- Thin slams require greater evidence.

# Common Mistakes

- Accepting every invitation.
- Declining with excellent playing strength.
- Counting unsupported honors equally.
- Ignoring distribution.
- Confusing invitational and forcing sequences.
- Evaluating only HCP.

Modern expert partnerships evaluate invitations using a combination of

- high-card strength,

This approach consistently outperforms simple point-count methods.
