# Help Suit Game Try (HSGT)

## Definition

A **Help Suit Game Try (HSGT)** is a **natural invitational bidding method** used after a major-suit fit has been established. The opener bids a new suit below the game level to ask responder whether they hold **helpful values** in that specific suit.

Unlike a simple invitation based primarily on high-card points, a Help Suit Game Try enables the partnership to evaluate **how well the two hands fit together**.

The underlying message is:

> **"I have enough extra strength to consider game if you can help me in this suit."**

Responder then evaluates whether their cards are useful in the trial suit before deciding whether to bid game or sign off.

---

# Purpose

The convention is designed to answer one question:

> **Do our combined hands have enough trick-taking potential for game?**

Rather than relying only on total HCP, the partnership evaluates:

- Working honors
- Suit texture
- Controls
- Distribution
- Shortness
- Trump quality
- Fitting values

The Help Suit Game Try is therefore an important application of **hand evaluation** rather than simple point counting.

---

# Historical Background

Early bridge methods relied almost exclusively on **high-card points** when deciding whether to invite game.

Experience showed that many apparently adequate games failed because honors were poorly placed, while many thin games succeeded because the partnership's cards fit exceptionally well.

To improve these decisions, expert players developed **natural game tries**, eventually leading to the widespread adoption of the **Help Suit Game Try**.

Today HSGT is the standard game-try method in many partnerships using:

- Standard American
- SAYC
- 2/1 Game Force
- ACBL Standard
- Numerous expert partnerships

Some systems replace or supplement HSGT with alternative methods, but it remains one of the most widely taught and frequently used game-invitation techniques.

---

# Objectives

The Help Suit Game Try seeks to:

- Reach sound games.
- Avoid poor games.
- Improve partnership hand evaluation.
- Identify fitting honors.
- Identify useful controls.
- Discover useful shortness.
- Exchange information economically.
- Stop safely below game when appropriate.

---

# Why "Help Suit"?

The convention asks responder for **help** in a specific suit.

Help may include:

- Ace
- King
- Queen
- Useful intermediates
- Extra length
- Singleton
- Void
- Doubleton opposite length

The opener is **not** asking responder whether they have extra points.

Instead, opener asks:

> **"Can your cards improve my suit?"**

---

# Basic Principle

Suppose opener bids:

```
1♠
2♠
3♦
```

The 3♦ bid does **not** necessarily promise long diamonds.

Instead it asks:

> "Do you have useful diamond values?"

If responder does, game becomes much more attractive.

---

# Philosophy

Modern bridge emphasizes:

> **Working values are worth more than raw values.**

Example

Opener

```
♠ AKQJ6
♥ AQ5
♦ J874
♣ K4
```

Responder A

```
♦ KQ2
```

Responder B

```
♣ QJ3
```

Although both responders may have identical HCP, responder A contributes significantly more because the honors are located where opener needs assistance.

The Help Suit Game Try allows the partnership to discover this difference.

# Advantages

The Help Suit Game Try offers several important benefits:

- Excellent hand evaluation.
- Natural bidding.
- Simple to learn.
- Efficient communication.
- Better game decisions.
- Easy integration with natural systems.
- Widely accepted by tournament players.
- Compatible with most major bidding systems.

For many partnerships, it is the preferred game-invitation method because it focuses on **fit**, not merely **high-card strength**.

---

# Disadvantages

The method also has limitations.

- Requires partnership agreement.
- Different partnerships evaluate "help" differently.
- Trial suit meaning may conflict with other conventions.
- Interference can complicate the auction.
- Does not identify every type of useful hand.

Nevertheless, it remains one of the most practical and effective methods for exploring game after a major-suit fit has been established.

# Basic Mechanism

Once a major-suit fit has been established, opener bids a **new suit below game**.

This bid is:

- Natural
- Invitational
- Non-forcing to game
- Artificial only in its purpose (asking for help)

The suit selected is called the **trial suit**.

Example

```
South      North

1♠
2♠
3♣
```

Meaning:

- Spades are agreed as trumps.
- Opener has extra values.
- Clubs are the trial suit.
- Responder evaluates club help before deciding whether to bid game.

---

# What Does the Trial Suit Mean?

There are two common partnership styles.

## Standard (Expert) Meaning

The trial suit is the suit in which opener **needs help**.

Example

```
♠ AKQJ7
♥ AQ64
♦ J85
♣ K3
```

Auction

```
1♠
2♠
3♦
```

The diamond suit is relatively weak.

Responder should examine their diamond holding.

---

## Alternative Style

Some partnerships treat the trial suit as opener's **long side suit**.

Example

```
♠ AKQJ7
♥ A2
♦ KQ875
♣ 74
```

Auction

```
1♠
2♠
3♦
```

Here opener shows long diamonds rather than weakness.

Because both treatments exist, partnerships **must agree** which style they play.

Throughout this document, **Help Suit Game Try refers to the standard expert treatment**, where the trial suit asks for assistance.

# Opener's Perspective

When selecting a trial suit, opener should ask:

- Where are my losers?
- Which suit needs partner's help?
- Which honors would improve this suit?
- Can partner realistically provide that help?

The bid should direct responder's attention to the partnership's greatest weakness.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Every partnership should define:

- Does the suit show weakness or length?
- Is the cheapest suit always used?
- Can opener bypass a suit?
- Are short-suit game tries played instead?
- What strength range justifies a game try?
- How should responder evaluate shortness?
- What constitutes sufficient "help"?

Clear agreements prevent misunderstandings and improve judgment.

---

# Common Misunderstandings

Many partnerships incorrectly assume:

- The bid shows a second suit.
- The bid promises length.
- The bid asks for points.
- Every Ace guarantees acceptance.

None of these assumptions is universally correct.

A Help Suit Game Try is primarily a request for **useful fitting values**, not simply additional high-card strength.

---

# Summary

The mechanics of the Help Suit Game Try are based on one fundamental question:

> **Can responder's cards improve opener's weakest suit enough to make game likely?**

The answer depends on:

- Working honors
- Trump quality
- Controls
- Distribution
- Fit
- Playing strength

rather than high-card points alone.

# Expert Recommendations

Experienced players rarely make a Help Suit Game Try based solely on
high-card points.

Instead, they consider:

- Playing strength
- Likely losers
- Trump quality
- Suit texture
- Controls
- Distribution
- Partnership style
- Vulnerability
- Form of scoring

A successful game try is based upon **expected tricks**, not merely points.

---

# Matchpoints versus IMPs

The value of accepting a game try depends partly on the scoring method.

## Matchpoints

At matchpoints:

- Thin games are less attractive.
- Overtricks often matter.
- Conservative judgment may be rewarded.

Example

```
Making 3♠ +2
```

may score better than

```
Down one in 4♠.
```

---

## IMPs

At IMP scoring:

- Making vulnerable games produces large gains.
- Thin but reasonable games are often worthwhile.
- Experts tend to be more aggressive.

---

# Vulnerability

Vulnerability affects judgment.

### Favorable Vulnerability

Game bonuses are larger.

Partnerships often stretch slightly.

---

### Unfavorable Vulnerability

Risk increases.

Marginal games should be avoided.

---

# Position at the Table

Help Suit Game Tries usually occur after a simple raise by responder.

However, the quality of the opening bid itself may depend upon:

- First seat
- Second seat
- Third seat
- Fourth seat

A light third-seat opening often reduces enthusiasm for inviting game.

# Influence of Opponents

When opponents compete,

- Maximal Game Try
- Cue Bid Game Try
- Artificial methods

Expert partnerships discuss these situations before play.

# Practical Judgment

Help Suit Game Tries are not mathematical.

conclusions.

Good judgment develops through

- experience
- partnership discussion
- post-mortem analysis
- comparison with expert auctions

# Frequently Asked Questions

## Does opener promise another suit?

No.

It is **not** a second suit in the normal descriptive sense.

## Must opener have extra values?

Yes.

A minimum opening should usually sign off.

---

## Does responder need extra points?

No.

Useful fitting cards often outweigh raw HCP.

---

## Is an Ace always enough?

Not necessarily.

The entire hand must be evaluated.

---

## Is a singleton useful?

Often yes.

Shortness may create additional trump tricks.

---

Absolutely.

---

## Can opener have a balanced hand?

Yes.

Others restrict them.

Agreement is essential.

---

## Does the trial suit always show weakness?

No.

Although this is the standard expert treatment,

some partnerships use

- two-way methods

instead.

No.

# Summary

The Help Suit Game Try is one of the most effective natural bidding tools
for determining whether a partnership should bid game after agreeing on a
major-suit fit.

Its success comes from recognizing that:

- honors are valuable only when they fit partner's hand,
- distribution often creates more tricks than high-card points,
- trump quality matters,
- controls matter,
- location of honors is frequently more important than their quantity.

Rather than asking

> **"How many points do you have?"**

the convention asks

> **"Can your cards improve my hand?"**

That simple change in perspective makes Help Suit Game Tries one of the
cornerstones of modern natural bidding.

---

# Key Points

- Help Suit Game Try (HSGT) is the standard natural game-invitation method used after a major-suit fit has been established.
- Opener bids a new suit below game asking responder for useful values in that suit.
- The trial suit usually represents opener's weakest or most problematic suit, depending on partnership agreement.
- Responder evaluates **working values**, not merely HCP.
- Honors located in the trial suit are especially valuable.
- Distribution, controls, trump quality, and double fits frequently justify accepting game.
- Soft honors and wasted values often justify declining.
- Partnership agreements should define the meaning of the trial suit, strength requirements, and methods after interference.
- Modern expert partnerships often combine HSGT with Maximal, Two-Way, or Artificial Game Try methods.
- HSGT remains one of the most widely used and effective game-invitation tools in natural bidding systems.
