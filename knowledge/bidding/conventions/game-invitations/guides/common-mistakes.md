# Common Mistakes in Game Try Bidding

## Purpose

Game Try conventions are only effective when both partners evaluate their hands
correctly and understand the partnership agreements.

Most failures occur **not because the convention is wrong**, but because one or
both players make incorrect assumptions.

This guide summarizes the mistakes most frequently encountered at every level
of play and explains how to avoid them.

---

# The Golden Rule

> **A Game Try asks partner to re-evaluate the hand.**

It does **not** ask partner simply to count High Card Points.

---

# Opener Mistakes

## 1. Making a Game Try with a Minimum Opening

Many players invite automatically with

- 13 HCP
- flat distribution
- poor trump suit

Example

```
♠ KQJ84
♥ J73
♦ K84
♣ Q52
```

This hand has little offensive potential.

Simply sign off.

---

## 2. Passing with Excellent Playing Strength

Some players fail to invite because they hold only

13–14 HCP.

Example

```
♠ AKQ964
♥ A4
♦ K74
♣ 72
```

Excellent suit quality and distribution justify a Game Try.

---

## 3. Counting Only High Card Points

HCP alone rarely determine whether game is correct.

Consider

- controls,
- trump quality,
- distribution,
- suit texture.

---

## 4. Choosing the Wrong Game Try

Examples

Using

Help Suit

when

Long Suit

better describes the hand.

Or

using

Long Suit

when

Short Suit

is the real source of tricks.

---

## 5. Ignoring Competition

After interference,

many natural Game Tries no longer apply.

Maximal Game Try often replaces them.

---

# Responder Mistakes

## 6. Accepting Automatically with Maximum HCP

Example

```
♠ Q84
♥ KJ74
♦ AQ4
♣ J84
```

These honors may fit poorly.

Maximum points do not always mean maximum playing strength.

---

## 7. Declining Automatically with Minimum HCP

Distribution may compensate for lower HCP.

Example

```
♠ J1095
♥ Q874
♦ 6
♣ K943
```

Useful shortness and trump support can justify game.

---

## 8. Ignoring Trump Quality

Excellent trumps

```
KJ10x
```

often matter more than

```
Qxxx
```

Trump quality should always influence the decision.

---

## 9. Ignoring Controls

Upgrade

- Aces,
- Kings,
- singleton,
- void.

These cards produce tricks.

---

## 10. Ignoring Distribution

Flat hands usually underperform.

Shape frequently produces extra tricks.

---

# Partnership Mistakes

## 11. Different Agreements

One partner believes

```
3♦
```

shows

Help Suit.

The other believes

Long Suit.

Disaster.

---

## 12. No Convention Card

Undocumented agreements inevitably produce misunderstandings.

---

## 13. Too Many Methods

Some partnerships attempt

- Help Suit,
- Long Suit,
- Short Suit,
- Two-Way,
- Artificial,
- Relay,

without sufficient practice.

A smaller set played accurately is usually stronger.

---

## 14. Optional Meanings

Never play

> "Sometimes natural."

> "Sometimes artificial."

Each bid should have one clear meaning.

---

# Evaluation Mistakes

## 15. Overvaluing Queens

Queens frequently produce defensive tricks,

not offensive tricks.

Upgrade

Aces

before

Queens.

---

## 16. Ignoring Working Values

A King supporting opener's suit

is far more valuable

than

a Queen opposite opener's singleton.

---

## 17. Ignoring Wasted Values

Example

Responder

```
♦AQ
```

Opener

singleton diamond.

Much of responder's strength is wasted.

---

## 18. Forgetting Source of Tricks

Ask

Where will ten tricks come from?

If no answer exists,

game is unlikely.

---

# Artificial Method Mistakes

## 19. Forgetting Relay Structure

Artificial methods depend entirely upon partnership memory.

One forgotten response destroys the auction.

---

## 20. Evaluating Before Information Is Complete

Artificial Game Tries

require patience.

Wait until opener has completed the agreed description.

---

## 21. Assuming One Standard Structure Exists

Artificial Game Tries

have many implementations.

Always follow

your partnership agreement.

---

# Maximal Game Try Mistakes

## 22. Using Maximal Without Competition

Maximal exists

because bidding space has been removed.

Without interference,

natural methods usually apply.

---

## 23. Forgetting Maximal Applies

After competition,

many partnerships accidentally continue using

Help Suit Game Tries.

This often creates ambiguity.

---

# Long Suit Game Try Mistakes

## 24. Showing a Weak Long Suit

The long suit should have

real trick-taking potential.

A poor six-card suit is rarely sufficient.

---

# Short Suit Game Try Mistakes

## 25. Forgetting Ruffing Values

Shortness alone does not create tricks.

It must combine with

- trump support,
- entries,
- offensive strength.

---

# Help Suit Game Try Mistakes

## 26. Asking for Help in the Wrong Suit

The requested suit should be

the partnership's problem,

not merely opener's longest side suit.

---

# Two-Way Game Try Mistakes

## 27. Forgetting Which Structure Is Played

Different partnerships assign

different meanings.

Review agreements regularly.

---

# System Mistakes

## 28. Mixing Treatments

Do not combine

SAYC,

2/1,

Precision,

and

Polish Club

agreements without explicit discussion.

---

# Tournament Mistakes

## 29. Failure to Disclose

Artificial agreements

must appear on the convention card.

---

## 30. Overcomplicating the System

Complexity should solve problems,

not create them.

---

# Checklist Before Every Game Try

Opener

✓ Extra playing strength?

✓ Offensive values?

✓ Correct Game Try?

---

Responder

✓ Trump quality?

✓ Controls?

✓ Distribution?

✓ Working values?

✓ Wasted honors?

---

Partnership

✓ Same agreements?

✓ Competition discussed?

✓ Convention card updated?

---

# The Ten Most Serious Errors

1. Counting only HCP.
2. Ignoring trump quality.
3. Ignoring controls.
4. Ignoring distribution.
5. Forgetting working values.
6. Choosing the wrong Game Try.
7. Forgetting partnership agreements.
8. Ignoring competition.
9. Playing methods beyond the partnership's experience.
10. Failing to re-evaluate after the Game Try.

---

# How Experts Avoid These Mistakes

Expert partnerships

- keep agreements simple,
- document everything,
- practice regularly,
- evaluate playing strength,
- review misunderstandings after sessions.

Consistency is more important than complexity.

---

# Memory Rules

Remember

> **Game Tries evaluate tricks—not points.**

> **Good trumps beat extra Queens.**

> **Distribution creates tricks.**

> **Controls win games.**

> **Partnership agreement always comes first.**

---

# Summary

Nearly every Game Try error can be traced to one of three causes:

- incorrect hand evaluation,
- unclear partnership agreements,
- misuse of the chosen convention.

Partnerships that consistently evaluate

- offensive strength,
- trump quality,
- controls,
- distribution,

and apply a single, well-understood Game Try structure will reach better game
contracts and avoid costly misunderstandings.
