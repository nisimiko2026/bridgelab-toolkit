# Game Try Comparison

## Purpose

Modern bridge offers numerous methods for inviting game after a major-suit fit
has been established.

Each method was developed to solve a particular bidding problem.

This guide compares the principal game-try methods, helping partnerships choose
the treatment that best matches their bidding system, experience level, and
partnership style.

Unlike the individual convention files, this document focuses on **comparison**
rather than detailed explanation.

---

# Evolution of Game Try Methods

The development of game-try bidding has progressed from simple natural methods
to highly sophisticated artificial structures.

```
Simple Invitation

↓

Help Suit Game Try

↓

Long Suit Game Try

↓

Short Suit Game Try

↓

Maximal Game Try

↓

Two-Way Game Try

↓

Artificial Game Tries

↓

Artificial 2NT Game Try
```

Each stage generally increases

- descriptive power,
- partnership accuracy,
- memory requirements.

---

# Primary Objective

Every Game Try attempts to answer the same question:

> **Should the partnership bid game?**

Different methods answer this question using different information.

Some evaluate

- weak suits,

others evaluate

- long suits,
- shortness,
- controls,
- offensive strength,
- relay information.

# Advantages

## Help Suit

Advantages

- Easy.
- Natural.
- Widely played.

Limitations

- Only evaluates one weak suit.

---

## Long Suit

Advantages

- Shows source of tricks.
- Natural evaluation.

Limitations

- Cannot describe shortness.

---

## Short Suit

Advantages

- Excellent for distributional hands.

Limitations

- Requires accurate evaluation of wasted values.

---

## Maximal

Advantages

- Solves competitive auctions.

Limitations

- Applies only after interference.

---

## Two-Way

Advantages

- Combines Long and Short Suit methods.

Limitations

- Partnership-specific agreements.

---

## Artificial

Advantages

- Maximum flexibility.

Limitations

- High memory load.

---

## Artificial 2NT

Advantages

- Preserves bidding space.
- Supports relay methods.
- Highly descriptive.

Limitations

- No standard continuation.

# Expert Recommendations

Most modern expert partnerships eventually play

- Maximal Game Try,
- Long Suit,
- Short Suit,

or

- Artificial 2NT.

Help Suit remains extremely popular because of its simplicity.

Artificial methods become worthwhile only when partnerships practice together
regularly.

---

# Common Misconceptions

- One method is not universally superior.
- More complexity does not guarantee better results.
- Every partnership does not play identical agreements.
- Artificial methods require written agreements.

# Summary

Every Game Try attempts to answer the same fundamental question:

> **Should we bid game?**

The methods differ only in **how** they obtain the information needed to answer
that question.

Simple partnerships often achieve excellent results with Help Suit or Long Suit
Game Tries.

Advanced partnerships benefit from Two-Way and Artificial methods.

The best choice is **not the most complicated method**, but the one that both
partners understand thoroughly and can apply consistently at the table.
