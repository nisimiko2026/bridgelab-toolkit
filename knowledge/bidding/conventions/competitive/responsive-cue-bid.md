# Responsive Cue Bid

## Overview

The **Responsive Cue Bid** is an advanced competitive bidding convention used
after **partner has made a takeout double** and **the opponents have raised
their own suit**.

It allows responder to distinguish stronger competitive hands from minimum
responses while searching for the correct contract, particularly a major-suit
fit.

Unlike a **Negative Cue Bid**, which occurs after partner opens and the
opponents interfere, the Responsive Cue Bid occurs **after partner has already
made a takeout double**.

Today it is considered a standard treatment among tournament partnerships.

---

# Objectives

Responsive Cue Bid enables the partnership to

- distinguish invitational from minimum responses,
- locate major-suit fits,
- compete accurately,
- preserve bidding space,
- investigate game,
- describe stronger hands without jumping.

---

# Basic Principle

After partner doubles for takeout and the opponents raise their suit,

a cue bid of the opponents' suit shows

- constructive or invitational values,
- interest in competing,
- usually both unbid suits,
- no clear natural bid.

The cue bid is **artificial**.

---

# Basic Auction

```
West     North     East     South

1♦        Double    2♦       3♦*
```

The cue bid of diamonds is artificial.

South does **not** promise diamonds.

Instead, South asks partner to further describe the hand.

# Why Use Responsive Cue Bid?

Without Responsive Cue Bid,

the responder often cannot distinguish

```
8 HCP

from

12 HCP
```

when holding support for both unbid suits.

The cue bid preserves this distinction.

---

# Typical Strength

Modern expert partnerships generally require

- approximately 10–12 HCP,
- or equivalent playing strength,
- invitational values,
- game interest opposite a maximum double.

Some partnerships stretch slightly favorable vulnerability.

- trump length,
- defensive strength,
- genuine penalty prospects.

# Modern Expert Treatments

Responsive Cue Bid is **not completely standardized**. Three principal expert
styles exist.

## Style 1 (Most Common)

After a **minor-suit opening**,

the cue bid shows

- both majors,
- invitational or better values.

---

## Style 2

After a **major-suit opening**,

the cue bid asks partner to further describe

- strength,
- preferred strain,
- game interest.

---

## Style 3

Some partnerships integrate Responsive Cue Bids into broader competitive
systems that include

- Responsive Doubles,
- Maximal Doubles,
- Support Doubles,
- Fit Jumps,

creating a unified competitive structure.

---

# Expert Partnership Agreements

Every partnership should define

- Minimum HCP or playing strength
- Support requirements
- Meaning after minor openings
- Meaning after major openings
- Forcing status

- Continuation structure

---

# Historical Perspective

Responsive Cue Bids evolved as partnerships recognized that simple suit bids
after a takeout double were insufficient to distinguish constructive hands
from invitational hands. The convention complements the Responsive Double by
giving responder another artificial tool for describing stronger competitive
hands while preserving bidding space for accurate game investigation.

---

# Summary

The **Responsive Cue Bid** is an advanced competitive convention used after
partner's takeout double and an opponent's raise. By cue bidding the opponents'
suit, responder shows **constructive or invitational values** and asks partner
to further clarify the best strain.

Widely used by tournament partnerships, Responsive Cue Bid works hand in hand
with **Responsive Doubles**, **Takeout Doubles**, and **Maximal Doubles** to
create a highly effective competitive bidding structure.
