# Fit Jump Shift

## Overview

**Fit Jump Shift (FJS)** is an advanced competitive bidding convention in which
a **jump shift** simultaneously shows

- support for partner's suit,
- a long side suit,
- offensive distribution,
- and constructive competitive values.

Unlike natural jump shifts, which primarily describe the bidder's own suit,
Fit Jump Shifts immediately communicate **two important pieces of information**:

- "We have a fit."
- "I have another long suit."

This dual-purpose description allows the partnership to compete accurately,
evaluate offensive potential, and make more informed game and slam decisions.

Fit Jump Shifts are particularly valuable in competitive auctions where bidding
space is limited.

---

# Objectives

The convention enables the partnership to

- identify trump support immediately,
- describe a second suit,
- distinguish constructive raises from preemptive raises,
- improve competitive judgment,
- evaluate offensive potential,
- facilitate game and slam investigation.

---

# Basic Principle

A Fit Jump Shift always promises

- support for partner's suit,
- a good side suit,
- constructive offensive values.

Example

```
West     North     East     South
          1♥        Pass
3♣*
```

The jump to **3♣** shows

- four-card or better heart support,
- a good club suit,
- constructive values.

The club suit is genuine, not merely a control.

---

# Why Use Fit Jump Shifts?

Without the convention

```
1♥
3♣
```

would normally show

- clubs,
- little information about heart support.

Using Fit Jump Shifts,

the partnership immediately discovers

- a heart fit,
- secondary source of tricks,
- offensive distribution.

---

# Requirements

Typical expert requirements

- four-card trump support,
- five-card or longer side suit,
- approximately 8–11 support points,
- offensive distribution,
- no game-forcing values.

Many partnerships evaluate

- playing tricks,
- Losing Trick Count,
- support points,

rather than HCP.

---

# Trump Support

Support normally requires

- four cards,

occasionally

- five cards.

Three-card support is uncommon.

---

# Side Suit Quality

The side suit should generally contain

- five or more cards,
- two of the top three honors,
- or three of the top five honors.

Typical examples

```
AKJ98

KQJ76

AQ1098
```

Poor suits should not qualify.

---

# Offensive Hand Types

Typical shapes include

```
5-5

5-4

6-4

4-5-2-2

5-4-3-1
```

Shortness increases offensive value.

# Hand Evaluation

Fit Jump Shifts are evaluated primarily using

- support points,
- playing tricks,
- Losing Trick Count,
- offensive controls.

Raw HCP is less important.

---

# Opener's Evaluation

Opener now knows

- an immediate fit exists,
- responder has offensive distribution,
- a side suit may provide additional tricks.

Opener evaluates

- wasted values,
- trump quality,
- controls,
- side-suit fit,
- vulnerability.

---

# Continuations

Possible continuations include

- Pass
- Compete further
- Invite game
- Bid game
- Cue bid
- Begin slam investigation

depending on combined strength.

---

# Competitive Advantages

Fit Jump Shifts

- consume bidding space,
- identify double fits,
- improve sacrifice decisions,
- clarify offensive strength.

Double fits frequently produce additional tricks.

---

# Double Fits

One of the strongest reasons to play Fit Jump Shifts is the discovery of
**double fits**.

Example

```
Responder

♠ KJ84
♥ AQ987
♦ 5
♣ KJ84
```

After

```
1♣
1♥
Pass
3♠*
```

Partner immediately knows

- club fit,
- spade fit,
- excellent offensive potential.

Double fits often justify competing one level higher than a single fit would
suggest.

---

# Law of Total Tricks

Fit Jump Shifts complement the

**Law of Total Tricks**.

Additional side-suit fit frequently increases

- offensive trick-taking,
- ruffing power,
- competitive safety.

Many partnerships allow Fit Jump Shifts by a passed hand.

These show

- offensive values,
- fit,
- limited strength.

```

(1♥)

```

```

(2♦)

```

where bidding room is limited.

# Expert Style

Modern expert partnerships generally use Fit Jump Shifts as

- constructive,
- non-forcing,
- highly descriptive.

The convention fills the gap between

- Mixed Raises,
- Splinters,
- Preemptive Raises.

---

# Fit Jump Shift vs Splinter

A common misunderstanding is that these conventions serve similar purposes.

A **Fit Jump Shift**

- shows a genuine side suit,
- expects tricks from that suit,
- does **not** promise shortness.

A **Splinter**

- shows singleton or void,
- does **not** promise length in the bid suit.

---

# Fit Jump Shift vs Mixed Raise

Mixed Raise

- support only,
- intermediate strength.

Fit Jump Shift

- support,
- side suit,
- offensive distribution.

---

# Fit Jump Shift vs Weak Jump Shift

Weak Jump Shift

- preemptive,
- own suit only.

Fit Jump Shift

- constructive,
- fit plus side suit.

---

# Opener's Rebid Priorities

After a Fit Jump Shift, opener should evaluate

1. Trump quality.
2. Fit with responder's side suit.
3. Controls.
4. Offensive potential.
5. Wasted honors.
6. Vulnerability.

The discovery of a double fit often justifies aggressive competition.

# Modern Expert Treatments

There is **no single standard** Fit Jump Shift structure. The most common expert
styles are:

### Standard Fit Jump Shift

- Constructive (about 8–11 support points)
- Four-card support
- Five-card side suit
- Non-forcing

### Mini Fit Jump Shift

Popularized by Eric Rodwell and others.

- Weaker range (approximately 5–9 support points)
- More preemptive
- Greater emphasis on distribution than HCP
- Frequently used in highly competitive auctions

### Strong Fit Jump Shift

Less common.

- Invitational or better
- Strong side suit
- Excellent offensive potential

Partnership agreement should specify which style is used.

---

# Modern Expert Perspective

Fit Jump Shifts have become one of the most valuable tools in modern
competitive bidding because they identify **double fits**, which are often more
important than raw high-card strength.

Expert partnerships evaluate these hands primarily by

- total trumps,
- playing tricks,
- controls,
- distribution,
- offensive potential,

rather than HCP alone.

The convention integrates naturally with **Mixed Raises**, **Splinters**,
**Bergen Raises**, and the **Law of Total Tricks**, providing a complete
framework for constructive and competitive raising methods.

---

# Summary

The **Fit Jump Shift** convention is an advanced competitive bidding treatment
that simultaneously shows **support for partner's suit** and a **strong side
suit**. By identifying two-suited offensive hands in a single bid, it enables
the partnership to evaluate double fits, compete more effectively, and make
more accurate game and slam decisions.

Although several expert styles exist—including Standard, Mini, and Strong Fit
Jump Shifts—the underlying principle is the same: **describe both the trump fit
and the secondary source of tricks as early and as economically as possible.**
