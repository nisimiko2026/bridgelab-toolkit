# Hamilton Convention

## Overview

The **Hamilton Convention** (also known as **Cappelletti-Hamilton** in some regions) is a defense against an opponent's **1NT opening**. It enables the partnership to enter the auction with both one-suited and two-suited hands while making it difficult for the opponents to reach their preferred contract.

Unlike natural overcalls, Hamilton uses **artificial two-level bids** to describe specific suit combinations.

Hamilton is popular because it is relatively easy to learn, highly descriptive, and effective against both weak and strong notrump openings.

---

# Purpose

Hamilton is designed to:

- Compete effectively over 1NT.
- Show two-suited hands economically.
- Locate major-suit fits.
- Increase preemptive pressure.
- Make life difficult for the notrump side.

---

# When to Use

Hamilton applies after:

```
(1NT) ?
```

The exact HCP requirements depend on:

- Vulnerability
- Seat position
- Strength of the opponents' notrump

Distribution and playing strength are usually more important than high-card points.

---

# Standard Structure

The most common Hamilton structure is:

| Bid | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Double | Strong balanced hand (penalty or equivalent) |
| 2♣ | Any one-suited hand |
| 2♦ | Both major suits |
| 2♥ | Hearts + a minor |
| 2♠ | Spades + a minor |
| 2NT | Both minor suits |

Some partnerships reverse or modify these meanings.

---

# The Double

```
(1NT)
Double
```

Usually shows:

- Approximately 15–18 HCP (depending on the strength of 1NT)
- Balanced hand
- Interest in defending

Some partnerships play the double as penalty only.

---

# 2♣ — One-Suited Hand

```
(1NT)
2♣*
```

Artificial.

Shows:

- Any single long suit (usually six or more cards).

Advancer normally bids:

```
2♦
```

as a relay.

Overcaller then passes or corrects to the actual suit.

Example:

```
1NT
2♣
Pass
2♦
Pass
2♥
```

Shows a long heart suit.

---

# 2♦ — Both Majors

```
(1NT)
2♦*
```

Shows:

- Hearts
- Spades

Usually at least:

- 5-4
- 5-5
- Occasionally good 4-4

Advancer selects the preferred major.

---

# 2♥ — Hearts and a Minor

```
(1NT)
2♥*
```

Shows:

- Hearts
- An unknown minor

Typically:

- 5-5
- Sometimes 5-4

Responder chooses:

- Hearts
- Clubs
- Diamonds

according to strength and fit.

---

# 2♠ — Spades and a Minor

```
(1NT)
2♠*
```

Shows:

- Spades
- An unknown minor

Again, usually at least 5-5.

---

# 2NT — Both Minors

```
(1NT)
2NT*
```

Shows:

- Clubs
- Diamonds

Usually 5-5 or better.

---

# Strength Requirements

Typical ranges:

### Minimum

- 8-10 HCP
- Good distribution

### Normal

- 10-14 HCP

### Strong Hands

Usually begin with the penalty double.

Playing tricks matter more than HCP.

---

# Suit Quality

Hamilton emphasizes:

- Long suits
- Playing strength
- Offensive potential

Excellent suits justify lighter overcalls.

---

# Advancer's Responsibilities

Advancer should evaluate:

- Suit fit
- Playing strength
- Distribution
- Vulnerability

Possible actions:

- Pass
- Correct
- Invite game
- Bid game
- Compete further

---

# Balancing

Many partnerships also use Hamilton in the balancing seat.

Example

```
1NT
Pass
Pass
?
```

The exact agreements should be discussed beforehand.

---

# Advantages

Hamilton:

- Shows two-suited hands efficiently.
- Makes it difficult for opponents.
- Locates major fits quickly.
- Provides constructive continuations.
- Works against weak and strong notrumps.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Requires partnership agreement.
- Gives up natural two-level overcalls.
- Has several regional variations.
- May be restricted in some competitions.

---

# Variations

Common variations include:

- Standard Hamilton
- Cappelletti-Hamilton
- Modified Hamilton
- Hamilton with transfer responses
- Hamilton over weak notrump only

Every partnership should agree on its methods.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should discuss:

- Meaning of the double.
- Minimum suit lengths.
- Relay structure after 2♣.
- Responses to two-suited bids.
- Balancing methods.
- Competitive continuations.

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

After Hamilton is introduced, the opponents frequently compete to recover the auction. Because your distribution has already been described, the partnership should continue naturally while preserving flexibility whenever possible.

---

## Opponent Doubles

Example

```
1NT
2♣*
(Double)
?
```

Advancer should follow partnership agreements.

Typical treatments include:

- Pass.
- Complete the relay.
- Redouble showing values.
- Natural bidding.

---

## Opponent Raises Notrump

Example

```
1NT
2♦*
3NT
?
```

The partnership should evaluate:

- Combined strength.
- Suit fit.
- Offensive potential.
- Vulnerability.

Sometimes defending is best; other times sacrificing is profitable.

---

## Competition After the Relay

Example

```
1NT
2♣*
Pass
2♦*
(Double)
?
```

The overcaller normally continues describing the suit if practical.

Both partners should reevaluate:

- Playing tricks.
- Distribution.
- Law of Total Tricks.
- Vulnerability.

---

## Balancing Auctions

Hamilton may also be used after:

```
1NT
Pass
Pass
?
```

Many partnerships adopt the same structure in balancing position.

---

## Partnership Agreements

Partners should discuss:

- Responses after doubles.
- Responses after overcalls.
- Relay interference.
- Penalty doubles.
- Balancing methods.
- Competitive raises.

---

## General Principles

When using Hamilton:

- Prioritize distribution over HCP.
- Compete with offensive hands.
- Let vulnerability influence aggressive actions.
- Continue describing your hand naturally.
- Follow partnership agreements consistently.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Forgetting the Artificial Meanings

Hamilton bids are rarely natural.

---

## Using Weak Suits

Distribution alone is insufficient.

Suit quality still matters.

---

## Forgetting the 2♣ Relay

Advancer should normally bid:

```
2♦
```

unless holding exceptional values or a special agreement.

---

## Overcompeting

Not every Hamilton sequence should continue to game.

Reevaluate after every competitive action.

---

## Ignoring Partnership Agreements

Many partnerships play different versions.

Always agree on:

- Double.
- 2♣ relay.
- Two-suited meanings.
- Continuations.

---

# Tips

- Learn the entire structure—not just the first bid.
- Practice the 2♣ relay auctions.
- Use Hamilton aggressively only with suitable distribution.
- Let vulnerability influence your style.
- Remember that the convention is designed to disturb the opponents while helping your own partnership find its best fit.

---

# Summary

The Hamilton Convention is one of the most popular defenses against a 1NT opening. By using artificial two-level bids to describe one-suited and two-suited hands, it allows partnerships to compete efficiently while making it difficult for the opponents to judge the auction. Its combination of simplicity, flexibility, and constructive follow-ups has made it a standard part of many modern bidding systems. Successful use depends on clear partnership agreements, disciplined hand evaluation, and accurate competitive judgment.
