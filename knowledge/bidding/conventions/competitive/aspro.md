# Aspro Convention

## Overview

**Aspro** is a conventional defense against an opponent's **1NT opening**. Developed in the United Kingdom, it is particularly popular in Europe and Commonwealth countries.

Unlike Landy or Cappelletti, Aspro emphasizes **finding a major-suit fit as quickly as possible**. Most versions begin by identifying whether the overcaller holds:

- Hearts
- Spades
- Both majors
- A major together with a minor

The convention is especially effective against strong notrump openings because it frequently uncovers a playable major-suit contract while disrupting the opponents' communication.

There are several published versions of Aspro. The structure below is the **most common modern version**.

---

# Purpose

Aspro is designed to:

- Compete aggressively over 1NT.
- Find major-suit fits quickly.
- Show two-suited hands efficiently.
- Preempt the opponents.
- Preserve bidding space.
- Improve competitive accuracy.

---

# When to Use

Aspro applies after:

```
(1NT)
?
```

It may be used against:

- Strong Notrump
- Weak Notrump

Many partnerships become more aggressive against strong notrump ranges.

---

# Standard Aspro Structure

A commonly played version is:

| Bid | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Double | Penalty (or strong balanced hand) |
| 2♣ | Hearts and another suit |
| 2♦ | Spades and another suit |
| 2♥ | Natural Hearts |
| 2♠ | Natural Spades |
| 2NT | Both minors |

Some partnerships modify the meanings of 2♥ and 2♠.

---

# Double

```
(1NT)
Double
```

Usually shows:

- Penalty interest
- Balanced hand
- Approximately 15–18 HCP

Against weak notrump it is often purely penalty.

---

# 2♣ — Hearts Plus Another Suit

```
(1NT)
2♣*
```

Shows:

- Hearts
- Another unspecified suit

Usually:

- 5-4
- 5-5

Responder normally bids:

```
2♦
```

as a relay.

Overcaller then:

- Passes (diamonds)
- Bids hearts
- Bids clubs
- Bids spades

depending upon the second suit.

---

## Example

```
West   North

1NT    2♣*
Pass   2♦
Pass   2♥
```

Shows:

- Hearts
- Clubs

(or hearts plus another suit depending on partnership agreement).

# Natural Major Overcalls

Many partnerships retain:

```
2♥
```

Natural:

- Six-card heart suit.

```
2♠
```

Natural:

- Six-card spade suit.

Other partnerships use these bids as artificial.

Agreement is essential.

---

# 2NT

Usually shows:

- Clubs
- Diamonds

Normally:

- 5-5

---

# Strength Requirements

Typical values:

Against Strong NT:

- 8–14 HCP

Against Weak NT:

- 10–15 HCP

Distribution is considerably more important than HCP.

---

# Suit Requirements

Recommended:

- At least 5-4.
- Preferably 5-5.
- Good suit quality.

Natural overcalls generally promise:

- Six-card suit.

---

# Responses by Advancer

Advancer evaluates:

- Fit.
- Playing strength.
- Distribution.
- Vulnerability.

Possible actions include:

- Relay.
- Pass.
- Preference.
- Invite.
- Game.

---

# Relay Responses

After:

```
1NT
2♣*
```

Responder usually bids:

```
2♦
```

asking overcaller to clarify.

Similarly:

```
1NT
2♦*
```

Responder usually bids:

```
2♥
```

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should discuss:

- Exact relay structure.
- Minimum suit length.
- Meaning of Double.
- Natural or artificial 2♥/2♠.
- Balancing methods.
- Responses after interference.

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

Once Aspro enters the auction, bidding generally continues naturally after the overcaller has clarified the distribution.

---

## Opponent Doubles

Example

```
1NT
2♣*
(Double)
?
```

Typical agreements include:

- Pass.
- Redouble.
- Relay.
- Natural preference.

---

## Opponent Competes to Game

Example

```
1NT
2♦*
3NT
?
```

Evaluate:

- Playing strength.
- Fit.
- Vulnerability.
- Sacrifice versus defense.

---

## Balancing Position

Some partnerships extend Aspro into balancing seat.

Others use natural balancing methods instead.

Agreement is required.

---

## Slam Investigation

Aspro is almost entirely a competitive convention.

If a major fit and unexpected strength are discovered, slam investigation proceeds naturally using:

- Cue Bids.
- Control Bids.
- Roman Key Card Blackwood.

# Advantages

Aspro:

- Quickly identifies major fits.
- Excellent against strong NT.
- Good preemptive effect.
- Flexible.
- Popular in Europe.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Requires partnership agreements.
- Several incompatible versions exist.
- Relay auctions require practice.
- Less common outside Europe.

---

# Variations

Published versions include:

- Standard Aspro
- Aspro Two
- Extended Aspro
- Reverse Aspro
- Modified Aspro

Each partnership should define:

- Double.
- Relay responses.
- Natural major bids.
- Minor-suit continuations.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Forgetting the Relay

The relay after 2♣ and 2♦ is fundamental.

---

## Confusing Aspro with Cappelletti

Although both defend against 1NT, the structures differ significantly.

---

## Ignoring Partnership Agreements

Numerous incompatible Aspro structures exist.

Always agree on the exact version.

---

## Showing Weak Suits

Distribution is important, but suit quality remains essential.

---

## Forgetting Vulnerability

Aggressive actions should always reflect vulnerability.

---

# Tips

- Learn Landy before Aspro.
- Memorize the relay sequences.
- Agree on one published version.
- Practice continuation auctions.
- Focus on locating major-suit fits quickly.

---

# Modern Usage

Aspro remains one of the most popular notrump defenses in the United Kingdom, Australia, New Zealand, and many European bridge federations.

Although less common in North America than DONT or Cappelletti, it is respected for its efficient handling of major-suit two-suiters and its ability to disrupt strong notrump auctions.

---

# Summary

The Aspro Convention is an advanced defense against a 1NT opening that emphasizes rapid identification of major-suit fits through artificial relay bids. By showing hearts or spades together with another suit, Aspro enables partnerships to compete accurately while putting immediate pressure on the opponents. Although several published versions exist, the convention remains a highly effective and popular defensive method for experienced partnerships, particularly in European bridge.
