# Unusual Notrump

## Overview

The **Unusual Notrump (Unusual 2NT)** is one of the cornerstone conventions of competitive bidding. Instead of showing a balanced hand, an immediate **2NT overcall** is **artificial**, showing the **two lowest unbid suits**.

Together with the **Michaels Cue Bid**, the Unusual Notrump allows a partnership to describe virtually every two-suited hand immediately after an opponent's opening bid.

The convention is played by almost every tournament partnership worldwide.

---

# Purpose

The Unusual Notrump is designed to:

- Show two-suited hands.
- Compete aggressively.
- Identify fits quickly.
- Consume bidding space.
- Increase preemptive pressure.
- Describe distribution immediately.

---

# When to Use

The convention is used after an opponent opens:

```
(1♣)
2NT*
```

```
(1♦)
2NT*
```

```
(1♥)
2NT*
```

```
(1♠)
2NT*
```

The **2NT** bid is artificial.

---

# Basic Principle

The bid always shows:

> **The two lowest unbid suits.**

# Strength Requirements

Most partnerships use:

### Weak Style

- 6–11 HCP
- Excellent distribution

### Intermediate Style

- 8–14 HCP

### Strong Style

Very strong two-suiters.

Modern partnerships emphasize:

- Playing tricks.
- Distribution.
- Vulnerability.

---

# Distribution Requirements

Usually:

- 5-5

Some partnerships permit:

- Excellent 5-4.

Most experts recommend genuine 5-5 shape.

---

# Suit Quality

Preferred:

- Two of top three honors.
- Three of top five honors.

Long weak suits should be avoided when vulnerable.

---

# Why Use Unusual Notrump?

Suppose the opponents open:

```
1♠
```

You hold:

```
♠ 5
♥ AJ982
♦ KQJ83
♣ 74
```

Natural bidding cannot describe both suits.

```
2NT*
```

Immediately shows:

- Hearts
- Diamonds.

# Unusual Notrump and Michaels

These conventions complement each other perfectly.

| Michaels Cue Bid | Unusual Notrump |
|------------------|-----------------|
| Cue bid | 2NT |
| Shows both majors or major + minor | Shows lowest two unbid suits |
| Uses opponent's suit | Uses 2NT |

Together they describe nearly every two-suited hand.

---

# Unusual vs Unusual

When both partnerships play Unusual Notrump, many partnerships use **Unusual vs Unusual (UvU)**.

General principles:

- A cue bid of the opponents' **artificially shown lower suit** typically shows support for the **lower** of partner's suits.
- A cue bid of the **higher shown suit** shows support for the **higher** suit.
- Double often shows general values.

Several published versions exist.

# Advantages

Unusual Notrump:

- Describes two suits immediately.
- Excellent competitive tool.
- Highly preemptive.
- Easy to remember.
- Standard worldwide.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Reveals both suits.
- Requires genuine distribution.
- Can force the partnership to the three level.
- May help opponents judge correctly.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Forgetting "Lowest Two Unbid"

This is the defining rule.

---

## Using the Convention with 5-3 Shape

Most partnerships require:

- At least 5-5.

---

## Confusing It with Natural 2NT

An immediate **2NT overcall** is almost never natural.

---

## Forgetting Vulnerability

Adjust aggressiveness according to vulnerability.

---

## Ignoring Michaels

The two conventions should always be learned together.

# Tips

- Memorize the phrase:

> **Lowest Two Unbid Suits**

- Learn Michaels and Unusual Notrump as a pair.
- Prefer genuine 5-5 distribution.
- Practice balancing-seat auctions.
- Agree on your style before tournament play.

# Modern Usage

Unusual Notrump is one of the most widely played competitive conventions in the world.

It is standard in:

- Standard American
- 2/1 Game Force
- SAYC
- Precision
- Acol
- Polish Club

Nearly every expert partnership plays **Michaels Cue Bid** together with **Unusual Notrump**, creating a complete system for describing two-suited overcalls.

---

# Summary

The Unusual Notrump convention uses an artificial **2NT overcall** to show the **two lowest unbid suits**, allowing partnerships to describe distributional hands immediately after an opponent's opening bid. Together with the Michaels Cue Bid, it forms the foundation of modern competitive bidding, enabling partnerships to locate fits quickly, compete aggressively, and disrupt the opponents' constructive auctions. Its logical structure, ease of use, and worldwide acceptance make it one of the essential conventions for tournament bridge.
