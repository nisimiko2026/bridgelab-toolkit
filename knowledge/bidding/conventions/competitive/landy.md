# Landy Convention

## Overview

The **Landy Convention** is one of the oldest and simplest defenses against an opponent's **1NT opening**. Developed by Alvin Landy in the 1940s, it remains popular because of its simplicity and effectiveness.

The convention uses:

```
(1NT) 2♣*
```

to show **both major suits**.

Unlike more elaborate defenses such as Hamilton, Cappelletti, or DONT, Landy focuses solely on identifying major-suit fits quickly while consuming minimal memory.

---

# Purpose

Landy is designed to:

- Show both major suits.
- Compete immediately over 1NT.
- Locate an eight-card major-suit fit.
- Disrupt the opponents' notrump auction.
- Make the opening lead more difficult for the opponents.

---

# When to Use

Landy applies after:

```
(1NT) ?
```

It is commonly played against:

- Strong Notrump
- Weak Notrump

Some partnerships use different styles depending on the opponents' notrump range.

---

# Standard Structure

The traditional Landy structure is:

| Bid | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Double | Penalty (or strong balanced hand by agreement) |
| 2♣ | Both major suits |
| 2♦ | Natural |
| 2♥ | Natural |
| 2♠ | Natural |
| 2NT | Natural or minors (partnership agreement) |

Unlike Hamilton or Cappelletti, only **2♣** is artificial.

---

# 2♣ — Both Majors

```
(1NT)
2♣*
```

Shows:

- Hearts
- Spades

Typically:

- 5-4
- 5-5
- Occasionally strong 4-4

The partnership agreement determines the minimum shape.

Artificial relay.

Used when advancer has equal support for both majors.

Natural.

Natural.

## 2NT

Natural invitation in many partnerships.

Some partnerships assign an artificial meaning.

## Jump Raises

Show strong support and game interest.

# Opener's Rebids

After partner selects a major, overcaller normally:

- Passes.
- Invites game.
- Accepts game invitation.
- Continues naturally.

The convention usually identifies the trump suit immediately.

---

# Advantages

Landy:

- Extremely easy to remember.
- Quickly locates major fits.
- Leaves natural meaning for other bids.
- Works against both weak and strong notrump.
- Has very few partnership misunderstandings.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Shows only both major suits.
- Does not describe minor two-suiters.
- Is less flexible than Hamilton or Cappelletti.
- Gives up the natural 2♣ overcall.

---

# Variations

Common variations include:

- Standard Landy.
- Extended Landy.
- Landy with relay responses.
- Landy over Weak Notrump only.
- Landy combined with Multi-Landy methods.

Every partnership should define its preferred structure.

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

Once Landy has identified both major suits, the partnership has an excellent foundation for competing. The remaining decisions depend on strength, fit, vulnerability, and the opponents' actions.

---

## Opponent Doubles

Example

```
1NT
2♣*
(Double)
?
```

Typical partnership agreements include:

- Pass.
- Redouble showing strength.
- Natural major-suit bids.
- Transfer-style escapes.

Every partnership should define these continuations before play.

---

## Opponent Bids 2NT or 3NT

Example

```
1NT
2♣*
3NT
?
```

The partnership evaluates:

- Combined strength.
- Major-suit fit.
- Offensive potential.
- Vulnerability.

Possible actions:

- Pass.
- Compete in a major.
- Double (if appropriate).

---

## Competition After the Major Is Chosen

Example

```
1NT
2♣*
Pass
2♥
3♦
?
```

Both partners reevaluate:

- Combined strength.
- Playing tricks.
- Distribution.
- Law of Total Tricks.
- Vulnerability.

---

## Balancing Auctions

Landy is often equally effective in balancing position.

Example


---

## Partnership Agreements

Partners should discuss:

- Responses after doubles.
- Relay interference.
- Responses after 2NT.
- Penalty doubles.
- Balancing methods.
- Competitive raises.

---

## General Principles

When using Landy:

- Prioritize finding the best major fit.
- Let distribution outweigh HCP.
- Compete aggressively only with suitable playing strength.
- Reevaluate after every competitive bid.
- Follow partnership agreements consistently.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Showing Only One Major

Landy always promises **both majors**.

---

## Using Weak Four-Card Suits

Most partnerships require at least:

- 5-4

Many require:

- 5-5

---

## Forgetting the Relay

If playing the 2♦ relay, both partners must know how opener identifies the longer major.

---

## Overcompeting

Finding a fit does not automatically justify bidding another level.

---

## Ignoring Vulnerability

Distribution is important, but vulnerability should influence aggressive decisions.

---

# Tips

- Think of Landy as the simplest two-suited defense against 1NT.
- Agree whether 2♦ is a relay or natural.
- Discuss the minimum shape before playing.
- Practice balancing auctions as well as direct overcalls.
- Remember that the primary objective is to locate the best major-suit fit quickly.

---

# Summary

The Landy Convention is one of the classic defenses against a 1NT opening. By using an artificial **2♣** overcall to show both major suits, it allows partnerships to compete efficiently, locate major-suit fits, and disrupt the opponents' notrump auction. Its simplicity has made it one of the most enduring conventions in bridge, particularly for partnerships that prefer straightforward methods over more complex notrump defenses.
