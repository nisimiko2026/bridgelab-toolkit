# Negative Free Bid (NFB)

## Overview

The **Negative Free Bid (NFB)** is an advanced competitive bidding treatment
used after an opponent overcalls partner's opening bid.

Under standard methods, a new suit bid by responder after interference is
usually **non-forcing**, making it difficult to distinguish between

- weak competitive hands,
- constructive hands,
- invitational hands,
- and game-forcing hands.

The Negative Free Bid convention solves this problem by assigning a
**constructive, invitational meaning** to a free bid at the two level,
allowing the partnership to preserve forcing auctions for stronger hands.

Today, Negative Free Bids are widely played by expert partnerships using
**Two-over-One Game Force** and modern natural systems.

---

# Objectives

Negative Free Bid enables the partnership to

- distinguish constructive from forcing hands,
- compete safely,
- preserve game-forforcing sequences,
- improve competitive judgment,
- accurately evaluate part-score and game contracts,
- reduce ambiguity after interference.

---

# The Problem

Consider the auction

```
West     North     East     South

          1♦        1♠
2♣
```

Without agreement,

2♣ might mean

- weak,
- invitational,
- forcing,
- competitive.

The partnership has no precise definition.

Negative Free Bid assigns a specific range.

---

# Basic Principle

After an opponent overcalls,

a new suit bid at the two level shows

- constructive values,
- approximately 8–11 HCP,
- a good five-card or longer suit,
- no game force.

The bid is **not forcing to game**.

---

# Why "Negative"?

The term **Negative** refers to the fact that responder **denies sufficient
strength for a game-forcing action**.

Instead,

the bid shows a constructive but limited hand.

---

# Typical Requirements

Expert partnerships generally require

- 8–11 HCP,
- five-card suit,
- reasonable suit quality,
- constructive values,
- no support for opener's suit,
- no suitable negative double.

Distribution matters more than raw HCP.

---

# Suit Quality

The suit should normally contain

- five cards,
- two of the top three honors,
- or three of the top five honors.

Examples

```
AQJ98

KQJ76

AJ1098
```

Poor suits should not qualify.

---

# Hand Evaluation

Negative Free Bids are evaluated using

- HCP,
- playing tricks,
- Losing Trick Count,
- vulnerability,
- offensive potential.

---

# When to Use Negative Free Bid

Responder chooses a Negative Free Bid when

- holding a good suit,
- lacking support,
- lacking game-forcing strength,
- unable to make a negative double.

---

# When NOT to Use It

Do not use a Negative Free Bid with

- game-forcing values,
- weak competitive hands,
- support for opener,
- balanced invitational hands,
- penalty-oriented hands.

---

# Opener's Responsibilities

After a Negative Free Bid,

opener should evaluate

- fit,
- controls,
- maximum versus minimum,
- vulnerability,
- offensive potential.

Possible actions include

- pass,
- raise,
- rebid naturally,
- invite game,
- bid game.

# Relationship to Negative Double

Negative Double

- shows unbid majors,
- flexible shape.

Negative Free Bid

- promises a real suit,
- constructive values,
- no suitable negative double.

---

# Relationship to Two-over-One

Without interference

```
1♥
2♦
```

may be game forcing in Two-over-One.

After interference

```
1♥
(1♠)
2♦*
```

becomes

constructive,

not game forcing.

This distinction is one of the primary reasons experts play Negative Free Bids.

# Modern Expert Treatments

There are **two principal expert approaches**.

## Standard Treatment

A free bid at the two level shows

- constructive values,
- approximately 8–11 HCP,
- good five-card suit,
- non-forcing.

---

## Two-over-One Treatment

Many expert partnerships redefine

```
1♥
(1♠)
2♦
```

as

- constructive,
- invitational,
- approximately 9–11 HCP.

Game-forcing hands instead use

- cue bids,
- support doubles,
- forcing notrump,
- or later forcing sequences.

This treatment preserves the integrity of the Two-over-One Game Force system.

---

# Expert Partnership Agreements

A partnership should define

- Exact strength range
- Minimum suit quality
- Five-card versus six-card suit
- Treatment after weak jump overcalls
- Treatment after two-level overcalls
- Opener's rebids
- Whether cue bids establish a game force

# Historical Perspective

Negative Free Bids developed alongside modern **Two-over-One Game Force**
methods. As interference became more frequent in tournament bridge, players
needed a way to distinguish constructive hands from game-forcing hands after an
overcall. The convention has become a popular expert treatment because it
restores much of the descriptive accuracy lost when opponents enter the auction.

---

# Summary

The **Negative Free Bid** is an advanced competitive convention that assigns a
constructive, non-game-forcing meaning to a natural free bid after an opponent's
overcall. By clearly separating constructive hands from stronger forcing
actions, it improves competitive judgment, preserves the structure of
Two-over-One Game Force, and enables partnerships to compete more accurately in
interference auctions.

Although not part of standard beginner systems, Negative Free Bids are widely
used by expert partnerships and are considered one of the most valuable modern
treatments for auctions disturbed by opponent interference.
