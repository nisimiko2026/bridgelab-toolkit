# Ghestem Convention

## Overview

**Ghestem** is one of the most comprehensive two-suited overcall conventions in bridge. Its name comes from the initials of its inventor **Pierre Ghestem**, one of France's greatest bridge players.

Unlike Michaels Cue Bid and Unusual Notrump, which together describe most two-suited hands, **Ghestem uses three artificial bids to show all three possible two-suit combinations** after every opening bid.

The convention provides complete coverage of every 5-5 (or better) two-suited hand while allowing responder to identify the exact suits immediately.

Because of its complexity, Ghestem is primarily played by experienced tournament partnerships.

---

# Purpose

Ghestem is designed to:

- Show every possible two-suited hand.
- Identify both suits immediately.
- Compete aggressively.
- Increase preemptive pressure.
- Locate major-suit fits.
- Eliminate ambiguity found in Michaels.

---

# Basic Principle

After any one-level opening, Ghestem assigns three artificial bids:

- Cue Bid
- 2NT
- 3♣

Each bid represents one of the three possible two-suit combinations.

---

# Standard Ghestem Structure

The most widely played modern version uses:

| Bid | Shows |
|------|-------|
| Cue Bid | Highest + Lowest unbid suits |
| 2NT | Lowest two unbid suits |
| 3♣ | Highest two unbid suits |

This simple memory rule works after every opening bid.

---

# Memory Rule

Think:

**Cue = High + Low**

**2NT = Low + Low**

**3♣ = High + High**

Unbid suits:

- ♦

| Bid | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| 2♣* | ♥ + ♦ |
| 2NT* | ♦ + ♥ |
| 3♣* | ♥ + ♠ |

Notice:

The Cue Bid and 2NT appear similar because only three suits remain.

The partnership agreement determines which pair is assigned to each bid.

Normally:

- At least 5-5

Some partnerships allow:

- Excellent 5-4

Most experts recommend genuine 5-5 shape.

---

# Strength Requirements

Typical style:

Minimum:

- 8 HCP

Normal:

- 8–14 HCP

Very strong two-suited hands are also suitable.

Distribution is considerably more important than HCP.

---

# Why Play Ghestem?

Without Ghestem:

Some hands cannot be described immediately.

Example:

```
♠ AQJ97
♥ —
♦ KQJ84
♣ 9832
```

After:

```
1♥
?
```

Neither Michaels nor Unusual Notrump shows:

- Spades
- Diamonds

Ghestem does.

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

Ghestem is itself a competitive convention.

After the artificial overcall, bidding generally continues naturally.

---

Example

```

3♠

```

- Playing tricks.
- Vulnerability.
- Fit.

Possible actions:

- Compete.
- Sacrifice.
- Defend.

# Ghestem vs Michaels

| Ghestem | Michaels |
|----------|----------|
| Shows every two-suited hand | Shows selected two-suited hands |
| More artificial | Simpler |
| More memory | Easier to learn |

---

# Ghestem vs Unusual Notrump

| Ghestem | Unusual NT |
|----------|------------|
| Three artificial bids | One artificial bid |
| Complete coverage | Lowest two suits only |
| Expert system | Standard convention |

# Advantages

Ghestem:

- Covers every two-suited hand.
- Extremely descriptive.
- Excellent competitive tool.
- Strong preemptive effect.
- Very accurate.

# Limitations

The convention:

- Difficult to remember.
- Requires detailed partnership agreements.
- Several published versions exist.
- Rare among casual partnerships.

# Common Mistakes

## Forgetting Which Two Suits Are Shown

Always identify:

- Lowest suit.
- Highest suit.

before bidding.

---

## Confusing Ghestem with Michaels

Michaels shows only selected combinations.

Ghestem covers every combination.

---

## Using Weak Distribution

Prefer genuine 5-5 shape.

---

## Ignoring Vulnerability

Aggressiveness should depend upon vulnerability.

---

## Forgetting Partnership Style

Different books assign different meanings.

Always review agreements.

# Tips

- Learn Michaels and Unusual Notrump before Ghestem.
- Memorize one version only.
- Practice all four opening bids.
- Focus on identifying the two unbid suits.
- Carry a convention card.

---

# Modern Usage

Ghestem is primarily played by advanced tournament partnerships seeking complete coverage of every two-suited overcall. While less common than Michaels and Unusual Notrump because of its greater complexity, it offers unmatched descriptive accuracy for partnerships willing to invest in detailed agreements.

Many expert partnerships continue to prefer the simpler **Michaels + Unusual Notrump** combination, while others adopt Ghestem because it can identify all six possible two-suited combinations immediately.

---

# Summary

The Ghestem Convention is an expert-level two-suited overcall system that uses three artificial bids—the cue bid, **2NT**, and **3♣**—to identify every possible two-suited hand after an opponent's opening bid. By eliminating the ambiguity present in simpler methods such as Michaels Cue Bid, Ghestem allows partnerships to compete with exceptional precision. Although its complexity limits its popularity, it remains one of the most complete and sophisticated competitive conventions in modern bridge.
