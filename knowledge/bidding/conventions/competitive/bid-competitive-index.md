# Competitive Bidding

> *Winning the auction when both partnerships are bidding.*

Competitive bidding begins when both partnerships participate in the auction.
Unlike constructive bidding, where partners exchange information without
interference, competitive auctions require balancing offensive opportunities
against defensive risks while continuously evaluating the opponents' likely
contract.

Modern competitive bidding combines sound judgment, partnership agreements,
distributional evaluation, and well-defined conventional methods to compete
effectively without excessive risk.

---

# Introduction

The objectives of competitive bidding are to

- compete for the best contract,
- obstruct the opponents,
- locate the correct strain,
- identify profitable sacrifices,
- improve defensive decisions,
- exchange information despite interference,
- maximize the partnership's expected score.

Successful competitive bidding depends upon both partnership agreements and
practical judgment.

# Fundamental Principles

Successful competitive bidding is based upon

- offensive potential,
- defensive potential,
- distribution,
- fit,
- vulnerability,
- level of the auction,
- partnership style,
- scoring method.

No single factor determines every decision.

---

# Hand Evaluation

Competitive auctions emphasize

- playing strength,
- trump fit,
- suit quality,
- controls,
- distribution,

often more than raw high-card points.

---

# Vulnerability

Vulnerability strongly influences competitive decisions.

### Favorable Vulnerability

- Compete aggressively.
- Sacrifice more frequently.
- Stretch distributional actions.

### Equal Vulnerability

- Follow normal partnership style.

### Unfavorable Vulnerability

- Require better suits.
- Penalize speculative sacrifices.
- Defend more often.

---

# Matchpoints versus IMPs

## Matchpoints

- Fight for every partscore.
- Compete aggressively.
- Thin games are less attractive.
- Small gains matter.

---

## IMPs

- Bid vulnerable games aggressively.
- Avoid expensive penalties.
- Exercise greater discipline in sacrifices.

# Common Mistakes

- Competing with poor suit quality.
- Ignoring vulnerability.
- Overvaluing high-card points.
- Misjudging offensive versus defensive strength.
- Confusing penalty and takeout doubles.
- Playing conventions without complete agreements.

conventions, flexible doubles, and judgment-based agreements rather than
