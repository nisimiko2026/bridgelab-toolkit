# Control Bidding

## Bridge Insight

**Control Bidding is a cooperative slam-bidding method in which partners exchange information about first- and second-round controls. Rather than asking specific questions, each partner voluntarily shows controls, allowing the partnership to determine whether slam is safe while identifying any uncontrolled suits.**

---

# Overview

**Control Bidding**, often called **Cue Bidding**, is a slam investigation method used after the partnership has agreed a trump suit.

Instead of asking about aces or key cards, partners take turns showing controls by bidding suits in which they hold:

- First-round control (Ace or void)
- Second-round control (King or singleton), depending on partnership agreement

The primary objective is to determine whether every suit is adequately controlled before committing to slam.

---

# Objectives

Control Bidding enables the partnership to:

- Exchange control information naturally.
- Detect missing controls.
- Investigate slams below game level.
- Preserve bidding space.
- Complement RKCB or Blackwood.

---

# When to Use

After:

- A trump suit has been agreed.
- Both partners have slam interest.

Typical auction:

```text
1♠      3♠

4♣
```

4♣ is **not natural**.

It shows a Club control.

---

Another example:

```text
1♦      3♦

4♣

4♥

4NT
```

Partners exchange controls before using RKCB.

---

# Requirements

Before beginning Control Bidding:

- Trump suit agreed.
- Slam interest.
- Partnership agreement on control style.

---

# What Is a Control?

### First-Round Control

Prevents the opponents from taking the first trick.

Examples:

- Ace
- Void

---

### Second-Round Control

Prevents the opponents from taking the second trick.

Examples:

- King
- Singleton

---

# Control-Bidding Styles

## First-Round Controls Only

Partners first show:

- Aces
- Voids

A second round of cue bidding may later show Kings or singletons.

---

## First- and Second-Round Controls

Modern expert style.

Each cue bid promises either:

- First-round control, or
- Second-round control

Partner assumes the better control unless already denied.

---

# Principles

A player normally:

- Shows the cheapest available control.
- Skips a suit without a control.
- Continues cue bidding until enough information is known.

Skipping a suit generally denies a control in that suit.

# Partnership Agreements

The partnership should define:

- First-round only or first/second-round controls.
- Whether voids are shown immediately.
- Cue bidding order.
- Whether cue bidding is mandatory.
- Treatment after interference.

These agreements should appear on the convention card.

---

# System Variations

### Italian Cue Bidding

Shows first- or second-round controls.

Cheapest control first.

Most common expert treatment.

---

### American Cue Bidding

Often emphasizes first-round controls before second-round controls.

---

### Serious 3NT Integration

Many expert partnerships combine Control Bidding with Serious 3NT.

---

# Advantages

Control Bidding:

- Finds uncontrolled suits.
- Preserves bidding space.
- Excellent slam accuracy.
- Cooperative and descriptive.
- Complements RKCB.

---

# Limitations

Control Bidding:

- Requires partnership discipline.
- Can become complex.
- Different styles exist.
- Misunderstandings are expensive.

| Exclusion Blackwood | Ask outside a void suit |

---

# Expert Thinking

Expert partnerships rarely rely on RKCB alone.

Control Bidding often begins **before** any key-card ask because discovering an uncontrolled suit immediately ends the slam investigation. There is little value in counting key cards if the opponents can cash two quick tricks in an unprotected suit.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Beginning cue bidding before agreeing trumps.
- Confusing cue bids with natural bids.
- Skipping a control.
- Showing controls in the wrong order.
- Using RKCB before identifying uncontrolled suits.

---

# Best Practices

- Agree the trump suit first.
- Bid controls as cheaply as possible.
- Stop investigating if a suit lacks control.
- Use RKCB only after cue bidding has confirmed adequate controls.
- Record your style on the convention card.

---

# Expert Tip

**Control Bidding usually comes before RKCB, not after it.**

A partnership that discovers an uncontrolled suit can stop below slam immediately. Only after confirming that every suit is adequately controlled does it become worthwhile to ask about key cards. This sequence is one of the hallmarks of expert slam bidding.

---

# Memory Aid

**Control Bidding = "Show Controls Before Counting Key Cards."**

Remember:

- Agree the trump suit first.
- Bid controls from the bottom up.
- Skipping a suit usually denies a control.
- Use RKCB or Kickback only after control bidding has established that no suit is dangerously uncontrolled.
