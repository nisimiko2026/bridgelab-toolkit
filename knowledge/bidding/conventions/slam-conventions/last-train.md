````md id="lasttr1"
# Last Train Convention

## Purpose

The **Last Train** convention (also known as **Last Train to Game** or **Last Train Try**) is a slam-investigation tool used **after a trump suit has been agreed and cue bidding has begun**. It allows a player to express **slam interest without showing a specific control**.

A Last Train bid says:

> "I am interested in slam, but I do not have a suitable cue bid. Please continue describing your hand."

It is one of the most widely used expert slam conventions.

---

# When It Applies

Last Train applies when:

- A trump suit has been agreed.
- The partnership has cue bid controls.
- The next available bid immediately below game is available.
- A direct game bid would normally end the auction.

Example:

```
1♠
3♠
4♣
4♦
4♥
?
```

The bid of **4♥** is the **last available step below 4♠ game**.

Rather than showing a heart control, it may be **Last Train**.

---

# Basic Principle

A Last Train bid:

- Shows **slam interest**.
- Does **not** promise first- or second-round control in the bid suit.
- Asks partner to continue evaluating for slam.
- Leaves room for further cue bidding.

---

# Standard Meaning

A Last Train bid shows:

- Better than a minimum.
- Interest in slam.
- No convenient cue bid available.
- Willingness to cooperate if partner has extras.

It is **not** a control bid.

---

# Typical Auction

```
1♠
3♠
4♣
4♦
4♥
```

Meaning:

- Slam interest.
- No suitable cue available.
- Partner should continue with extra values.

---

# Another Example

```
1♥
3♥
4♣
4♦
4♠
```

Here:

**4♠** is Last Train.

It does **not** necessarily promise a spade control.

# Partner's Continuations

After a Last Train bid:

## Minimum Hand

Bid game.

Example:

```
4♠
```

No slam interest.

---

## Maximum Hand

Continue cue bidding.

Example:

```
5♣
```

Shows additional control.

```

Continue with additional cue bids or specific agreements.

# What Last Train Does NOT Show

Last Train does **not** promise:

- First-round control in the bid suit.
- Second-round control in the bid suit.
- Extra length in the bid suit.
- A natural suit.

It is an **artificial slam try**.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Agree on:

- Which auctions qualify.
- Whether Last Train denies control.
- Whether Last Train is forcing.
- Relationship with Serious 3NT.
- Continuations after interference.
- Whether cue bids remain first-round only or mixed controls.

---

# Relationship with Cue Bidding

Typical order:

1. Agree trump.
2. Cue bid controls.
3. Use Last Train when no convenient cue exists.
4. Continue cue bidding.
5. Roman Key Card Blackwood.

---

# Advantages

- Preserves bidding space.
- Shows slam interest without inventing controls.
- Helps partner judge marginal slam decisions.
- Extremely popular among expert partnerships.
- Works naturally with Italian Cue Bidding.

---

# Disadvantages

- Artificial meaning.
- Requires partnership agreement.
- Exact auctions vary between partnerships.

---

# Comparison

| Bid | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Cue Bid | Shows a control |
| Last Train | Slam interest, no control promised |
| Serious 3NT | Strong slam try before game level |
| RKCB | Key-card inquiry |

---

# Common Partnership Style

Most expert partnerships play:

- Last available bid below game.
- Does **not** promise control.
- Shows extras and slam interest.
- Partner continues only with useful additional values.

Last Train is commonly combined with:

- Italian Cue Bids.
- Serious 3NT.
- Roman Key Card Blackwood.
- DOPI/ROPI.

# Memory Aid

**Last Train = "I'm still interested in slam."**

Remember:

- **Last bid below game.**
- **Artificial.**
- **Shows slam interest.**
- **Does not promise a control in the bid suit.**
- **Invites partner to continue if maximum.**

Think of Last Train as the **final opportunity before stopping in game**. It keeps the "last train" to slam alive when no suitable cue bid is available, allowing the partnership to exchange one more piece of information before deciding whether to investigate further.
````
