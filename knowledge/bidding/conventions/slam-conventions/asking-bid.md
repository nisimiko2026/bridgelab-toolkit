# Asking Bid

## Purpose

An **Asking Bid** is an **artificial bid** whose primary purpose is to obtain specific information from partner rather than to describe the bidder's own hand.

Unlike natural bidding, where each player primarily describes their own cards, an asking bid shifts the focus to **question and answer**:

- One partner asks.
- The other partner responds according to an agreed structure.

Asking bids are fundamental to many advanced bidding systems and slam conventions.

---

# General Principles

An asking bid is:

- Artificial
- Usually forcing
- Based entirely on partnership agreement
- Intended to obtain one specific type of information

The response is predetermined and should not depend on judgment beyond the agreed meanings.

---

# Information That May Be Requested

An asking bid may request information about:

- Strength
- Distribution
- Suit length
- Suit quality
- Controls
- Key cards
- Stoppers
- Shortness
- Specific honors
- Slam potential

The exact question depends on the convention.

---

# Common Families of Asking Bids

## Ace-Asking Bids

Request:

- Number of aces
- Number of key cards

Examples:

- Blackwood
- Roman Key Card Blackwood
- Gerber
- Exclusion Blackwood

---

## Control-Asking Bids

Request:

- First-round controls
- Second-round controls
- Location of controls

Typically found in:

- Precision
- Blue Team Club
- Relay systems

---

## Relay Asks

The cheapest available artificial bid asks partner to continue describing the hand.

Common in:

- Symmetric Relay
- MOSCITO
- Relay Precision

---

## Shape Asks

Request information about:

- Distribution
- Exact pattern
- Suit lengths

Usually implemented through relay bidding.

---

## Feature Asks

Request:

- Side ace
- Side king
- Singleton
- Void
- Source of tricks
- Useful honor

Common after:

- Weak two openings
- Preemptive openings
- Strong artificial openings

---

## Stopper Asks

Request information about:

- A stopper in a particular suit

Common in:

- Lebensohl
- Transfer Lebensohl (Rubensohl)
- Checkback methods

---

## Suit Quality Asks

Request:

- Honor strength
- Playing tricks
- Suit texture

Typically used after weak or preemptive openings.

---

# Advantages

- Exchanges information efficiently
- Improves game and slam bidding
- Allows precise hand evaluation
- Preserves bidding space
- Forms the basis of many relay systems

---

# Disadvantages

- Memory intensive
- Requires complete partnership agreements
- Vulnerable to competitive interference
- Many methods are not standardized

---

# Asking Bids vs. Relay Bids

| Asking Bid | Relay Bid |
|-------------|-----------|
|Requests specific information|Requests the next descriptive step|
|May require only one response|Often continues for several rounds|
|Can exist independently|Usually part of a relay structure|

Every relay bid is an asking bid, but not every asking bid is a relay.

---

# Asking Bids vs. Cue Bidding

| Asking Bid | Cue Bidding |
|-------------|-------------|
|One player asks, the other answers|Both players voluntarily exchange information|
|Predetermined responses|Natural cooperative descriptions|
|Usually artificial|Often semi-natural|

# Examples

| Convention | Information Requested |
|-------------|-----------------------|
|Blackwood|Number of aces|
|Roman Key Card Blackwood|Number of key cards|
|Gerber|Number of aces after notrump|
|Control Ask|Specific controls|
|Puppet Stayman|Major-suit holdings|
|Checkback Stayman|Three-card support and distribution|
|Symmetric Relay|Strength, shape, and controls|
|Feature Ask|Specific honor or feature|

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partnerships should agree on:

- Which asking bids are in use
- Response structure
- Priority when multiple asks are possible
- Continuations after interference
- Whether responses are step-based or suit-specific

# Summary

An **Asking Bid** is an artificial bid that requests partner to provide specific information according to predefined partnership agreements. Asking bids form the foundation of many advanced bidding methods, including **Ace-Asking Bids**, **Control-Asking Bids**, **Relay Bidding**, **Feature Asks**, and **Shape Asks**, enabling highly accurate game and slam bidding through structured question-and-answer sequences.
