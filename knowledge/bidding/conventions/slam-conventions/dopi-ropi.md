# DOPI / ROPI

## Overview

**DOPI** and **ROPI** are conventional agreements used when the opponents interfere over an **Ace-asking bid**, usually **Roman Key Card Blackwood (RKCB)** or **Blackwood**.

Without these agreements, the partnership may lose the ability to show the correct number of key cards after the opponents interfere.

DOPI and ROPI restore a simple and logical response structure.

---

# Purpose

DOPI and ROPI are designed to:

- Handle interference over Blackwood.
- Preserve key-card responses.
- Continue slam investigation.
- Minimize ambiguity.
- Allow the partnership to compete despite interference.

---

# When to Use

They apply after:

- Blackwood
- Roman Key Card Blackwood
- Gerber (less commonly)

when an opponent:

- Doubles
- Overcalls

depending on the agreement.

---

# DOPI

## Meaning

**DOPI** stands for:

**Double = 0 (or 3)**
**Pass = 1 (or 4)**

It is normally used after the opponents make a **bid** over Blackwood.

Example:

```
1♠
3♠
4NT*
(5♣)
?
```

Responder now answers:

| Call | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Double | 0 or 3 Key Cards |
| Pass | 1 or 4 Key Cards |
| Cheapest available bid | 2 Key Cards without trump Queen |
| Next available bid | 2 Key Cards with trump Queen |

The exact continuation depends on partnership agreement.

---

## Example

```
1♥
3♥
4NT*
(5♦)
Double
```

Shows:

- 0 or 3 Key Cards.

---

# ROPI

## Meaning

**ROPI** stands for:

**Redouble = 0 (or 3)**
**Pass = 1 (or 4)**

ROPI is used after the opponents **double** the Ace-asking bid.

Example

```
1♠
3♠
4NT*
(Double)
?
```

Responder now answers:

| Call | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Redouble | 0 or 3 Key Cards |
| Pass | 1 or 4 Key Cards |
| Cheapest available bid | 2 without trump Queen |
| Next available bid | 2 with trump Queen |

---

## Example

```
1♠
3♠
4NT*
(Double)
Redouble
```

Shows:

- 0 or 3 Key Cards.

---

# Why Use These Methods?

Without DOPI/ROPI:

```
4NT
(5♣)
?
```

The partnership loses the normal response structure.

With DOPI:

- Double and Pass become artificial responses.

This preserves almost the entire RKCB structure.

---

# Complete RKCB 1430 Example

Auction:

```
1♠
3♠
4NT*
(5♣)
?
```

Responder:

| Call | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Double | 0 or 3 Key Cards |
| Pass | 1 or 4 Key Cards |
| 5♦ | 2 without Q |
| 5♥ | 2 with Q |

---

# Complete RKCB 3014 Example

Auction:

```
4NT*
(5♣)
?
```

Partnerships playing 3014 simply reverse the first two responses.

---

# Continuations

After learning the number of Key Cards the partnership continues naturally.

Possible actions:

- Sign off.
- Queen Ask.
- King Ask.
- Cue bids.
- Grand Slam Force.

---

# Interference Above the Five Level

If interference occurs above the normal response level:

```
4NT*
(5♠)
?
```

Many partnerships still apply DOPI principles:

- Double
- Pass
- Cheapest bid

Others revert to natural bidding.

---

# DOPI vs ROPI

| DOPI | ROPI |
|------|------|
| Used after opponent bids | Used after opponent doubles |
| Double shows first step | Redouble shows first step |
| Pass shows second step | Pass shows second step |

---

# DOPI vs DEPO

Some partnerships instead play **DEPO**.

| Convention | Meaning |
|------------|---------|
| DOPI | Double = 0/3, Pass = 1/4 |
| DEPO | Double = Even, Pass = Odd |

DEPO is more common after higher levels of interference.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should discuss:

- RKCB version (1430 or 3014).
- DOPI or DEPO.
- ROPI treatment.
- Queen Ask after interference.
- King Ask after interference.
- When the convention is "On."

# Competitive Auctions

## General Principles

When opponents interfere:

- Keep the agreed response structure.
- Do not panic because bidding space is reduced.
- Continue slam investigation if practical.
- Reevaluate after every response.

---

## Queen Ask

After DOPI/ROPI responses, Queen Ask proceeds normally whenever bidding space permits.

---

## King Ask

King Ask also proceeds normally.

Some partnerships omit King Ask after heavy interference.

---

## Void Showing

If your partnership uses:

- Exclusion Blackwood
- Void responses

these agreements should override DOPI.

---

## High-Level Interference

When bidding space disappears entirely:

```
4NT*
(6♣)
?
```

Most partnerships simply bid naturally.

No universal agreement exists.

---

# Advantages

DOPI/ROPI:

- Preserve RKCB.
- Easy to remember.
- Handle interference efficiently.
- Reduce misunderstandings.
- Standard expert treatment.

---

# Limitations

The conventions:

- Require prior agreement.
- Lose information after high interference.
- Do not solve every interference problem.
- May conflict with DEPO.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Forgetting Which Convention You Play

Many partnerships mix up:

- DOPI
- DEPO

Agree beforehand.

---

## Confusing DOPI and ROPI

Remember:

**Opponent bids → DOPI**

**Opponent doubles → ROPI**

---

## Forgetting 1430 vs 3014

The later responses still depend on which RKCB version your partnership uses.

---

## Ignoring Queen Ask

The Queen Ask usually remains available after DOPI/ROPI.

---

## Overusing the Convention

DOPI/ROPI only applies after agreed Ace-asking bids.

---

# Tips

- Learn DOPI together with RKCB—not separately.
- Think:

  - **Opponent bids → DOPI**
  - **Opponent doubles → ROPI**

- Practice complete interference auctions.
- Agree how DEPO fits into your system.
- Remember that distribution still matters after learning the key-card count.

---

# Modern Usage

Nearly every expert partnership that plays **Roman Key Card Blackwood** also adopts an interference agreement such as **DOPI/ROPI** or **DEPO**.

DOPI/ROPI has become the most common treatment because it is:

- Logical.
- Easy to memorize.
- Compatible with both **1430** and **3014** RKCB structures.

Although alternative methods exist, DOPI/ROPI is the de facto standard in tournament bridge.

---

# Summary

DOPI and ROPI are practical conventions that preserve the effectiveness of **Blackwood** and **Roman Key Card Blackwood** when opponents interfere. DOPI applies after an opponent **bids** over the Ace ask, while ROPI applies after an opponent **doubles** it. By assigning artificial meanings to **Double**, **Redouble**, and **Pass**, these methods allow partnerships to continue exchanging key-card information and pursue slam with confidence despite competitive interference. They are now considered an essential complement to modern RKCB methods.
