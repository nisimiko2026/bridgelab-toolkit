# Do Something Intelligent (DSI)

## Overview

**DSI (Do Something Intelligent)** is not a conventional bidding system but an
expert partnership philosophy used in competitive auctions. It recognizes that
many highly competitive situations cannot be described adequately by rigid
definitions such as **Penalty Double**, **Takeout Double**, or **Action
Double**.

Instead, DSI asks partner to evaluate the complete auction and choose the action
most likely to produce the best practical result.

A DSI action does **not** prescribe a specific call.

Instead it communicates

> "Partner, use your judgment."

Modern expert partnerships frequently employ DSI whenever a purely descriptive
call is impossible.

---

# Philosophy

Bridge contains many auctions where

- passing feels wrong,
- bidding is unattractive,
- doubling is not purely penalty,
- taking out is uncertain.

Rather than forcing the auction into an artificial category, expert
partnerships simply agree

> **Do Something Intelligent.**

The emphasis is on

- judgment,
- flexibility,
- partnership cooperation.

---

# Objectives

DSI enables the partnership to

- avoid rigid bidding rules,
- compete intelligently,
- preserve flexibility,
- improve practical decisions,
- trust partner's judgment,
- adapt to unusual auctions.

---

# DSI Is Not a Convention

Unlike

- Stayman,
- Michaels,
- Lebensohl,

DSI assigns **no fixed meanings** to bids.

Instead,

it is a partnership agreement governing how unusual competitive auctions should
be interpreted.

---

# Typical Situations

DSI commonly arises

- after Action Doubles,
- after Optional Doubles,
- after competitive doubles,
- after delayed doubles,
- after highly competitive auctions,
- when no natural call exists.

# Judgment Factors

Experts evaluate

---

# DSI versus Action Double

Action Double

is a specific call.

DSI

is the philosophy behind that call.

Most Action Doubles are examples of DSI.

Not every DSI decision involves a double.

---

# DSI versus Optional Double

Optional Double

offers partner a choice between

- defending,
- bidding.

DSI applies more broadly,

including auctions where no optional double exists.

---

# DSI versus Penalty Double

Penalty Double

asks partner to defend.

DSI

asks partner to judge.

---

# Partnership Trust

DSI succeeds only when partners trust each other's judgment.

Neither player attempts to describe every detail.

Instead,

both players evaluate

- offense,
- defense,
- distribution,
- vulnerability,

before acting.

# Modern Expert Style

Many world-class partnerships no longer attempt to classify every competitive
double precisely.

Instead,

they adopt broad DSI principles,

allowing judgment to replace rigid definitions whenever auctions become highly
competitive.

This reflects modern bridge's emphasis on partnership understanding rather than
mechanical bidding.

# Historical Perspective

The expression **"Do Something Intelligent"** emerged from expert tournament
bridge rather than from a formal convention card. It reflects the realization
that highly competitive auctions often resist strict classification.

Rather than assigning every double a rigid meaning, experienced partnerships
increasingly relied on shared judgment and practical evaluation. Over time,
**DSI** became shorthand for this philosophy and is now widely recognized among
advanced and expert players.

---

# Summary

**DSI (Do Something Intelligent)** is an expert partnership philosophy used in
competitive bidding when rigid bidding definitions are inadequate. Rather than
prescribing a specific action, DSI asks partner to evaluate the auction,
distribution, vulnerability, and likely outcome before choosing the most
practical call.

Although frequently associated with **Action Doubles**, DSI is broader than any
single convention. It represents the modern expert approach of replacing rigid
rules with informed partnership judgment in complex competitive auctions.
