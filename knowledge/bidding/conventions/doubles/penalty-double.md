# Penalty Double

## Overview

A **Penalty Double** is a double made with the intention of defeating the opponents' contract for a significant score. Unlike conventional doubles, which convey information or request partner to bid, a Penalty Double says:

> **"I believe we should defend this contract because it is likely to fail."**

Penalty doubles require sound judgment, accurate defensive evaluation, and confidence that defeating the contract will produce a better result than declaring a contract of your own.

Although some doubles are penalty by default, many auctions require partnership agreement to determine whether a double is for penalty or conventional purposes.

---

# Purpose

Penalty Doubles are used to:

- Defeat the opponents' contract.
- Increase the score for each undertrick.
- Convert defensive strength into a profitable result.
- Punish overbidding or aggressive sacrifices.
- Discourage reckless competitive bidding.

---

# When to Use

Penalty Doubles occur most frequently when:

- The opponents have overreached.
- The partnership has defensive strength.
- The opponents have no obvious escape.
- Defending scores better than bidding.

Typical situations include:

- Doubled part-score contracts.
- Doubled game contracts.
- Doubled sacrifices.
- Doubled notrump contracts.

---

# Requirements

A successful Penalty Double usually requires:

- Defensive strength.
- Good trump holding (if applicable).
- Accurate count of partner's likely values.
- Confidence that the contract will fail.

Penalty doubles should be based on **expected defensive tricks**, not optimism.

---

# Hand Evaluation

Important considerations include:

- High-card points.
- Defensive tricks.
- Honor location.
- Trump length.
- Vulnerability.
- Distribution.
- Likely opening lead.

Defensive values are more important than offensive values.

---

# Typical Auctions

## Penalty Double of 1NT

```
West   North   East   South
       1NT     Pass   Pass
Double
```

Depending on partnership agreement, this may be for penalty.

---

## Penalty Double of a Suit Contract

```
1♥
Pass
4♥
Double
```

The doubler expects to defeat 4♥.

---

## Penalty Double of a Sacrifice

```
4♠
5♦
5♠
6♦
Double
```

The partnership judges that defending 6♦ doubled is more profitable than bidding higher.

---

# Partner's Responsibilities

After a Penalty Double, partner should normally:

- Pass.
- Prepare for defense.
- Avoid removing the double without compelling reason.

Removing a penalty double should occur only with clear partnership agreement.

---

# Defensive Planning

Once the contract has been doubled, both defenders should immediately begin planning:

- Opening lead.
- Trump control.
- Entries.
- Communication.
- Promotion of defensive tricks.
- Declarer's likely distribution.

Accurate defense becomes even more important because each undertrick is worth considerably more.

---

# Penalty Double Versus Takeout Double

| Penalty Double | Takeout Double |
|----------------|----------------|
| Wants to defend | Wants partner to bid |
| Defensive strength | Shortness in opponent's suit |
| Usually balanced | Usually unbalanced |
| Shows confidence in defeating the contract | Asks partner to choose a suit |

---

# Penalty Double Versus Negative Double

| Penalty Double | Negative Double |
|----------------|-----------------|
| Defensive | Constructive |
| Ends the auction | Continues the auction |
| Shows strength against the contract | Shows unbid suits |
| No desire to compete | Looking for the best fit |

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should agree on:

- Which doubles are penalty.
- Which doubles remain conventional.
- When doubles become penalty later in the auction.
- Whether Pass is forcing.
- Rescue agreements.

# Advantages

Penalty Doubles:

- Produce large scores.
- Punish aggressive bidding.
- Reward accurate defense.
- Discourage reckless sacrifices.
- Improve matchpoint results.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Requires excellent judgment.
- May fail when declarer makes the contract.
- Depends upon partnership understanding.
- Can become expensive if incorrect.

---

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

Penalty Doubles frequently arise during competitive auctions in which neither side is certain whether declaring or defending will produce the better result.

Judgment becomes more important than convention.

---

## Balancing Decisions

Before doubling, consider whether:

- Defending is better than bidding.
- Partner has already limited the hand.
- The opponents are sacrificing.
- Vulnerability favors defending.

---

## Sacrifice Decisions

Many Penalty Doubles occur after apparent sacrifices.

Example

```
4♠
5♦
Double
```

Before doubling, estimate:

- Number of defensive tricks.
- Expected undertricks.
- Vulnerability.
- Whether your own game or slam is making.

A successful penalty should outscore any available contract for your side.

---

## Penalty Double of Notrump

Penalty doubles are particularly effective against:

```
1NT
```

or

```
3NT
```

when:

- The defense has significant strength.
- Declarer lacks an escape.
- Opening lead and communication favor the defenders.

Partnership agreements determine whether a double is penalty or conventional.

---

## Removing the Double

Most penalty doubles should remain in effect.

However, some partnerships allow removal when:

- Partner has an extreme distribution.
- The partnership has misunderstood the auction.
- Rescue methods have been agreed.

Such agreements should be discussed before play.

---

## Partnership Agreements

Every partnership should define:

- Which doubles are penalty.
- Which doubles remain conventional.
- Pass forcing situations.
- Rescue sequences.
- Penalty doubles after artificial bids.
- Penalty doubles after balancing.

---

## General Principles

When considering a Penalty Double:

- Count defensive tricks rather than HCP.
- Consider vulnerability carefully.
- Think about partner's likely hand.
- Do not double on hope alone.
- Defend accurately after doubling.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Doubling With Offensive Hands

Hands suitable for declaring often make poor penalty doubles.

---

## Ignoring Vulnerability

One additional undertrick may not compensate for the risk if declarer unexpectedly makes the contract.

---

## Forgetting Partnership Agreements

Many doubles change meaning during the auction.

Know when they become penalty.

---

## Doubling Emotionally

Never double because the opponents "must be going down."

Double only when your analysis supports it.

---

## Poor Defense After Doubling

Winning the auction is only half the battle.

Careful defense is essential to collect the expected penalty.

---

# Tips

- Count defensive winners before doubling.
- Consider the opening lead.
- Visualize declarer's likely distribution.
- Be especially cautious at favorable vulnerability for the opponents.
- Remember that accurate defense is as important as accurate bidding.

---

# Summary

A Penalty Double is one of bridge's most powerful competitive tools. Unlike conventional doubles that request partner to bid, it expresses confidence that defending the opponents' contract will produce a superior score. Successful penalty doubles require disciplined hand evaluation, careful consideration of vulnerability and defensive potential, and strong partnership agreements regarding when doubles are penalty and when they remain conventional. Sound judgment, followed by accurate defense, is the key to converting a penalty double into a winning result.
