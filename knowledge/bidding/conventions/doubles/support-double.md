# Support Double

## Overview

A **Support Double** is an artificial double made by **opener** after responder has bid a new suit and the opponents interfere. The double shows **exactly three-card support** for responder's suit.

Without Support Doubles, opener often cannot distinguish between holding three-card support and four-card support. The convention solves this problem by allowing opener to show three-card support through a double, while a direct raise promises four-card support.

Support Doubles have become a standard feature of modern competitive bidding because they improve the partnership's ability to locate eight-card major-suit fits.

---

# Purpose

Support Doubles are used to:

- Distinguish three-card support from four-card support.
- Locate eight-card major-suit fits.
- Preserve bidding space.
- Improve competitive bidding.
- Eliminate ambiguity in opener's rebids.

---

# When to Use

A Support Double is available when:

- Opener has opened the bidding.
- Responder bids a new suit.
- The next opponent overcalls.
- Opener has exactly three-card support for responder's suit.

Typical auction:

```
1♣   Pass
1♥   1♠
Double*
```

The double shows **exactly three hearts**.

---

Another example:

```
1♦   Pass
1♠   2♣
Double*
```

Shows exactly three spades.

---

# Requirements

Typical requirements include:

- Opening values.
- Exactly three-card support.
- No more descriptive natural rebid.

Partnerships usually play Support Doubles through **2♥** or **2♠**, although some extend them higher.

---

# Standard Meaning

Support Double promises:

- Exactly three-card support.
- Opening strength.
- Interest in competing.

It does **not** show penalty interest.

---

# Support Redouble

Many partnerships also play **Support Redoubles**.

Example:

```
1♣
Pass
1♥
Double
Redouble*
```

The redouble shows exactly three-card support after an opponent's takeout double.

Together, Support Doubles and Support Redoubles provide a complete system for showing three-card support after interference.

---

# Direct Raise

Without using a Support Double:

```
1♣
Pass
1♥
1♠
2♥
```

The raise usually promises:

- Four-card support.

This distinction allows responder to judge whether the partnership has:

- A 7-card fit.
- An 8-card fit.
- A 9-card fit.

---

# Opener's Alternatives

Without three-card support, opener simply makes the most descriptive bid.

Typical rebids include:

- Rebid the opening suit.
- Bid notrump.
- Introduce a second suit.
- Cue bid (with extra values).

---

# Responder's Continuations

After a Support Double, responder evaluates:

- Combined strength.
- Trump fit.
- Distribution.
- Offensive potential.

Possible continuations include:

- Pass.
- Raise to the appropriate level.
- Invite game.
- Bid game.
- Continue competing.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should discuss:

- Highest level for Support Doubles.
- Whether Support Redoubles are played.
- Minimum strength.
- Whether doubles always promise exactly three cards.
- Cue-bid continuations.

# Advantages

Support Doubles:

- Eliminate ambiguity.
- Improve major-suit bidding.
- Preserve bidding space.
- Improve competitive judgment.
- Help responder evaluate game prospects accurately.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Requires partnership agreement.
- Is unavailable once the auction becomes too high.
- May occasionally reveal information to the opponents.
- Requires accurate follow-up bidding.

---

# Variations

Common partnership styles include:

- Support Doubles through 2♥.
- Support Doubles through 2♠.
- Support Doubles through 3♥.
- Support Redoubles.
- Modern Support Double methods.

Every partnership should agree on its style.

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

Support Doubles occur in competitive auctions by definition. After opener makes a Support Double, the auction often continues with additional competition. Both partners should continue describing their hands naturally.

---

## Opponent Raises

Example

```
1♣
Pass
1♥
1♠
Double
2♠
?
```

Responder should reevaluate:

- Combined strength.
- Eight-card fit.
- Offensive values.
- Vulnerability.

Possible actions include:

- Pass.
- Compete to 3♥.
- Invite game.
- Bid game.

---

## Further Competition

Example

```
1♦
Pass
1♠
2♣
Double
3♣
?
```

Opener and responder should consider:

- Distribution.
- Playing tricks.
- Law of Total Tricks.
- Offensive versus defensive values.

The Support Double has already identified the likely trump fit, making competitive decisions easier.

---

## Cue Bids

After a Support Double, cue bids may be used to show:

- Game-forcing values.
- Strong support.
- Slam interest.

The exact treatment depends upon partnership agreement.

---

## Balancing

If the opponents stop in a low-level contract after a Support Double sequence, either partner may later have a balancing opportunity.

Example

```
1♣
Pass
1♥
1♠
Double
Pass
Pass
?
```

Balancing decisions should be based on:

- Combined strength.
- Distribution.
- Vulnerability.
- Partnership style.

---

## Partnership Agreements

Partners should define:

- Highest level for Support Doubles.
- Highest level for Support Redoubles.
- Responses after further competition.
- Cue-bid meanings.
- Competitive raises.
- Balancing methods.

---

## General Principles

When using Support Doubles:

- Show exactly three-card support.
- Raise directly with four-card support.
- Continue describing the hand naturally.
- Reevaluate after every competitive action.
- Let distribution guide competitive decisions.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Doubling With Four-Card Support

A direct raise—not a Support Double—normally shows four-card support.

---

## Forgetting the Three-Card Promise

Support Doubles lose their value if opener occasionally holds only two cards or four cards.

---

## Treating the Double as Penalty

Support Doubles are constructive, not penalty.

---

## Ignoring Partnership Agreements

Some partnerships play the convention only through 2♥, while others extend it higher.

Know your partnership style.

---

## Competing Automatically

Showing support does not require the partnership to compete to the next level regardless of strength.

---

# Tips

- Think of the Support Double as a descriptive rebid rather than a competitive action.
- Raise immediately with four-card support.
- Practice auctions where responder holds invitational and game-going values.
- Combine Support Doubles with Negative Doubles for accurate major-suit bidding.
- Discuss the highest level at which the convention applies.

---

# Summary

The Support Double is one of the most valuable modern competitive bidding conventions. By allowing opener to show **exactly three-card support** for responder's suit, it removes ambiguity that would otherwise exist after opponent interference. This simple distinction between three-card and four-card support greatly improves the partnership's ability to judge part-score, game, and competitive decisions. Successful use depends on disciplined partnership agreements, consistent application, and accurate follow-up bidding.
