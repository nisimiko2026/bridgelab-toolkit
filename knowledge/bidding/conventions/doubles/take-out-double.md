# Takeout Double

## Overview

The **Takeout Double** is one of the fundamental conventions in bridge. It is an artificial double made over an opponent's opening bid that asks partner to bid the most suitable unbid suit rather than defend the doubled contract.

Unlike a Penalty Double, which expresses confidence in defeating the opponents' contract, a Takeout Double is constructive. It shows opening strength, shortness in the opponent's suit, and support for the unbid suits.

Takeout Doubles allow partnerships to enter the auction immediately, locate major-suit fits, compete effectively, and avoid missing game.

Example:

```
West   North   East   South
1♦     Double*
```

The double requests partner to bid.

---

# Purpose

A Takeout Double is used to:

- Compete immediately after an opponent opens.
- Show opening strength.
- Show support for the unbid suits.
- Locate the best trump fit.
- Keep all suitable contracts in the picture.
- Avoid overcalling on unsuitable hands.

---

# When to Use

A Takeout Double is appropriate after an opponent opens with:

- 1♣
- 1♦
- 1♥
- 1♠

It may also be used over higher-level openings according to partnership agreement.

Typical auctions:

```
1♣   Double*
```

```
1♦   Double*
```

```
1♥   Double*
```

```
1♠   Double*
```

---

# Requirements

## Strength

A Takeout Double normally promises:

- Opening values (approximately 12+ HCP)

Some partnerships require:

- 13+ HCP when vulnerable
- Slightly lighter values with ideal distribution

Playing strength and distribution are often more important than raw HCP.

---

## Distribution

The doubler should normally have:

- Shortness in the opponent's suit
- Support for every unbid suit

Typical shapes include:

```
4-4-4-1
```

```
5-4-3-1
```

```
4-3-5-1
```

Singletons and voids are ideal.

Balanced hands are generally unsuitable unless exceptionally strong.

---

## Suit Support

The doubler should expect to support whichever unbid suit partner selects.

Typical support:

- At least three cards in each unbid suit.
- Preferably four-card support for the majors.

---

# Hand Evaluation

Before doubling, consider:

- Distribution.
- Playing tricks.
- Defensive values.
- Vulnerability.
- Position.
- Suit quality.

Distribution is frequently more important than HCP.

# Partner's Responsibilities

Partner should normally bid the best available unbid suit.

Priority:

1. Longest suit.
2. Major suit.
3. Cheapest suitable bid.

Passing a Takeout Double is uncommon unless the partnership has agreed that sufficient defensive strength exists.

---

# Responses

## Minimum Hand

Bid the cheapest suitable suit.

Example:

```
1♦
Double
Pass
1♥
```

---

## Invitational Hand

Jump in the chosen suit.

Example:

```
1♣
Double
Pass
2♠
```

---

## Strong Hand

Cue bid or make another forcing action according to partnership agreement.

---

## Passing the Double

Partner may pass with:

- Defensive strength.
- Length in the opponent's suit.
- Expectation of defeating the contract.

Such actions require partnership agreement.

# Takeout Double Versus Overcall

| Takeout Double | Overcall |
|----------------|----------|
| Supports all unbid suits | Shows one specific suit |
| Short in opener's suit | Good long suit |
| Asks partner to choose | Describes your own suit |
| Usually opening values | May be lighter |

---

# Takeout Double Versus Penalty Double

| Takeout Double | Penalty Double |
|----------------|----------------|
| Constructive | Defensive |
| Requests partner to bid | Requests partner to defend |
| Support for unbid suits | Strength against declarer |
| Continues the auction | Usually ends the auction |

# Advantages

Takeout Doubles:

- Enter the auction immediately.
- Locate major-suit fits.
- Preserve flexibility.
- Improve competitive bidding.
- Describe distribution accurately.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Requires partnership agreement.
- Is unsuitable with balanced minimum hands.
- May occasionally help the opponents judge the auction.
- Requires disciplined responses.

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

A Takeout Double often begins a long competitive auction. After partner responds, the opponents may continue competing, requiring both partners to reevaluate their hands continuously.

---

## Opponent Raises

Example

```
1♥
Double
2♥
?
```

Partner should now evaluate:

- Combined strength.
- Trump fit.
- Playing tricks.
- Offensive versus defensive values.
- Vulnerability.

Possible actions include:

- Pass.
- Compete.
- Cue bid.
- Double later for penalty.
- Bid game.

---

## Opponent Rebids

Example

```
1♦
Double
1♠
2♦
?
```

The doubler should continue describing:

- Extra strength.
- Support.
- Distribution.

Avoid making automatic competitive bids.

---

## Cue Bids

After a Takeout Double, cue bids commonly show:

- Strong hands.
- Game-forcing values.
- Support.
- Continued interest in the auction.

The exact treatment depends upon partnership agreement.

---

## Responsive Doubles

If partner responds with a **Responsive Double**, the partnership continues searching for the best fit.

Example

```
1♦
Double
2♦
Double*
```

The second double is responsive, not penalty.

---

## Penalty Conversion

Occasionally partner may pass the Takeout Double for penalty.

Example

```
1♠
Double
Pass
Pass
```

This usually promises:

- Strong defensive values.
- Length in spades.
- Confidence that the contract will fail.

Penalty conversions should be specifically agreed.

---

## Balancing and Reopening

Takeout principles frequently apply during balancing and reopening auctions.

Example

```
1♦
Pass
Pass
Double
```

Although technically a reopening double, the underlying principles remain similar:

- Support for unbid suits.
- Opening values or equivalent.
- Desire to compete.

---

## Partnership Agreements

Partners should define:

- Maximum level for Takeout Doubles.
- Responses after opponent raises.
- Cue-bid meanings.
- Penalty conversions.
- Responsive Doubles.
- Reopening Doubles.
- Balancing style.

---

## General Principles

When making a Takeout Double:

- Be short in the opponent's suit.
- Be prepared to support every unbid suit.
- Let distribution influence your decision.
- Continue describing the hand naturally.
- Reevaluate after every competitive action.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Doubling With Balanced Minimum Hands

Balanced hands without shortness are often better suited to a natural notrump overcall.

---

## Doubling Without Support

A Takeout Double promises support for the unbid suits.

Do not double with only one playable suit.

---

## Forgetting Distribution

The convention is based primarily on shape rather than HCP.

---

## Passing Too Often

Partner should normally bid.

Passing is reserved for hands with strong defensive prospects.

---

## Confusing Takeout and Penalty Doubles

Takeout Doubles ask partner to bid.

Penalty Doubles ask partner to defend.

---

# Tips

- Think "shortness plus support" before doubling.
- Count playing tricks as well as HCP.
- Prefer a Takeout Double to an overcall with support for several suits.
- Practice responses as carefully as opening doubles.
- Combine Takeout, Negative, Support, Responsive, and Reopening Doubles into one consistent competitive system.

---

# Summary

The Takeout Double is the cornerstone of modern competitive bidding. By showing opening strength, shortness in the opponent's suit, and support for the remaining suits, it enables partnerships to compete accurately while preserving flexibility. It serves as the foundation for many related conventions—including Negative Doubles, Responsive Doubles, Support Doubles, and Reopening Doubles—and remains one of the most important tools in every bridge player's bidding system.
