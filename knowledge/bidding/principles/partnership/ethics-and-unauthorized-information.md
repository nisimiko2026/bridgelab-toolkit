# Ethics and Unauthorized Information

## Overview

Bridge is a game of **logic, skill, and partnership trust**. The Laws of Duplicate Bridge require that all players compete fairly by making decisions **only from authorized information**. Any information obtained through partner's mannerisms, tempo, hesitations, facial expressions, or other unintended actions is called **Unauthorized Information (UI)** and must not influence your decisions.

Ethical conduct is fundamental to duplicate bridge. The integrity of the game depends on every player following both the **letter** and the **spirit** of the Laws.

The guiding principle is:

> **Play the cards—not your partner.**

---

# Purpose

Understanding ethics and unauthorized information helps players to:

- Compete fairly.
- Avoid illegal advantages.
- Protect the integrity of the game.
- Build trust between partnerships.
- Comply with the Laws of Duplicate Bridge.

---

# Authorized Information (AI)

**Authorized Information** includes:

- The auction.
- Cards played.
- Dummy after it is exposed.
- Opponents' legal explanations.
- Properly disclosed partnership agreements.
- Information available equally to all four players.

Players are free to base decisions on authorized information.

---

# Unauthorized Information (UI)

Unauthorized information includes anything that **only one player receives from partner** outside the normal legal methods of communication.

Examples include:

- Hesitations (breaks in tempo).
- Unusual speed of bidding or play.
- Tone of voice.
- Facial expressions.
- Gestures.
- Body language.
- Sighs or comments.
- Questions asked for partner's benefit.
- Placement of cards or bidding cards.
- Any other unintended clue.

Such information is **not** part of the game and must not influence decisions.

---

# Examples of Unauthorized Information

## Example 1 – Hesitation

Auction:

```
West   North   East   South

1♠     Pass    2♠     Pass

Pass   ?
```

Partner thinks for a long time before passing.

The hesitation may suggest uncertainty or extra values.

You **must ignore the hesitation** and choose a call that is justified by the auction and your hand alone.

---

## Example 2 – Fast Pass

Partner passes unusually quickly.

This may suggest weakness or confidence.

You must ignore the tempo and bid only on authorized information.

---

## Example 3 – Facial Expression

Partner reacts visibly after an opponent's bid.

This information is unauthorized and must not affect your decision.

---

# Logical Alternatives

When unauthorized information exists, a player must choose among the **logical alternatives** that are available without considering the UI.

A player **must not** select an action that is demonstrably suggested by the unauthorized information if another logical action exists.

This is one of the central principles of duplicate bridge ethics.

---

# Hesitations

Not every hesitation is improper.

Players are entitled to think.

However:

- A hesitation creates unauthorized information for partner.
- Partner has a legal obligation to ignore it.
- If the hesitation demonstrably influences partner's action, the director may adjust the score.

---

# Extraneous Information

Players should avoid creating unnecessary UI by:

- Maintaining consistent tempo.
- Avoiding emotional reactions.
- Keeping facial expressions neutral.
- Remaining silent except when permitted.

---

# Partnership Communication

Legal communication occurs only through:

- Bids.
- Calls.
- Card play.
- Proper alerts and explanations.

Everything else is prohibited as a source of partnership information.

---

# Alert Explanations

When explaining a bid:

Explain:

- Partnership agreements.

Do **not** explain:

- What you think partner currently holds.
- Personal interpretations.
- Inferences.

---

# Questions

Players may ask opponents about:

- Partnership agreements.
- Conventional meanings.
- Carding methods.

Questions must never be used to send messages to partner.

---

# Director Calls

Call the director whenever:

- Unauthorized information may have affected play.
- There is an irregularity.
- An incorrect explanation is given.
- A revoke or procedural error occurs.

Calling the director is a normal part of duplicate bridge and should never be viewed as unsporting.

---

# Ethical Responsibilities

Every player should:

- Compete fairly.
- Avoid taking advantage of partner's mistakes.
- Provide full disclosure.
- Maintain consistent tempo.
- Respect opponents.
- Protect the integrity of the game.

# Common Mistakes

- Taking advantage of partner's hesitation.
- Explaining current hands instead of agreements.
- Asking unnecessary questions.
- Revealing emotions during the auction.
- Varying tempo intentionally.

---

# Best Practices

Develop habits such as:

- Maintaining steady tempo whenever practical.
- Keeping expressions neutral.
- Speaking only when necessary.
- Explaining agreements clearly.
- Calling the director immediately when needed.

---

# Advantages of Ethical Play

Ethical conduct:

- Creates fair competition.
- Builds partnership trust.
- Improves tournament integrity.
- Protects reputations.
- Makes bridge more enjoyable for everyone.

# Player's Checklist

During every session:

- Base every decision on authorized information.
- Ignore partner's hesitations and mannerisms.
- Explain agreements honestly.
- Maintain consistent tempo.
- Call the director whenever appropriate.

# Memory Guide

## Five Rules

1. Use only authorized information.
2. Ignore unauthorized information.
3. Explain agreements, not opinions.
4. Maintain consistent tempo.
5. Protect the integrity of the game.

# Memory Aid

**Ethics = "Win by Skill, Never by Secret Information."**

Remember:

- **Use only information that is legally available to all four players.**
- **Partner's hesitations, tempo, expressions, and mannerisms are unauthorized information and must be ignored.**
- **When unauthorized information exists, choose from the logical alternatives that would exist without it.**
- **Always explain partnership agreements honestly and completely.**
- **The integrity of bridge depends on every player competing fairly, even when no one is watching.**

Ethics and Unauthorized Information are at the heart of duplicate bridge. Technical skill alone is not enough; players are expected to uphold the highest standards of honesty, fairness, and sportsmanship. By relying only on authorized information and respecting both the Laws and the spirit of the game, players preserve the integrity that makes bridge one of the world's great mind sports.
