# Rule of 22

## Purpose

The **Rule of 22** is an opening-hand evaluation guideline used to determine whether a borderline hand should be opened in **first or second seat**. It improves upon the Rule of 20 by considering not only high-card strength and distribution but also **quick tricks**, giving greater weight to controls.

## Formula

Open the bidding when:

**HCP + Length of Two Longest Suits + Quick Tricks ≥ 22**

Where:

- **HCP** = High Card Points
- **Length** = Number of cards in your two longest suits
- **Quick Tricks** = Immediate trick-taking potential

## Counting Quick Tricks

Count quick tricks suit by suit.

| Holding | Quick Tricks |
|---------|-------------:|
| AK | 2.0 |
| AQ | 1.5 |
| A | 1.0 |
| KQ | 1.0 |
| K | 0.5 |
| Other holdings | 0 |

Examples:

- AKxx = 2
- AQxx = 1.5
- KQxx = 1
- Axxx = 1
- Kxxx = 0.5

## Examples

### Example 1 – Open

```
♠ AQJ84
♥ K73
♦ A74
♣ 962
```

- HCP = 14
- Two longest suits = 5 + 3 = 8
- Quick Tricks
  - ♠ AQ = 1.5
  - ♥ K = 0.5
  - ♦ A = 1
  - Total = 3

**14 + 8 + 3 = 25**

**Action:** Open **1♠**.

---

### Example 2 – Pass

```
♠ KJ974
♥ 952
♦ QJ
♣ Q75
```

- HCP = 9
- Two longest suits = 5 + 4 = 9
- Quick Tricks = 0.5

**9 + 9 + 0.5 = 18.5**

**Action:** Pass.

---

### Example 3 – Borderline

```
♠ AJ9432
♥ A542
♦ 9
♣ 95
```

- HCP = 9
- Two longest suits = 6 + 4 = 10
- Quick Tricks = 2

**9 + 10 + 2 = 21**

Borderline. Most partnerships would pass in first or second seat, although favorable vulnerability and partnership style may justify opening.

## When to Apply

Use the Rule of 22 in:

- First seat
- Second seat

It is **not normally used** in:

- Third seat (lighter opening style)
- Fourth seat (Rule of 15 is preferred)

## Advantages

Compared with the Rule of 20, the Rule of 22:

- Rewards controls (Aces and Kings)
- Penalizes scattered values
- Produces more sound opening bids
- Reduces poor light openings

## Comparison

| Rule | Formula | Typical Use |
|------|---------|-------------|
| Rule of 20 | HCP + two longest suits ≥ 20 | General opening guideline |
| **Rule of 22** | HCP + two longest suits + quick tricks ≥ 22 | Borderline openings in first and second seat |
| Rule of 15 | HCP + spade length ≥ 15 | Fourth-seat opening decisions |

## Partnership Notes

- The Rule of 22 is a **guideline**, not a convention.
- Shape, suit quality, vulnerability, and partnership style remain important.
- Hands with excellent controls may justify opening even with relatively modest HCP.
- Hands meeting the numerical test but containing poor suit quality should still be evaluated carefully.

## Summary

The Rule of 22 extends the Rule of 20 by incorporating **quick tricks**, making it a more reliable method for evaluating borderline opening hands in first and second seat. It emphasizes controls and immediate trick-taking potential while maintaining the importance of distribution.
