# Offensive vs Defensive Values

## Purpose

Every bridge hand contains a combination of **offensive** and **defensive** values.

Understanding the difference helps determine whether to:

- Bid higher
- Double the opponents
- Defend
- Sacrifice
- Compete for the contract

The distinction is especially important in **competitive auctions**, where deciding whether to declare or defend often determines the final result.

---

# Offensive Values

A hand has **offensive values** when it is likely to take tricks **as declarer**, particularly in a suit contract.

Characteristics include:

- Long suits
- Good suit quality
- Shortages (singleton or void)
- Distributional shape
- Trump control
- Fit with partner

These hands usually produce more tricks than their HCP suggest.

### Typical Offensive Hands

```
♠ AKQJ96
♥ 7
♦ 95
♣ J843
```

- Long powerful suit
- Singleton
- Excellent ruffing potential

Although only moderate HCP, this is a highly offensive hand.

---

```
♠ QJ10974
♥ A4
♦ 3
♣ KQ83
```

- Six-card suit
- Singleton
- Distributional shape

Good hand for competing or preempting.

---

# Defensive Values

A hand has **defensive values** when it is likely to take tricks **against declarer**, regardless of trump fit.

Characteristics include:

- Aces
- Kings
- Quick tricks
- Balanced shape
- Honor combinations in several suits
- Controls

These hands defend well but may not play as well in suit contracts.

### Typical Defensive Hands

```
♠ AQ4
♥ K83
♦ AQ5
♣ K943
```

Balanced hand with:

- Four quick tricks
- Controls in three suits

Excellent defensive strength.

---

```
♠ KQ3
♥ AQ4
♦ KJ4
♣ A753
```

High controls spread throughout the hand.

Excellent for defending.

# Comparing Similar HCP Hands

## Offensive Hand

```
♠ AKQJ87
♥ 8
♦ 74
♣ Q842
```

- 13 HCP
- Six-card suit
- Singleton

High offensive value.

---

## Defensive Hand

```
♠ AQ4
♥ KQ4
♦ AJ3
♣ K983
```

- 13 HCP
- Balanced
- Multiple controls

High defensive value.

Although both hands have identical HCP, they should be bid very differently.

---

# Indicators of Offensive Strength

Features that increase offensive values:

- Long suits
- Two-suited shape
- Singleton
- Void
- Excellent trump suit
- Fit with partner
- Good intermediates (10s and 9s)
- Playing Tricks

Offensive hands improve dramatically after discovering a fit.

---

# Indicators of Defensive Strength

Features that increase defensive values:

- Aces
- Protected Kings
- Balanced shape
- Controls
- Honors in several suits
- Quick Tricks

These hands often perform well defending notrump or suit contracts.

---

# Bidding Applications

## Opening Bids

Distributional hands often justify opening with fewer HCP.

Example:

```
♠ AKQJ96
♥ 75
♦ 64
♣ 873
```

Highly offensive despite only 10 HCP.

---

## Competitive Auctions

Offensive hands:

- Compete aggressively
- Raise partner freely
- Bid vulnerable games cautiously
- Consider sacrifices

Defensive hands:

- Prefer doubles
- Defend rather than sacrifice
- Penalize opponents

---

## Takeout Doubles

A good takeout double usually combines:

- Defensive values (opening strength)
- Offensive distribution (shortness in opponents' suit)

Both qualities are required.

---

## Penalty Doubles

Penalty doubles rely primarily on:

- Defensive values
- Quick tricks
- Controls
- Balanced strength

---

## Sacrifices

Hands suitable for sacrificing usually have:

- Excellent offensive values
- Poor defensive values
- Long trump fit
- Few quick tricks

# Practical Guidelines

### Lean Toward Bidding When

- Distribution is extreme.
- You have long, strong suits.
- You expect many Playing Tricks.
- A fit has been found.
- The hand improves with trump support.

### Lean Toward Defending When

- Honors are concentrated in Aces and Kings.
- The hand is balanced.
- You have several Quick Tricks.
- There is little ruffing potential.
- You hold defensive controls in multiple suits.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Valuing all HCP equally.
- Overvaluing balanced hands after a fit is found.
- Underestimating long suits.
- Ignoring shortness.
- Sacrificing with defensive hands.
- Doubling with offensive hands.

# Summary

Bridge hands are not judged solely by HCP. Some hands are **offensive**, relying on long suits, distribution, and trump tricks, while others are **defensive**, relying on controls and immediate winners. Successful players recognize the difference and adjust their bidding accordingly—competing aggressively with offensive hands and defending confidently with defensive hands.
