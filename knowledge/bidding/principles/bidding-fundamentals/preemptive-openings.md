# Preemptive Openings

## Overview

A **preemptive opening** is an opening bid at the **three level or higher** that shows a long suit, limited high-card strength, and the intention of making it difficult for the opponents to exchange information.

Unlike constructive openings, preempts emphasize **playing strength and distribution** rather than high-card points. Their primary objective is to consume bidding space while suggesting that the partnership may compete safely in the named suit.

Typical preemptive openings include:

- 3♣
- 3♦
- 3♥
- 3♠
- 4♣
- 4♦
- 4♥
- 4♠
- Higher-level natural preempts

The **Gambling 3NT** opening is treated separately because it has different requirements and objectives.

---

# Objectives

The objectives of a preemptive opening are to:

- Disrupt the opponents' auction.
- Consume valuable bidding space.
- Describe a long, self-sufficient suit.
- Compete effectively with distributional hands.
- Make life difficult for opponents seeking game or slam.

---

# Requirements

## Strength

Preemptive openings usually show:

- Approximately 5–10 HCP
- Strong playing strength
- Limited defensive values

Playing strength is more important than high-card points.

---

## Distribution

Typical distributions include:

- Seven-card suit
- Eight-card suit
- Occasionally a very strong six-card suit (partnership agreement)

Distribution should justify bidding at a high level.

---

## Suit Quality

A preempt suit should generally be:

- Long
- Robust
- Capable of producing tricks

Typical characteristics include:

- Two or three top honors
- Strong intermediates
- Few outside values

---

## Vulnerability

Vulnerability significantly influences preemptive style.

### Favorable Vulnerability

More aggressive preempts are appropriate.

### Equal Vulnerability

Normal style.

### Unfavorable Vulnerability

Require better suit quality and greater playing strength.

---

## Seat Position

### First Seat

Normal preemptive requirements.

### Second Seat

Slightly more disciplined style.

### Third Seat

Often the most aggressive position for preempting.

### Fourth Seat

Natural preempts are uncommon because both opponents have already passed.

---

# Hand Evaluation

Preemptive openings emphasize:

- Playing tricks
- Suit quality
- Distribution
- Offensive potential

rather than:

- Raw HCP
- Defensive strength

Useful evaluation tools include:

- Losing Trick Count
- Playing Tricks
- Offensive vs Defensive Values

---

# Responses

Responder should first evaluate:

- Trump support
- Combined playing strength
- Defensive values
- Vulnerability
- Likelihood of game

---

## Minimum Hands

Responder usually passes.

---

## Invitational Hands

Responder may:

- Raise preemptively.
- Bid a new suit naturally.
- Invite game when appropriate.

---

## Game-Going Hands

Responder may:

- Bid game directly.
- Explore game through feature-asking methods if played.

---

## Slam Interest

Slam is uncommon opposite a preempt but occasionally possible with exceptional support and controls.

---

# Opener Rebids

After responder acts, opener should continue describing:

- Suit quality
- Controls
- Outside values
- Playing strength

Because opener has already limited the hand, rebids are generally straightforward.

If the partnership uses **Feature Ask**, opener should answer according to partnership agreement.

---

# Responder Rebids

After opener's rebid, responder should reevaluate:

- Combined playing strength
- Trump quality
- Controls
- Vulnerability
- Offensive potential

Responder then chooses whether to:

- Sign off
- Bid game
- Continue investigating slam (rare)

---

# Partnership Agreements

Every partnership should agree on:

- Minimum suit length.
- Required suit quality.
- HCP range.
- Vulnerability adjustments.
- Third-seat style.
- Responses.
- Feature Ask.
- Competitive methods.

# Common Mistakes

## Preempting With Defensive Hands

Hands rich in defensive values are often unsuitable for preemptive openings.

---

## Weak Suit Quality

Long suits without trick-taking potential rarely make effective preempts.

---

## Ignoring Vulnerability

Aggressive unfavorable-vulnerability preempts frequently produce expensive penalties.

---

## Overvaluing High Cards

Distribution and playing tricks matter far more than raw HCP.

---

## Competing Automatically

Once the opponents compete, reevaluate rather than continuing the preempt automatically.

---

# Tips

- Count playing tricks before counting HCP.
- Let vulnerability influence your style.
- Favor offensive hands over defensive hands.
- Trust your long suit.
- Make preempts difficult for the opponents but safe for your partnership.

---

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

The responses and continuations described above assume an uncontested auction. In practice, however, preemptive openings almost always invite competition because their purpose is to disrupt the opponents' bidding.

When competition develops, the partnership must balance aggression with sound judgment.

The primary objectives remain:

- Make life difficult for the opponents.
- Compete only with sufficient offensive values.
- Avoid unnecessary penalties.
- Know when to defend rather than continue bidding.

---

## Opponent Overcalls

Opponents frequently overcall when they believe they have the balance of strength.

Example

```
3♥   (3♠)   ?
```

Responder may choose among:

- Pass
- Raise opener's suit
- Bid a new suit naturally
- Cue bid (partnership agreement)
- Double (when appropriate)

Every decision should be based on offensive potential rather than optimism.

---

## Opponent Doubles

Takeout doubles are common against preemptive openings.

Example

```
3♠   (Double)   ?
```

Typical responses include:

- Pass
- Raise opener's suit
- Natural new suits
- Redouble (if specifically agreed)

Partnerships should define these treatments before play.

---

## Competition After Responder's Action

Competition often continues after responder has acted.

Example

```
3♦
Pass
4♦
4♥
?
```

Both partners should now reevaluate:

- Trump fit
- Playing tricks
- Vulnerability
- Offensive versus defensive values
- Total trick expectation

The decision to compete further should be based on expected trick-taking potential rather than the original preempt.

---

## Feature Ask After Competition

If the partnership employs **Feature Ask**, interference may alter the available continuations.

Partnerships should agree whether:

- Feature Ask remains available.
- Natural bidding replaces conventional methods.
- Alternative continuations apply after interference.

---

## Sacrifice Decisions

Preemptive openings frequently lead to sacrifice decisions.

Before bidding again, consider:

- Vulnerability
- Expected number of tricks
- Opponents' likely contract
- Potential penalty if doubled

A successful sacrifice should cost less than allowing the opponents to make their contract.

---

## Balancing and Reopening

If the opponents appear ready to buy the contract, either partner may have an opportunity to balance.

Balancing decisions should consider:

- Distribution
- Playing strength
- Vulnerability
- Partnership style

---

## General Principles

When the opponents compete after a preemptive opening:

- Continue only with sufficient offensive values.
- Do not compete automatically.
- Reevaluate the hand after every bid.
- Consider vulnerability before sacrificing.
- Recognize when defending offers the better result.

# Summary

Preemptive openings are powerful tactical weapons that emphasize distribution, playing strength, and disruption rather than high-card points. Successful preempts consume valuable bidding space while accurately describing long suits and offensive potential. Because competitive auctions frequently follow preemptive openings, partnerships should establish clear agreements regarding responses, feature-asking methods, sacrifices, and vulnerability adjustments. Used with discipline and sound judgment, preemptive openings remain one of the most effective strategies in modern bridge.
