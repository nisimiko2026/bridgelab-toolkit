# Losing Trick Count (LTC)

## Overview

**Losing Trick Count (LTC)** is a hand evaluation method used primarily for **suit contracts** after a trump fit has been found. Unlike High Card Points (HCP), which measure honor strength, LTC estimates a hand's **offensive trick-taking potential** by counting likely losers.

LTC is particularly valuable when deciding whether to:

- Bid game.
- Explore slam.
- Compete in suit contracts.
- Evaluate distributional hands.

The guiding principle is:

> **The fewer losers your partnership has, the more tricks it is likely to take.**

---

# Purpose

LTC helps players to:

- Evaluate offensive strength.
- Judge game and slam potential.
- Reevaluate hands after finding a trump fit.
- Improve competitive bidding decisions.
- Estimate the partnership's total trick-taking ability.

---

# When to Use LTC

LTC is most effective when:

- A trump fit (usually **8 or more cards**) has been found.
- Both hands are relatively unbalanced.
- Evaluating suit contracts.

LTC is **not** recommended for:

- Notrump contracts.
- Highly balanced hands.
- Hands with fewer than three cards in several suits.

---

# Basic Principle

Only the **first three cards** in each suit are evaluated.

Each suit can contain a maximum of **three losers**.

Ignore all cards beyond the third card in a suit.

# Partnership LTC

After both hands are evaluated:

```
Responder

+

Opener

=

Combined Losers
```

Estimated tricks:

```
24

−

Combined Losers

=

Expected Tricks
```

Example:

```
Opener = 7 losers

Responder = 7 losers

Total = 14

24 − 14 = 10 tricks
```

A partnership with **14 losers** is expected to take approximately **10 tricks**, suggesting game in a major suit.

---

# Typical Guidelines

| Combined Losers | Expected Tricks | Typical Contract |
|----------------:|----------------:|------------------|
| 18 | 6 | Part-score |
| 17 | 7 | Part-score |
| 16 | 8 | Invitational |
| 15 | 9 | Game possible |
| 14 | 10 | Major-suit game |
| 13 | 11 | Small slam possible |
| 12 | 12 | Strong slam interest |

These are guidelines, not guarantees.

---

# Reevaluation After a Fit

Once partner supports your suit:

Upgrade:

- Voids.
- Singletons.
- Useful controls.

Downgrade:

- Unsupported queens.
- Honors in short side suits.

Distribution becomes more valuable than HCP.

---

# Strengths of LTC

LTC works particularly well for:

- Major-suit contracts.
- Distributional hands.
- Competitive auctions.
- Game and slam decisions.
- Hands with long suits.

---

# Limitations

LTC is less accurate for:

- Balanced hands.
- Notrump contracts.
- Misfitting hands.
- Extremely weak honor holdings.
- Hands with many unsupported queens and jacks.

Always combine LTC with judgment.

---

# Combining LTC with Other Methods

Expert players combine LTC with:

- High Card Points.
- Controls.
- Distribution.
- Rule of 20.
- Rule of 15.
- Partnership bidding.

No single evaluation method should be used alone.

Opener:

6 Losers

```

```
12 Losers
```

Estimated tricks:

```

A slam investigation is justified.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Using LTC before a trump fit exists.
- Counting more than three cards in a suit.
- Applying LTC to Notrump contracts.
- Ignoring suit quality.
- Relying solely on LTC without considering controls or partnership bidding.

---

# Advantages

LTC:

- Measures offensive potential.
- Handles distribution better than HCP.
- Excellent for suit contracts.
- Simple to calculate.
- Widely used by expert players.

---

# Disadvantages

- Less effective for balanced hands.
- Ignores intermediate cards.
- Not suitable for Notrump.
- Requires a known trump fit.
- Should not replace judgment.

# Player's Checklist

Before using LTC, ask:

- Have we found a trump fit?
- Is this a suit contract?
- Have I counted only the first three cards in each suit?
- Have I combined both hands' loser counts?
- Have I considered controls and distribution?

---

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Evaluate only the first three cards in each suit.
2. Count losers in each suit.
3. Add the losers for your hand.
4. Combine with partner's loser count.
5. Estimate tricks using **24 − Combined Losers**.

# Memory Aid

**Losing Trick Count = "Count Losers, Predict Winners."**

Remember:

- **LTC is designed for suit contracts after a trump fit has been found.**
- **Only the first three cards in each suit are evaluated.**
- **Estimate partnership tricks with the formula: 24 − Combined Losers.**
- **Distribution and controls become increasingly important once a fit is established.**
- **Use LTC together with HCP, controls, and bidding judgment for the most accurate evaluation.**

The **Losing Trick Count (LTC)** is one of the most effective methods for evaluating offensive strength in suit contracts. By focusing on likely losers instead of honor points, LTC provides a practical estimate of a partnership's trick-taking potential, making it an invaluable tool for competitive bidding, game decisions, and slam exploration.
