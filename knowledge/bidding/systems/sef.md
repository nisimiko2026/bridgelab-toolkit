# SEF (Système d'Enseignement Français)

## Overview

**SEF (Système d'Enseignement Français)** is the official standard bidding system taught and promoted by the **French Bridge Federation (Fédération Française de Bridge – FFB)**. It is by far the most widely played system in France and serves as the foundation for bridge instruction, club play, and many national competitions.

SEF is a **natural system** featuring **five-card major openings**, a **strong notrump**, and modern bidding methods. While comparable to Standard American and 2/1 Game Force, it incorporates several uniquely French treatments and standardized continuations.

---

# System Characteristics

- Natural bidding
- Five-card major openings
- Strong notrump
- Natural minor openings
- Standardized teaching system
- Modern competitive methods
- Scientific slam bidding
- Strong emphasis on partnership agreements

---

# Basic Opening Structure

| Opening | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| **1♣** | Natural, 3+ clubs |
| **1♦** | Natural, 3+ diamonds |
| **1♥** | Natural, 5+ hearts |
| **1♠** | Natural, 5+ spades |
| **1NT** | Balanced, 15–17 HCP |
| **2♣** | Strong artificial, game forcing or nearly so |
| **2♦** | Multi-purpose strong opening (commonly forcing, often based on a strong unbalanced hand) |
| **2♥** | Weak Two |
| **2♠** | Weak Two |
| **2NT** | Balanced, 20–21 HCP |
| **3-level** | Preemptive |

> **Note:** One distinctive feature of SEF is the treatment of the **2♦ opening**, which differs from Standard American and varies slightly between editions of the official SEF manual.

# Notrump Opening

## 1NT

Shows:

- Balanced hand
- **15–17 HCP**

Typical shapes:

- 4-3-3-3
- 4-4-3-2
- 5-3-3-2

With a balanced 18–19 HCP hand, opener generally starts with a suit and rebids **2NT**.

---

# Strong Openings

## 2♣

Artificial.

Shows:

- Very strong hand
- Normally game forcing

---

## 2♦

Artificial.

Typically shows:

- Strong unbalanced hand
- Forcing opening
- Usually based on substantial playing strength rather than pure HCP

The exact continuation structure is defined by the official SEF methods.

---

# Weak Two Openings

## 2♥ / 2♠

Show:

- Six-card suit
- Approximately 5–10 HCP
- Preemptive intent

Weak **2♦** is **not** part of standard SEF.

# Responses to 1NT

Standard treatments include:

- Stayman
- Jacoby Transfers
- Texas Transfers
- Minor-suit Stayman (partnership agreement)
- Quantitative invitations

Common methods include:

- Jacoby 2NT
- Splinter bids

- Two-over-One Game Force (widely used in advanced partnerships)

# Legacy

SEF has become one of the world's most successful national teaching systems. By providing a standardized framework for French bridge players, it has greatly simplified partnership formation and instruction while remaining compatible with modern tournament methods.

# Summary

**SEF (Système d'Enseignement Français)** is the official French natural bidding system. Built around **five-card majors**, a **15–17 HCP notrump**, and standardized modern methods, it provides a consistent framework for bridge education and tournament play throughout France. While broadly similar to Standard American and 2/1 Game Force, its treatment of **strong two-level openings**, particularly **2♦**, gives the system a distinctive French character.
