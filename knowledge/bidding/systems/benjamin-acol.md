# Benjamin Acol (Benjaminised Acol / Benji Acol)

## Overview

**Benjamin Acol** (commonly called **Benji Acol** or **Benjaminised Acol**) is a variation of the standard Acol bidding system developed by Scottish bridge expert **Albert Benjamin**. Its primary goal is to retain both strong two-bids and weak two-bids without increasing the complexity of the system. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

The main innovation is reallocating the strong opening bids:

- **2♣** = Strong one-suited hand (about 8 playing tricks)
- **2♦** = Very strong game-forcing hand (23+ HCP or equivalent)
- **2♥ / 2♠** = Weak Two openings

This allows Acol partnerships to enjoy the preemptive value of weak twos while still preserving strong artificial openings.

---

# Why Play Benjamin Acol?

Advantages include:

- Keeps the natural four-card major structure of Acol.
- Adds effective Weak Two openings.
- Makes competitive bidding more disruptive.
- Strong hands remain accurately described.
- Widely used in tournament Acol partnerships.

---

# Opening Structure

| Opening | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| 1-level bids | Standard Acol |
| 1NT | 12–14 balanced |
| 2♣ | Strong one-suited hand (≈8 playing tricks) |
| 2♦ | Artificial game force (≈23+ HCP) |
| 2♥ | Weak Two |
| 2♠ | Weak Two |
| 2NT | 20–22 balanced (partnership agreement) |
| 3-level | Preemptive |

---

# 2♣ Opening

Shows:

- about **8 playing tricks**
- strong one-suited hand
- not necessarily game-forcing immediately

Examples:

♠ AKQJ98 ♥ AK7 ♦ A4 ♣ 54

or

♠ A ♥ AKQJ1098 ♦ AK4 ♣ 73

---

# Responses to 2♣

Standard structure:

| Responder | Meaning |
|------------|---------|
| 2♦ | Forced relay |
| Any other bid | Rare partnership agreement |

After:

2♣ – 2♦

Opener now shows the real suit.

Example:

2♣ – 2♦

2♠ = strong spade hand

2♥ = strong heart hand

2NT = strong balanced (if played)

---

# 2♦ Opening

Artificial.

Shows:

- approximately **23+ HCP**
- or any game-forcing hand unsuitable for 2♣

Typical hands:

- 24 balanced
- 26 balanced
- huge two-suiter
- massive single-suiter lacking 8 playing tricks

---

# Responses to 2♦

| Response | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| 2♥ | Negative (0–7 HCP) |
| 2♠ | Positive |
| 2NT | Positive |
| 3-level | Positive with suit |

Many partnerships simply use:

- 2♥ = waiting / negative

and continue naturally.

---

# Weak Two Openings

## 2♥

Shows

- 6-card heart suit
- about 6–10 HCP
- normal Weak Two requirements

## 2♠

Same.

---

# Responses to Weak Twos

Common agreements:

### 2NT

Ogust enquiry.

Responder asks opener to describe hand quality.

Typical replies:

| Reply | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| 3♣ | Weak hand + poor suit |
| 3♦ | Weak hand + good suit |
| 3♥ | Maximum hand + poor suit |
| 3♠ | Maximum hand + good suit |
| 3NT | AKQ suit (optional agreement) |

Some partnerships instead use Feature Ask.

---

# Playing Tricks vs High Card Points

Benjamin Acol relies heavily on **playing tricks**.

Approximate guide:

| Playing Tricks | Typical Strength |
|----------------|-----------------|
| 8 | Strong 2♣ |
| 9 | Near game opposite bust |
| 10 | Slam interest |

This differs from pure HCP evaluation.

# Comparison with Standard Acol

| Standard Acol | Benjamin Acol |
|---------------|---------------|
| 2♣ = strongest opening | 2♣ = 8 playing tricks |
| 2♦ = strong diamonds (rare) | 2♦ = artificial game force |
| No Weak Twos | Weak 2♥/2♠ |
| Less preemptive | Much more preemptive |

---

# Reverse Benji

Some partnerships play **Reverse Benji**:

- 2♦ = 8 playing tricks
- 2♣ = game force

The philosophy is identical; only the artificial openings are exchanged.

---

# Advantages

- Excellent competitive style.
- Strong preemptive effect.
- Keeps strong openings.
- Easy extension to modern conventions.
- Very popular among experienced Acol players.

---

# Disadvantages

- Artificial 2♣/2♦ require memory.
- Slightly more complex than basic Acol.
- Partnership agreements are essential.

---

# Typical Convention Card

- 1NT: 12–14
- Four-card majors
- Weak 2♥ / 2♠
- 2♣ = 8 playing tricks
- 2♦ = Game force
- Stayman
- Transfers
- Negative Doubles
- Fourth Suit Forcing
- Blackwood / RKCB
- Ogust over Weak Twos

---

# Summary

Benjamin Acol modernizes traditional Acol by replacing infrequent strong two-bids with a more efficient structure. It preserves strong artificial openings while adding highly effective Weak Two bids, making the system both more competitive and more flexible. It is one of the most widely played tournament variants of Acol in the UK and other Acol-playing countries. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}
