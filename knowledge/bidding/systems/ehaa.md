# EHAA (Every Hand An Adventure)

## Overview

**EHAA (Every Hand An Adventure)** is a highly aggressive natural bidding system developed in Australia by **Eric Landau** and **Ron Klinger** during the 1970s. The system is designed to maximize constructive and competitive bidding by using **very light opening bids** and **wide-ranging hand evaluations**.

Unlike standard natural systems, EHAA encourages opening many hands that would be passes in other methods, making it difficult for opponents to enter the auction while allowing the partnership to compete more effectively.

Although never as widely adopted as Acol, Standard American, or Precision, EHAA has had a lasting influence on aggressive bidding styles and remains popular among some Australian partnerships.

---

# System Philosophy

The fundamental principles of EHAA are:

- Natural bidding
- Extremely light opening bids
- Frequent competitive auctions
- Five-card major openings
- Strong notrump
- Distribution valued highly
- Shape often more important than HCP
- Aggressive preemptive style

The name **"Every Hand An Adventure"** reflects the willingness to enter the auction with borderline opening values.

# Opening Requirements

One of EHAA's defining features is **very light openings**.

Typical opening values:

- Around **8–10 HCP** with excellent shape
- Normal openings with **10–12 HCP**
- Standard opening hands
- Distributional hands with fewer points

Distribution frequently compensates for low point count.

Example:

♠ KQJ97  
♥ J9842  
♦ A6  
♣ 73

Many EHAA partnerships would consider opening **1♠**.

# Advantages

- Wins many part-score battles
- Difficult for opponents to judge
- Makes effective use of distribution
- Highly competitive style
- Encourages active bidding

---

# Disadvantages

- Greater risk of overbidding
- Requires excellent partnership judgment
- More vulnerable to penalties when aggressive openings are unsound
- Not ideal for inexperienced partnerships

# Legacy

EHAA demonstrated that opening bids need not be restricted to traditional point-count requirements. Its emphasis on **shape, initiative, and competitive pressure** anticipated many modern aggressive bidding styles. While relatively uncommon outside Australia, its influence can be seen in contemporary expert partnerships that routinely open light in first or third seat.

# Summary

EHAA (**Every Hand An Adventure**) is an aggressive natural system emphasizing **light opening bids**, **distributional values**, and **competitive auctions**. Developed in Australia, it combines a standard five-card-major framework with an adventurous opening philosophy, making it well suited to experienced partnerships that value active bidding and competitive pressure.
