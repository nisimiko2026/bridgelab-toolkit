# MOSCITO

## Overview

**MOSCITO** (**Major-Oriented Strong Club International Tournament Organization**) is one of the world's best-known **strong club relay systems**. Developed by **Paul Marston** and **Stephen Burgess** in Australia during the 1980s, it combines a **strong artificial 1♣ opening**, **five-card majors**, and an extensive **relay structure** to describe hands with exceptional precision.

MOSCITO gained international recognition through the success of Australian teams and expert partnerships. It is regarded as one of the most sophisticated relay systems in tournament bridge and is intended for experienced partnerships willing to invest significant study.

---

# System Characteristics

- Strong artificial **1♣**
- Five-card majors
- Limited natural openings
- Extensive relay methods
- Strong notrump
- Symmetrical hand description
- Highly descriptive slam bidding
- Scientific competitive structure

# Basic Opening Structure

| Opening | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| **1♣** | Artificial, strong (16+ HCP or equivalent) |
| **1♦** | Limited natural or semi-artificial, depending on partnership style |
| **1♥** | Natural, 5+ hearts, limited |
| **1♠** | Natural, 5+ spades, limited |
| **1NT** | Balanced, 15–17 HCP |
| **2♣** | Natural clubs (or partnership-defined) |
| **2♦** | Weak Two |
| **2♥** | Weak Two |
| **2♠** | Weak Two |
| **2NT** | Balanced, 20–21 HCP |
| **3-level** | Preemptive |

> **Note:** Modern MOSCITO partnerships use several different versions. Exact meanings of the **1♦** and **2♣** openings vary, but the **strong 1♣** and relay philosophy are consistent across versions.

# The Strong Club

## 1♣

Artificial.

Shows:

- Approximately **16+ HCP**, or
- Equivalent playing strength
- Any distribution

The opening promises a hand too strong for the limited one-level natural openings.

Example:

♠ AKQ7  
♥ AQ4  
♦ AK6  
♣ K84

Open:

**1♣**

---

# Responses to 1♣

MOSCITO employs an extensive **relay-based response structure**.

A simplified outline is:

| Response | Typical Meaning |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **1♦** | Negative / relay |
| **1♥** | Positive response |
| **1♠** | Positive response |
| **1NT** | Positive balanced hand |
| **2♣ / 2♦** | Positive with specific suit patterns |

After the initial response, numerous relay sequences are used to identify:

- Distribution
- Strength
- Controls
- Suit lengths
- Singletons and voids
- Slam potential

Exact relay structures differ between partnerships.

---

# Limited Natural Openings

Natural one-level openings generally show:

- Approximately **11–15 HCP**
- Defined suit length
- Limited strength

Because stronger hands begin with **1♣**, responder can judge game prospects quickly.

## 1♥

Shows:

- Approximately 11–15 HCP

Show:

- Approximately 6–10 HCP

# Relay Philosophy

The defining feature of MOSCITO is its extensive use of **relays**.

A relay bid asks partner to provide additional information according to an agreed step structure.

Relays efficiently describe:

- Exact shape
- Strength
- Controls
- Singletons
- Voids
- Suit quality
- Slam potential

This allows expert partnerships to exchange far more information than standard natural methods while remaining below game.

# Competitive Methods

Modern MOSCITO partnerships generally use:

- Negative Doubles
- Support Doubles
- Responsive Doubles
- Michaels Cue Bid
- Unusual 2NT
- Lebensohl
- Rubensohl
- Weak Jump Overcalls

---

# Slam Methods

MOSCITO excels in slam bidding.

Typical agreements include:

- Roman Key Card Blackwood
- Cue Bidding
- Control Bids
- Serious 3NT
- Last Train
- Extensive relay continuations

Because shape and controls are identified accurately, slam exploration is one of the system's greatest strengths.

# Advantages

- Extremely accurate hand description
- Outstanding slam bidding
- Strong relay structure
- Limited natural openings simplify responder's evaluation
- Excellent for expert partnerships
- Powerful scientific methods

---

# Disadvantages

- Very memory-intensive
- Large number of partnership agreements
- Difficult for occasional partnerships
- Long relay sequences require extensive practice
- Opponents may employ specialized defenses against the strong club

# Legacy

MOSCITO is widely regarded as one of the most sophisticated relay systems ever developed. Although relatively few partnerships play it in its full form, many of its relay concepts and descriptive techniques have influenced other advanced strong-club systems and modern expert bidding methods.

# Summary

MOSCITO (**Major-Oriented Strong Club International Tournament Organization**) is an advanced **strong club relay system** that combines a **strong artificial 1♣ opening**, **five-card majors**, and highly structured relay sequences to achieve exceptionally accurate hand description and slam bidding. Designed for expert partnerships, it remains one of the most sophisticated and influential relay-based bidding systems in modern bridge.
