# Culbertson System

## Overview

The **Culbertson System** was the first widely successful contract bridge bidding system and dominated tournament bridge during the **1930s and early 1940s**. It was developed by **Ely Culbertson**, whose books, magazine articles, and international bridge matches helped popularize contract bridge around the world.

Unlike modern systems, Culbertson emphasized **honor tricks**, **quick tricks**, and **playing tricks** rather than simply counting High Card Points (HCP). Although the system is no longer played in serious competition, its concepts profoundly influenced modern bidding theory.

---

# Historical Background

When contract bridge replaced auction bridge in the late 1920s, there was no universally accepted bidding system. Ely Culbertson introduced a comprehensive approach based on scientific evaluation and partnership agreements.

During the famous **Culbertson–Lenz Match (1931)**, Culbertson's team defeated Sidney Lenz's team, bringing enormous publicity to both contract bridge and the Culbertson System. By the late 1930s, it had become the dominant system worldwide before gradually being replaced by Goren, Acol, and other modern methods.

---

# Basic Philosophy

The Culbertson System is based on:

- Honor tricks rather than HCP
- Playing tricks
- Quick tricks
- Natural bidding
- Four-card major openings
- Strong emphasis on suit quality
- Descriptive rebids
- Judgment over rigid point-count methods

---

# Hand Evaluation

Instead of counting HCP, Culbertson evaluated hands using **Honor Tricks (HT)**.

Typical values:

| Holding | Honor Tricks |
|----------|-------------:|
| AK | 2 |
| AQ | 1½ |
| A | 1 |
| KQ | 1 |
| K | ½ |
| QJ | ½ |

Partnerships also considered:

- Distribution
- Suit texture
- Playing tricks
- Defensive strength
- Controls

These concepts remain relevant in modern bridge, especially for slam bidding.

# Opening Bids

### 1♣

Natural.

Shows:

- Clubs
- Opening strength
- Usually at least 3 clubs

---

### 1♦

Natural.

Shows:

- Diamonds
- Opening strength

---

### 1♥ / 1♠

Natural.

Shows:

- Four or more cards in the suit
- Opening values

Unlike modern Standard American, **four-card major openings were standard**.

---

### 1NT

Shows:

- Balanced hand
- Approximately 16–18 HCP equivalent
- Exact ranges varied over time and by partnership

---

# Strong Two Bids

The Culbertson System used **strong natural two-level openings**.

### 2♣

Strong natural clubs.

### 2♦

Strong natural diamonds.

### 2♥

Strong natural hearts.

### 2♠

Strong natural spades.

These openings showed powerful hands with long suits and significant trick-taking potential.

Weak Two openings had not yet become standard.

---

# Responses

Responses emphasized:

- Honor tricks
- Fit
- Playing strength
- Suit quality

Responder frequently introduced a new suit naturally before supporting opener's suit.

Limit raises and forcing raises were less standardized than today.

---

# Slam Bidding

One of the strengths of the Culbertson System was slam investigation.

The system stressed:

- Controls
- Aces
- Kings
- Quick tricks
- Suit establishment

Many modern cue-bidding principles evolved from these ideas.

---

# Defensive Bidding

Overcalls were:

- Natural
- Conservative
- Based on suit quality

Takeout doubles existed but were less sophisticated than modern treatments.

Competitive conventions such as Michaels Cue Bid, Unusual 2NT, and Negative Doubles had not yet been developed.

# Advantages

- Excellent emphasis on judgment
- Strong evaluation of trick-taking potential
- Natural bidding
- Sound slam principles
- Historically revolutionary

---

# Disadvantages

- Complex honor-trick calculations
- Less precise than HCP methods
- No Weak Two openings
- Limited competitive bidding tools
- Superseded by more efficient modern systems

---

# Historical Importance

Many ideas introduced or popularized by Culbertson remain part of modern bridge:

- Hand evaluation beyond HCP
- Playing tricks
- Honor tricks
- Control evaluation
- Slam investigation
- Partnership agreements
- Scientific bidding

Although the bidding structure itself is obsolete, its theoretical foundations continue to influence expert bidding.

---

# Legacy

The Culbertson System dominated world bridge for more than a decade and established many of the principles that underpin modern bidding. While the detailed methods have been replaced by newer systems, its concepts of hand evaluation, partnership understanding, and scientific bidding remain fundamental to contract bridge.

---

# Typical Convention Card (Historical)

- Four-card majors
- Natural minor openings
- Strong natural two-bids
- Strong notrump
- Honor Trick evaluation
- Natural overcalls
- Takeout doubles
- Slam bidding based on controls and quick tricks

---

# Summary

The Culbertson System was the first internationally successful bidding system for contract bridge. Developed by **Ely Culbertson**, it emphasized **honor tricks**, **playing tricks**, and **natural bidding**, laying the groundwork for many principles still used today. Although no longer played in modern tournament bridge, its historical significance and influence on bridge theory are immense.
