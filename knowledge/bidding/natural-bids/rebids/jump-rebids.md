# Jump Rebids

## Overview

A **jump rebid** occurs when a player intentionally skips one or more bidding
levels while making a natural rebid.

Examples

```
1♣   1♠
3♣
```

```
1♦   1♥
3♦
```

```
1♥   1♠
3♣
```

```
1♠   2♣
3♥
```

Unlike simple rebids, jump rebids emphasize

- extra strength,
- additional playing tricks,
- offensive potential,
- interest in game.

Although the exact meanings vary by partnership agreement, jump rebids almost
always show **more than a minimum opening**.

---

# Objectives

A jump rebid aims to

- describe extra strength,
- narrow the hand,
- encourage game bidding,
- investigate slam,
- consume bidding space efficiently.

---

# General Principles

A jump rebid generally shows

- extra values,
- an offensive hand,
- interest beyond partscore.

The exact strength depends upon

- the auction,
- the suit rebid,
- partnership agreement.

---

# Types of Jump Rebids

The most common jump rebids are

- Jump Rebid of Opener's Suit
- Jump Shift
- Jump Reverse
- Jump Notrump Rebid
- Jump Raise of Responder's Suit

Each has a different meaning.

---

# Jump Rebid of Opener's Suit

Example

```
1♠
2♣
3♠
```

(Simple rebid)

compared with

```
1♠
2♣
4♠
```

(Jump rebid)

A jump rebid of opener's suit usually promises

- six or more cards,
- extra playing strength,
- game interest.

Typical strength

- approximately 16–18 HCP,
- or equivalent playing strength.

---

# Jump Shift

Example

```
1♦
1♠
3♣
```

Shows

- extra strength,
- a second suit,
- an unbalanced hand.

A jump shift is stronger than a simple new-suit rebid.

---

# Jump Reverse

Example

```
1♣
1♠
3♥
```

A jump reverse normally shows

- significant extra values,
- at least 5-4 distribution,
- strong game interest.

Many partnerships treat it as forcing to game.

---

# Jump Raise of Responder's Suit

Example

```
1♣
1♥
3♥
```

Usually shows

- four-card support,
- approximately 16–18 support points,
- invitational or stronger values.

Modern systems may replace this with other conventions.

---

# Jump Notrump Rebid

Example

```
1♣
1♠
2NT
```

Usually shows

- 18–19 balanced HCP.

A true jump

```
1♣
1♠
3NT
```

typically shows

- a balanced hand,
- long-running suit,
- no slam interest.

Exact meanings vary.

---

# Hand Evaluation

Jump rebids depend upon

- High Card Points,
- controls,
- playing tricks,
- distribution,
- suit quality,
- offensive potential.

Distribution often justifies upgrading.

---

# Suit Quality

A jump rebid in a suit generally promises

- excellent suit quality,
- strong honors,
- good trick-taking ability.

Typical holdings

```
AKQJxx
AQJxxx
KQJxxx
```

---

# Playing Tricks

Experts emphasize

- playing tricks,
- controls,
- offensive potential

rather than HCP alone.

A long solid suit frequently justifies a jump rebid.

---

# Balanced Hands

Balanced hands usually jump only in notrump.

Typical ranges

| Rebid | Typical Strength |
|--------|-----------------:|
| 2NT | 18–19 HCP |
| 3NT | Long-running source of tricks |

---

# Unbalanced Hands

Distributional hands frequently employ

- jump shifts,
- jump reverses,
- jump suit rebids.

These hands often contain

- extra playing tricks,
- strong controls,
- excellent suits.

---

# Forcing Nature

Partnership agreements vary.

Common treatments

| Jump Rebid | Typical Status |
|------------|----------------|
| Jump Suit Rebid | Invitational or stronger |
| Jump Shift | Game forcing |
| Jump Reverse | Game forcing |
| Jump Raise | Invitational or stronger |
| Jump NT | Natural |

---

# Competitive Auctions

After interference,

jump rebids become less descriptive.

Some partnerships use them

- preemptively,
- competitively,
- as fit-showing bids.

Detailed agreements are recommended.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should define

- minimum strength,
- forcing status,
- jump shifts,
- jump reverses,
- jump raises,
- jump notrump rebids,
- competitive meanings.

---

# Modern Expert Style

Expert partnerships emphasize

- descriptive bidding,
- playing tricks,
- distribution,
- controls,
- efficient space usage.

Many traditional jump rebids have been replaced by artificial conventions in
specific auctions, but the underlying principles remain the same.

# Common Mistakes

## Jumping with Minimum Hands

Jump rebids promise extra strength.

---

## Ignoring Distribution

Playing strength often matters more than HCP.

---

## Overusing Jump Rebids

Many hands are described more accurately with simple rebids.

---

## Forgetting Partnership Agreements

Jump shifts and jump reverses vary considerably.

---

## Misjudging Suit Quality

Poor suits rarely justify jumping.

# Expert Evaluation

Experts evaluate

- controls,
- playing tricks,
- suit quality,
- offensive potential,
- honor concentration,
- distribution,

before choosing a jump rebid.

# Universal Principles

A jump rebid should

- show extra strength,
- accurately describe the hand,
- encourage constructive bidding,
- preserve partnership understanding,
- move efficiently toward the correct contract.

# Summary

A **jump rebid** is a powerful descriptive tool that communicates **extra
strength**, **offensive potential**, and **interest in reaching game or slam**.
Whether it is a jump rebid of opener's suit, a jump shift, a jump reverse, a
jump raise, or a jump notrump rebid, the common theme is that opener has more
than a minimum opening and wishes to convey this immediately.

Modern bidding systems continue to use jump rebids selectively, often replacing
some traditional sequences with specialized conventions. Nevertheless, the
principles of **accurate hand evaluation**, **good suit quality**, **playing
tricks**, and **clear partnership agreements** remain fundamental to successful
use of jump rebids.
