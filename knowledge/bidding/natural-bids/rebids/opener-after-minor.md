# Opener After a Minor-Suit Opening

## Overview

The opener's rebid after opening **1♣** or **1♦** is one of the most important
descriptive stages of the auction.

Unlike the opening bid, opener now has valuable information from responder's
first call and can begin describing

- strength,
- distribution,
- suit quality,
- support,
- game interest.

The primary objective is to place the partnership on the correct path toward

- partscore,
- game,
- slam,
- or occasionally the best sacrifice.

Although every natural bidding system has its own agreements, the underlying
principles remain remarkably consistent.

---

# Objectives

The opener's rebid should answer as many of the following questions as possible.

- How strong is opener?
- What is opener's exact shape?
- Is there a fit?
- Is notrump likely?
- Is game probable?
- Should slam be explored?

---

# Information Available

Before making the rebid opener knows

- responder's first bid,
- approximate strength,
- probable distribution,
- whether responder is forcing,
- whether a major-suit fit exists.

This additional information allows opener to narrow the description
considerably.

---

# General Priorities

Most natural systems follow this priority.

1. Support responder's major.
2. Show a second suit.
3. Rebid the original minor.
4. Bid notrump.
5. Make a strength-showing jump.

---

# Strength Ranges

## Minimum

Approximately

12–14 HCP

No significant extras.

---

## Medium

Approximately

15–17 HCP

Good playing strength.

---

## Strong

18–19 HCP

Usually invites game.

---

## Very Strong

20+ HCP

Normally forces game.

---

# Supporting Responder

Finding a major-suit fit has the highest priority.

---

## Four-Card Support

Example

```
1♦ — 1♠
2♠
```

Shows

- four-card support
- minimum values
- no stronger descriptive rebid

---

## Jump Raise

Shows

- extra values
- excellent support
- invitational or better

(System dependent.)

---

## Game Raise

Shows

- sufficient values for game
- strong trump support

---

# Showing a Second Suit

Without support, opener usually introduces another suit.

Requirements

- normally four cards
- natural
- may be longer than the opening minor

Example

```
1♣ — 1♠
2♦
```

Shows

- at least four diamonds
- natural
- minimum or medium values

---

# Rebidding the Minor

Rebidding the opening suit generally shows

- six-card suit

or

- no better descriptive rebid.

Example

```
1♦
1♠
2♦
```

---

## Jump Rebid

```
1♣
1♠
3♣
```

Shows

- strong six-card suit
- extra playing strength
- invitational or better

---

# Reverse Bids

A reverse promises

- extra strength,
- unbalanced hand,
- interest in further bidding.

Example

```
1♣
1♠
2♥
```

Normally shows

- at least 16+ HCP
- longer clubs than hearts
- forcing for one round

Exact strength depends on the system.

---

# Notrump Rebids

Balanced hands frequently rebid notrump.

---

## 1NT

Shows

- balanced
- approximately 12–14 HCP
- stopper(s)
- no support
- no suitable second suit

Example

```
1♣
1♥
1NT
```

---

## 2NT

Shows

- balanced
- approximately 18–19 HCP

Usually invites game.

---

## 3NT

Shows

- balanced game values
- no slam investigation implied

---

# Jump Shifts

A jump shift into a new suit generally shows

- strong hand
- excellent suit
- game-forcing values

Some partnerships use alternative treatments.

---

# Rebids After Different Responses

## After 1♥ or 1♠

Priority

1. Raise major
2. Show second suit
3. Notrump
4. Rebid minor

---

## After 1NT

Clarify

- strength
- shape
- second suit
- long minor

---

## After Minor Response

Decide whether

- notrump,
- minor fit,
- second suit,
- reverse,

best describes the hand.

---

## After Raise of Minor

Evaluate

- fit quality
- controls
- distribution
- possibility of notrump
- game prospects

Minor games require eleven tricks, so notrump is often preferable.

---

# Balanced Hands

Balanced hands emphasize

- accurate notrump rebids,
- limiting strength,
- preserving bidding space.

---

# Unbalanced Hands

Distribution frequently outweighs HCP.

Hands with

- six-card suit,
- second suit,
- singleton,
- void,

should describe shape naturally.

---

# Minor-Suit Philosophy

Minor openings are often exploratory.

The partnership continues searching for

- a major fit,
- notrump,
- the best minor contract.

Unlike major openings, a minor fit alone does not guarantee the best contract.

---

# After Game Force

Once game is forced

opener should focus on

- strain,
- controls,
- cue bids,
- key cards,
- slam investigation.

---

# After Fourth Suit Forcing

Opener clarifies

- stopper(s),
- distribution,
- delayed support,
- extra strength.

---

# After Two-over-One

In 2/1 systems

- opener usually keeps the auction forcing,
- avoids unnecessary jumps,
- emphasizes accurate description.

# Common Mistakes

## Missing a Major Fit

Always support responder before introducing another descriptive bid whenever
appropriate.

---

## Overusing Notrump

Balanced hands should bid notrump.

Unbalanced hands should describe distribution.

---

## Rebidding Weak Five-Card Minors

Avoid repeating a weak five-card suit when another natural rebid better
describes the hand.

---

## Reversing Without Extra Strength

A reverse promises considerably more than a minimum opening.

---

## Ignoring Distribution

Shape often matters more than one or two high-card points.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partnerships should agree on

- reverse strength,
- jump rebids,
- jump shifts,
- fourth-suit forcing,
- inverted minors,
- support with three cards,
- Walsh style,
- two-over-one structure,
- notrump rebid ranges,
- cue-bidding methods.

---

# System Differences

## Standard American

- Natural rebids
- Five-card majors
- Reverse shows extra values

---

## SAYC

- Similar to Standard American
- Strong jump shifts
- Standard notrump ranges

---

## 2/1 Game Force

- More game-forcing auctions
- Slower descriptive bidding
- Better distinction between minimum and extras

---

## Acol

- Four-card majors possible
- Slightly different rebid priorities
- Reverse requirements may vary

---

## Precision

- Artificial 1♣ opening
- Natural rebids after 1♦
- Similar rebid principles following natural minor openings

---

## Polish Club

- Artificial club opening
- Natural rebids after 1♦
- Similar descriptive philosophy after natural minor auctions

---

# Principles

Always

- support a major before showing another suit,
- describe shape naturally,
- limit the hand whenever possible,
- preserve bidding space,
- distinguish minimum from extra values,
- cooperate once game is forced.

---

# Summary

The opener's rebid after opening a minor progressively defines the hand's
strength and distribution while helping the partnership answer three critical
questions:

- Is there a major-suit fit?
- Is notrump the best strain?
- Should the partnership play partscore, game, or slam?

Accurate rebids form the foundation of successful natural bidding systems and
allow both partners to make well-informed decisions throughout the remainder of
the auction.
