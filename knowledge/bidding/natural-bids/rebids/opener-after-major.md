# Opener After a Major-Suit Opening

## Overview

The opener's rebid after opening **1♥** or **1♠** is one of the most
important decisions in natural bidding systems.

Unlike the opening bid, the rebid occurs after opener has considerably more
information about responder's hand.

The rebid should

- describe strength,
- clarify distribution,
- limit or expand the range,
- establish the strain,
- guide the partnership toward the proper contract.

---

# Objectives

The opener's rebid attempts to answer four questions:

1. How strong is opener?
2. What is opener's exact distribution?
3. Is game possible?
4. Which denomination should be played?

---

# Information Available

Before rebidding, opener knows

- responder's first bid,
- responder's approximate strength,
- responder's likely distribution,
- whether a fit exists,
- whether game is possible.

---

# Strength Categories

## Minimum

12–14 HCP

Usually no extras.

---

## Medium

15–17 HCP

Good playing strength.

---

## Strong

18–19 HCP

Near forcing values.

---

## Very Strong

20+ HCP

Usually requires forcing action.

---

# General Priorities

The normal order is

1. Support partner.
2. Show a second suit.
3. Rebid the original suit.
4. Bid notrump.
5. Make a strength-showing jump.

---

# Supporting Responder

Support has highest priority.

With

- four-card support,
- sometimes good three-card support,

opener normally raises immediately.

---

## Simple Raise

Example

```
1♠ — 2♣
2♠
```

Shows

- minimum
- support
- no extra values

---

## Jump Raise

Shows

- extra values
- strong support
- invitational or better

(System dependent.)

---

## Game Raise

Shows

- enough values for game
- usually four-card support

---

# Showing a Second Suit

Without support, opener next looks for another suit.

Requirements

- normally four cards
- good quality preferred
- natural

Example

```
1♥ — 1♠
2♦
```

Shows

- at least 4 diamonds
- minimum or medium values

---

## Reverse

Example

```
1♦ — 1♠
2♥
```

(Not a major opening example, but illustrates the concept.)

A reverse shows

- extra strength
- unbalanced hand

When opener began with a major, reverses occur less often but remain important.

---

# Rebidding the Major

When no better description exists

```
1♠ — 2♣
2♠
```

Usually shows

- six-card suit
- minimum hand

---

## Jump Rebid

Example

```
1♥ — 1♠
3♥
```

Shows

- strong six-card suit
- extra values
- invitational or better

---

# Notrump Rebids

Notrump describes

- balanced hands
- stoppers
- limited distribution

---

## 1NT Rebid

Possible after

```
1♥ — 1♠
1NT
```

Shows

- balanced
- minimum
- no support
- no second suit

---

## 2NT

Shows

- balanced
- approximately 18–19 HCP
- invitational to game

---

## 3NT

Shows

- balanced
- game values
- no slam interest implied

---

# Jump Shifts

Jumping into a new suit usually shows

- extra strength
- long suit
- forcing values

Treatment varies by system.

---

# Rebids After Different Responses

## After 1NT Response

Usually

- clarify shape
- rebid long suit
- show second suit
- invite game with extras

---

## After New Suit Response

Support if possible.

Otherwise

- show second suit
- rebid original suit
- bid notrump

---

## After Raise

Evaluate

- combined strength
- distribution
- shortness
- controls

Then choose among

- partscore
- invitation
- game
- slam investigation

---

# Distributional Hands

Extra distribution may justify aggressive rebids.

Examples

- 6-5
- 5-5
- singleton
- void

Playing strength often exceeds HCP.

---

# Balanced Hands

Balanced hands emphasize

- accurate notrump rebids
- limiting the hand
- preserving bidding space

---

# Maximum Hands

With extras opener should

- invite game
- force to game
- begin slam investigation

depending on responder's previous action.

---

# After Jacoby 2NT

Opener

- shows shortness,
- shows extras,
- signs off with minimum,
- cooperates toward slam.

---

# After Limit Raise

Evaluate

- controls
- trump quality
- side suits
- losing trick count

before deciding

- pass
- game
- slam try

---

# After Game Force

Once game is forced

priorities become

1. strain
2. controls
3. cue bids
4. keycards
5. grand slam

# Common Mistakes

## Overbidding Balanced Hands

Describe strength accurately.

---

## Missing Major Fits

Support before introducing another suit whenever appropriate.

---

## Rebidding Weak Five-Card Suits

Prefer another descriptive call when possible.

---

## Hiding Distribution

Distribution frequently determines the final contract.

Describe shape whenever practical.

---

## Ignoring Partnership Agreements

System agreements always override general principles.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partnerships should define

- reverse strength,
- jump rebids,
- jump shifts,
- support with three cards,
- fourth-suit forcing,
- game-force structure,
- 2NT meanings,
- splinter rebids,
- cue-bid style.

---

# System Differences

## SAYC

- Standard raises
- Natural rebids
- Jump shifts strong

---

## 2/1 Game Force

- Wider game-forcing auctions
- Better definition of minimum rebids
- Frequent slow arrival

---

## Acol

- Four-card major openings possible
- Slightly different rebid structure

---

## Precision

- Artificial opening structure
- Similar rebid philosophy after natural majors

---

## Polish Club

- Major rebids largely resemble Standard American
- Artificial sequences occur after club openings

---

# Principles

Always

- support before introducing another suit,
- describe shape before strength when practical,
- limit the hand whenever possible,
- preserve bidding space,
- cooperate after game is forced,
- bid naturally unless system agreements specify otherwise.

---

# Summary

An opener's rebid after opening a major should progressively narrow the hand's
description while helping partner determine

- the correct strain,
- the correct level,
- whether game is likely,
- whether slam should be investigated.

Successful partnerships consistently apply the same rebid structure so that
every call communicates maximum information with minimum ambiguity.
