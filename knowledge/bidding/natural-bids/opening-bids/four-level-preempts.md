# Four-Level Preempts

## Overview

Four-level preemptive openings represent the most aggressive standard
preemptive openings in natural bridge. They immediately consume a large portion
of the bidding space while describing a weak hand with an exceptionally long
suit.

The standard four-level preemptive openings are

- 4♣
- 4♦
- 4♥
- 4♠

Unlike lower-level openings, these bids leave very little room for constructive
exploration. Their primary purpose is to make it extremely difficult for the
opponents to reach their best contract while giving partner sufficient
information to judge whether to pass, bid game, or sacrifice.

---

# Objectives

A four-level preempt aims to

- consume the maximum amount of bidding space,
- obstruct the opponents,
- describe a long, powerful suit,
- suggest an opening lead,
- create profitable sacrifice opportunities,
- allow partner to judge game or defensive prospects.

---

# Typical Requirements

Most partnerships require

- **5–10 HCP**
- an **excellent seven-card suit** or a **good eight-card suit**
- strong offensive distribution
- no suitable one-level opening
- no hand appropriate for a lower preempt

Many partnerships prefer eight-card suits, especially when vulnerable.

---

# Typical Suit Quality

The long suit should normally contain

- two of the top three honors,

or

- three of the top five honors,

or equivalent playing strength.

Typical suits include

```
AKQxxxxx
KQJxxxxx
AQJxxxxx
KQTxxxxx
QJTxxxxx
```

Excellent intermediates (10s and 9s) significantly improve suit quality.

---

# Hand Evaluation

Experts evaluate

- Playing Tricks
- Suit Quality
- Offensive Potential
- Controls
- Distribution
- Vulnerability
- Seat Position

Playing strength is considerably more important than High Card Points.

# Vulnerability

Vulnerability is critical.

## Not Vulnerable

Aggressive preempting is encouraged.

Slightly weaker suits may be acceptable.

---

## Vulnerable

Require

- better suit quality,
- greater trick-taking potential,
- fewer defensive losers.

Poor vulnerable preempts frequently produce severe penalties.

---

# Seat Position

## First Seat

Standard opening requirements.

---

## Second Seat

Disciplined style.

Avoid preempting with constructive hands.

---

## Third Seat

Aggressive openings are common.

Lead-directing value becomes especially important.

---

## Fourth Seat

Four-level preempts are rare.

Many partnerships require a nearly automatic game hand because there is no
opponent left to preempt.

---

# Responder's Responsibilities

Responder evaluates

- trump support,
- outside controls,
- offensive strength,
- defensive potential,
- sacrifice possibilities.

Since opener has limited strength, responder must make practical decisions
rather than search for perfect information.

---

# Passing

The most common action.

Example

```
4♠
Pass
```

Responder passes whenever game is unlikely.

---

# Raising to Game

When opener preempts in a minor

```
4♣
5♣
```

or

```
4♦
5♦
```

shows confidence that game can be made.

Major-suit preempts are already at game level.

---

# New Suit Response

Rare.

A new suit generally shows

- exceptional strength,
- slam interest,
- natural values.

Because little bidding space remains, new suits are uncommon.

---

# Notrump Contracts

Occasionally responder bids

```
5NT
```

or

```
6NT
```

when holding

- exceptional stoppers,
- powerful outside values,
- confidence that opener's long suit will provide enough tricks.

These auctions are extremely rare.

---

# Slam Investigation

Slam exploration after a four-level preempt is unusual.

Only exceptional responder hands justify further investigation.

Modern partnerships generally avoid lengthy slam auctions because bidding space
is severely restricted.

---

# Defensive Value

Four-level preempts

- consume nearly all available bidding space,
- force opponents to make difficult decisions,
- suggest opening leads,
- frequently prevent accurate slam investigation.

They are among the most disruptive bids in bridge.

---

# Sacrifice Decisions

Four-level preempts often create profitable sacrifices.

Responder evaluates

- vulnerability,
- expected tricks,
- defensive potential,
- likely opponent contract.

Judgment is considerably more important than point count.

---

# Competitive Auctions

After opponent intervention,

partnership agreements should define

- doubles,
- cue bids,
- raises,
- sacrifices,
- forcing actions.

Most competitive decisions are based on fit and distribution rather than HCP.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should define

- minimum suit quality,
- seven- versus eight-card requirements,
- vulnerability style,
- third-seat style,
- fourth-seat agreements,
- responses,
- sacrifice philosophy,
- competitive methods.

---

# Modern Expert Style

Expert partnerships emphasize

- disciplined vulnerable openings,
- aggressive non-vulnerable style,
- excellent suit quality,
- offensive distribution,
- lead-directing value,
- tactical obstruction.

# Common Mistakes

## Preempting with Constructive Hands

Hands suitable for one-level openings should not preempt.

---

## Poor Suit Quality

Weak suits often lead to expensive doubled contracts.

---

## Ignoring Vulnerability

Aggressive vulnerable preempts frequently become costly.

---

## Overvaluing HCP

Shape and playing tricks are more important than raw points.

---

## Ignoring Partnership Agreements

Responses and sacrifice philosophy require detailed agreements.

# Expert Evaluation

Experts evaluate

- playing tricks,
- suit texture,
- offensive potential,
- vulnerability,
- distribution,
- lead-directing value,

rather than relying primarily on High Card Points.

---

# Universal Principles

A four-level preempt should

- promise an exceptionally long, respectable suit,
- describe a limited hand,
- maximize obstruction,
- assist partner's judgment,
- force difficult decisions upon the opponents.

# Summary

Four-level preemptive openings are the most aggressive standard preemptive bids
in natural bridge. They combine **a weak hand**, **an exceptionally long suit**,
and **strong offensive distribution** to consume almost all available bidding
space and place maximum pressure on the opponents.

Successful use of four-level preempts depends on disciplined attention to
**suit quality**, **playing tricks**, **vulnerability**, and **seat position**.
When used with sound judgment, they are among the most effective tactical
weapons in bridge, frequently disrupting the opponents' auctions while helping
partner make informed decisions about game, defense, or sacrifice.
