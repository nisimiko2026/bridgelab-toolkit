# Response to Four-Level Preempt

## Overview

Responding to a **four-level preemptive opening** requires practical judgment
rather than scientific investigation.

By opening at the four level, opener has already announced

- a weak hand,
- an exceptionally long suit,
- strong offensive distribution,
- a highly preemptive intention.

Very little bidding space remains, so responder must make immediate practical
decisions based on

- fit,
- distribution,
- offensive potential,
- vulnerability.

Responder normally becomes **captain of the auction** because opener has already
limited the hand.

---

# Meaning of a Four-Level Preempt

A natural four-level opening normally promises

- 5–10 HCP,
- a good seven- or eight-card suit,
- offensive distribution,
- no suitable lower-level opening.

The bid is

- natural,
- preemptive,
- highly obstructive,
- limited.

---

# Objectives

Responder should determine whether the partnership should

- pass,
- bid game (after a minor preempt),
- compete,
- sacrifice,
- or very rarely investigate slam.

---

# Evaluating the Hand

Responder evaluates

- trump support,
- controls,
- playing tricks,
- distribution,
- vulnerability,
- offensive versus defensive values.

Because opener's strength is known, shape is often more important than HCP.

---

# Typical Strength Categories

| Strength | Typical Action |
|----------|----------------|
| 0–12 HCP | Usually Pass |
| Good fit | Consider game |
| Exceptional hand | Consider slam only rarely |

---

# Passing

Passing is by far the most common response.

Example

```
4♠
Pass
```

Responder should pass with

- insufficient offensive strength,
- no realistic game prospects,
- no profitable sacrifice.

---

# Raising a Minor

When opener preempts in a minor,

```
4♣
5♣
```

or

```
4♦
5♦
```

shows

- excellent trump support,
- confidence that game can be made.

Responder should compare the expected score with

```
3NT
```

before choosing five of a minor.

---

# Raising a Major

Major-suit preempts are already at the game level.

Responder normally passes.

Occasionally responder may bid

```
5♥
```

or

```
5♠
```

only when

- game appears certain,
- or the auction has become competitive.

---

# Notrump Contracts

After

```
4♣
```

or

```
4♦
```

Responder may occasionally choose

```
3NT
```

(if available through partnership methods)

or more commonly

```
5NT
```

or

```
6NT
```

only with exceptional values.

These auctions are rare.

---

# New Suit Responses

Because almost no bidding space remains,

new suits are uncommon.

When used they generally show

- exceptional strength,
- natural suit,
- slam interest.

Most partnerships avoid lengthy investigations.

---

# Slam Investigation

Slam auctions after a four-level preempt are extremely unusual.

Responder should investigate only when holding

- outstanding controls,
- excellent trump support,
- significant extra strength.

Even then,

limited bidding space often forces practical rather than scientific decisions.

---

# Sacrifice Decisions

One of responder's most important responsibilities.

Responder evaluates

- vulnerability,
- expected tricks,
- offensive strength,
- likely opponent contract,
- penalty risk.

Many successful four-level preempts produce profitable sacrifices.

---

# Competitive Auctions

When opponents continue bidding,

responder should evaluate

- fit,
- distribution,
- vulnerability,
- sacrifice potential,
- defensive strength.

Typical actions include

- Pass
- Double
- Raise
- Cue Bid (partnership agreement)

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should define

- minimum suit quality,
- response philosophy,
- sacrifice style,
- competitive doubles,
- cue bids,
- vulnerability adjustments.

---

# Modern Expert Style

Expert partnerships emphasize

- practical judgment,
- offensive potential,
- disciplined vulnerable actions,
- aggressive sacrifice decisions,
- maximum obstruction.

Little artificial bidding is used because bidding space is almost exhausted.

# Common Mistakes

## Overestimating Opener's Strength

Remember opener has already limited the hand.

---

## Ignoring Distribution

Trump support and shape outweigh raw HCP.

---

## Missing 3NT

After a minor-suit preempt,

always consider whether notrump will outscore five of a minor.

---

## Investigating Slam Too Frequently

Most four-level preempt auctions should remain practical.

---

## Ignoring Vulnerability

Sacrifice decisions depend heavily on vulnerability.

---

# Expert Evaluation

Experts evaluate

- playing tricks,
- trump support,
- offensive potential,
- controls,
- vulnerability,
- distribution,

rather than relying solely on High Card Points.

---

# Universal Principles

Responder should

- assume captaincy,
- preserve the obstructive value of the opening,
- bid game aggressively with excellent fits,
- avoid unnecessary investigation,
- make practical decisions.

# Summary

Responding to a **four-level preempt** is almost entirely a matter of **practical
judgment**. Since opener has already shown a **weak hand with an exceptionally
long suit**, responder must decide whether the partnership should pass, bid
game, compete, or sacrifice without the benefit of extensive bidding space.

Successful responses rely on accurate evaluation of **trump support**,
**distribution**, **playing tricks**, **vulnerability**, and **offensive
potential**. Because detailed investigation is rarely possible, expert players
focus on making sound practical decisions while preserving the powerful
obstructive effect of the original preempt.
