# Responding to Preemptive Opening Bids at the Three Level

## Overview

Three-level preemptive openings are designed to

- obstruct the opponents,
- accurately describe a long suit,
- consume bidding space,
- suggest a weak hand with offensive distribution.

Responder's task is fundamentally different from responding to a normal opening.

Since opener has already described both

- strength, and
- distribution,

responder usually becomes captain of the auction and decides whether to

- pass,
- invite game,
- bid game,
- investigate slam (rare),
- compete further after interference.

---

# Objectives

Responder attempts to determine

- whether game is possible,
- whether notrump is superior,
- whether opener's suit should remain trumps,
- whether slam is realistic,
- whether to compete safely.

---

# Typical Meaning of a 3-Level Opening

A natural weak three opening usually promises

- 6-10 HCP
- seven-card suit (occasionally six with excellent quality)
- good suit quality
- no outside four-card major
- no opening strength

Example

```
3♦
```

typically shows

- 7-card diamond suit
- weak hand
- obstructive intent

---

# General Principles

Responder should

1. Evaluate offensive strength.
2. Count playing tricks.
3. Consider game.
4. Prefer notrump with stoppers.
5. Avoid inviting with unsuitable hands.

Unlike normal auctions, there is very little room for exploration.

---

# Hand Evaluation

Responder values

- controls,
- quick tricks,
- stoppers,
- trump support,
- side-suit winners,

more than pure HCP.

Example

```
♠ AQ5
♥ KQ
♦ 84
♣ AKJ862
```

may justify aggressive action opposite

```
3♥
```

---

# Strength Guidelines

## Weak

0-11 HCP

Usually pass.

---

## Invitational

12-15 HCP

Occasionally invite, depending on agreements.

---

## Game Values

Approximately

15-17+ HCP

with suitable fitting cards.

---

## Slam Interest

Rare.

Requires

- exceptional controls,
- excellent fit,
- source of tricks.

---

# Passing

Most common action.

Example

```
3♠
Pass
```

Responder lacks sufficient strength to continue.

---

# Raising to Game

Bid game with

- strong support,
- useful controls,
- offensive values,
- confidence opener's suit will produce sufficient tricks.

Example

```
3♥
4♥
```

---

# Raising One Level

Some partnerships use

```
3♠
4♠
```

as purely to play.

Others treat

```
3♦
4♦
```

as constructive.

Agreement required.

---

# New Suit Response

A new suit is generally

- forcing,
- natural,
- game-oriented.

Example

```
3♠
4♣
```

Usually shows

- excellent club suit,
- game interest.

---

# Notrump Responses

## 3NT

The most common game action.

Responder should have

- stoppers,
- outside controls,
- expectation that opener's long suit provides tricks.

Example

```
3♦
3NT
```

---

## 4NT

Usually quantitative or Blackwood depending upon partnership agreement.

Rare.

---

# Cue Bids

Some expert partnerships use cue bids to show

- slam interest,
- controls,
- agreeing opener's suit.

---

# Asking Bids

Many partnerships employ an asking bid.

Example

```
3♠
4♣
```

Artificial.

May ask

- suit quality,
- outside ace,
- feature.

Most common treatment is **Feature Asking**.

---

# Feature Asking

One popular agreement

```
3♥
4♣
```

asks opener to show

- outside ace,
- protected king,
- useful feature.

Replies vary by partnership.

---

# Ogust

Ogust is **not** normally used after three-level openings.

It is primarily associated with Weak Two openings.

---

# Evaluating Opener's Suit

Responder considers

- top honors,
- intermediates,
- expected losers,
- side entries.

Good suits justify aggressive bidding.

---

# Distributional Hands

Responder upgrades with

- singleton,
- void,
- trump support,
- long side suit,
- controls.

---

# Playing 3NT

Choose 3NT when

- responder has stoppers,
- opener's long suit provides running tricks,
- notrump scores better than five of a minor.

---

# Minor-Suit Preempts

Example

```
3♦
```

Responder frequently evaluates

- 3NT,
- 5♦,
- slam possibilities.

Notrump often scores higher.

---

# Major-Suit Preempts

Example

```
3♠
```

Responder evaluates

- game,
- slam,
- offensive controls.

Major games require only ten tricks.

---

# After Interference

Competition changes priorities.

Responder may

- double,
- cue bid,
- support,
- bid game,
- sacrifice.

Judgment becomes critical.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should define

- feature asks,
- new-suit forcing,
- 4NT meaning,
- cue bids,
- raises,
- slam methods,
- notrump requirements.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Inviting Without Room

Three-level openings consume bidding space.

Invitations are often impractical.

---

## Ignoring Playing Strength

Distribution matters more than HCP.

---

## Missing 3NT

Minor-suit openings frequently produce excellent notrump games.

---

## Overestimating Weak Hands

Remember opener has already limited strength.

---

## Ignoring Suit Quality

A poor seven-card suit may not provide enough tricks.

---

# Expert Evaluation

Experts evaluate

- controls,
- quick tricks,
- source of tricks,
- suit quality,
- stoppers,
- offensive potential,

rather than relying solely on HCP.

---

# System Differences

## Standard American

Natural raises.

Feature asks optional.

---

## SAYC

Similar treatment.

Most partnerships use Feature Asking.

---

## 2/1 Game Force

Little difference.

Preemptive auctions remain largely unchanged.

---

## Acol

Natural treatment.

Ranges vary slightly.

---

## Precision

Natural three-level openings are similar when used.

---

## Polish Club

Natural three-level preempts resemble Standard American.

---

# Universal Principles

Responder should

- trust opener's description,
- become captain,
- value controls highly,
- bid games aggressively,
- choose 3NT whenever appropriate,
- avoid unnecessary exploration.

---

Study in the following order

1. Weak Two Responses
2. Three-Level Preempts
3. Feature Asking
4. Minor-Suit Preempts
5. Major-Suit Preempts
6. Competitive Auctions

# Summary

Responding to a **three-level preemptive opening** is primarily an exercise in
judgment rather than investigation. Because opener has already limited the hand
to a weak, distributional holding, responder assumes the role of captain and
must decide quickly whether to stop, bid game, or occasionally explore slam.

Successful partnerships emphasize **playing strength, controls, suit quality,
and notrump potential** over raw high-card points. Modern methods such as
**Feature Asking** further improve accuracy, allowing responder to judge
whether opener's outside values are sufficient for game or slam while still
preserving the obstructive nature of the preempt.
