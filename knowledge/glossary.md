# A

**Ace Asking Bid**
: A conventional bid requesting information about aces.

**Advance**
: A bid made by the partner of an overcaller.

**Advancer**
: The partner of the player who overcalled.

**Alert**
: A notification informing opponents that a bid has an artificial meaning.

**Artificial Bid**
: A bid that does not show the suit or strength normally associated with that
bid.

**Attitude Signal**
: A defensive signal indicating encouragement or discouragement.

---

# B

**Balanced Hand**
: A hand with no singleton or void and no more than one doubleton.

**Balancing Seat**
: The position immediately after two consecutive passes.

**Bid**
: Any call other than Pass, Double, or Redouble.

**Blackwood**
: A convention used to investigate slam by asking for aces or key cards.

**Blocking**
: Preventing access between hands by playing cards in the wrong order.

**Break**
: The distribution of outstanding cards in a suit.

---

# C

**Captain**
: The player who directs the partnership auction.

**Card Reading**
: Inferring unseen cards from the auction and play.

**Control**
: An Ace, King, singleton, or void capable of preventing losers.

**Cue Bid**
: A bid of the opponent's suit used for conventional purposes.

---

# D

**Declarer**
: The player who first bid the denomination of the final contract.

**Defense**
: The two players attempting to defeat the contract.

**Discard**
: A card played when unable to follow suit.

**Distribution**
: The pattern of suit lengths in a hand.

**Double**
: A call increasing the score if the contract succeeds or fails.

**Dummy**
: Declarer's partner, whose cards are exposed after the opening lead.

---

# E

**Entry**
: A card providing access to one hand.

**Endplay**
: A play forcing an opponent to make a favorable lead.

**Equal-Level Conversion**
: Converting a takeout double into another suit at the same level.

---

# F

**Falsecard**
: A deceptive card intended to mislead declarer or defenders.

**Feature**
: A useful holding, often an Ace or King, shown by conventional agreement.

**Finesse**
: A play attempting to win a trick with a lower honor against a missing higher
honor.

**Fit**
: Combined trump length of eight cards or more.

**Forcing Bid**
: A bid that partner may not pass.

---

# G

**Game**
: A contract worth at least 100 trick points.

**Game Force**
: A sequence requiring the partnership to reach game.

**Gerber**
: A convention asking for aces using 4♣.

---

# H

**Honor**
: Ace, King, Queen, Jack, or Ten.

**Honor Location**
: The identification of specific honors through bidding or play.

---

# I

**IMP**
: International Match Point, the standard scoring unit for team matches.

**Interference**
: An opponent's bid disrupting the partnership auction.

---

# J

**Jump Bid**
: A bid made higher than necessary.

**Jump Shift**
: A jump into a new suit.

---

# K

**Key Card**
: An Ace or the trump King in Roman Key Card Blackwood.

---

# L

**Lead**
: The first card played to a trick.

**Lead-Directing Double**
: A double requesting a particular opening lead.

**Limit Raise**
: An invitational raise showing defined strength.

**Loser**
: A trick likely to be lost.

---

# M

**Matchpoints**
: A scoring method comparing each result against all other tables.

**Minor Suit**
: Clubs or Diamonds.

---

# N

**Natural Bid**
: A bid showing the suit bid.

**Negative Double**
: A conventional double showing unbid suits after an overcall.

**Notrump**
: A contract played without a trump suit.

---

# O

**Opening Bid**
: The first non-pass bid of the auction.

**Opening Lead**
: The first card played against the contract.

**Overcall**
: A bid made after an opponent has opened.

---

# P

**Pair Game**
: A duplicate event scored by Matchpoints.

**Partscore**
: A contract worth fewer than 100 trick points.

**Pass**
: A call declining to bid.

**Penalty Double**
: A double intended to defeat the contract.

**Planning**
: Developing the overall strategy before playing to the first trick.

**Preference**
: A bid or signal indicating partner's preferred suit.

---

# Q

**Quantitative Bid**
: A bid inviting slam based primarily on high-card strength.

---

# R

**Raise**
: Supporting partner's suit.

**Redouble**
: A call made after a double, increasing the stakes again.

**Relay**
: An artificial bid requesting additional information.

**Responder**
: Partner of the opening bidder.

**Reverse**
: A rebid showing extra strength through higher-ranking suit preference.

---

# S

**Safety Play**
: A technically correct play sacrificing an extra chance to protect against an
unfavorable layout.

**Sacrifice**
: Deliberately bidding a contract expected to fail to prevent the opponents
from scoring more.

**Signal**
: A defensive card conveying information.

**Singleton**
: Exactly one card in a suit.

**Slam**
: A contract of six or seven.

**Splinter**
: A double jump showing support and a singleton or void.

**Stayman**
: A convention asking opener about four-card major suits.

**Strip**
: Eliminating safe exit cards before an endplay.

**Suit Establishment**
: Developing additional winners in a suit.

---

# T

**Takeout Double**
: A double requesting partner to bid.

**Tempo**
: The timing and pace of bidding or play.

**Tenace**
: A combination of honors that benefits from leading toward it.

**Transfer**
: An artificial bid asking partner to bid another suit.

**Trump**
: The designated suit that outranks all others.

---

# U

**Unbalanced Hand**
: Any hand that is not balanced.

**Upside-Down Count**
: A signaling method where high cards show an odd number.

---

# V

**Void**
: No cards in a suit.

---

# W

**Waiting Bid**
: A bid requesting further description while revealing little information.

**Winner**
: A card expected to take a trick.
