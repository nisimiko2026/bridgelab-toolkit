# Cover an Honor with an Honor

## Overview

**Cover an Honor with an Honor** is one of the classic principles of defensive play in bridge. When declarer or dummy leads an **honor card**, the defender should often cover it with the **next higher honor**, provided doing so promotes partner's cards or destroys declarer's honor sequence.

The principle is a guideline rather than an absolute rule. There are many situations where **not** covering is the correct play, so expert defenders must understand both the rule and its exceptions.

---

# Purpose

Covering an honor with an honor may:

- Promote partner's lower honors.
- Break up declarer's honor sequence.
- Force declarer to use multiple honors.
- Remove dummy's entries.
- Create additional defensive tricks.

---

# Basic Principle

When an opponent leads an honor, ask:

> **Will covering this honor improve the defense?**

If the answer is yes:

**Cover the honor.**

If not:

Play low.

---

# Why It Works

Suppose dummy holds:

```
♠ QJ4
```

You hold:

```
♠ K83
```

If declarer leads the **Queen**, covering with the **King** forces the Ace (if held by declarer) and often promotes partner's 10 or 9 into a winner.

Without covering, declarer may score both the Queen and Jack.

---

# Example 1 – Standard Cover

Dummy

```
♠ QJ4
```

You

```
♠ K83
```

Declarer leads the **♠Q**.

Correct play:

```
♠K
```

The King forces the Ace and breaks up dummy's sequence.

---

# Example 2 – Promoting Partner's Card

Dummy

```
♥ QJ10
```

You

```
♥ K74
```

Partner

```
♥ 9652
```

Declarer leads the **♥Q**.

Covering with the King promotes partner's remaining hearts after the Ace is forced.

---

# Example 3 – Destroying an Entry

Dummy

```
♦ Q
```

You

```
♦ K76
```

The ♦Q is dummy's only entry to a long side suit.

Covering with the King may remove that vital entry.

---

# Common Situations to Cover

Covering is often correct when:

- Declarer leads a touching honor sequence.
- You can promote partner's honor.
- You can remove a key entry.
- You can force declarer to spend an additional honor.
- The contract is notrump.

---

# Common Exceptions

There are many situations where **not covering** is correct.

---

## AQ Opposite Small Cards

Dummy

```
♠ AQ
```

You

```
♠ Kxx
```

If declarer leads the Queen, covering may simply establish the Ace.

Playing low may be superior.

---

## No Promotion Possible

If partner cannot benefit from the cover, playing high may gain nothing.

---

## Preserve Communication

Sometimes covering destroys an important defensive entry.

---

## Holding a Sequence

Holding

```
KJ10
```

Covering with the King may unnecessarily waste the sequence.

---

## Suit Contracts

In suit contracts, declarer may be able to ruff later.

Sometimes preserving honors is preferable.

---

# Against Notrump Contracts

Covering is more common because:

- Every trick matters.
- Promoting partner's honors is valuable.
- Destroying dummy's entries is often decisive.

---

# Against Suit Contracts

Covering still applies, but defenders must also consider:

- Ruffing possibilities.
- Trump control.
- Dummy's short suits.
- Whether promoting partner's honor is worthwhile.

---

# Planning the Defense

Before covering, ask:

- Will partner gain a trick?
- Will declarer gain extra tricks if I duck?
- Am I destroying a key entry?
- What does the auction suggest?
- Can I preserve communication?

---

# Common Mistakes

- Covering automatically.
- Failing to cover when partner's honor will be promoted.
- Wasting an honor unnecessarily.
- Ignoring dummy's entries.
- Forgetting the contract type.

---

# Defensive Counterplay

The principle works together with:

- Second Hand Low.
- Third Hand High.
- Active vs. Passive Defense.
- Opening Leads.
- Count signals.
- Suit preference.

The correct play depends on the entire defensive picture.

---

# Advantages

- Promotes partner's honors.
- Breaks declarer's honor sequences.
- Removes dummy's entries.
- Forces declarer to spend honors.
- Creates additional defensive tricks.

---

# Disadvantages

- Not always correct.
- Can waste honors.
- May establish declarer's remaining cards.
- Requires careful judgment.

# Defender's Checklist

Before covering an honor:

- Will I promote partner's cards?
- Will I destroy dummy's entry?
- Is this notrump or a suit contract?
- Does partner's likely holding justify the cover?
- Is ducking a better choice?

---

# Memory Guide

## Five Questions

1. Was an honor led?
2. Will covering help the defense?
3. Can partner's cards be promoted?
4. Will covering destroy an important entry?
5. Are there reasons to duck instead?

---

# Memory Aid

**Cover an Honor with an Honor = "Cover Only If It Helps."**

Remember:

- **Cover to promote partner's cards.**
- **Cover to break declarer's honor sequence.**
- **Cover to remove dummy's entries.**
- **Do not cover automatically—consider the entire position.**
- **Use judgment; this is a guideline, not a fixed rule.**

"Cover an Honor with an Honor" is one of bridge's oldest and most useful defensive principles. Expert defenders understand that its real purpose is **not simply to cover honors**, but to improve the overall defensive position by promoting partner's cards, breaking declarer's communication, and preventing declarer from making easy tricks.
