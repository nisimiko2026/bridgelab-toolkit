# Third Hand High

## Overview

**Third Hand High** is one of the fundamental principles of defensive play in bridge. It states that, when partner leads a suit, the defender playing **third to the trick** should generally play the **highest card necessary** to win the trick.

The objective is to help the partnership establish tricks, maintain the initiative, and prevent declarer from gaining unnecessary tempo.

Like all bridge maxims, **Third Hand High** is a guideline rather than an absolute rule. Expert defenders know when exceptions are appropriate.

---

# Purpose

The Third Hand High principle helps defenders:

- Win tricks for the defense.
- Establish partner's suit.
- Maintain the lead.
- Prevent declarer from developing extra tricks.
- Preserve partnership communication.

---

# Basic Principle

When playing **third** to a trick after partner has led:

> **Play the highest card necessary to win the trick.**

The emphasis is on **necessary**—do not waste a higher honor if a lower one will suffice.

---

# Why It Works

Winning the trick when possible allows the defense to:

- Continue attacking the suit.
- Cash established winners.
- Prevent declarer from setting up the suit.
- Keep control of the hand.

---

# Example 1 – Basic Position

Partner leads:

```
♠5
```

Dummy

```
♠ Q84
```

You

```
♠ AK6
```

Dummy plays the ♠4.

Correct play:

```
♠K
```

The King is the **highest card necessary** to win the trick. Preserve the Ace for later.

---

# Example 2 – Do Not Waste an Honor

Partner leads:

```
♥3
```

Dummy

```
♥ J72
```

You

```
♥ AQ5
```

Dummy plays the ♥2.

Correct play:

```
♥Q
```

The Queen wins the trick. There is no need to waste the Ace.

---

# Example 3 – Playing High

Partner leads:

```
♦7
```

Dummy

```
♦ Q84
```

You

```
♦ KJ5
```

Dummy plays the ♦4.

Play:

```
♦K
```

Win the trick and continue the defense.

---

# Highest Card Necessary

Always ask:

> **"What is the lowest card that will win the trick?"**

Examples:

| Holding | Dummy Plays | Correct Play |
|---------|-------------|--------------|
| AKx | Small | K |
| AQx | Small | Q |
| KJx | Small | J |
| QJx | Small | J |

Preserve higher honors whenever possible.

---

# Common Exceptions

Third Hand High is **not** always correct.

---

## Preserve an Entry

Sometimes winning with a lower honor preserves an important higher honor as an entry.

---

## Let Partner Win

If partner's card is already winning, play low.

Example:

Partner leads the Ace.

Do **not** overtake unless partnership agreements require it.

---

## Hold Up

Occasionally, declarer can be defeated only by ducking rather than winning immediately.

---

## Falsecarding

Expert defenders sometimes play an unexpected card to conceal the location of honors or confuse declarer.

---

# Against Notrump Contracts

Third Hand High is especially important because:

- Defenders race to establish long suits.
- Winning early may allow the defense to cash several tricks before declarer gains control.

---

# Against Suit Contracts

The principle still applies, but defenders must also consider:

- Trump promotion.
- Ruffing opportunities.
- Entry preservation.
- Passive versus active defense.

---

# Partnership Communication

Winning the trick often allows you to:

- Continue partner's suit.
- Switch intelligently.
- Give attitude or count signals.
- Maintain the initiative.

---

# Planning the Defense

Before playing third hand, ask:

- Can I win with a lower honor?
- Should I preserve a higher honor?
- Is partner's lead from strength?
- Will winning now benefit the defense?
- Should I continue the suit?

---

# Common Mistakes

- Automatically playing the Ace.
- Wasting an unnecessary honor.
- Forgetting to preserve entries.
- Failing to win when necessary.
- Ignoring partnership agreements.

---

# Defensive Counterplay

Third Hand High works together with:

- Second Hand Low.
- Opening Leads.
- Count signals.
- Attitude signals.
- Suit preference.
- Active defense.

Each defensive decision should support the overall defensive plan.

---

# Advantages

- Establishes partner's suit.
- Wins important tricks.
- Preserves communication.
- Maintains the initiative.
- Fundamental defensive principle.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires judgment.
- Exceptions are common.
- Wasting honors may cost later tricks.
- Sometimes ducking is superior.

# Memory Aid

**Third Hand High = "Win the Trick, But Don't Waste a Higher Honor."**

Remember:

- **Try to win the trick when partner leads.**
- **Use the lowest card that will win.**
- **Preserve higher honors whenever possible.**
- **Continue partner's attack when appropriate.**
- **Apply judgment—Third Hand High is a guideline, not an absolute rule.**

Third Hand High is one of the cornerstones of defensive play. By winning tricks efficiently while conserving higher honors, defenders maximize their partnership's ability to establish suits, maintain communication, and defeat contracts. Expert defenders understand both the principle and its exceptions, making thoughtful decisions based on the auction, dummy, and overall defensive strategy.
