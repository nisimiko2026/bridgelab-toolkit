````md id="avoidplay1"
# Avoidance Play Technique

## Overview

**Avoidance Play** is a declarer-play technique in which declarer deliberately avoids giving the lead to a particular defender, known as the **dangerous opponent**. By controlling which defender wins tricks, declarer prevents the dangerous opponent from attacking a threatening suit or cashing established winners.

Avoidance Play is especially important in **Notrump contracts**, where allowing the wrong defender to gain the lead may immediately defeat the contract.

---

# Purpose

Avoidance Play allows declarer to:

- Keep the dangerous opponent off lead.
- Protect vulnerable holdings.
- Preserve communications.
- Prevent defenders from cashing long suits.
- Improve the chances of making the contract.

---

# Basic Principle

The guiding principle is:

> **If one opponent is dangerous, avoid letting that opponent gain the lead.**

Declarer may:

- Duck tricks.
- Take or avoid finesses.
- Choose one defender over the other.
- Sacrifice an overtrick.
- Alter the normal line of play.

---

# The Dangerous Opponent

A defender is considered **dangerous** when winning the lead allows them to make a return that defeats the contract.

Typical reasons include:

- An established long suit.
- An entry to partner's long suit.
- A threatening switch.
- The ability to cash immediate winners.

The other defender is often called the **safe opponent**.

---

# Example 1 – Notrump Hold-Up

## Contract

```
3NT
```

West leads:

```
♠K
```

West has:

```
♠ AKQJ8
```

East has:

```
♠ 32
```

Declarer ducks the first two spade tricks.

When West regains the lead later, East has no spades left to return.

West's long suit is no longer dangerous.

This is both a **Hold-Up Play** and an **Avoidance Play**.

---

# Example 2 – Choosing a Finesse

Holding

```
Dummy
♦ AQ10

Declarer
♦ 543
```

The bidding suggests:

- West holds the ♦K.

Instead of taking a finesse that would lose to West, declarer chooses another line, keeping West off lead.

---

# Example 3 – Ducking an Ace

Dummy

```
♣ AQJ
```

Declarer

```
♣ K43
```

West is the dangerous opponent.

Declarer ducks a club to East rather than winning immediately, ensuring West never gains the lead.

---

# Common Situations

Avoidance Play frequently occurs:

- In Notrump contracts.
- Against established long suits.
- Before a squeeze.
- Before an endplay.
- When one defender has dangerous entries.
- When declarer must choose between two finesses.

---

# Planning the Play

Before Trick One, ask:

- Which defender is dangerous?
- Which defender is safe?
- Can I control who wins tricks?
- Can I avoid a dangerous finesse?
- Should I duck to break communications?

---

# Timing

Timing is critical.

Generally:

1. Identify the dangerous opponent.
2. Plan to keep that defender off lead.
3. Preserve entries.
4. Avoid unnecessary risks.

---

# Typical Methods

Declarer may use:

- Ducking.
- Hold-Up Play.
- Choosing the correct finesse.
- Delaying winners.
- Safety Plays.
- Elimination Plays.
- Endplays.

---

# Example 4 – Two Finesses

Contract

```
3NT
```

Declarer can finesse either:

- Hearts.
- Clubs.

The heart finesse loses to West (dangerous).

The club finesse loses to East (safe).

Choose the club finesse.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Misidentifying the dangerous opponent.
- Winning tricks too early.
- Taking the wrong finesse.
- Ignoring communications.
- Forgetting the defenders' established suits.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Preserve entries to the dangerous hand.
- Hide suit distribution.
- Maintain communication.
- Switch suits to create new dangers.
- Recognize declarer's avoidance strategy.

---

# Advantages

- Prevents dangerous defenders from gaining the lead.
- Protects vulnerable suits.
- Improves contract chances.
- Frequently avoids immediate defeat.
- Integrates naturally with Hold-Up Play.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires careful planning.
- Accurate counting is essential.
- Sometimes sacrifices overtricks.
- New information may change which defender is dangerous.

---

# Declarer's Checklist

Before playing to Trick One:

- Which opponent is dangerous?
- Which opponent is safe?
- Can I avoid a finesse that loses to the dangerous hand?
- Should I duck?
- Will giving up an overtrick increase my chance of making the contract?

---

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Identify the dangerous opponent.
2. Protect vulnerable suits.
3. Choose the safe line.
4. Preserve communication.
5. Keep the dangerous opponent off lead.

---

# Comparison

| Technique | Main Idea |
|-----------|-----------|
| Avoidance Play | Keep one defender off lead |
| Hold-Up Play | Duck to break defenders' communications |
| Safety Play | Maximize the chance of making the contract |
| Endplay | Force a favorable defender to gain the lead |
| Percentage Play | Choose the statistically best line |

```

---

# Memory Aid

**Avoidance Play = "Keep the Dangerous Opponent Off Lead."**

Remember:

- **Identify the dangerous defender before Trick One.**
- **Choose lines that lose to the safe opponent whenever possible.**
- **Use ducking and hold-up play to cut communications.**
- **Avoid risky finesses that lose to the dangerous hand.**
- **Making the contract is more important than overtricks.**

Avoidance Play is one of the most important planning techniques in bridge, particularly in notrump contracts. By recognizing which defender poses the greatest threat and carefully controlling who gains the lead, declarer can prevent devastating defensive attacks and significantly improve the chances of making the contract.
````
