# Communication

## Overview

**Communication** is the ability to transfer the lead between declarer and dummy—or between defenders—in order to reach winners, continue attacks, or execute a planned line of play.

Good communication is fundamental to successful bridge. Many contracts fail not because declarer lacks sufficient tricks, but because the winning tricks cannot be reached. Likewise, many defensive opportunities are lost because the defenders cannot reach each other's hands.

Communication depends on careful **entry management**, **timing**, **unblocking**, and occasionally **overtaking**.

---

# Objectives

Good communication allows a player to:

- Reach established winners.
- Maintain control of the play.
- Continue attacking a suit.
- Execute advanced techniques.
- Preserve flexibility.
- Prevent winners from becoming stranded.

---

# Declarer's Communication

For declarer, communication means maintaining access between:

- Declarer's hand.
- Dummy.

Successful declarer play often depends on moving between the two hands at the correct moments.

Typical objectives include:

- Cashing a long suit.
- Taking repeated finesses.
- Executing squeezes.
- Performing endplays.
- Ruffing losers.

---

# Defensive Communication

For defenders, communication means maintaining access between the two defenders.

Examples include:

- Reaching partner's established suit.
- Continuing the opening attack.
- Giving partner a ruff.
- Cashing defensive winners in the proper order.

Defenders should always consider not only **who wins the trick**, but **which defender should remain on lead**.

---

# Communication Begins Before Trick One

Before playing from dummy, ask:

- Where are the entries?
- Which entries are essential?
- How will I reach my established winners?
- Can either hand become stranded?

Planning communication early avoids many common mistakes.

---

# Entries Create Communication

Communication depends on entries.

Typical entries include:

- Aces.
- Guarded kings.
- Established winners.
- High trumps in suit contracts.
- Occasionally successful finesses.

Every unnecessary use of an entry weakens communication.

---

# Preserving Communication

Communication is preserved by:

- Saving critical entries.
- Winning tricks with the correct hand.
- Delaying unnecessary winners.
- Planning the order of play.

Many expert declarers solve the communication problem before deciding how to develop tricks.

---

# Unblocking

A suit may become blocked if honors are played in the wrong order.

Example:

```
Dummy
♦ A J 10 9

Declarer
♦ K Q
```

Playing the honors in the correct order keeps the suit open and preserves communication.

Unblocking and communication are closely related.

---

# Overtaking

Sometimes the correct play is to overtake a winning card.

Example:

```
Dummy
♥ A Q

Declarer
♥ K J
```

Overtaking allows the correct hand to remain on lead and may preserve communication for later tricks.

---

# Communication in Notrump

Communication is especially important in notrump contracts because:

- Long suits must be established.
- Ruffing cannot create new entries.
- Entries are limited.
- A blocked suit may defeat the contract.

Good notrump declarers constantly monitor communication between the two hands.

---

# Communication in Suit Contracts

Suit contracts provide additional flexibility because trumps can sometimes serve as entries.

However, declarer must still avoid:

- Drawing trumps too early.
- Ruffing away important entries.
- Blocking long side suits.

Communication remains a key part of trump management.

---

# Communication and Timing

Timing and communication are inseparable.

Declarer must decide:

- When to use an entry.
- When to preserve an entry.
- Which hand should win each trick.
- When to lose the lead.

Poor timing often destroys communication.

---

# Example 1 – Long Suit

Contract:

```
3NT
```

Dummy:

```
♣ A J 10 8 5
```

Declarer:

```
♣ K 6 4
```

Declarer first establishes clubs while preserving the ♣A as the entry to the remaining club winners.

---

# Example 2 – Overtaking

Dummy:

```
♥ A Q
```

Declarer:

```
♥ K J
```

Declarer overtakes one honor to remain in the correct hand for the next stage of the play.

---

# Example 3 – Defensive Communication

West leads a spade.

East wins the trick but realizes only East can continue the heart attack through declarer's king.

East overtakes partner's winner on an earlier trick to retain the lead and preserve the defensive attack.

---

# Common Communication Problems

- Using the only entry too early.
- Blocking a long suit.
- Winning a trick with the wrong hand.
- Drawing trumps before using important trump entries.
- Failing to plan the order of play.
- Allowing defenders to break communication in notrump.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Thinking only about the current trick.
- Ignoring future entries.
- Cashing honors in the wrong order.
- Forgetting that established winners must still be reached.
- Overlooking communication between defenders.

# Key Principles

- Communication is the ability to move the lead to the correct hand at the correct time.
- Entries are the foundation of communication.
- Plan communication before developing long suits or taking finesses.
- Unblocking and overtaking are common methods of preserving communication.
- Good communication is essential for both declarer play and defense.
