# Ducking Technique

## Overview

**Ducking** (also called **Holding Up**) is a declarer-play or defensive technique in which a player deliberately **refuses to win a trick that they could have won**. By allowing an opponent to win the trick, the player gains a strategic advantage later in the hand.

Ducking is one of the most important techniques in bridge because it helps:

- Break the opponents' communications.
- Preserve entries.
- Prevent defenders from establishing long suits.
- Create endplay opportunities.
- Control the timing of the play.

Both declarers and defenders use ducking, although the reasons differ.

---

# Purpose

Ducking allows a player to:

- Cut communications between opponents.
- Prevent a dangerous opponent from gaining the lead.
- Preserve an important entry.
- Rectify the count before a squeeze.
- Set up an endplay.
- Improve timing.

---

# Basic Principle

Instead of taking an available winner:

1. Play a lower card.
2. Allow the opponents to win the trick.
3. Gain a strategic advantage later.

The temporary loss of a trick often produces additional tricks later.

---

# Declarer Ducking

Declarer commonly ducks to:

- Break defenders' communication.
- Exhaust one defender's winners.
- Preserve entries.
- Prepare a squeeze or endplay.
- Keep the dangerous defender off lead.

---

# Example 1 – Hold Up in Notrump

## Contract

```
3NT
```

Dummy

```
♠ AQ4
```

Declarer

```
♠ K73
```

West leads:

```
♠J
```

Declarer ducks the first two spade tricks.

When declarer later wins the third spade, East no longer has a spade to return.

The defenders' communication is broken.

---

# Example 2 – Preserving an Entry

Dummy

```
♦ A
```

Declarer

```
♦ KQ
```

Instead of winning immediately with the Ace, declarer ducks.

The Ace remains available as an entry later.

---

# Example 3 – Preparing a Squeeze

Declarer intentionally ducks an early trick.

The lost trick rectifies the count.

Later, a squeeze becomes possible.

---

# Defensive Ducking

Defenders also duck for strategic reasons.

Typical goals include:

- Keeping declarer in the wrong hand.
- Preserving communications.
- Preventing declarer from locating honors.
- Waiting for partner to gain the lead.

---

# Example 4 – Defensive Duck

Dummy

```
♣ AQJ
```

Declarer

```
♣ K109
```

West holds:

```
♣ 8765
```

West ducks twice.

Declarer misjudges the position and later loses control of the suit.

---

# Dangerous Opponent

One of the most common reasons for ducking is to keep the **dangerous opponent** off lead.

Suppose:

West has established spades.

East has no spades left.

Declarer ducks the first two spades.

When West later gains the lead, the spade suit is exhausted.

---

# Timing

Timing is critical.

Generally:

- Duck early.
- Preserve entries.
- Win only when advantageous.

Winning too early often destroys the benefit.

---

# Common Situations

Ducking frequently occurs:

- In notrump contracts.
- Before a squeeze.
- Before an endplay.
- Before elimination plays.
- To preserve entries.
- Against long established suits.

---

# Planning the Play

Before ducking, ask:

- Who is the dangerous opponent?
- What communication am I breaking?
- Can I afford to lose the trick?
- What happens if I win immediately?
- Will ducking improve my timing?

---

# Common Mistakes

- Ducking one trick too many.
- Winning too early.
- Forgetting entry requirements.
- Allowing the opponents too many winners.
- Ducking when immediate control is required.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Recognize declarer's hold-up play.
- Switch suits when appropriate.
- Preserve communication.
- Avoid helping declarer establish timing.
- Count declarer's winners.

---

# Advantages

- Breaks communications.
- Preserves entries.
- Creates timing advantages.
- Enables squeezes and endplays.
- Keeps the dangerous opponent off lead.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires careful counting.
- Easy to mistime.
- Incorrect ducking may lose the contract.
- Sometimes immediate action is preferable.

---

---

# Declarer's Checklist

Before ducking:

- Can I safely lose this trick?
- Which defender is dangerous?
- Am I preserving communication?
- Do I need this winner later?
- Will ducking improve my timing?

---

# Memory Guide

## Four Steps

1. Recognize the strategic opportunity.
2. Refuse to win.
3. Preserve communication.
4. Gain the advantage later.

---

# Comparison

| Technique | Main Idea |
|-----------|-----------|
| Ducking | Deliberately lose a trick now |
| Hold Up | Ducking specifically to cut communications in notrump |
| Avoidance Play | Keep one opponent off lead |
| Endplay | Force a defender to make a favorable return |

---

# Typical Hold-Up Sequence

```
Opponent Leads Long Suit
↓

Duck

↓

Duck Again (if appropriate)

↓

Win the Next Trick

↓

Communication Broken
```

---

# Memory Aid

**Ducking = "Lose Now to Win Later."**

Remember:

- **Do not win automatically.**
- **Break the opponents' communication.**
- **Preserve important entries.**
- **Keep the dangerous opponent off lead.**
- **Use ducking to improve timing and create future opportunities.**

Ducking is one of the fundamental techniques of expert bridge. Whether used by declarer to cut communications or by defenders to preserve strategic control, the willingness to refuse an immediate winner often determines the success of the entire hand.
