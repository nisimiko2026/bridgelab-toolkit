# Delayed Finesse

## Overview

A **Delayed Finesse** is a finesse that declarer **postpones until later in the play** rather than taking at the first opportunity. The delay allows declarer to gather additional information, preserve communication, improve the odds, or eliminate alternative lines before committing to the finesse.

Expert declarers often delay a finesse whenever the contract permits. Additional information from the auction, defenders' carding, or revealed distributions frequently turns a guessing situation into an informed decision.

One of the fundamental principles of declarer play is:

> **Delay a guess until it is no longer a guess.**

---

# Objectives

A delayed finesse aims to:

- Gather additional information before making a critical decision.
- Improve the percentage chance of success.
- Reduce unnecessary guessing.
- Preserve flexibility.
- Keep alternative lines available.

---

# Basic Principle

Instead of immediately taking a finesse, declarer first:

- Cashes certain winners.
- Counts the defenders' distribution.
- Observes signals and discards.
- Establishes another suit.
- Identifies the dangerous opponent.

Only then is the finesse taken.

---

# Why Delay?

A delayed finesse may become easier because declarer learns:

- Which defender has extra length.
- Where missing high-card points are likely to be.
- Which defender is dangerous.
- Whether another line of play has become superior.
- Whether the finesse is needed at all.

---

# Sources of Additional Information

Useful information may come from:

## The Auction

The bidding often reveals:

- High-card strength.
- Suit length.
- Distribution.
- Likely honor locations.

---

## Card Play

Each trick may reveal:

- Original suit lengths.
- Honor combinations.
- Defensive signals.
- Remaining entries.

---

## Counting

As the hand develops, declarer should count:

- Distribution.
- Remaining trumps.
- High-card points.
- Vacant spaces.

This information may determine the correct direction of the finesse.

---

# Example 1 – Counting Before Finessing

Contract:

```
3NT
```

Declarer needs a successful heart finesse.

Instead of taking it immediately, declarer first establishes clubs.

The defenders reveal their distribution.

The location of the missing heart queen becomes much more likely.

Declarer then takes the finesse with greater confidence.

---

# Example 2 – Dangerous Opponent

West has established a long spade suit.

If West gains the lead too early, the contract fails.

Declarer delays the finesse until enough winners have been established to survive losing the lead.

---

# Example 3 – Finesse Becomes Unnecessary

Declarer initially plans a diamond finesse.

Later, a successful strip and endplay forces a defender to lead diamonds into declarer's tenace.

The delayed finesse is never taken because it is no longer needed.

---

# Delayed Finesse vs. First-Round Finesse

| First-Round Finesse | Delayed Finesse |
|---------------------|-----------------|
| Taken immediately | Postponed |
| Uses only initial information | Uses information gathered during play |
| Often the normal percentage play | Often the practical percentage play |
| Less flexible | More flexible |

---

# Delayed Finesse vs. Two-Way Finesse

A delayed finesse is **not** a separate type of finesse.

Rather, it is a matter of **timing**.

Many delayed finesses are also:

- Two-way finesses.
- Marked finesses.
- Repeat finesses.

The delay allows declarer to choose the correct direction.

---

# Planning the Delay

Before postponing a finesse, ask:

- Can I safely delay the decision?
- Will I gain useful information?
- Will delaying preserve my entries?
- Could delaying make the finesse impossible?
- Is there another line that may eliminate the need for a finesse?

A delayed finesse is valuable only if the delay improves declarer's position.

---

# When Not to Delay

Do **not** postpone the finesse if:

- An essential entry may disappear.
- Defenders may attack a dangerous suit.
- Communication may be lost.
- Timing requires immediate action.
- Waiting provides no additional information.

Sometimes the immediate finesse remains the best play.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Delaying without a purpose.
- Losing an essential entry while waiting.
- Ignoring useful information that becomes available.
- Assuming every finesse should be delayed.
- Forgetting to reconsider the plan after new information emerges.

# Key Principles

- A delayed finesse postpones the decision until more information is available.
- Delay a finesse only when the expected information is likely to improve the decision.
- Continue counting and observing throughout the hand.
- Reassess whether the finesse is still necessary before taking it.
- Expert declarers prefer informed decisions over early guesses whenever the timing allows.
