# Repeat Finesse

## Overview

A **Repeat Finesse** is a finesse that is taken **more than once in the same suit**, usually because declarer holds multiple touching honors opposite intermediate cards. After a successful first finesse, declarer regains the lead and repeats the finesse to win additional tricks.

Repeat finesses are common in both notrump and suit contracts. They are particularly effective when several missing honors are favorably positioned behind the same defender.

---

# Objectives

A repeat finesse aims to:

- Win multiple tricks in the same suit.
- Maximize the value of a favorable honor position.
- Establish additional winners.
- Exploit repeated opportunities against the same defender.

---

# Basic Principle

Instead of taking a single finesse and then cashing winners, declarer:

1. Takes the first finesse.
2. Regains the lead.
3. Leads the suit again.
4. Repeats the finesse.

Each successful finesse may win an additional trick.

---

# Typical Holding

A common example is:

```
Dummy
♠ A J 10 5

Declarer
♠ K 9 8 2
```

Missing:

```
♠ Q 7 6 4 3
```

If East holds the queen, declarer can:

- Lead toward the jack.
- Win the finesse.
- Regain the lead.
- Lead the suit again toward the ten.

Two successful finesses produce additional tricks.

---

# When Is a Repeat Finesse Possible?

A repeat finesse is possible when:

- Declarer retains enough entries.
- The suit contains multiple touching honors.
- The missing honors are likely to be in the same hand.
- The first finesse does not exhaust the suit.

---

# Planning the Play

Before beginning the suit, ask:

- Can I reach the hand containing the lead again?
- Do I have enough entries?
- Will the first finesse leave another finesse available?
- Is there enough information to choose the correct defender?

A repeat finesse often succeeds or fails because of entry management.

# Example 1 – Two Successful Finesses

Contract:

```
3NT
```

Dummy:

```
♥ A J 10 4
```

Declarer:

```
♥ K 8 7 2
```

Missing:

```
♥ Q
```

Declarer:

1. Leads toward the jack.
2. Wins.
3. Regains the lead.
4. Leads toward the ten.

Both honors score if the queen remains with East.

---

# Example 2 – Information from the Auction

West has shown extra values during the auction.

Declarer concludes that East is more likely to hold the missing queen.

The repeat finesse is directed through East on both occasions.

---

# Example 3 – Entry Management

Declarer has only one entry to dummy.

Although two finesses are theoretically available, the second cannot be taken because declarer cannot regain the lead to repeat the process.

The contract depends on preserving the necessary entry.

---

# Repeat Finesse vs. Deep Finesse

| Repeat Finesse | Deep Finesse |
|----------------|--------------|
| Same finesse is taken more than once | Finesse is taken against a lower honor after assuming a higher honor has already been located |
| Usually involves multiple entries | Often involves unusual honor combinations |
| Common technique | Relatively uncommon |

---

# Repeat Finesse vs. Two-Way Finesse

| Repeat Finesse | Two-Way Finesse |
|----------------|-----------------|
| Repeats the same finesse direction | Declarer chooses between two possible directions |
| Requires multiple entries | Usually requires only one decision |
| Exploits repeated favorable positioning | Exploits information before choosing the direction |

---

# Timing

Sometimes declarer should delay the second finesse until:

- Additional information is available.
- Entries have been preserved.
- Side suits have been developed.
- The dangerous opponent has been neutralized.

The second finesse need not immediately follow the first.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Failing to preserve entries for the second finesse.
- Cashing winners too early.
- Assuming the second finesse is automatic.
- Ignoring information gained after the first finesse.
- Forgetting to reassess the position before repeating the finesse.

# Key Principles

- A repeat finesse involves taking the same finesse more than once in a suit.
- Multiple entries are usually required to execute the technique successfully.
- Reassess the position after the first finesse before committing to the second.
- Entry management is often the key to a successful repeat finesse.
- A repeat finesse allows declarer to extract maximum value from a favorable honor position.
