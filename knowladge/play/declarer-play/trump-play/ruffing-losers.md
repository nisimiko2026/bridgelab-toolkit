# Ruffing Losers

## Overview

**Ruffing losers** is one of the most fundamental declarer play techniques in suit contracts. Declarer uses a trump from one hand to ruff a losing card from the opposite hand, converting what would have been a losing trick into a winning one.

Unlike **crossruffing**, where both hands repeatedly score ruffs, ruffing losers is usually part of a normal declarer plan. It often precedes drawing the defenders' trumps.

A successful declarer always asks:

> **Can I eliminate any losers by ruffing them before drawing trumps?**

---

# Objectives

Ruffing losers allows declarer to:

- Eliminate losing tricks.
- Increase the total number of winners.
- Improve the chances of making the contract.
- Reduce dependence on successful finesses.
- Take advantage of shortness in one hand.

---

# Basic Principle

A loser in one hand becomes a winner by being ruffed in the opposite hand.

Example:

```
Dummy
♦ 4

Declarer
♦ K 8 7
```

Instead of losing the fourth diamond, declarer ruffs it with a trump in dummy.

---

# Identifying Ruffing Opportunities

Before playing to trick one, count:

1. Sure winners.
2. Sure losers.
3. Potential ruffs.

Typical candidates include:

- Small cards opposite a singleton or void.
- Long suits with one or more losers.
- Side suits that cannot be established in time.

---

# Short-Trump Hand Principle

As a general rule:

> Ruff losers in the hand with **fewer trumps**.

This preserves trump length in the long-trump hand for drawing the defenders' trumps later.

Example:

```
Dummy
♠ J 8 5

Declarer
♠ A K Q 10 7
```

If possible, ruff losers in dummy rather than in declarer's hand.

---

# Ruff Before Drawing Trumps

Often the correct sequence is:

1. Ruff one or more losers.
2. Draw the defenders' remaining trumps.
3. Cash side-suit winners.

If declarer draws trumps first, the opportunity to ruff the losers disappears.

---

# When to Delay Drawing Trumps

Delay drawing trumps when:

- Dummy has shortness.
- Losers can be ruffed safely.
- Defenders cannot overruff.
- Entries remain available.

Once the planned ruffs have been completed, declarer usually draws the remaining trumps.

---

# Count the Outstanding Trumps

Before ruffing:

- Count the missing trumps.
- Estimate their distribution.
- Consider whether defenders can overruff.
- Decide whether one round of trumps should be drawn first.

Good trump management is essential.

---

# Example 1

Contract:

```
4♥
```

Dummy:

```
♣ 4
```

Declarer:

```
♣ K J 8 5
```

Declarer:

- Wins the opening lead.
- Ruffs a losing club in dummy.
- Then draws trumps.

The club loser disappears.

---

# Ruffing Losers vs. Crossruff

| Ruffing Losers | Crossruff |
|----------------|-----------|
| Usually ruffs in one hand | Ruffs repeatedly in both hands |
| Standard declarer technique | Specialized technique |
| Usually followed by drawing trumps | Often avoids drawing trumps until late |
| Eliminates losers | Creates additional trump tricks |

Most suit contracts involve ruffing losers rather than a full crossruff.

---

# Ruffing Losers vs. Dummy Reversal

In a normal hand:

- Ruff in the **short-trump hand**.

In a **dummy reversal**:

- Declarer intentionally ruffs in the **long-trump hand**.

The objectives of the two techniques are different.

---

# Planning the Hand

Before playing from dummy, ask:

- How many losers do I have?
- Which losers can be ruffed?
- Which hand should perform the ruffs?
- How many entries are available?
- Should I draw any trumps first?
- Can a defender overruff?

A complete plan prevents losing ruffing opportunities.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Drawing all the trumps before ruffing losers.
- Ruffing in the long-trump hand without a reason.
- Ignoring the possibility of an overruff.
- Wasting high trumps unnecessarily.
- Failing to preserve entries.
- Forgetting to count the outstanding trumps.

# Key Principles

- Ruff losers before drawing trumps when doing so gains additional tricks.
- As a general rule, ruff losers in the hand with fewer trumps.
- Count the outstanding trumps before beginning the ruffing plan.
- Preserve entries so you can reach the hand that will perform the ruffs.
- Let the overall declarer plan determine whether to ruff first or draw trumps first.
