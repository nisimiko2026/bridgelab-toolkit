# Crossruff

## Overview

A **crossruff** is a declarer play technique in which declarer scores tricks by **ruffing alternating suits in both hands** instead of drawing the opponents' trumps.

Unlike normal suit contracts, where declarer usually removes the defenders' trumps first, a crossruff deliberately **preserves trumps in both hands** to generate additional tricks.

Crossruffs are particularly effective when:

- Both declarer and dummy have short suits.
- Both hands have trump length.
- Side suits provide entries between the hands.
- Defenders cannot overruff or force declarer to abandon the plan.

---

# Objectives

A successful crossruff aims to:

- Convert losers into ruffing tricks.
- Score extra trump tricks.
- Avoid losing control of the hand.
- Maximize the value of trumps in both hands.
- Reach the required number of tricks without establishing side suits.

---

# Basic Principle

Instead of drawing trumps:

1. Ruff a loser in dummy.
2. Cross to hand.
3. Ruff a loser in hand.
4. Return to dummy.
5. Continue alternating ruffs.

Each hand repeatedly gains the lead by ruffing in the other hand.

---

# When to Use a Crossruff

A crossruff is attractive when:

- Both hands contain several trumps.
- Both hands have short suits that can be ruffed.
- Side-suit entries remain available.
- Drawing trumps would eliminate needed ruffs.
- Defenders cannot overruff easily.

---

# Typical Example

Contract:

```
4♠
```

Dummy:

```
♠ J 8 5
♥ —
♦ K 8 5
♣ A K 9 4
```

Declarer:

```
♠ A K Q 7
♥ 8 5 4
♦ —
♣ Q J 8 5
```

Rather than drawing trumps immediately, declarer:

- Ruffs a heart in dummy.
- Returns to hand with a club.
- Ruffs a diamond in hand.
- Returns to dummy.
- Continues alternating ruffs.

Several extra trump tricks are created.

---

# Planning the Crossruff

Before beginning, determine:

- How many ruffs are available?
- Which hand should perform each ruff?
- How many entries remain?
- Can defenders overruff?
- Will drawing trumps destroy the plan?

Good planning is essential because entries are often limited.

---

# Entries Are Critical

A crossruff succeeds only if declarer can move repeatedly between the two hands.

Typical entries include:

- Side-suit winners.
- Trump winners.
- Established long suits.

Without sufficient entries, the crossruff may fail before enough ruffs have been taken.

---

# Beware of Overruffs

The greatest danger is allowing a defender to overruff.

Consider:

- Outstanding trump length.
- Trump spots.
- Whether defenders have already shown shortages.

Sometimes declarer should draw one or two rounds of trumps before beginning the crossruff.

---

# Partial Trump Draw

Occasionally the correct plan is:

1. Draw one or two rounds of trumps.
2. Remove the dangerous defender's high trumps.
3. Begin the crossruff.

This reduces the risk of an overruff while preserving enough trumps for the planned ruffs.

---

# Timing

Do not begin the crossruff until:

- Essential side-suit winners have been cashed.
- Necessary entries have been preserved.
- Defenders cannot create unexpected ruffs.
- The overall plan has been established.

Poor timing can strand winners or leave declarer without entries.

---

# Crossruff vs. Drawing Trumps

| Draw Trumps | Crossruff |
|-------------|-----------|
| Removes defenders' trumps | Preserves trumps in both hands |
| Protects established winners | Creates additional trump tricks |
| Standard strategy | Specialized technique |
| Simplifies the play | Requires careful planning |

The choice depends on which approach produces more tricks.

# Example 1

Contract:

```
4♥
```

Both declarer and dummy hold four trumps.

Each hand also has a singleton in a different side suit.

Declarer alternates ruffing clubs and diamonds instead of drawing trumps.

---

# Example 2

Contract:

```
5♣
```

Dummy has three losing hearts.

Declarer has three losing spades.

Each hand is short in the other's losing suit.

A crossruff converts all six losers into trump tricks.

---

# Example 3

Outstanding trumps:

```
West
♠ 10 9

East
♠ 6
```

Declarer first draws one round of trumps to remove the 10 and 9 before beginning the crossruff.

This prevents an expensive overruff.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Drawing all the trumps before starting.
- Beginning the crossruff without enough entries.
- Ignoring the possibility of an overruff.
- Forgetting to cash side-suit winners first.
- Failing to count the outstanding trumps.
- Losing control of the hand through poor timing.

# Key Principles

- A crossruff scores tricks by alternating ruffs in both hands.
- Do not draw trumps automatically when a crossruff offers more tricks.
- Preserve enough trumps in both hands to complete the plan.
- Carefully manage entries between declarer and dummy.
- Always consider the danger of an overruff before beginning the crossruff.
