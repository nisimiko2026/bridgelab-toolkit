# Drawing Trumps

## Overview

**Drawing trumps** is one of the most fundamental techniques in declarer play. It involves leading trumps to remove the defenders' trump cards, preventing them from ruffing declarer's winners.

A common guideline is:

> **Draw trumps first, unless you have a good reason not to.**

While this is often the correct strategy, expert declarers recognize many situations where delaying or even avoiding drawing trumps produces a better result.

---

# Objectives

Drawing trumps allows declarer to:

- Prevent defenders from ruffing winners.
- Protect established side-suit tricks.
- Gain control of the hand.
- Simplify the play.
- Reduce defenders' tactical opportunities.

Removing the defenders' trumps often makes the remainder of the hand much easier to play.

---

# Why Drawing Trumps Is Important

Suppose declarer establishes a long diamond suit.

If defenders still hold trumps, they may ruff the winners before declarer can cash them.

By drawing trumps first, declarer ensures that side-suit winners remain winners.

---

# The General Rule

Whenever possible:

1. Win the opening lead.
2. Count the missing trumps.
3. Draw the defenders' trumps.
4. Develop side suits.
5. Cash winners.

This sequence succeeds on many routine contracts.

---

# Count the Missing Trumps

Before leading trumps, determine:

- How many trumps are missing?
- What is the most likely distribution?
- How many rounds are required?

Example:

```
You hold:
♥ A K 8 5

Dummy:
♥ Q J 7 3
```

You have eight trumps.

Five are missing.

The most likely split is **3–2**, so three rounds will usually draw all the defenders' trumps.

---

# Drawing Trumps Efficiently

Avoid wasting high honors.

Example:

```
Dummy
♠ A J 7

Declarer
♠ K Q 8 5
```

The normal play is:

- ♠K
- ♠Q
- ♠A

rather than cashing all the honors from one hand unnecessarily.

Efficient play helps preserve entries and communication.

---

# When NOT to Draw Trumps Immediately

There are many important exceptions.

## Establish a Side Suit First

Sometimes a long suit must be established before entries disappear.

Example:

Declarer may need to lose a club trick before drawing trumps.

---

## Ruff Losers

If dummy is short in a suit:

```
Dummy
♦ 3

Declarer
♦ K 8 7 6
```

Declarer may first ruff losing diamonds in dummy before removing the defenders' trumps.

---

## Dummy Reversal

In a **dummy reversal**, declarer intentionally ruffs losers in the long-trump hand.

Drawing trumps immediately would destroy the entire plan.

---

## Crossruff

In a crossruff, declarer scores numerous ruffs in both hands.

Drawing trumps first would eliminate the opportunity to crossruff.

---

## Preserve Entries

Drawing trumps too early may consume important entries between the two hands.

Sometimes declarer delays trump play until communication has been secured.

---

## Timing Considerations

Occasionally declarer must:

- Take a finesse.
- Establish a long suit.
- Execute a hold-up play.

Only afterward should trumps be drawn.

---

# Trump Management

Good declarers think about:

- How many rounds are needed.
- Which honors should be used.
- Preserving entries.
- Remaining trump control.

Trump management is as important as drawing trumps itself.

---

# Example 1 – Routine Trump Draw

Contract:

```
4♠
```

Dummy:

```
♠ A J 6
```

Declarer:

```
♠ K Q 8 5
```

Missing:

```
♠ 10 9 7 4 3
```

Declarer wins the opening lead and draws trumps before attacking side suits.

---

# Example 2 – Ruff First

Contract:

```
4♥
```

Dummy:

```
♣ 4
```

Declarer:

```
♣ K J 8 7
```

Instead of drawing trumps immediately, declarer first ruffs a club loser in dummy.

Only then are trumps drawn.

---

# Example 3 – Establish the Long Suit

Declarer must lose a diamond trick to establish four additional winners.

If trumps are drawn first, the necessary entry may disappear.

Correct play:

1. Establish diamonds.
2. Regain the lead.
3. Draw trumps.
4. Cash the winners.

---

# Planning Before Drawing Trumps

Before leading the first trump, ask:

- How many trumps are missing?
- How are they likely to divide?
- Do I need to ruff losers first?
- Do I need to establish a side suit?
- Are my entries secure?
- Is a dummy reversal or crossruff possible?
- Can defenders gain a ruff if I delay?

The correct answer determines whether trumps should be drawn immediately.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Automatically drawing trumps without a plan.
- Failing to count the missing trumps.
- Drawing trumps before ruffing losers.
- Wasting trump honors unnecessarily.
- Destroying entries between the hands.
- Missing opportunities for a dummy reversal or crossruff.

# Key Principles

- Drawing trumps is usually the first priority in a suit contract.
- Count the missing trumps before playing the first round.
- Draw trumps efficiently while preserving entries and communication.
- Delay drawing trumps when you need to ruff losers, establish a side suit, execute a dummy reversal, or perform a crossruff.
- Always let the overall plan of the hand determine when to draw trumps.
