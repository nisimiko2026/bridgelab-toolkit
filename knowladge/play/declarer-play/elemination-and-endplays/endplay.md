# Endplay (Throw-In) Technique

## Overview

The **Endplay**, also known as a **Throw-In**, is a declarer-play technique in which declarer deliberately gives a defender the lead at a time when every possible return benefits declarer.

The defender is said to be **endplayed** because they have no favorable exit. Whatever they lead gives declarer an extra trick.

Endplays are among the most powerful techniques in bridge and frequently occur in both suit and notrump contracts.

---

# Purpose

An Endplay is used to:

- Force an opponent to lead into declarer's tenace.
- Force a defender to concede a ruff-and-discard.
- Eliminate safe exit cards.
- Create extra tricks without establishing additional winners.
- Avoid dangerous guesses.

---

# Basic Principle

The technique consists of three steps:

1. Remove the defender's safe exit cards.
2. Put the defender on lead.
3. Profit from the forced return.

The key idea is:

> **The defender has no safe exit.**

---

# Requirements

A successful endplay usually requires:

- Elimination of side suits.
- A defender who must eventually win a trick.
- No safe return card for that defender.
- A favorable position after the forced return.

---

# Typical Situations

Endplays are commonly used to:

- Lead through an honor.
- Obtain a ruff-and-discard.
- Force a trump lead.
- Avoid a finesse guess.
- Create an extra trick in a side suit.

---

# Eliminating Side Suits

Before the throw-in, declarer often cashes all winners and removes side suits from both hands.

Example:

```
Dummy
♠ KQ
♥ A
♦ —
♣ —

Declarer
♠ A10
♥ —
♦ A
♣ —
```

If the defender is left holding only spades and diamonds, putting that defender on lead forces a favorable return.

---

# Example 1 – Lead into a Tenace

## Position

```
Dummy
♠ AQ

Declarer
♠ J10
```

West holds:

```
♠ Kx
```

After stripping the other suits, declarer exits with a diamond.

West wins and must return a spade.

Whatever West leads, declarer scores two spade tricks.

---

# Example 2 – Ruff and Discard

Trump = Hearts

West wins a club.

West's remaining cards:

```
♠
♦
```

Any return:

- Gives declarer a ruff in one hand.
- Allows a discard from the other.

Declarer gains an extra trick.

---

# Example 3 – Forced Trump Lead

Trump = Spades

West has only trumps remaining.

Declarer exits in a side suit.

West wins and must return a trump.

This often removes a defensive trump and benefits declarer.

---

# Throw-In Example

Contract:

```
4♠
```

Declarer:

- Draws trumps.
- Eliminates clubs and hearts.
- Throws West in with a diamond.

West must now:

- Lead away from the king.
- Or concede a ruff-and-discard.

Either return gives declarer the contract.

---

# Elimination and Endplay

The two techniques are closely related.

**Elimination** removes safe exit cards.

**Endplay** forces the defender to lead.

Many successful endplays begin with an elimination play.

---

# Dangerous Opponent

Often only one defender is dangerous.

Declarer tries to:

- Avoid losing tricks to the dangerous defender.
- Endplay the safe defender.

This is especially important in notrump contracts.

---

# Common Endplay Situations

- Lead into AQ.
- Lead into KJ.
- Ruff-and-discard.
- Forced finesse.
- Trump promotion.
- Entry destruction.

---

# Hand Example

```
Dummy
♠ AQ4
♥ K7
♦ —
♣ —

Declarer
♠ J10
♥ A
♦ A
♣ —
```

West:

```
♠ K8
♦ K
```

Declarer exits with the ♦A.

West wins and must lead spades.

Declarer scores two spade tricks.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Keep safe exit cards.
- Avoid winning too early.
- Preserve communication.
- Anticipate elimination plays.
- Avoid being thrown in.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Throwing in the wrong defender.
- Forgetting to eliminate side suits.
- Losing communication.
- Endplaying before removing exits.
- Miscounting defender's cards.

---

# Advantages

- Creates tricks without high cards.
- Avoids difficult guesses.
- Forces defenders to help declarer.
- Effective in both suit and notrump contracts.
- Frequently wins close contracts.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires careful planning.
- Depends on accurate counting.
- Timing is critical.
- Incorrect elimination ruins the ending.

# Declarer's Checklist

Before attempting an endplay:

- Have I eliminated all side suits?
- Which defender should win the trick?
- Does that defender have a safe exit?
- Can the defender avoid helping me?
- Have I preserved my entries?

---

# Memory Guide

## Three Steps

1. Eliminate.
2. Throw in.
3. Profit.

---

## Typical Results

The defender is forced to:

- Lead into a tenace.
- Give a ruff-and-discard.
- Lead trumps.
- Break a new suit.

All benefit declarer.

---

# Memory Aid

**ENDPLAY**

- **E**liminate safe exits.
- **N**o safe return remains.
- **D**rive the defender in.
- **P**reserve entries.
- **L**ead is forced.
- **A**dvantage goes to declarer.
- **Y**ou gain the extra trick.

An Endplay is one of declarer's most elegant weapons. By stripping the defender of safe exit cards and forcing that defender to win a trick at the right moment, declarer compels a return that creates the extra trick needed to make the contract.
