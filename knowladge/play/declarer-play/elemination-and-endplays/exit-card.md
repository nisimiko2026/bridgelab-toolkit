# Exit Card

## Overview

An **exit card** is a card that allows a player to surrender the lead **without giving an advantage to the opponents**. In endplay situations, the availability—or absence—of a safe exit card often determines whether a defender can escape an endplay or is forced to concede a trick.

Understanding exit cards is fundamental to **elimination**, **throw-ins**, and **strip-and-endplay** techniques.

---

# Objectives

Understanding exit cards helps a player to:

- Avoid being endplayed.
- Plan elimination plays.
- Recognize opportunities for throw-ins.
- Preserve safe defensive exits.
- Force favorable leads from the defenders.

---

# What Is an Exit Card?

An **exit card** is a safe card that transfers the lead to an opponent **without improving their position**.

A good exit card should:

- Avoid leading into declarer's tenace.
- Avoid conceding a ruff and discard.
- Avoid breaking an unplayed suit.
- Avoid giving away a trick.

When no such card exists, the player is said to be **without an exit card**.

---

# Safe Exit Cards

Typical safe exits include:

- A card in a suit already exhausted by declarer.
- A low card in a harmless suit.
- A suit in which declarer already holds all the winners.
- A suit that cannot establish an extra trick for declarer.

Safe exit cards are valuable defensive resources.

---

# Unsafe Exit Cards

An exit becomes unsafe when it:

- Leads into a tenace.
- Gives declarer a ruff and discard.
- Breaks a guarded honor.
- Establishes an extra trick for declarer.
- Gives declarer a free finesse.

These are precisely the situations declarer tries to create before executing an endplay.

---

# Elimination

The purpose of an **elimination** is to remove all of a defender's safe exit cards.

Declarer cashes winners in the side suits until only dangerous exits remain.

Once the defender wins a trick, every available return benefits declarer.

---

# Throw-In

A **throw-in** works only when the chosen defender has no safe exit card.

Declarer deliberately concedes a trick.

The defender wins—but every possible return helps declarer.

The absence of an exit card makes the throw-in effective.

---

# Strip and Endplay

A strip-and-endplay combines:

1. Eliminating all safe exit cards.
2. Throwing in the defender.

The defender cannot escape because every remaining exit is unfavorable.

---

# Example 1 – Safe Exit

West wins a club.

West still holds a small diamond that declarer has already exhausted.

Returning the diamond costs nothing.

The diamond is a **safe exit card**.

---

# Example 2 – No Safe Exit

West wins a heart.

Only two suits remain:

- Spades.
- Diamonds.

A spade lead gives declarer a finesse.

A diamond lead concedes a ruff and discard.

West has **no safe exit card**.

Declarer has successfully created an endplay.

---

# Example 3 – Elimination

Declarer first cashes:

- All clubs.
- All diamonds.

Only hearts remain.

After West wins a heart trick, every possible return gives declarer an extra trick.

West's exit cards have been eliminated.

---

# Defensive Planning

Defenders should try to preserve safe exit cards whenever possible.

Avoid:

- Discarding every small card in a side suit.
- Blocking a safe exit suit.
- Allowing declarer to eliminate all harmless returns.

Maintaining an exit card often prevents an endplay.

---

# Declarer's Planning

Before attempting an endplay, ask:

- Which defender do I want to throw in?
- What exit cards does that defender still hold?
- Can I eliminate them?
- Will every remaining lead benefit me?

If the answer is yes, the endplay is likely to succeed.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Throwing in a defender who still has a safe exit.
- Forgetting to eliminate one side suit.
- Assuming every forced lead is an endplay.
- Defenders discarding their last safe exit unnecessarily.
- Failing to recognize that an exit card remains.

# Key Principles

- An exit card is a safe way to surrender the lead.
- Endplays succeed because the defender has no safe exit card.
- Elimination removes the defender's safe exits before the throw-in.
- Defenders should preserve safe exit cards whenever possible.
- Always identify the available exit cards before planning or defending against an endplay.
