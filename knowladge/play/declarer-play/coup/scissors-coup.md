# Scissors Coup Technique

## Overview

The **Scissors Coup** is an advanced declarer-play technique in which declarer deliberately **cuts the defenders' communication** by forcing one defender to win a trick prematurely or by removing an important entry between the defenders.

Like a pair of scissors cutting a string, the coup **severs the connection** between the defenders' hands. Once communication is broken, established winners in one defender's hand can no longer be reached by the other defender.

The Scissors Coup is most commonly seen in **Notrump contracts**, although similar positions may arise in suit contracts.

---

# Purpose

The Scissors Coup allows declarer to:

- Break defensive communication.
- Isolate one defender's long suit.
- Prevent defenders from cashing established winners.
- Protect vulnerable holdings.
- Improve the chances of making the contract.

---

# Basic Principle

The guiding principle is:

> **Remove the defenders' entry before they can use it.**

Declarer deliberately forces one defender to take a trick at a time that destroys communication between the defenders.

Once the connecting entry is gone, the remaining winners in the opposite hand become stranded.

---

# Requirements

A successful Scissors Coup usually requires:

- A dangerous long suit held by one defender.
- An entry to that suit in the other defender's hand.
- A way for declarer to remove that entry.
- Accurate counting.
- Proper timing.

---

# Relationship to Other Techniques

The Scissors Coup is closely related to:

- Hold-Up Play
- Avoidance Play
- Entry Management
- Endplay
- Discovery Play

Unlike a Hold-Up Play, which usually cuts communication by ducking a suit, the Scissors Coup often removes a **specific entry** from one defender.

---

# Example 1 – Classic Scissors Coup

## Contract

```
3NT
```

West has established:

```
♠ KQJ10
```

East holds:

```
♠ A
```

The ♠A is West's only entry.

Declarer wins the first spade trick with the Ace from dummy instead of ducking.

Later, although West's spades are established, East no longer has an entry to reach them.

West's long suit is stranded.

---

# Example 2 – Removing an Entry

Dummy

```
♦ AQJ
```

Declarer

```
♦ K54
```

East's ♦10 is the only entry to West's long clubs.

Declarer forces East to win the ♦10 early.

When West later gains the lead, the clubs cannot be reached.

---

# Example 3 – Preventing a Switch

West has:

- Long hearts.
- No outside entry.

East has:

- The ♥A.
- Several side winners.

Declarer forces East to win the ♥A before West's hearts are established.

The communication between the defenders has been cut.

---

# Planning the Play

Before Trick One, ask:

- Which defender has the dangerous suit?
- Which defender provides the entry?
- Can I remove that entry?
- Will cutting communication protect my contract?
- Can I preserve my own entries?

---

# Timing

Timing is essential.

Typical sequence:

1. Identify the dangerous defender.
2. Locate the critical entry.
3. Force that entry to be used early.
4. Prevent further communication.
5. Execute the rest of the declarer plan.

---

# Common Situations

The Scissors Coup frequently occurs:

- In Notrump contracts.
- Against established long suits.
- Together with Hold-Up Play.
- During Avoidance Play.
- Before defenders can cash winners.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Misidentifying the critical entry.
- Removing the wrong entry.
- Waiting too long to execute the coup.
- Losing declarer's own communication.
- Miscounting defenders' entries.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Preserve communications whenever possible.
- Avoid using key entries unnecessarily.
- Recognize declarer's attempts to isolate one hand.
- Cash established winners before communication is lost.
- Coordinate entry management with partner.

---

# Advantages

- Cuts defensive communication.
- Neutralizes dangerous long suits.
- Prevents defenders from cashing winners.
- Particularly effective in Notrump contracts.
- Often turns a failing contract into a successful one.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires accurate counting.
- Depends on identifying the correct entry.
- Timing is critical.
- May not be available if defenders have multiple entries.

---

# Declarer's Checklist

Before attempting a Scissors Coup:

- Which defender has the dangerous suit?
- What is the critical entry?
- Can I force that entry to disappear?
- Will the dangerous hand become isolated?
- Are my own entries secure?

---

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Identify the dangerous defender.
2. Locate the critical entry.
3. Remove or force the entry.
4. Cut defensive communication.
5. Execute the remaining plan.

# Memory Aid

**Scissors Coup = "Cut the Connection."**

Remember:

- **The objective is to sever communication between the defenders.**
- **Identify the defender with the dangerous long suit.**
- **Locate and remove the critical entry.**
- **Execute the coup before the defenders establish communication.**
- **The Scissors Coup is often combined with Hold-Up Play and Avoidance Play.**

The **Scissors Coup** is a classic communication-cutting technique in declarer play. By eliminating the defenders' ability to reach one another, declarer neutralizes dangerous long suits and prevents the defense from cashing established winners. Mastering this coup requires careful counting, precise timing, and a clear understanding of both defenders' entry positions.
