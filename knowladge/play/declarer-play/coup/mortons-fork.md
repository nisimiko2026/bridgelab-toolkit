# Morton's Fork Technique

## Overview

The **Morton's Fork** is an advanced declarer-play technique in which declarer places a defender in a position where **either of two choices benefits declarer**. Regardless of which option the defender chooses, declarer gains an advantage.

The name comes from **John Morton**, Archbishop of Canterbury (15th century), who was said to use a line of reasoning that led to the same conclusion regardless of the answer. In bridge, the defender is similarly placed on the "fork" of an unavoidable dilemma.

A Morton's Fork is frequently seen in **Notrump contracts**, but it can also arise in suit contracts.

---

# Purpose

A Morton's Fork allows declarer to:

- Force a defender into a losing choice.
- Gain an extra trick without guessing.
- Avoid risky finesses.
- Create favorable end positions.
- Improve the chances of making the contract.

---

# Basic Principle

The guiding principle is:

> **Whichever choice the defender makes, declarer benefits.**

The defender must choose between:

- Winning a trick immediately and giving declarer an advantage later.
- Ducking the trick and allowing declarer to establish additional winners.

Neither defense is satisfactory.

---

# When to Use

A Morton's Fork is appropriate when:

- A defender controls an important honor.
- Declarer can benefit whether that honor is played or withheld.
- Timing is important.
- Declarer has sufficient entries.
- The position creates two equally unattractive defensive choices.

---

# Requirements

A successful Morton's Fork usually requires:

- A critical decision for one defender.
- Proper timing.
- Accurate counting.
- Good entry management.
- Recognition of the dilemma.

---

# Example 1 – Classic Morton's Fork

## Contract

```
3NT
```

Dummy

```
♠ AQ10
```

Declarer

```
♠ J32
```

West holds:

```
♠ Kxx
```

Declarer leads toward dummy.

If West:

- **Plays the King**, declarer later scores the ♠10 and ♠J.
- **Plays low**, declarer wins the Queen and later may establish additional spade tricks.

West cannot defend perfectly.

---

# Example 2 – Protecting an Entry

Dummy

```
♦ AQJ
```

Declarer

```
♦ 432
```

West holds the ♦K.

Declarer leads toward dummy.

- If West wins the King immediately, dummy's remaining honors become established.
- If West ducks, declarer wins the Queen and preserves communication.

Again, either choice favors declarer.

---

# Example 3 – Suit Contract

Contract

```
4♥
```

Dummy

```
♣ AQ10
```

Declarer

```
♣ J54
```

East holds the ♣K.

Declarer leads toward dummy.

- If East covers, declarer establishes the remaining club honors.
- If East ducks, declarer wins cheaply and later develops additional club tricks.

---

# Planning the Play

Before attempting a Morton's Fork, ask:

- Which defender faces the critical decision?
- What happens if they win immediately?
- What happens if they duck?
- Do both choices improve my position?
- Are my entries sufficient?

---

# Timing

Timing is essential.

Typical sequence:

1. Count winners or losers.
2. Identify the defender's dilemma.
3. Preserve entries.
4. Create the fork.
5. Exploit whichever choice the defender makes.

---

# Why It Works

The defender cannot protect two objectives at once.

Typically they must choose between:

- Preserving an honor.
- Preserving communication.
- Protecting an entry.
- Preventing suit establishment.

Declarer profits regardless of the decision.

---

# Common Situations

Morton's Fork frequently occurs:

- In Notrump contracts.
- Before establishing a long suit.
- During honor combinations.
- In endgame positions.
- Together with Discovery Play.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Attempting the play without sufficient entries.
- Creating only one favorable outcome instead of two.
- Miscounting the defenders' remaining cards.
- Missing a simpler winning line.
- Playing too quickly before recognizing the opportunity.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Count declarer's likely winners.
- Preserve communication.
- Avoid exposing honor positions.
- Recognize the developing dilemma early.
- Choose the line that gives declarer the fewest total tricks.

---

# Advantages

- Forces defensive errors.
- Avoids pure guessing.
- Creates additional tricks.
- Works naturally with strong honor combinations.
- Particularly effective in Notrump contracts.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires accurate planning.
- Often depends on precise timing.
- May disappear if entries are lost.
- Not every hand contains a Morton's Fork opportunity.

# Memory Aid

**Morton's Fork = "Heads I Win, Tails You Lose."**

Remember:

- **The defender must choose between two unfavorable options.**
- **Whichever choice is made, declarer gains an advantage.**
- **Good timing and entry management are essential.**
- **Look for honor combinations that create a dilemma.**
- **A successful Morton's Fork removes the need for guessing.**

The **Morton's Fork** is one of bridge's classic dilemma plays. Rather than relying on probability or deception, declarer constructs a position in which the defender has no satisfactory defense. By recognizing and creating these positions, expert declarers convert difficult contracts into successful ones through pure logic and careful planning.
