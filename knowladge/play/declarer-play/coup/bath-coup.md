# Bath Coup Technique

## Overview

The **Bath Coup** is a classic declarer-play technique used primarily in **Notrump contracts**. Declarer deliberately **ducks the opening lead of an Ace or King**, despite holding the Ace or King, in order to prevent the defenders from continuing the suit effectively.

The Bath Coup is a specialized form of **Hold-Up Play**. Its purpose is to confuse the opening leader about the location of the missing honor and to cut communication between the defenders.

The name comes from the city of **Bath, England**, where the play was first recorded in the 19th century.

---

# Purpose

The Bath Coup allows declarer to:

- Break the defenders' communication.
- Prevent the opening leader from continuing the suit.
- Conceal the location of a missing honor.
- Keep the dangerous opponent off lead.
- Gain tempo to establish winners elsewhere.

---

# Basic Principle

The guiding principle is:

> **Duck the first trick to create uncertainty and disrupt the defense.**

Instead of winning immediately with the Ace (or King), declarer intentionally plays low.

The opening leader may incorrectly believe partner holds the missing honor and switch to another suit.

---

# When to Use

The Bath Coup is appropriate when:

- Playing a Notrump contract.
- The opening lead is from a strong honor sequence.
- Declarer holds the missing top honor.
- Continuing the suit would endanger the contract.
- A defensive switch is desirable.

---

# Requirements

A successful Bath Coup usually requires:

- A stopper in the led suit.
- An opening lead headed by the King or Ace.
- Accurate judgment of the defenders' likely continuation.
- Good timing.
- A reason to discourage continuation of the suit.

---

# Classic Position

### Dummy

```
♥ 742
```

### Declarer

```
♥ AQ5
```

West leads:

```
♥K
```

Instead of winning with the Ace, declarer plays low.

West may conclude that East holds the Ace and switch to another suit.

Declarer later wins the next heart with the Ace, leaving the defense without enough heart tricks.

---

# Example 1 – Bath Coup in 3NT

## Contract

```
3NT
```

West leads:

```
♠K
```

Dummy

```
♠ 643
```

Declarer

```
♠ AQ5
```

Declarer ducks.

If West switches, declarer later wins the spade Ace and has only one spade loser.

---

# Example 2 – Avoiding a Dangerous Continuation

West leads the ♦K.

Declarer holds:

```
♦ AQ4
```

Winning immediately allows the defense to continue diamonds later.

By ducking, declarer may induce West to abandon the suit.

---

# Why the Bath Coup Works

The opening leader often expects partner to hold the missing honor.

When declarer ducks:

- The defenders may misread the position.
- Communication is disrupted.
- The dangerous suit is abandoned.
- Declarer gains valuable time.

---

# Planning the Play

Before attempting a Bath Coup, ask:

- Can I afford to duck?
- Will the leader continue the suit?
- Is a switch likely?
- Does ducking improve my timing?
- Which defender is dangerous?

---

# Timing

Timing is critical.

Typical sequence:

1. Identify the dangerous suit.
2. Duck the opening lead.
3. Encourage a defensive switch.
4. Win the next round.
5. Execute the remainder of the declarer plan.

---

# Bath Coup vs. Hold-Up Play

| Bath Coup | Hold-Up Play |
|------------|--------------|
| Usually ducks only the first trick | May duck one or more tricks |
| Seeks to induce a defensive switch | Seeks to cut defenders' communication |
| Often relies on deception | Usually relies on counting and timing |

The Bath Coup is therefore a **specialized form of Hold-Up Play**.

---

# Common Situations

The Bath Coup frequently occurs:

- In 3NT contracts.
- Against opening leads of the King.
- Against strong opening leads.
- Before establishing a long suit.
- When one defender is dangerous.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Ducking without a stopper.
- Ducking when the leader is certain to continue the suit.
- Misidentifying the dangerous opponent.
- Forgetting the defenders may read the position correctly.
- Confusing the Bath Coup with an ordinary Hold-Up Play.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Count declarer's likely holdings.
- Continue the suit when appropriate.
- Avoid assuming partner holds the missing honor.
- Use count and attitude signals.
- Recognize possible Bath Coup positions.

---

# Advantages

- May induce a favorable defensive switch.
- Breaks communication.
- Protects vulnerable suits.
- Gains valuable tempo.
- Classic expert declarer technique.

---

# Disadvantages

- Depends on defender psychology.
- Less effective against experienced defenders.
- Requires accurate judgment.
- May fail if the defenders continue the suit correctly.

# Memory Aid

**Bath Coup = "Duck Once, Make Them Guess."**

Remember:

- **The Bath Coup is a specialized Hold-Up Play.**
- **Its objective is to induce the opening leader to abandon the suit.**
- **It works best in Notrump contracts.**
- **Only duck when you can safely regain control later.**
- **Against expert defenders, rely on counting as well as deception.**

The **Bath Coup** is one of bridge's oldest and most elegant declarer techniques. By ducking the opening lead of a high honor, declarer may persuade the defense to abandon its strongest suit, gaining the time needed to establish winners elsewhere. Although it relies partly on psychology, it remains an important weapon in the expert declarer's arsenal.
