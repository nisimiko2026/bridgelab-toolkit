# Entries

## Overview

An **entry** is a card that allows declarer to gain the lead in one hand in
order to reach winners or continue the planned line of play.

Successful declarer play depends not only on creating winners, but also on
being able to **reach** those winners when needed. Many otherwise successful
contracts fail because declarer exhausts the necessary entries too early.

Entry management is one of the most fundamental aspects of declarer planning.

---

# Objectives

Effective entry management allows declarer to

- reach established winners,
- develop long suits,
- take repeated finesses,
- execute squeeze positions,
- maintain communication between the two hands,
- avoid blocked suits.

---

# Communication Between Hands

Dummy and declarer function as a single combined hand.

Entries provide the communication that allows declarer to

- cross to dummy,
- return to hand,
- continue developing suits,
- execute the planned line of play.

Without sufficient entries, many winning lines become impossible.

---

# Types of Entries

Typical entries include

- high cards,
- trump entries,
- established winners,
- ruffs,
- overtakes,
- endplays.

Each may become valuable depending on the contract.

---

# Natural Entries

Natural entries are immediate winners.

Example

```
Dummy
♠ A73

Declarer
♠ K42
```

The ace provides an entry to dummy.

---

# Trump Entries

In suit contracts,

small trumps frequently become valuable entries.

Example

```
Dummy
♠ 5

Declarer
♠ AKQJ82
```

After drawing trumps,

the small trump may become the only remaining entry.

---

# Ruffing Entries

Declarer may create an entry by ruffing.

Example

```
Dummy
♣ 5

Declarer
♣ A42
```

A club ruff later becomes an important transportation mechanism.

---

# Overtake Entries

Sometimes an honor should be overtaken to preserve communication.

Example

```
Dummy
♦ AQ

Declarer
♦ KJ
```

Correct overtaking may preserve future entries.

---

# Preserving Entries

One of declarer's primary planning tasks.

Avoid using an entry prematurely.

Example

Incorrect

```
Win dummy's ace immediately
```

Correct

```
Save the ace until it becomes necessary.
```

---

# Creating Entries

Declarer may create new entries by

- establishing long suits,
- ruffing,
- overtaking,
- ducking,
- endplaying an opponent.

Creating entries often transforms an otherwise impossible contract into a
successful one.

---

# Entry Shortages

Many contracts fail because declarer

- runs out of entries,
- uses entries too early,
- blocks communication.

Always count entries before playing from trick one.

---

# Entry Management

Before playing to trick one, declarer should determine

- how many entries exist,
- which entries are essential,
- which entries are expendable,
- whether additional entries can be created.

---

# Entries in Notrump Contracts

In notrump,

entries are especially valuable because

- no ruffing entries exist,
- transportation depends almost entirely on high cards.

Declarer should preserve entries to the hand containing long-suit winners.

---

# Entries in Suit Contracts

Suit contracts provide additional possibilities.

Entries may be created by

- trumping losers,
- preserving trumps,
- shortening one hand,
- timing trump draws.

---

# Entries and Long Suit Establishment

Long suits frequently require repeated entries.

Example

```
Dummy
♣ AQ7654

Declarer
♣ K3
```

Several entries may be needed to establish and cash the suit.

---

# Entries and Finesses

Repeated finesses require sufficient transportation.

Example

```
Dummy
♠ AQ4

Declarer
♠ J53
```

Multiple entries may be needed for repeated finesse attempts.

---

# Entries and Squeezes

Many squeeze positions fail because

- declarer reaches the squeeze position,
- but lacks the entry needed to enjoy the established winner.

Entry planning begins long before the squeeze itself.

---

# Entries and Blocked Suits

Blocked suits often result from poor entry management.

Unblocking honors and preserving communication are closely related skills.

---

# Planning Entries

Before trick one, ask

- Where are my winners?
- How will I reach them?
- Which entries must be preserved?
- Can I create additional entries?
- Which entry is the most valuable?

---

# Partnership Agreements

Although entries are primarily a declarer-play concept,

partnership agreements concerning

- opening leads,
- signaling,
- suit preference,

can influence future communication.

---

# Modern Expert Philosophy

Expert declarers

- count winners,
- count losers,
- identify every entry,
- preserve transportation,
- postpone unnecessary entry use.

Many contracts are won solely because entries were managed correctly.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Using an Entry Too Early

A premature entry often strands winners.

---

## Ignoring Future Communication

Always consider how to return after crossing once.

---

## Blocking a Suit

Winning with the wrong honor may destroy future entries.

---

## Forgetting Trump Entries

Small trumps frequently become critical transportation.

---

## Not Counting Entries

Entry counting should begin before trick one.

---

# Expert Evaluation

Experts evaluate

- entry location,
- transportation,
- blocked suits,
- long-suit establishment,
- future communication,

before selecting a line of play.

---

# Universal Principles

Good declarer play requires

- preserving entries,
- creating new entries,
- avoiding blocked communication,
- planning transportation before developing tricks.

Entries are often more valuable than winners themselves.

---

# Learning Progression

Study in the following order

1. Communication
2. Natural Entries
3. Trump Entries
4. Ruffing Entries
5. Preserving Entries
6. Creating Entries
7. Entry Management
8. Blocked Suits
9. Advanced Planning

```

The remaining trump becomes an entry.

---

```

A later ruff creates transportation.

---

# Summary

Entries are the **communication network** between declarer and dummy. They
allow declarer to reach established winners, repeat finesses, execute squeezes,
develop long suits, and maintain transportation throughout the play.

Expert declarers identify and preserve every important entry before playing to
the first trick. Careful entry management—combined with sound planning,
timing, and communication—is one of the defining skills of successful declarer
play and often determines whether an otherwise makeable contract succeeds or
fails.
