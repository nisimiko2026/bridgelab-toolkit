# End Position Analysis

## Overview

**End Position Analysis** is the process of evaluating the final few tricks of a bridge hand before they are actually reached. Expert declarers often work **backward from the ending**, visualizing the last four to eight cards and planning how to reach that position.

Many advanced techniques—including **Squeezes**, **Endplays**, **Strip Squeezes**, **Trump Coups**, **Vienna Coups**, and **Elimination Plays**—can only be executed successfully if the desired ending is recognized early in the play.

The guiding principle is:

> **Plan the ending before you begin the middle game.**

---

# Purpose

End Position Analysis allows declarer to:

- Visualize the final layout of the hand.
- Identify winning endgame techniques.
- Preserve critical entries.
- Manage timing correctly.
- Eliminate unnecessary cards.
- Maximize the chance of making difficult contracts.

---

# Basic Principle

Before playing to Trick One, ask:

> **"What do I want the last five or six cards to look like?"**

Then work backward to determine:

- Which cards must be preserved.
- Which cards can safely be cashed.
- Which techniques may become available.

---

# Why It Is Important

Many contracts fail because declarer:

- Cashes winners too early.
- Loses a critical entry.
- Blocks a suit.
- Eliminates the wrong suit.
- Misses a squeeze or endplay.

Proper end position analysis prevents these mistakes.

---

# The Planning Process

### Step 1 – Count Winners or Losers

- **Notrump:** Count winners.
- **Suit contracts:** Count losers.

Determine what still needs to be accomplished.

---

### Step 2 – Identify Possible Endings

Consider whether the hand may lead to:

- A Simple Squeeze.
- A Double Squeeze.
- An Endplay.
- A Strip Squeeze.
- A Trump Coup.
- A Cross Ruff.
- A Dummy Reversal.
- A Ruffing Finesse.

---

### Step 3 – Visualize the Last Few Cards

Imagine:

- Remaining trumps.
- Remaining entries.
- Threat cards.
- Guarded suits.
- Defenders' likely holdings.

The clearer the picture, the easier the planning.

---

### Step 4 – Work Backward

Ask:

- Which cards must still be in my hand?
- Which entries must survive?
- Which suits must be eliminated?
- Which winners should be delayed?

---

### Step 5 – Execute the Plan

Carry out the play while remaining flexible.

Reevaluate after each important trick as new information becomes available.

---

# Example 1 – Planning a Squeeze

Contract

```
6NT
```

Declarer identifies that West must guard:

- Hearts.
- Clubs.

The desired ending is:

```
West
♥ Guard
♣ Guard
```

Declarer works backward:

- Preserve entries.
- Rectify the count.
- Unblock honors (Vienna Coup).
- Cash the squeeze card at the correct time.

---

# Example 2 – Planning an Endplay

Contract

```
4♠
```

Declarer wants West to lead at the end.

Working backward:

- Strip clubs.
- Strip hearts.
- Preserve a diamond loser.
- Throw West in with the final diamond.

The ending determines the earlier play.

---

# Example 3 – Planning a Cross Ruff

Declarer notices:

- Four ruffs are available.

Instead of drawing trumps immediately:

- Preserve trumps.
- Alternate ruffs.
- Draw remaining trumps only after the cross ruff.

---

# Example 4 – Planning a Trump Coup

The desired ending:

Declarer and East have equal numbers of trumps.

Working backward:

- Delay drawing trumps.
- Shorten declarer's trump length.
- Force East to ruff first.

Without early planning, the Trump Coup becomes impossible.

---

# Common Questions

Before Trick One, ask:

- What will the ending look like?
- Which defender is dangerous?
- Which defender guards the threats?
- Which entries must survive?
- Which cards should remain until the end?

---

# Timing

End Position Analysis determines timing.

Examples:

- Should trumps be drawn now?
- Should I duck first?
- Should I unblock now?
- Should I preserve an entry?
- Should I delay cashing a winner?

Correct timing follows naturally from a clear picture of the ending.

---

# Common Situations

End Position Analysis is especially valuable in:

- Slam contracts.
- Notrump contracts.
- Squeeze endings.
- Endplays.
- Strip plays.
- Complex suit contracts.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Thinking only one trick ahead.
- Cashing winners automatically.
- Destroying communication.
- Missing squeeze positions.
- Drawing trumps too early.
- Failing to visualize the ending.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Recognize potential endings.
- Preserve communications.
- Exchange guarding responsibilities.
- Avoid becoming the sole guard of two suits.
- Force declarer to commit entries early.

---

# Advantages

- Improves planning.
- Prevents communication errors.
- Reveals hidden opportunities.
- Supports advanced declarer techniques.
- Leads to more consistent contract success.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires experience and visualization.
- Depends on accurate counting.
- New information may change the plan.
- Complex endings can be difficult to foresee.

# Memory Aid

**End Position Analysis = "See the Ending Before You Play the Beginning."**

Remember:

- **Visualize the last few tricks before playing the first one.**
- **Work backward from the desired ending.**
- **Preserve entries, communication, and timing.**
- **Many advanced techniques depend on reaching the correct end position.**
- **Reevaluate your plan as new information appears during the play.**

End Position Analysis is one of the defining characteristics of expert declarer play. Rather than reacting to each trick as it occurs, expert players imagine the ideal ending at the start of the hand and shape every decision to reach that position. This backward-planning approach is the foundation of successful squeezes, endplays, trump coups, elimination plays, and many other advanced bridge techniques.
