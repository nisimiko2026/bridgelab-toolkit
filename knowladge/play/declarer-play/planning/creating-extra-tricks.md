# Creating Extra Tricks

## Overview

After counting the sure winners (in notrump) or losers (in suit contracts), declarer must determine **how to create the additional tricks needed to fulfill the contract**.

This is one of the first and most important steps in planning the play. Every successful declarer asks:

> **Where will my extra tricks come from?**

The answer determines the entire line of play.

---

# Objectives

Creating extra tricks allows declarer to:

- Reach the required number of tricks.
- Develop winners from otherwise losing cards.
- Choose the best declarer plan.
- Compare alternative lines of play.
- Maximize the chance of making the contract.

---

# Sources of Extra Tricks

Extra tricks can be created in several different ways.

## 1. Establishing Long Suits

In notrump contracts, the most common source of additional tricks is establishing a long suit.

Example:

```
♣ A K J 10 8
opposite
♣ 7 6 5
```

After driving out the defenders' queen, the remaining clubs become winners.

---

## 2. Finesses

A finesse allows declarer to win a trick with a lower honor by playing for a favorable position of a missing honor.

Example:

```
Dummy
♠ A Q

Declarer
♠ 7 5
```

Lead toward the queen, hoping East holds the king.

---

## 3. Ruffing Losers

In suit contracts, losers can often be turned into winners by ruffing them in the short-trump hand.

Example:

Declarer ruffs a losing club in dummy instead of losing the trick.

---

## 4. Crossruff

Instead of drawing trumps immediately, declarer alternates ruffs between the two hands.

Each successful ruff creates an additional trick.

---

## 5. Promotion

Sometimes a small card becomes a winner after higher cards have been forced out.

Examples include:

- Long-suit establishment.
- Trump promotion (defenders).
- Promotion of small honors.

---

## 6. Endplays

Declarer forces a defender to lead a favorable suit.

The extra trick comes from:

- A lead into a tenace.
- A ruff and discard.
- Breaking a guarded honor.

---

## 7. Squeezes

A squeeze forces a defender to abandon a necessary guard.

The resulting discard establishes an extra winner.

---

## 8. Throw-Ins

Declarer deliberately loses a trick to place a defender on lead.

Without a safe exit, the defender is forced to concede a trick.

---

## 9. Defensive Errors

Sometimes extra tricks arise because defenders:

- Miscount.
- Discard incorrectly.
- Block a suit.
- Lead the wrong card.

Although declarer should never rely on errors, good play often creates opportunities for them.

---

# Choosing the Best Source

If several methods are available, compare them by asking:

- Which line has the highest probability?
- Which requires the fewest assumptions?
- Which preserves communication?
- Which keeps the dangerous opponent off lead?
- Which avoids unnecessary risk?

The safest line is often preferable to the most spectacular one.

---

# Combining Techniques

Many successful contracts use more than one method.

Example:

- Ruff one loser.
- Establish a long suit.
- Finish with a squeeze.

Expert declarers combine techniques into a single plan.

---

# Example 1 – Notrump

Contract:

```
3NT
```

Sure winners:

```
8
```

Declarer needs one more trick.

Possible sources:

- Establish clubs.
- Diamond finesse.

Declarer compares both lines before choosing.

---

# Example 2 – Suit Contract

Contract:

```
4♠
```

Losers:

- One club.
- Two diamonds.

Declarer plans to:

- Ruff one diamond.
- Discard the club on an established heart.

Only one loser remains.

---

# Example 3 – Multiple Plans

Declarer can either:

- Take a finesse.
- Execute a strip and endplay.

Although the finesse has a 50% chance, the endplay succeeds whenever the defenders' distribution is favorable.

Declarer chooses the higher-percentage line.

---

# Planning Checklist

Before playing to the first trick, ask:

1. How many tricks do I already have?
2. How many more do I need?
3. Where can those tricks come from?
4. Which method has the highest percentage?
5. Can I combine multiple techniques?
6. What are the defenders' likely countermeasures?

---

# Common Mistakes

- Beginning play without identifying a source of extra tricks.
- Focusing on only one possible line.
- Ignoring communication and entries.
- Taking unnecessary risks.
- Forgetting to compare alternative plans.
- Trying to create tricks before counting winners or losers.

# Key Principles

- Every declarer plan begins by identifying where the extra tricks will come from.
- Different contracts require different methods of creating tricks.
- Compare all available lines before committing to one.
- Communication, timing, and probability often determine the best plan.
- Expert declarers think several tricks ahead and combine multiple techniques into a coherent strategy.
