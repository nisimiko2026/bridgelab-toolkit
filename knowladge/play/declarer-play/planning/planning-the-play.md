# Planning the Play

## Overview

**Planning the Play** is the most important skill in declarer play. Before playing to **Trick One**, declarer should analyze the entire hand, identify the winning strategy, and decide on the order in which the tricks will be played.

Expert declarers spend more time thinking **before the first card is played** than during the rest of the hand. A clear plan reduces mistakes, preserves communication, and maximizes the chances of making the contract.

The golden rule is:

> **Plan before you play.**

---

# Purpose

Planning the Play allows declarer to:

- Determine how the contract will be made.
- Count winners or losers.
- Identify dangerous opponents.
- Choose the best line of play.
- Preserve entries.
- Avoid unnecessary risks.
- Coordinate multiple declarer-play techniques.

---

# The Planning Process

Before playing from dummy, follow these steps:

1. Analyze the opening lead.
2. Count winners or losers.
3. Identify the contract objective.
4. Evaluate entries.
5. Identify danger suits.
6. Choose the best line of play.
7. Consider alternative plans.
8. Execute the plan while remaining flexible.

---

# Step 1 – Analyze the Opening Lead

The opening lead often reveals valuable information.

Ask:

- What does the lead suggest?
- Is it fourth-best?
- Is it top of a sequence?
- Did partner bid the suit?
- What does the auction tell me?

The lead may indicate:

- Honor location.
- Suit length.
- Defensive strategy.

---

# Step 2 – Count Winners or Losers

## Notrump Contracts

Count **immediate winners**.

Example:

```
Need 9 tricks.
Have 7 winners.
Need 2 more.
```

Determine how to develop them.

---

## Suit Contracts

Count **potential losers**.

Example:

```
Can afford 3 losers.
Currently have 5.
Need to eliminate 2.
```

---

# Step 3 – Identify the Objective

Determine exactly how the contract will be made.

Possible objectives include:

- Establish a long suit.
- Ruff losers.
- Take a finesse.
- Execute a squeeze.
- Perform an endplay.
- Use a safety play.

---

# Step 4 – Count Entries

Entries are often more important than honors.

Ask:

- How many entries exist?
- Which entries must be preserved?
- Can I create additional entries?
- Will a suit become blocked?

Many contracts fail because of poor entry management.

---

# Step 5 – Identify Dangerous Opponents

Ask:

- Which defender has the dangerous long suit?
- Which defender should not gain the lead?
- Should I use Avoidance Play?
- Is a Hold-Up Play required?

---

# Step 6 – Evaluate Alternative Lines

Compare every reasonable line.

Examples:

- Finesse or play for the drop?
- Safety Play or maximum tricks?
- Ruff first or draw trumps first?
- Develop clubs or diamonds?

Choose the line with the highest chance of making the contract.

---

# Step 7 – Consider Timing

Many techniques depend on proper timing.

Examples:

- Draw trumps before or after ruffing?
- Duck before establishing winners?
- Cash winners now or later?
- Delay the finesse?

Timing errors often defeat otherwise sound contracts.

---

# Step 8 – Stay Flexible

Planning is essential, but bridge is a game of incomplete information.

As the play develops:

- Recount winners.
- Recount losers.
- Adjust to defenders' signals.
- Revise the plan if new information appears.

---

# Planning in Notrump Contracts

Typical priorities:

1. Count winners.
2. Establish additional winners.
3. Preserve entries.
4. Cut defenders' communication.
5. Keep the dangerous opponent off lead.

---

# Planning in Suit Contracts

Typical priorities:

1. Count losers.
2. Decide when to draw trumps.
3. Plan ruffs.
4. Preserve entries.
5. Eliminate losers.

---

# Example 1 – Notrump

Contract

```
3NT
```

Immediate winners:

```
7
```

Need:

```
2 more
```

Plan:

- Establish clubs.
- Preserve the Ace as an entry.
- Hold up in spades.
- Cash winners.

---

# Example 2 – Suit Contract

Contract

```
4♠
```

Losers:

```
4
```

Need to eliminate:

```
1
```

Plan:

- Ruff a club in dummy.
- Draw trumps.
- Establish diamonds.
- Discard remaining club.

---

# Example 3 – Slam

Contract

```
6♥
```

Plan:

- Count losers.
- Check for bad trump breaks.
- Preserve entries.
- Use a Safety Play.
- Avoid unnecessary finesses.

---

# Common Questions Before Trick One

Ask yourself:

- How many tricks do I already have?
- How many more do I need?
- Where are my losers?
- Which suit should I attack?
- Do I need a finesse?
- Can I ruff losers?
- When should I draw trumps?
- Who is the dangerous opponent?
- Are my entries sufficient?

---

# Common Mistakes

- Playing too quickly.
- Failing to count winners or losers.
- Ignoring entries.
- Missing a safer line.
- Drawing trumps too early.
- Forgetting to reconsider the plan after new information.

---

# Advantages

- Prevents careless mistakes.
- Improves timing.
- Maximizes contract success.
- Coordinates all declarer techniques.
- Develops expert-level thinking.

# Memory Aid

**Planning the Play = "Think Before Trick One."**

Remember:

- **Do not play automatically.**
- **Count before you play.**
- **Protect your entries.**
- **Choose the line with the highest chance of success.**
- **Adjust your plan as new information appears.**

Planning the Play is the foundation of expert declarer play. Every advanced technique—finesses, safety plays, squeezes, endplays, cross-ruffs, dummy reversals, and avoidance plays—depends on a sound plan made before the first trick. The habit of pausing to analyze the hand before playing from dummy is one of the defining characteristics of successful bridge players.
