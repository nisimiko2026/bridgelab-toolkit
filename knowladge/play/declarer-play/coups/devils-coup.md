# Devil's Coup Technique

## Overview

The **Devil's Coup** is a rare and advanced declarer-play technique in which declarer **deliberately sacrifices one or more seemingly valuable tricks to create an unavoidable winning end position**. The play typically combines elements of **endplay, elimination, squeeze, throw-in, and deceptive timing** to force the defenders into an impossible situation.

Unlike many named coups, the Devil's Coup is not a single fixed card combination. Instead, it describes a family of ingenious positions in which declarer appears to give the defense an advantage, only to discover that the sacrifice was necessary to achieve the contract.

The guiding principle is:

> **Give up something now to gain something greater later.**

---

# Purpose

The Devil's Coup allows declarer to:

- Create a winning end position.
- Force defenders into an impossible choice.
- Eliminate dangerous communications.
- Prepare an endplay or squeeze.
- Turn an apparent loser into a winning contract.

---

# Basic Principle

The key idea is:

> **Sacrifice a trick intentionally if it improves the final position.**

The sacrificed trick may:

- Rectify the count for a squeeze.
- Remove a dangerous entry.
- Force a defender to unblock.
- Improve the trump position.
- Create an endplay.

The immediate loss is outweighed by the later gain.

---

# When to Use

A Devil's Coup may arise when:

- A squeeze is available after rectifying the count.
- An endplay requires a defender to win a trick.
- Communication between defenders must be broken.
- A temporary sacrifice improves the ending.
- The contract depends on precise timing.

---

# Requirements

A successful Devil's Coup usually requires:

- Excellent visualization.
- Accurate counting.
- Proper timing.
- Careful entry management.
- Recognition of the desired end position.

---

# Example 1 – Sacrifice Before a Squeeze

## Contract

```
6NT
```

Declarer has:

- Eleven sure tricks.
- One unavoidable loser.

Instead of cashing winners immediately, declarer deliberately loses the unavoidable trick.

The count is now rectified.

Later the squeeze succeeds.

---

# Example 2 – Sacrifice to Remove an Entry

West has:

- Established clubs.

East has:

- The only outside entry.

Declarer intentionally gives East a trick early.

East's entry disappears.

West's club winners later become unreachable.

---

# Example 3 – Sacrifice Before an Endplay

Contract

```
4♠
```

Declarer strips the side suits.

Instead of taking an immediate winner, declarer concedes a diamond.

Later the same defender is thrown in and must lead into declarer's tenace.

The temporary loss produces an extra trick.

---

# Planning the Play

Before attempting a Devil's Coup, ask:

- What final position am I trying to reach?
- Can sacrificing a trick improve that ending?
- Will the sacrifice remove an entry or rectify the count?
- Are my entries preserved?
- Is there a simpler winning line?

---

# Timing

Timing is critical.

Typical sequence:

1. Count winners and losers.
2. Visualize the ending.
3. Make the intentional sacrifice.
4. Preserve communication.
5. Execute the squeeze, endplay, or coup.

---

# Why It Works

The defense often welcomes the extra trick.

However, by taking it:

- They lose communication.
- They rectify the count for declarer.
- They are forced into an endplay.
- They become squeezed later.

The apparent gain becomes a liability.

---

# Common Situations

A Devil's Coup may appear:

- Before a Simple Squeeze.
- Before a Strip Squeeze.
- During an Endplay.
- In advanced slam play.
- In difficult Notrump contracts.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Sacrificing a trick without a clear objective.
- Failing to preserve entries.
- Miscounting winners or losers.
- Executing the sacrifice too early or too late.
- Missing a simpler percentage play.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Ask why declarer is conceding a trick.
- Preserve communication.
- Avoid automatic returns.
- Count declarer's remaining winners.
- Watch for developing squeeze or endplay positions.

---

# Advantages

- Creates winning positions that would not otherwise exist.
- Supports advanced endgame techniques.
- Often avoids difficult guesses.
- Demonstrates expert planning.
- Can transform a losing position into a winning one.

---

# Disadvantages

- Extremely rare.
- Difficult to recognize.
- Requires perfect timing.
- Small errors usually destroy the position.

# Memory Aid

**Devil's Coup = "Lose a Trick to Win the Contract."**

Remember:

- **The sacrifice is intentional and purposeful.**
- **Always know what end position you are trying to create.**
- **Preserve entries and communication before making the sacrifice.**
- **The Devil's Coup often supports squeezes, endplays, and elimination plays.**
- **Never sacrifice a trick unless it clearly increases your chances of making the contract.**

The **Devil's Coup** is one of bridge's most imaginative declarer techniques. Rather than focusing on immediate trick-taking, declarer deliberately gives up a trick to reshape the end position in their favor. Although rare in practice, understanding the Devil's Coup deepens appreciation of advanced bridge strategy and reinforces the importance of planning the entire hand before playing to the first trick.
