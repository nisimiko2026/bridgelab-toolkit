# Grand Coup

## Overview

The **Grand Coup** is a rare declarer technique in which declarer **voluntarily ruffs with an unnecessarily high trump**, often sacrificing what appears to be a natural trump winner. The purpose is to **shorten declarer's trump holding** so that an opponent cannot obtain a trump trick later.

At first glance, the play appears wasteful because declarer intentionally uses a high trump to win a trick that could have been won more cheaply. However, by reducing declarer's trump length, the Grand Coup changes the ending and prevents a defender from scoring a promoted trump trick.

The Grand Coup is one of the most elegant and surprising coups in bridge.

---

# Objectives

The Grand Coup aims to:

- Prevent a defender from making a trump trick.
- Neutralize a potential trump promotion.
- Equalize or reduce trump length.
- Preserve control in the endgame.
- Gain an otherwise impossible trick.

---

# Basic Principle

Normally, declarer ruffs with the **lowest** trump that safely wins the trick.

In a Grand Coup, declarer intentionally ruffs **high**, even though a lower trump would suffice.

The purpose is not to win the current trick, but to improve the eventual trump ending.

---

# When Does It Occur?

A Grand Coup is most likely when:

- The defenders threaten a trump promotion.
- Declarer and a defender have similar trump length.
- A high ruff shortens declarer's trump holding.
- The remaining trump position favors declarer after the shortening.

These conditions usually arise in endgame positions.

---

# Why It Works

Suppose declarer has one more trump than a defender.

If declarer retains the extra trump, the defender may later promote a small trump into a winner.

By voluntarily using one of the high trumps earlier, declarer equalizes the trump length.

The defender's promotion disappears.

---

# Conceptual Example

Remaining trumps:

```
West
♠ 8

Dummy
♠ A

East
♠ —

Declarer
♠ K 10
```

Declarer can ruff a side-suit winner with either the ♠10 or the ♠K.

A normal play would use the ♠10.

A Grand Coup uses the ♠K instead.

Although the king is "wasted," declarer's trump length is reduced, preventing West's ♠8 from becoming a winning trump later.

---

# Relationship to Trump Promotion

The Grand Coup is often the declarer's answer to a possible **trump promotion**.

Instead of allowing the defenders to force declarer into an unfavorable trump ending, declarer voluntarily shortens the trump holding first.

In many classic examples:

- The defense is threatening an **Uppercut**.
- The Grand Coup removes the opportunity.

---

# Planning the Coup

Before playing a high trump unnecessarily, declarer should ask:

- Is a defender threatening a trump promotion?
- Will shortening my trump holding improve the ending?
- Will I still retain trump control?
- Can I afford to sacrifice this high trump?
- What will the remaining trump position look like?

The Grand Coup should always be planned several tricks in advance.

---

# Typical Situations

Grand Coups commonly arise when:

- Both sides have only a few trumps remaining.
- Declarer must ruff before drawing all the trumps.
- The defenders are preparing repeated forcing plays.
- A simple trump ending would otherwise favor the defense.

---

# Grand Coup vs. Uppercut

| Grand Coup | Uppercut |
|-------------|----------|
| Declarer technique | Defensive technique |
| Declarer voluntarily shortens trumps | Defenders promote a trump |
| Prevents trump promotion | Creates trump promotion |
| Offensive play | Defensive play |

The two techniques are closely related and often occur in the same types of endings.

---

# Grand Coup vs. Trump Coup

| Grand Coup | Trump Coup |
|-------------|------------|
| Prevents a defensive trump promotion | Wins by shortening trumps and controlling the ending |
| Often involves a deliberately high ruff | Usually depends on precise trump length and timing |
| Neutralizes the defenders' trump threat | Eliminates the defenders' remaining trump control |

Although both involve sophisticated trump endings, they are distinct techniques.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Automatically ruffing with the lowest trump.
- Failing to recognize a coming trump promotion.
- Using a high trump without understanding the resulting ending.
- Losing trump control after shortening the trump holding.
- Planning only the current trick instead of the entire ending.

# Key Principles

- The Grand Coup deliberately uses a high trump that appears unnecessary.
- The objective is to improve the final trump ending, not the current trick.
- It is frequently used to prevent a defensive trump promotion.
- Accurate counting of the remaining trumps is essential.
- The Grand Coup is one of bridge's classic endgame coups, rewarding precise planning and visualization.
