# Trump Coup Technique

## Overview

The **Trump Coup** is one of the most spectacular and advanced declarer-play techniques in bridge. Declarer deliberately **shortens their own trump holding** until it contains the same number of trumps as a defender's holding. At the critical moment, the defender is forced to ruff first, allowing declarer to score the remaining trump winner.

Unlike a **Trump Promotion**, which is a **defensive** technique, the Trump Coup is an **offensive** declarer play. It is often described as **an endplay conducted entirely in the trump suit**.

The Trump Coup usually appears in end positions and requires accurate counting, precise timing, and careful planning.

---

# Purpose

The Trump Coup allows declarer to:

- Neutralize a higher defensive trump.
- Create an extra trump trick.
- Avoid an otherwise unavoidable trump loser.
- Make contracts that appear impossible.
- Exploit superior timing in the trump suit.

---

# Basic Principle

The guiding principle is:

> **Shorten your trump holding until it matches the defender's length, then force the defender to ruff first.**

Once both hands have the same number of trumps, the defender cannot benefit from holding a higher trump because they are compelled to use it prematurely.

---

# Requirements

A successful Trump Coup usually requires:

- A suit contract.
- Accurate counting of the remaining trumps.
- The ability to shorten declarer's trumps through ruffing.
- A forced trump play at the end.
- Proper entries and timing.

---

# Relationship to Other Techniques

The Trump Coup is closely related to:

- Coup en Passant
- Endplay (Throw-In)
- Elimination Play
- Entry Management
- Rectifying the Count

Unlike **Coup en Passant**, the Trump Coup depends on **equalizing the trump length**, not merely allowing a defender to ruff first.

---

# Classic Example

## Contract

```
4♠
```

Remaining trumps:

Dummy

```
♠ A
```

Declarer

```
♠ J
```

West

```
♠ KQ
```

Declarer first ruffs a side suit, reducing the trump holding.

At the ending, West is forced to ruff before declarer.

The remaining Ace wins the final trump trick.

West's superior trump holding has been neutralized.

---

# Example 2 – Equal Trump Length

Remaining trumps:

Declarer

```
♥ AQ
```

West

```
♥ KJ
```

Declarer repeatedly ruffs side-suit winners until both declarer and West hold two trumps.

A final side-suit lead forces West to ruff first.

Declarer's remaining trump wins.

---

# Example 3 – End Position

Remaining cards:

Dummy

```
♣ A
♠ A
```

Declarer

```
♠ J
♦ 2
```

West

```
♠ KQ
```

Declarer leads the last diamond.

West must ruff.

Dummy's ♠A then wins the last trump trick.

---

# Planning the Play

Before Trick One, ask:

- Can I shorten my trumps?
- How many trumps does the key defender hold?
- Can I force the defender to ruff first?
- Are my entries sufficient?
- Will equal trump length be achieved?

---

# Timing

Timing is critical.

Typical sequence:

1. Count remaining trumps.
2. Preserve entries.
3. Ruff side-suit losers to shorten declarer's trumps.
4. Equalize trump length.
5. Force the defender to ruff.
6. Win the final trump trick.

---

# Why It Works

Normally:

The defender's higher trump wins.

After shortening:

The defender is forced to spend the higher trump before declarer.

Declarer's remaining trump then becomes the last winner.

---

# Common Situations

Trump Coups frequently occur:

- In slam contracts.
- In end positions.
- During elimination plays.
- After rectifying the count.
- Together with Throw-Ins.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Failing to shorten declarer's trumps.
- Miscounting the remaining trumps.
- Losing an essential entry.
- Forcing the defender too early.
- Confusing the Trump Coup with Coup en Passant.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Count declarer's remaining trumps.
- Avoid unnecessary ruffs.
- Preserve trump flexibility.
- Recognize shortening plays.
- Avoid becoming forced to ruff prematurely.

---

# Advantages

- Neutralizes higher defensive trumps.
- Produces an extra trump trick.
- Extremely effective in end positions.
- Often avoids an otherwise unavoidable loser.
- One of declarer's strongest endgame weapons.

---

# Disadvantages

- Rarely available.
- Requires perfect timing.
- Accurate counting is essential.
- Easy to overlook.

- Are all necessary entries preserved?

---

# Memory Aid

**Trump Coup = "Shorten First, Force Later."**

Remember:

- **The Trump Coup is a declarer technique, not a defensive one.**
- **The key is to equalize trump length with the defender.**
- **Shorten your own trumps by ruffing side suits.**
- **Force the defender to ruff before you do.**
- **Do not confuse the Trump Coup with Coup en Passant—they achieve similar goals by different methods.**

The **Trump Coup** is one of the masterpieces of declarer play. By carefully shortening the trump holding and forcing a defender to expend a higher trump at the wrong moment, declarer turns an apparent trump loser into a winning trick. Mastering the Trump Coup requires excellent visualization, accurate counting, and flawless timing, making it one of the hallmarks of expert bridge technique.
