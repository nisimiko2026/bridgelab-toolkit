# Crocodile Coup

## Overview

The **Crocodile Coup** is a defensive coup in which one defender deliberately **plays an unnecessarily high card to prevent partner from winning the trick**. By overtaking or covering partner's winning card, the defender preserves the partnership's communication or avoids placing partner on lead at an unfavorable moment.

Although the play may appear wasteful, it is made to improve the defense as a whole. The name comes from the image of a crocodile swallowing its young—one defender "swallows" partner's winner for the good of the partnership.

The Crocodile Coup is an unusual but elegant example of defensive cooperation.

---

# Objectives

The Crocodile Coup aims to:

- Preserve defensive communication.
- Keep the correct defender on lead.
- Prevent partner from making a harmful return.
- Avoid blocking a defensive suit.
- Maintain control of the defense.

---

# Basic Principle

Instead of allowing partner to win a trick, a defender wins it with a higher card.

The purpose is **not** to win the trick itself—the trick was already secure—but to ensure that the correct defender retains the lead.

---

# When Does It Occur?

A Crocodile Coup is most likely when:

- Either defender could win the current trick.
- Only one defender knows the correct continuation.
- Partner's winning the trick would destroy communication.
- The defender on lead must continue a particular suit.
- A dangerous return must be avoided.

---

# Typical Example

Suppose West leads clubs.

Later:

```
Dummy
♣ Q

West
♣ K

East
♣ A
```

West's king is already winning.

East nevertheless overtakes with the ace.

East now remains on lead and can continue hearts through declarer's vulnerable holding.

If West had remained on lead, the defense might have failed.

---

# Communication Is the Key

The Crocodile Coup is almost always about **communication**.

The defender overtakes partner's winner in order to:

- Continue attacking the correct suit.
- Reach partner's established winners later.
- Prevent declarer from gaining control.
- Preserve the defensive position.

---

# Recognizing the Opportunity

Ask:

- Who should remain on lead?
- Does partner know the correct return?
- Will overtaking improve the defense?
- Is communication more important than conserving an honor?

If the answer is yes, a Crocodile Coup may be correct.

---

# Example 1 – Preserving the Attack

Contract:

```
3NT
```

West has established spades.

East gains the lead but realizes only East can continue hearts effectively.

East overtakes partner's winning card to retain the lead and continue the attack.

---

# Example 2 – Avoiding a Dangerous Return

Partner's king is winning the trick.

If partner wins, they are likely to return a suit that helps declarer.

By overtaking with the ace, declarer is prevented from gaining that advantage.

---

# Example 3 – Maintaining Communication

East overtakes partner's winner because:

- East has the only remaining entry.
- East can cash additional winners immediately.
- Returning the lead to West would block the defense.

---

# Crocodile Coup vs. Uppercut

| Crocodile Coup | Uppercut |
|----------------|----------|
| Defender overtakes partner's winning card | Defender forces declarer to overruff |
| Preserves communication | Promotes partner's trump |
| Usually occurs in any contract | Occurs in suit contracts |
| Concerned with who wins the trick | Concerned with trump promotion |

Although both are defensive coups, they achieve entirely different objectives.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Conserving a high card when overtaking is necessary.
- Allowing the wrong defender to gain the lead.
- Ignoring communication between the defenders.
- Overtaking without a clear purpose.
- Blocking the defensive attack.

# Key Principles

- The Crocodile Coup is a defensive overtake of partner's winning card.
- The objective is to preserve communication or keep the correct defender on lead.
- Winning the trick is not enough—the right defender must win it.
- Communication between defenders is often more valuable than conserving an honor.
- The Crocodile Coup is a rare but elegant example of partnership cooperation in defense.
