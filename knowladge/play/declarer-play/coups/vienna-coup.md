# Vienna Coup Technique

## Overview

The **Vienna Coup** is an advanced declarer-play technique used primarily in **squeeze endings**. Declarer deliberately **cashes or unblocks a high card early in the play**, even though it appears unnecessary at the time, to prevent communication problems later when executing a squeeze.

The Vienna Coup is not a squeeze by itself—it is a **preparatory maneuver** that makes a squeeze possible by preserving entries and avoiding blocked suits.

Named after the famous Vienna school of bridge, the Vienna Coup is one of the classic expert techniques in declarer play.

---

# Purpose

The Vienna Coup allows declarer to:

- Prepare for a squeeze.
- Unblock high cards.
- Preserve communication between declarer and dummy.
- Prevent suits from becoming blocked.
- Maximize the effectiveness of squeeze endings.

---

# Basic Principle

The guiding principle is:

> **Unblock today so you can squeeze tomorrow.**

Declarer intentionally cashes or unblocks a high honor **before** it is immediately needed, ensuring that communication remains intact after the squeeze.

---

# When to Use

A Vienna Coup is appropriate when:

- Planning a squeeze.
- A suit may become blocked.
- Communication between the hands is limited.
- A high card will later interfere with entries.
- The squeeze requires access to both hands.

---

# Requirements

A successful Vienna Coup usually requires:

- A potential squeeze position.
- A blocked or potentially blocked suit.
- Proper entries.
- Accurate timing.
- Careful planning before the squeeze begins.

---

# Relationship to Other Techniques

The Vienna Coup is commonly used with:

- Simple Squeeze.
- Double Squeeze.
- Strip Squeeze.
- Entry Management.
- Ducking (to rectify the count).

It is a **preparation**, not the squeeze itself.

---

# Example 1 – Basic Vienna Coup

Dummy

```
♥ AQ
```

Declarer

```
♥ K
```

Later in the hand, declarer will need dummy's ♥Q as a squeeze threat.

Before the squeeze develops, declarer cashes the ♥K.

Now the ♥Q can be reached after the squeeze.

Without unblocking the King, communication would be lost.

---

# Example 2 – Unblocking a Long Suit

Dummy

```
♣ AQJ
```

Declarer

```
♣ K10
```

Declarer cashes the ♣K early.

Later, after the squeeze, dummy's ♣AQJ remain as accessible winners.

---

# Example 3 – Notrump Squeeze

Contract

```
6NT
```

Declarer identifies a possible squeeze against West.

Before rectifying the count, declarer unblocks a high diamond.

The unblock allows access to dummy after the squeeze card is played.

---

# Planning the Play

Before Trick One, ask:

- Am I planning a squeeze?
- Will a suit become blocked?
- Which entries are critical?
- Should I unblock an honor now?
- Will communication survive the ending?

---

# Timing

Timing is everything.

Typical order:

1. Identify the squeeze.
2. Execute the Vienna Coup.
3. Rectify the count.
4. Preserve entries.
5. Play the squeeze card.

If the unblock is delayed too long, the squeeze may fail.

---

# Common Situations

The Vienna Coup frequently occurs:

- In Notrump contracts.
- During squeeze endings.
- In slam contracts.
- Before Double Squeezes.
- Before Strip Squeezes.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Forgetting to unblock early.
- Preserving an honor that later blocks communication.
- Misidentifying the squeeze position.
- Losing a critical entry.
- Cashing winners in the wrong order.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Recognize possible squeeze positions.
- Force declarer to use entries early.
- Preserve communication.
- Exchange guarding responsibilities.
- Count declarer's remaining entries.

---

# Advantages

- Makes squeezes possible.
- Prevents blocked suits.
- Preserves communication.
- Enables advanced endgame techniques.
- Essential in many expert squeeze positions.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires advanced planning.
- Easy to overlook.
- Depends on accurate timing.
- Usually useful only in complex endings.

# Declarer's Checklist

Before executing a Vienna Coup:

- Am I preparing for a squeeze?
- Which honor may block communication?
- Should I unblock now?
- Have I preserved enough entries?
- Is the count properly rectified?

---

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Recognize the squeeze.
2. Identify blocked communication.
3. Unblock the high card.
4. Preserve entries.
5. Execute the squeeze.

# Comparison

# Memory Aid

**Vienna Coup = "Unblock Before You Squeeze."**

Remember:

- **The Vienna Coup prepares a squeeze—it is not the squeeze itself.**
- **Unblock honors before they become obstacles.**
- **Preserve communication between declarer and dummy.**
- **Plan the entire ending before Trick One.**
- **Many squeeze positions fail because the Vienna Coup was overlooked.**

The Vienna Coup is one of the classic techniques of expert declarer play. By unblocking a high card at the correct moment, declarer preserves vital communication and enables squeezes that would otherwise be impossible. Although often invisible to spectators, the Vienna Coup is frequently the key move that transforms a potential squeeze into a successful one.
