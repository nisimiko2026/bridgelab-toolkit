# Counting Distribution

## Overview

**Counting Distribution** is one of the most fundamental skills in bridge. It is the process of determining **how many cards each player originally held in every suit** and continuously updating that picture as the play progresses.

Expert players rarely rely on guesswork. Instead, they reconstruct the complete layout of the unseen hands by combining information from:

- The auction
- Dummy
- The opening lead
- Cards played
- Discards
- Signals
- Ruffs

Distribution counting is equally important for **declarer play** and **defense**, forming the basis for techniques such as squeezes, endplays, safety plays, and accurate defense.

The guiding principle is:

> **Count the shape, and the cards will tell their own story.**

---

# Purpose

Counting Distribution allows players to:

- Reconstruct unseen hands.
- Locate shortages and long suits.
- Predict remaining cards.
- Improve finesse decisions.
- Plan squeezes and endplays.
- Defend more accurately.
- Eliminate unnecessary guessing.

---

# Basic Principle

Every player begins with:

```
13 cards
```

As each player follows suit, discards, or ruffs, update the estimated distribution.

Eventually, many or all of the unseen hands become known.

---

# The Counting Process

The recommended order is:

1. Analyze the auction.
2. Observe dummy.
3. Count each suit separately.
4. Count trumps continuously.
5. Update after every trick.
6. Reconstruct each player's original shape.

---

# Step 1 – Use the Auction

The bidding often provides strong clues.

Examples:

```
1♠
```

Usually promises:

- Five or more spades.

```
1NT
```

Suggests a balanced hand.

```
Weak Two
```

Shows a long suit and shortness elsewhere.

Use this information as the starting point.

---

# Step 2 – Study Dummy

When dummy appears, count:

- Suit lengths.
- Shortages.
- Long suits.
- Entries.
- Potential ruffs.

Dummy immediately eliminates many possibilities.

---

# Step 3 – Count One Suit at a Time

Suppose dummy has:

```
5 hearts
```

Declarer shows:

```
3 hearts
```

You hold:

```
2 hearts
```

Partner therefore started with:

```
13 − (5 + 3 + 2) = 3 hearts
```

Repeat this process for every suit.

---

# Step 4 – Track Every Card

Each time someone:

- Follows suit,
- Discards,
- Ruffs,

update the count immediately.

Do not wait until later.

---

# Example 1 – Simple Count

Auction

```
1♠ – 2♠ – 4♠
```

Dummy shows:

```
3 spades
```

Declarer later ruffs two clubs.

You now know declarer began with:

- Five spades.
- A singleton club.

The remaining distribution becomes much easier to determine.

---

# Example 2 – Counting Trumps

Contract

```
4♥
```

Dummy

```
4 hearts
```

Declarer

```
5 hearts
```

You hold:

```
2 hearts
```

Partner therefore has:

```
2 hearts
```

Track each trump played until you know exactly how many remain.

---

# Example 3 – Reconstructing Shape

By Trick 8 you know declarer has shown:

- Five spades.
- Three hearts.
- Two diamonds.

Therefore declarer must have started with:

```
5–3–2–3
```

The club length is now certain.

---

# Counting During Defense

Defenders should continually ask:

- How many cards remain in each suit?
- Which defender is now void?
- Is declarer preparing a Cross Ruff?
- Is dummy becoming entryless?
- Is a squeeze developing?

---

# Counting During Declarer Play

Declarer should ask:

- Which defender has the long suit?
- Which defender is dangerous?
- Who guards the missing honors?
- How many trumps remain?
- Are the defenders becoming squeezed?

---

# Distribution and the Rule of 13

Every player's hand always totals:

```
13 cards
```

Once three suit lengths are known, the fourth is determined automatically.

Example:

```
♠ 5
♥ 3
♦ 2

Remaining clubs = 3
```

---

# Combining Distribution with HCP

Distribution counting becomes even stronger when combined with:

- High Card Point counting.
- Auction analysis.
- Opening lead interpretation.
- Discovery Play.

Together they greatly reduce uncertainty.

---

# Planning the Play

Before Trick One, ask:

- What shape did the auction suggest?
- Which suit should I count first?
- Who is likely to be short?
- Which defender is dangerous?
- How will the distribution affect my plan?

---

# Common Mistakes

- Counting only trumps.
- Forgetting earlier discards.
- Ignoring the auction.
- Updating the count too late.
- Assuming rather than counting.

---

# Advantages

- Reduces guessing.
- Locates shortages.
- Predicts remaining cards.
- Supports squeezes and endplays.
- Essential for expert bridge.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires concentration.
- Easy to lose count.
- False carding may mislead.
- New information may require revising earlier conclusions.

# Memory Aid

**Counting Distribution = "Know the Shape, Know the Hand."**

Remember:

- **Every hand begins with 13 cards.**
- **Count one suit at a time and update after every trick.**
- **Use the auction, dummy, and every played card to refine your picture.**
- **When three suit lengths are known, the fourth is automatic.**
- **Expert players count continuously—they do not guess.**

Counting Distribution is one of the cornerstones of expert bridge. By carefully tracking each player's suit lengths throughout the auction and play, declarers and defenders can reconstruct unseen hands, locate shortages, anticipate endgame positions, and make high-percentage decisions with confidence. Combined with High Card Point counting, it forms the foundation of advanced bridge analysis.
