# Percentage Plays Technique

## Overview

A **Percentage Play** is a declarer-play technique in which declarer chooses the line of play that offers the **highest mathematical probability of success**. Rather than relying on instinct or hope, expert players compare all reasonable alternatives and select the play with the greatest expected chance of making the contract.

Percentage play is one of the fundamental skills of declarer play and applies to both suit and notrump contracts.

---

# Purpose

Percentage Plays allow declarer to:

- Maximize the probability of making the contract.
- Choose between competing lines of play.
- Reduce unnecessary risks.
- Make mathematically sound decisions.
- Improve long-term results over many deals.

---

# Basic Principle

Whenever more than one line of play is available, declarer should ask:

> **"Which line gives me the greatest chance of success?"**

The answer depends on:

- Card probabilities.
- Information from the auction.
- Information gained during the play.
- Suit distributions.
- Opponents' signals.

The mathematically best line is not always the successful one on a particular deal, but it wins most often over time.

---

# Sources of Information

Before selecting a percentage play, consider:

- The bidding.
- Opening lead.
- Dummy's cards.
- Opponents' signals.
- Carding agreements.
- Missing card combinations.
- Known distributions.

Bridge is a game of **conditional probabilities**—new information can change the odds.

---

# Common Percentage Decisions

Typical decisions include:

- Finesse versus playing for the drop.
- Which finesse to take first.
- Safety play versus maximum tricks.
- Playing for a 3-2 suit split or a 4-1 split.
- Choosing between two suits to establish.

---

# Example 1 – Finesse vs. Playing for the Drop

Holding:

```
Dummy
♠ AQJ

Declarer
♠ 432
```

Missing:

```
♠ K
```

Options:

1. Take a finesse.
2. Play for the king to fall.

Probability:

- **Finesse:** about **50%**.
- **Singleton king ("drop"):** about **26%**.

**Correct percentage play:** Take the finesse.

---

# Example 2 – Missing Queen

Holding:

```
Dummy
♥ AKJ

Declarer
♥ 432
```

Missing:

```
♥ Q
```

Normal percentage play:

Cash the Ace and King, then finesse only if additional information suggests the queen has not dropped.

---

# Example 3 – Suit Break

You need five tricks from:

```
♣ AKQJ10
♣ 543
```

Missing:

```
♣ 98762
```

Percentage expectations:

| Split | Approximate Probability |
|--------|-------------------------|
| 3–2 | 68% |
| 4–1 | 28% |
| 5–0 | 4% |

Normally, play for the **3–2 split**, unless the bidding or play indicates otherwise.

---

# Example 4 – Two Possible Finesses

Contract:

```
3NT
```

You can:

- Finesse diamonds.
- Finesse clubs.

If one finesse is likely to provide enough tricks, avoid taking both unnecessarily. Each additional finesse introduces another chance of failure.

---

# Safety Play vs. Percentage Play

Sometimes the mathematically best chance to make the contract differs from the best chance to make the maximum number of tricks.

Example:

```
Need 9 tricks → Safety Play.
Need 12 tricks → Percentage for extra tricks.
```

Matchpoint and IMP scoring may influence the correct decision.

---

# Matchpoints vs. IMPs

## Matchpoints

Often favor:

- Taking calculated risks.
- Maximizing overtricks.
- Thin percentage plays.

---

## IMPs

Usually favor:

- Making the contract.
- Safety plays.
- Conservative percentage decisions.

---

# Using Information

Theoretical percentages change when additional information becomes available.

For example:

If West opened the bidding, missing honors are more likely to be held by West.

Similarly, defenders' signals and discards may reveal suit distribution, making one line superior to another.

---

# Planning the Play

Before Trick One:

1. Count winners.
2. Count losers.
3. Identify all possible lines.
4. Estimate each line's probability.
5. Choose the highest-percentage line.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Ignoring the bidding.
- Taking unnecessary finesses.
- Playing for unlikely distributions.
- Choosing spectacular rather than practical lines.
- Forgetting that percentages change during the play.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Conceal distribution.
- Falsecard when appropriate.
- Avoid revealing honor locations.
- Force declarer into low-percentage guesses.
- Preserve ambiguity.

---

# Advantages

- Maximizes long-term success.
- Produces consistent results.
- Reduces avoidable errors.
- Integrates naturally with planning the play.
- Essential for expert declarer play.

---

# Disadvantages

- The correct percentage play may fail on a particular deal.
- Requires knowledge of card probabilities.
- Additional information may alter the theoretical percentages.

---

# Related Techniques

| Technique | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|
| Finesse | Win against a missing honor |
| Safety Play | Maximize the chance of making the contract |
| Restricted Choice | Refine probabilities after an honor appears |
| Elimination Play | Remove safe exits before an endplay |
| Endplay | Force a favorable lead |
| Squeeze | Create extra tricks through pressure |

---

# Declarer's Checklist

Before choosing a line:

- Have I counted winners and losers?
- What are the likely suit breaks?
- What does the bidding suggest?
- Have the defenders revealed useful information?
- Which line has the highest probability?

---

# Common Probability Guidelines

| Situation | Approximate Percentage |
|-----------|-----------------------:|
| Finesse against one missing honor | 50% |
| Missing five cards split 3–2 | 68% |
| Missing five cards split 4–1 | 28% |
| Missing five cards split 5–0 | 4% |
| Missing six cards split 3–3 | 36% |
| Missing six cards split 4–2 | 48% |
| Missing six cards split 5–1 | 15% |
| Missing six cards split 6–0 | 1% |

---

# Comparison

| Technique | Main Idea |
|-----------|-----------|
| Percentage Play | Choose the mathematically best line |
| Safety Play | Maximize the chance of making the contract |
| Finesse | Play for a favorable honor position |
| Restricted Choice | Adjust odds after an honor appears |

---

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Count winners.
2. Count losers.
3. Identify all reasonable lines.
4. Estimate the odds.
5. Choose the highest-percentage play.

---

# Memory Aid

**Percentage Play = "Play the Odds, Not the Hope."**

Remember:

- **Use mathematics as your guide.**
- **Consider the bidding and defenders' signals.**
- **Choose the line that succeeds most often over time.**
- **Update your probabilities as new information appears.**
- **At IMPs, making the contract is usually more important than extra tricks.**

Percentage Plays are at the heart of expert declarer play. While the best mathematical line may occasionally fail on a single deal, consistently choosing the highest-probability option produces the best long-term results and is a hallmark of successful bridge players.
