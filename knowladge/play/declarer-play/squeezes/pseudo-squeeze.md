# Pseudo Squeeze

## Overview

A **Pseudo Squeeze** (also called an **Apparent Squeeze**) is a position that resembles a squeeze but **does not satisfy all the requirements of a true squeeze**. Although a defender may appear to be under pressure, declarer does not gain the extra trick because of a genuine squeeze mechanism.

Instead, the winning trick results from another factor, such as:

- An endplay.
- A blocked suit.
- A defensive communication problem.
- An entry problem.
- A defensive error.
- An incorrect discard.

Recognizing pseudo squeezes helps declarers distinguish between genuine squeeze positions and endings that merely resemble them.

---

# What Makes It "Pseudo"?

In a true squeeze:

- A defender is forced to abandon one of two or more necessary guards.
- Declarer gains a trick regardless of the defender's choice.

In a pseudo squeeze:

- The defender appears to be squeezed.
- However, the extra trick comes from another tactical feature rather than from the discard itself.

The discard is not the true cause of declarer's success.

---

# Characteristics

Pseudo squeezes often involve:

- Forced discards.
- Apparent pressure.
- Threat cards.
- End-position play.

However, one or more essential elements of a genuine squeeze are missing.

---

# Missing Elements

A pseudo squeeze may lack:

- A genuine squeeze card.
- Multiple active threats.
- Rectification of the count.
- Proper communication.
- A defender who is genuinely overburdened.

Without these elements, the ending is not technically a squeeze.

---

# Typical Causes

A pseudo squeeze may arise because of:

## Blocked Suit

A defender cannot reach an established winner after winning a trick.

---

## Entry Problem

The defender holds the correct guard but has no entry to enjoy it.

---

## Endplay

After winning a trick, the defender must return a favorable suit.

The apparent squeeze is actually an endplay.

---

## Defensive Error

The defender discards the wrong card even though a safe discard exists.

Declarer benefits from the mistake rather than from a forced squeeze.

---

## Communication Failure

The defenders cannot reach each other's winners.

The apparent squeeze is really a communication problem.

---

# Conceptual Example

Near the end of the hand:

West appears to guard both hearts and clubs.

Declarer cashes a winner.

West discards a club.

Declarer later wins a club trick.

Analysis shows that West could safely have discarded a heart because East already protected the suit.

No genuine squeeze occurred.

The apparent squeeze was caused by an incorrect discard.

---

# Pseudo Squeeze vs. True Squeeze

| True Squeeze | Pseudo Squeeze |
|---------------|----------------|
| Defender has no successful discard | Defender has a safe discard |
| Extra trick is forced | Extra trick comes from another cause |
| Technical squeeze requirements are satisfied | One or more requirements are missing |
| Declarer succeeds regardless of defender's choice | Declarer often depends on a defensive mistake or another tactical factor |

---

# Why It Matters

Studying pseudo squeezes helps players:

- Recognize genuine squeeze opportunities.
- Avoid claiming squeezes where none exist.
- Analyze end positions more accurately.
- Understand the interaction between squeezes, endplays, and communication.

Many endings that appear spectacular are actually based on simpler principles.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Assuming every forced discard is a squeeze.
- Ignoring the possibility of a safe discard.
- Overlooking an accompanying endplay.
- Forgetting that communication may be the real issue.
- Confusing defensive errors with forced squeezes.

# Key Principles

- A pseudo squeeze resembles a squeeze but is **not** a true squeeze.
- The extra trick comes from another tactical feature, not from the defender's forced discard.
- Always verify that all the requirements of a genuine squeeze are present.
- Distinguishing true squeezes from pseudo squeezes leads to more accurate declarer analysis.
- Many apparent squeezes are actually examples of endplays, communication problems, or defensive errors.
