# Squeeze Without the Count

## Overview

A **Squeeze Without the Count** is a squeeze that succeeds **without first rectifying the count**. Unlike the classical squeeze, declarer does **not** need to lose all unavoidable losers before applying the squeeze because the defender who wins the eventual trick cannot profit from doing so.

Although uncommon, squeeze-without-the-count positions demonstrate that **rectifying the count is not an absolute requirement** for every squeeze.

---

# What Is "The Count"?

In squeeze play, **the count** refers to the number of unavoidable losers declarer still has.

Normally, before executing a squeeze:

- Declarer must lose every trick that can safely be lost.
- There must be no remaining losers when the squeeze card is played.

This process is called **rectifying the count**.

---

# Why Most Squeezes Require the Count

Suppose declarer still has one unavoidable loser.

If declarer attempts a normal squeeze:

1. The defender discards.
2. Declarer establishes an extra winner.
3. Declarer later loses the unavoidable trick.

The defender simply wins and the squeeze gains nothing.

For this reason, most squeezes require the count to be rectified first.

---

# Why This Squeeze Is Different

In a squeeze without the count:

- Declarer still has a loser.
- The squeeze is played before that loser disappears.
- When the defender eventually wins the outstanding trick, they **cannot profit** from the lead.

The squeeze therefore succeeds despite the remaining loser.

---

# Basic Principle

The squeeze works because:

- The defender who gains the lead has no effective return.
- The established winner remains available.
- Declarer's remaining loser is harmless.

The squeeze succeeds because **tempo** is irrelevant after the defender wins.

---

# Typical Situations

Squeeze without the count commonly occurs when:

- The defender who wins the remaining trick is endplayed.
- The defender has no safe exit card.
- Declarer's remaining loser cannot be exploited.
- Every return gives declarer the remaining tricks.

These positions often combine squeeze and endplay techniques.

---

# Planning the Play

Declarer should identify:

- The squeeze card.
- The remaining loser.
- Which defender will eventually gain the lead.
- Whether that defender has a safe return.
- The established winner after the squeeze.

If the defender cannot benefit from winning the remaining trick, rectifying the count may not be necessary.

---

# Conceptual Example

Near the end of the hand:

Declarer has:

- One unavoidable loser.
- One squeeze card.
- Two threat suits.

West guards both threat suits.

Declarer cashes the squeeze card.

West is forced to discard.

Later, West wins the remaining loser.

However, every return gives declarer access to the established winner.

The squeeze succeeds even though the count was never rectified.

---

# Relationship to Endplays

Many squeezes without the count include an **endplay**.

The defender who wins the remaining trick:

- Cannot cash additional winners.
- Cannot attack declarer's threats.
- Must return a favorable suit.

The endplay compensates for the lack of count rectification.

---

# Squeeze Without the Count vs. Simple Squeeze

| Simple Squeeze | Squeeze Without the Count |
|----------------|---------------------------|
| Count must be rectified | Count may remain unrectified |
| No losers remain | Declarer still has a loser |
| Defender must discard | Defender must discard, but later wins a harmless trick |
| Pure squeeze | Often combined with an endplay |

---

# Recognizing the Position

Ask yourself:

- Do I still have a loser?
- If so, who will win it?
- Can that defender safely continue the defense?
- Will they be forced to concede the remaining tricks?

If the answer to the last question is **yes**, a squeeze without the count may exist.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Assuming every squeeze requires rectifying the count.
- Attempting this squeeze when the defender has a safe exit card.
- Destroying the entries needed after the squeeze.
- Failing to recognize the accompanying endplay.
- Misidentifying the defender under pressure.

# Key Principles

- Most squeezes require the count to be rectified, but important exceptions exist.
- A squeeze without the count succeeds because the defender who wins the remaining loser cannot benefit from the lead.
- These squeezes frequently combine squeeze play with an endplay.
- Accurate counting and careful planning remain essential.
- Always consider whether the defender's eventual winning trick is actually dangerous before assuming the count must be rectified.
