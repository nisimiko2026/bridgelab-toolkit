# Criss-Cross Squeeze

## Overview

A **Criss-Cross Squeeze** is a form of squeeze in which the **entries to the threat cards are reversed** from those found in a standard squeeze. Instead of each threat being reached from its own hand, declarer crosses repeatedly between declarer and dummy to enjoy the winners after the squeeze.

The squeeze derives its name from this crossing pattern of communication.

Although less common than the simple squeeze, the criss-cross squeeze is a standard squeeze variation that demonstrates the importance of entry management and communication.

---

# Objectives

A criss-cross squeeze aims to:

- Force a defender to abandon a critical guard.
- Establish an additional winner.
- Exploit carefully preserved entries.
- Take advantage of an unusual communication pattern.

---

# Requirements

A successful criss-cross squeeze normally requires:

- A squeeze card.
- Two threat cards.
- A defender guarding both threat suits.
- Reversed (criss-cross) entries.
- Rectification of the count.
- Accurate timing.

---

# The Basic Idea

In a normal squeeze:

- Declarer reaches each threat from the same hand in which it is located.

In a criss-cross squeeze:

- Declarer reaches the threat in one hand **through an entry in the opposite hand**.

The communication "crosses" between declarer and dummy.

---

# Why It Is Different

The distinguishing feature is not the squeeze itself but the **entry structure**.

Instead of:

```
Threat
↓

Same hand entry
```

the pattern becomes:

```
Dummy threat
↑
Entry from declarer's hand

Declarer threat
↑
Entry from dummy
```

This crossed communication gives the squeeze its name.

---

# Planning the Criss-Cross Squeeze

Declarer should identify:

- The squeeze card.
- The two threats.
- The defender under pressure.
- The crossed entries.
- The correct order for cashing winners.

The communication pattern must be preserved throughout the hand.

---

# Preserving Entries

Entry management is more critical than in a standard squeeze.

Declarer must avoid:

- Using the crossed entry too early.
- Blocking either threat suit.
- Destroying communication between the hands.

A single careless play may eliminate the squeeze.

---

# Rectifying the Count

As with most squeezes:

Declarer must first lose every trick that can safely be lost.

Only then will the squeeze force a meaningful discard.

---

# Conceptual Example

Near the end of the hand:

```
Dummy
♥ A
♣ 5

Declarer
♣ A
♥ 5
♦ A  (squeeze card)
```

West guards:

- Hearts
- Clubs

Declarer cashes the ♦A.

West must discard.

Whichever suit West abandons becomes established.

The preserved crossed entries allow declarer to reach both threat cards.

---

# Criss-Cross vs. Simple Squeeze

| Simple Squeeze | Criss-Cross Squeeze |
|----------------|---------------------|
| Normal communication | Reversed communication |
| Entries remain in the same hand as the threats | Entries are in the opposite hand |
| Standard squeeze ending | Specialized communication pattern |

The squeeze mechanism is the same; only the communication differs.

---

# Typical Situations

Criss-cross squeezes commonly arise when:

- Declarer has carefully preserved entries.
- The threat cards lie in opposite hands.
- The obvious entry has disappeared.
- Only crossed communication remains.

These positions often appear unexpectedly in complex endings.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Destroying a crossed entry too early.
- Blocking one of the threat suits.
- Cashing winners in the wrong order.
- Failing to rectify the count.
- Misidentifying the defender under pressure.

# Key Principles

- A criss-cross squeeze is distinguished by **crossed entries**, not by a different squeeze mechanism.
- Preserve communication carefully throughout the hand.
- Rectify the count before applying the squeeze.
- Plan the order of entries and winners well before the ending.
- Criss-cross squeezes demonstrate that successful squeeze play depends as much on communication as on the squeeze itself.
