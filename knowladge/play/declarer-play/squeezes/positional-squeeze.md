# Positional Squeeze

## Overview

A **Positional Squeeze** is a squeeze that succeeds **only if one particular defender holds the critical guards**. Unlike an **Automatic Squeeze**, where the location of the guards is irrelevant, a positional squeeze depends entirely on the defenders' card placement.

The squeeze succeeds because one defender is forced to protect two threat suits simultaneously. If the guards are divided differently between the defenders, the squeeze fails.

Positional squeezes are the most common type of squeeze encountered in practical bridge.

---

# Objectives

A positional squeeze aims to:

- Force a specific defender to abandon a necessary guard.
- Establish an additional winner.
- Exploit the known or inferred location of critical honors.
- Gain an extra trick without taking a finesse.

---

# Basic Principle

Suppose declarer has threats in:

- Hearts.
- Clubs.

The squeeze succeeds **only if West guards both suits**.

When declarer cashes the squeeze card:

- West must discard.
- If West lets go of a heart, declarer's heart becomes a winner.
- If West lets go of a club, declarer's club becomes a winner.

If East guards one suit and West the other, no defender is under sufficient pressure and the squeeze fails.

---

# Requirements

A positional squeeze generally requires:

- A squeeze card.
- Two threat cards.
- One defender guarding both threat suits.
- Adequate communication.
- Correct timing.
- Rectification of the count.

Unlike an automatic squeeze, the **position** of the guards is critical.

---

# Why It Is "Positional"

The squeeze depends on **where** the missing honors are located.

Declarer must correctly identify—or infer—which defender holds the necessary guards.

If the critical cards are with the wrong defender, the squeeze cannot succeed.

---

# Locating the Defender

Declarer may determine the correct defender by using:

- The auction.
- The opening lead.
- Counting the distribution.
- Revealed honors.
- Discards.
- Vacant Spaces.
- Restricted Choice.

The more information declarer gathers, the more reliable the positional squeeze becomes.

---

# Planning the Play

Before attempting the squeeze, declarer should identify:

- The squeeze card.
- The threat cards.
- The defender under pressure.
- The required entries.
- Whether the count has been rectified.

If the location of the guards remains uncertain, declarer should consider whether an alternative line offers a higher percentage.

---

# Rectifying the Count

As with most squeezes:

Declarer must first lose every unavoidable loser.

Only then will the defender's discard produce an extra trick.

---

# Communication

Communication is essential.

Declarer must preserve the entries needed to:

- Cash the squeeze card.
- Reach the established threat.
- Enjoy the newly created winner.

Many positional squeezes fail because of poor entry management rather than incorrect analysis.

---

# Conceptual Example

Near the end of the hand:

```
Dummy
♥ A
♣ K

Declarer
♦ A  (squeeze card)
♣ A
```

West guards:

- Hearts.
- Clubs.

Declarer cashes the ♦A.

West must discard.

Whichever suit West abandons becomes declarer's additional winner.

If East had guarded one of the suits instead, no squeeze would exist.

---

# Positional vs. Automatic Squeeze

| Positional Squeeze | Automatic Squeeze |
|--------------------|-------------------|
| Depends on one defender holding both guards | Works regardless of which defender holds the guards |
| Requires identifying the correct defender | No need to identify the defender |
| Card placement is critical | Card placement is largely irrelevant |

Understanding this distinction is one of the foundations of squeeze theory.

---

# Positional vs. Simple Squeeze

Most simple squeezes encountered in practice are **positional squeezes**.

Only a smaller group qualify as **automatic squeezes**, where the location of the guards does not matter.

---

# Recognizing the Position

Ask yourself:

- Which defender guards the threat suits?
- Can one defender protect both?
- Have I identified that defender?
- Have I preserved communication?
- Has the count been rectified?

Only if all of these conditions are satisfied is a positional squeeze available.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Assuming the squeeze is automatic.
- Misidentifying the defender under pressure.
- Failing to count the defenders' distribution.
- Destroying an essential entry.
- Cashing the squeeze card before the position is ready.

# Key Principles

- A positional squeeze succeeds only when one specific defender holds the critical guards.
- Declarer must identify the defender under pressure before committing to the squeeze.
- Accurate counting and inference are essential to locating the guards.
- Communication, timing, and rectifying the count remain fundamental requirements.
- Most practical squeeze positions are positional rather than automatic, making careful analysis of the defenders' holdings essential.
