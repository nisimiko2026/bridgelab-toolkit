# Rectifying the Count

## Overview

**Rectifying the Count** is a fundamental declarer-play technique used primarily in **squeeze play**. It means deliberately **losing the tricks you can afford to lose before executing the squeeze**. By doing so, declarer reaches a position where every remaining trick is needed to fulfill the contract.

Without rectifying the count, many squeezes simply do not work because the squeezed defender can safely win a trick after being forced to discard.

Rectifying the count is one of the essential prerequisites for successful squeeze play.

---

# Purpose

Rectifying the Count allows declarer to:

- Prepare the hand for a squeeze.
- Ensure that every remaining trick is critical.
- Force defenders into impossible discard decisions.
- Execute squeeze endings correctly.
- Coordinate timing in advanced declarer play.

---

# Basic Principle

The guiding principle is:

> **Lose your unavoidable losers before applying the squeeze.**

After the count has been rectified:

- Declarer cannot afford to lose another trick.
- Every remaining trick must be won.
- The squeezed defender has no safe option.

---

# Why It Is Necessary

Suppose declarer still has a loser available when the squeeze card is played.

The squeezed defender can simply:

1. Discard safely.
2. Win the later losing trick.

The squeeze gains nothing.

Rectifying the count removes this escape.

---

# When to Use

Rectifying the Count is essential in:

- Simple Squeezes.
- Double Squeezes.
- Strip Squeezes.
- Many Vienna Coup positions.
- Some endplay combinations.

---

# Requirements

A successful rectification usually requires:

- Knowledge of the total number of losers.
- Accurate counting.
- Proper timing.
- Preservation of entries.
- Recognition of the squeeze position.

---

# Example 1 – Simple Squeeze

Contract

```
6NT
```

Declarer has:

- Eleven sure tricks.
- One unavoidable loser.

Correct play:

Lose the unavoidable trick early.

Now every remaining trick is required.

The squeeze succeeds.

---

# Example 2 – Delaying Winners

Declarer holds:

```
12 potential tricks
```

One loser remains.

Rather than cashing winners immediately, declarer first concedes the loser.

The squeeze now becomes effective.

---

# Example 3 – Suit Contract

Contract

```
6♠
```

Declarer must lose:

```
♦A
```

Instead of drawing all trumps first, declarer immediately loses the diamond.

Later the squeeze functions correctly.

---

# Planning the Play

Before Trick One, ask:

- How many losers can I afford?
- Which loser is unavoidable?
- Can I lose it now?
- Will losing it later destroy the squeeze?
- Are my entries preserved?

---

# Timing

Timing is critical.

Typical sequence:

1. Count winners and losers.
2. Identify unavoidable losers.
3. Lose them early.
4. Preserve entries.
5. Execute the squeeze.

Delaying the unavoidable loser is a common mistake.

---

# Relationship to Other Techniques

Rectifying the Count is frequently used with:

- Simple Squeeze.
- Double Squeeze.
- Strip Squeeze.
- Vienna Coup.
- Entry Management.

It is often the first step in successful squeeze play.

---

# Common Situations

Rectifying the Count frequently occurs:

- In slam contracts.
- In notrump contracts.
- Before squeeze endings.
- During advanced declarer planning.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Forgetting to rectify the count.
- Cashing winners too early.
- Losing the unavoidable trick too late.
- Destroying communication.
- Miscounting the remaining losers.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Recognize impending squeeze positions.
- Force declarer to lose additional tricks.
- Attack declarer's entries.
- Preserve communication.
- Exchange guarding responsibilities.

---

# Advantages

- Makes squeezes possible.
- Improves timing.
- Simplifies endgame planning.
- Prevents failed squeeze attempts.
- Essential for advanced declarer play.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires careful planning.
- Accurate counting is essential.
- New information may change the plan.
- Usually relevant only in advanced endings.

---

# Memory Aid

**Rectifying the Count = "Lose Early to Win Later."**

Remember:

- **Lose unavoidable tricks before attempting a squeeze.**
- **Every remaining trick should be essential to making the contract.**
- **Preserve entries while rectifying the count.**
- **Many squeeze positions fail simply because the count was not rectified.**
- **Rectifying the count is preparation—not the squeeze itself.**

Rectifying the Count is one of the fundamental building blocks of expert squeeze play. By deliberately conceding unavoidable losers before beginning the endgame, declarer ensures that the squeeze places maximum pressure on the defenders. Together with proper entry management and techniques such as the Vienna Coup, rectifying the count transforms many theoretical squeeze positions into practical winning contracts.
