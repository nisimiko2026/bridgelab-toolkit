# Establishing Long Suits

## Overview

One of the primary objectives in a notrump contract is to **establish long suits**. Since declarer cannot score extra tricks by ruffing, additional winners are usually created by driving out the defenders' high cards until the remaining small cards become winners.

Successfully establishing a long suit often provides the extra tricks needed to make the contract. However, it requires careful planning, proper timing, and good entry management.

---

# Objectives

Establishing a long suit allows declarer to:

- Develop additional winners.
- Reach the required number of tricks.
- Force defenders to surrender their high cards.
- Maximize the value of small cards.
- Create a source of tricks in one suit.

---

# The Basic Principle

A long suit becomes established when the defenders' higher cards have been exhausted.

Example:

```
Dummy
♣ A J 10 8 5

Declarer
♣ K 7 6 4
```

After driving out the defenders' queen, the remaining clubs become winners.

---

# Choosing the Right Suit

When more than one suit can be developed, consider:

- Number of potential winners.
- Number of entries required.
- Number of rounds needed to establish the suit.
- Risk of losing the lead.
- Information from the auction.

The longest suit is not always the best choice.

---

# Count Winners First

Before attacking a long suit, count:

- Sure winners.
- Potential winners.
- Tricks still required.

If the contract can already be made safely, there may be no need to establish an additional suit.

---

# Preserve Entries

A long suit is valuable only if declarer can reach it after it has been established.

Ask:

- How many entries remain?
- Which entries are essential?
- Can I preserve an entry to dummy?

Many contracts fail because declarer establishes a suit but cannot reach the winners.

---

# Timing

Timing is critical.

Declarer often must:

1. Lose the lead to establish the suit.
2. Regain the lead.
3. Return to the established winners.

Poor timing may allow the defenders to establish and cash their own long suit first.

---

# Hold-Up Play

If defenders attack their own long suit first, declarer may need a **hold-up play** before establishing the long suit.

The hold-up:

- Breaks defenders' communication.
- Prevents them from cashing all their winners.
- Gives declarer time to establish the desired suit.

---

# Choosing the Correct Defender

Sometimes the suit should be developed through one opponent rather than the other.

Factors include:

- Dangerous opponent.
- Known honor locations.
- Information from the auction.
- Restricted Choice.
- Vacant Spaces.

These considerations often improve the percentage play.

---

# Long Suits in Dummy

Frequently the long suit is located in dummy.

Declarer should:

- Preserve dummy's entries.
- Avoid blocking the suit.
- Delay unnecessary entry cards.
- Plan the order of play carefully.

Good communication is essential.

---

# Long Suits in Declarer's Hand

If the long suit is in declarer's hand:

- Preserve entries back to hand.
- Avoid unnecessary overtakes.
- Plan the return after establishing the suit.

The same principles apply regardless of which hand contains the long suit.

---

# Example 1

Contract:

```
3NT
```

Dummy:

```
♣ A J 10 9 5
```

Declarer:

```
♣ K 6 4
```

Declarer drives out the defenders' queen before cashing the remaining club winners.

---

# Example 2

Declarer has:

```
♦ A K 10 8 7
```

Opposite:

```
♦ 6 5 4
```

Declarer gives up a diamond trick early.

Later, after regaining the lead, the remaining diamonds provide several winners.

---

# Example 3

Declarer has potential long-suit winners in both clubs and diamonds.

Clubs require losing one trick.

Diamonds require losing two tricks.

The club suit is normally developed first because it reaches the necessary tricks more quickly.

---

# Common Problems

### Blocked Suit

Winning with the wrong honor may block the established winners.

Plan the order in which honors are played.

---

### No Remaining Entry

Declarer establishes the suit but has no entry to cash the winners.

Always identify the necessary entries before attacking the suit.

---

### Defenders Win the Race

While declarer establishes one suit, defenders establish another.

Proper timing and hold-up play often determine which side reaches its winners first.

---

# Planning Checklist

Before establishing a long suit, ask:

1. Which suit offers the greatest number of additional tricks?
2. How many rounds are required?
3. How many entries will I need?
4. Can I preserve those entries?
5. Do I need a hold-up play first?
6. Which defender is dangerous?
7. Can the defenders establish their own suit before I do?

---

# Common Mistakes

- Establishing a suit without preserving entries.
- Blocking the suit by cashing honors in the wrong order.
- Choosing a slower suit when a quicker source of tricks exists.
- Ignoring the defenders' potential long suit.
- Failing to plan before losing the lead.
- Forgetting that communication is as important as establishment.

# Key Principles

- Long suits are the primary source of additional tricks in notrump contracts.
- Establishing a suit usually requires giving up the lead at least once.
- Preserve the entries needed to reach the established winners.
- Timing and communication are just as important as the long suit itself.
- Always compare the speed and risk of developing each potential long suit before choosing your plan.
