# Notrump Play

## Overview

Playing a **notrump contract** differs fundamentally from playing a suit contract. Without a trump suit, declarer cannot eliminate losers by ruffing. Instead, success depends on **establishing long suits, preserving entries, maintaining communication, and careful timing**.

In notrump contracts, declarer usually wins by developing additional tricks rather than by ruffing losers. Every trick must therefore be planned carefully from the opening lead.

---

# Objectives

The goals of declarer in a notrump contract are to:

- Count immediate winners.
- Identify additional tricks that can be developed.
- Establish long suits.
- Preserve entries between the two hands.
- Prevent defenders from cashing their long suits.
- Maintain communication.
- Keep the dangerous opponent off lead.

---

# Planning the Play

Before playing to the first trick, declarer should answer four questions:

1. How many sure tricks do I have?
2. Where will the additional tricks come from?
3. How will I establish those tricks?
4. How will I reach them after they are established?

A complete plan should be formed before playing from dummy.

---

# Counting Winners

Unlike suit contracts, notrump contracts are usually planned by **counting winners**, not losers.

Count:

- Sure tricks.
- Potential tricks.
- Additional tricks that can be developed.

Example:

```
Sure winners:

♠ 2
♥ 3
♦ 2
♣ 2

Total = 9
```

If nine tricks are already available, no unnecessary risks should be taken.

---

# Establishing Long Suits

Most extra tricks in notrump come from establishing long suits.

Typical methods include:

- Driving out defenders' high cards.
- Establishing the fifth or sixth card of a suit.
- Preserving entries until the suit is established.

Long-suit development is the hallmark of successful notrump play.

---

# Hold-Up Play

One of the most important notrump techniques is the **hold-up play**.

Declarer deliberately ducks one or more rounds of the defenders' long suit in order to:

- Break their communication.
- Exhaust the attacker's suit.
- Prevent defenders from cashing all of their winners.

A well-executed hold-up often determines the outcome of the contract.

---

# Preserving Communication

Communication between declarer and dummy is essential.

Declarer should:

- Preserve important entries.
- Avoid blocking long suits.
- Win tricks with the correct hand.
- Avoid unnecessary overtakes.

Established winners are worthless if they cannot be reached.

---

# Timing

Timing is often the deciding factor.

Declarer must balance:

- Establishing tricks.
- Maintaining entries.
- Preventing defenders from establishing their suits first.

Frequently, declarer must lose a trick at the correct moment to gain several tricks later.

---

# The Dangerous Opponent

Often one defender is more dangerous than the other.

Reasons include:

- Holding the established long suit.
- Leading through vulnerable honors.
- Having the only remaining entry to partner.

Declarer should plan to keep that defender off lead whenever possible.

---

# Choosing the Right Suit

If more than one suit can be developed, consider:

- Number of potential tricks.
- Number of entries required.
- Risk of losing the lead.
- Probability of success.
- Information from the auction.

The highest-percentage line is not always the safest line.

---

# Reading the Opening Lead

The opening lead often provides valuable information about:

- Defender's suit length.
- Honor holdings.
- Partnership lead agreements.
- Possible distribution.

Declarer should use this information when planning the play.

---

# Common Techniques

Important notrump techniques include:

- Hold-up play.
- Ducking.
- Establishing long suits.
- Preserving entries.
- Finessing.
- Safety plays.
- Avoiding the dangerous opponent.
- Careful timing.

Although these techniques also appear elsewhere in the knowledge base, they are especially important in notrump contracts.

---

# Example 1 – Establishing a Long Suit

Contract:

```
3NT
```

Declarer has:

```
♣ A K 10 9 7
```

Dummy:

```
♣ 6 5 4
```

Declarer drives out the defenders' queen, then later cashes the established club winners.

---

# Example 2 – Hold-Up Play

West leads:

```
♠K
```

Dummy:

```
♠ A 7 4
```

Declarer:

```
♠ Q 8 3
```

Declarer ducks twice before winning the third round, cutting the defenders' communication.

---

# Example 3 – Preserving Entries

Dummy contains an established diamond suit.

Instead of using dummy's only entry early, declarer first establishes the diamonds, then later uses the preserved entry to cash the winners.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Failing to count winners before playing.
- Establishing a suit without preserving an entry.
- Winning too early instead of executing a hold-up play.
- Ignoring the dangerous opponent.
- Blocking long suits.
- Playing too quickly without forming a complete plan.

# Key Principles

- Notrump contracts are won by **developing winners**, not by ruffing losers.
- Count winners before playing to the first trick.
- Plan how to establish and reach long suits.
- Preserve communication and entries throughout the hand.
- Use hold-up play and timing to prevent defenders from cashing their long suits.
- Identify and avoid giving the lead to the dangerous opponent whenever possible.
