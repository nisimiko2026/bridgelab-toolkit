# Timing in Notrump

## Overview

**Timing** is one of the most critical aspects of declarer play in notrump contracts. Since there is no trump suit to eliminate losers by ruffing, declarer must carefully decide **when** to establish winners, **when** to lose the lead, and **when** to cash established tricks.

Many notrump contracts fail not because declarer lacks enough winners, but because the winners are developed **too late** or the defenders establish and cash their own suits first.

Good timing ensures that declarer develops winners before the defenders can exploit their own.

---

# Objectives

Good timing allows declarer to:

- Establish long suits before the defenders do.
- Preserve communication between the hands.
- Delay losing the lead until the proper moment.
- Prevent defenders from cashing their long suits.
- Maximize the value of entries.
- Reach established winners safely.

---

# Timing Begins at Trick One

Before playing from dummy, declarer should answer:

1. How many sure winners do I have?
2. Where will the additional tricks come from?
3. How many times must I lose the lead?
4. Can the defenders establish their suit first?
5. What sequence of play gives me the best chance?

A complete timing plan should be formed before playing to the first trick.

---

# The Race to Establish Winners

In many notrump contracts, both sides are trying to establish long suits.

Declarer may be trying to establish clubs.

The defenders may be trying to establish spades.

The side that completes its plan first often wins the race.

Timing determines who succeeds.

---

# Cash Immediate Winners First

Before giving up the lead, declarer should usually cash any winners that cannot be lost later.

Example:

Declarer has:

- Two spade winners.
- Three heart winners.
- Two diamond winners.

Before losing a club trick to establish clubs, declarer cashes the safe winners.

This reduces the defenders' opportunities after they regain the lead.

---

# Lose the Lead at the Right Time

Giving up the lead is often unavoidable.

The important question is:

> **When should declarer lose the lead?**

Often the correct answer is:

- After preserving entries.
- After cashing immediate winners.
- After identifying the dangerous opponent.

Poor timing frequently allows defenders to defeat the contract.

---

# Hold-Up Play

A hold-up play is a classic timing technique.

Declarer deliberately ducks the defenders' long suit to:

- Break their communication.
- Exhaust the leader's suit.
- Gain time to establish winners.

The objective is not simply to duck, but to improve the timing of the entire hand.

---

# Preserve Communication

Timing and communication are inseparable.

Before establishing a long suit, declarer must ensure that:

- The necessary entries remain.
- The suit will not become blocked.
- The established winners can be reached.

Poor timing often results in stranded winners.

---

# Choosing the Order of Play

Several lines may produce the same number of tricks.

Timing determines which should be played first.

Example:

Declarer can:

- Establish clubs.
- Take a diamond finesse.

If the club suit requires losing the lead, the finesse may need to wait until the clubs have been developed.

---

# Timing and the Dangerous Opponent

Sometimes declarer should delay a finesse simply because losing to one defender would be disastrous.

Timing therefore includes deciding:

- Which defender should gain the lead.
- When that defender should gain it.
- Whether declarer is ready for that to happen.

---

# Example 1 – Establish Before Defenders

Contract:

```
3NT
```

Declarer has:

```
♣ A K J 10 7
```

Declarer first drives out the defenders' queen.

After regaining the lead, the established clubs provide enough tricks before the defenders can cash their spades.

---

# Example 2 – Cash Winners First

Declarer needs one additional trick.

Instead of immediately giving up a diamond trick, declarer first cashes all certain winners.

When the defenders finally gain the lead, there are fewer tricks remaining for them to establish.

---

# Example 3 – Hold-Up Play

West leads:

```
♠K
```

Dummy:

```
♠A 7 4
```

Declarer:

```
♠Q 8 3
```

Declarer ducks twice.

The hold-up delays the defenders' attack long enough for declarer to establish the club suit.

---

# Common Timing Errors

- Giving up the lead too early.
- Establishing a suit before preserving entries.
- Forgetting to cash immediate winners.
- Ignoring the defenders' potential long suit.
- Blocking an established suit.
- Failing to identify the dangerous opponent.

---

# Planning Checklist

Before playing from dummy, ask:

1. How many winners do I already have?
2. Which suit should I establish?
3. How many times must I lose the lead?
4. Can I cash immediate winners first?
5. Are my entries preserved?
6. Do I need a hold-up play?
7. Which defender is dangerous?

# Key Principles

- Timing is the art of deciding **when** to establish winners and **when** to surrender the lead.
- Cash immediate winners before giving the defenders an opportunity to attack.
- Preserve communication and entries throughout the hand.
- Hold-up plays are classic timing techniques in notrump contracts.
- Good timing allows declarer to establish winners before the defenders can establish theirs.
