# Communication in Notrump

## Overview

In notrump contracts, **communication** refers to the ability to move the lead efficiently between declarer and dummy. Since there is no trump suit to create additional entries through ruffing, maintaining communication is often the key to developing and cashing winners.

Many notrump contracts fail not because declarer lacks sufficient tricks, but because the established winners cannot be reached.

Communication is therefore one of the most important aspects of planning a notrump contract.

---

# Objectives

Good communication allows declarer to:

- Reach established long-suit winners.
- Take repeated finesses.
- Preserve entries to both hands.
- Maintain control of the play.
- Execute the planned sequence of tricks.
- Prevent suits from becoming blocked.

---

# Communication Begins at Trick One

Before playing from dummy, declarer should ask:

- How many entries does each hand have?
- Which entries are essential?
- Which entries can be used safely?
- How will I reach my established winners?

Planning communication early prevents many avoidable mistakes.

---

# Entries Create Communication

Communication depends on **entries**.

Typical entries include:

- Aces.
- Kings protected by lower cards.
- Established winners.
- Occasionally successful finesses.

Every unnecessary use of an entry weakens communication.

---

# Preserve Important Entries

Not all entries have equal value.

Example:

```
Dummy
♣ A 8 5

Declarer
♣ K Q 4
```

If dummy's ♣A is the only entry to an established long suit, it should not be used prematurely.

Always identify which entries are critical before playing the first trick.

---

# Avoid Blocking Suits

Communication can be destroyed if a suit becomes blocked.

Example:

```
Dummy
♦ A J 10

Declarer
♦ K Q
```

If the honors are played in the wrong order, the suit may become blocked and the remaining winners stranded.

Plan the order of cashing honors to keep the suit open.

---

# Communication and Long Suits

Most notrump contracts are won by establishing a long suit.

Successful development requires:

1. Preserve an entry.
2. Establish the suit.
3. Return to the correct hand.
4. Cash the established winners.

Without communication, the long suit may never produce its extra tricks.

---

# Communication and Hold-Up Play

A hold-up play often improves communication by:

- Breaking the defenders' communication.
- Preventing them from cashing their long suit.
- Giving declarer time to establish and enjoy winners.

Communication between declarer and dummy is only useful if the defenders' communication has first been disrupted.

---

# Communication and Timing

Communication and timing work together.

Declarer must decide:

- When to use an entry.
- When to preserve an entry.
- Which hand should win each trick.

Using an entry too early may make later winners unreachable.

---

# Example 1 – Preserving an Entry

Contract:

```
3NT
```

Dummy:

```
♣ A J 10 8 5
```

Declarer:

```
♣ K 6 4
```

Declarer first drives out the defenders' queen.

Only later is the ♣A used as the entry to reach the established club winners.

---

# Example 2 – Avoiding a Blocked Suit

Dummy:

```
♥ A Q J
```

Declarer:

```
♥ K 10
```

By cashing the honors in the correct order, declarer keeps communication intact and prevents the suit from blocking.

---

# Example 3 – Multiple Entries

Dummy contains:

- ♠A
- ♦A

The diamonds are the source of extra tricks.

Declarer preserves the ♦A as the entry to the established diamond suit and uses the ♠A for another purpose.

Not all entries should be treated equally.

---

# Common Communication Problems

## Using Entries Too Early

A valuable entry disappears before the long suit has been established.

---

## Blocking the Suit

The winners remain stranded because the honors were played in the wrong order.

---

## Wrong Hand on Lead

Declarer reaches the correct suit from the wrong hand and cannot continue the planned sequence.

---

## Running Out of Entries

The suit is successfully established, but there is no remaining way to reach it.

---

# Planning Checklist

Before playing to the first trick, ask:

1. Where are my entries?
2. Which entries are essential?
3. Which suit will provide additional tricks?
4. How will I reach that suit later?
5. Can any suit become blocked?
6. Should I preserve a particular ace or king?
7. Will a hold-up play improve my communication?

---

# Common Mistakes

- Using the only entry to dummy too early.
- Blocking a long suit by cashing honors in the wrong order.
- Failing to plan communication before establishing a suit.
- Ignoring which hand should gain the lead.
- Forgetting that entries are limited resources.

# Key Principles

- Communication is the ability to move the lead efficiently between declarer and dummy.
- Preserve the entries needed to reach established winners.
- Avoid blocking long suits by planning the order of play.
- Communication and timing are closely connected.
- Many notrump contracts are won—or lost—because of effective communication management.
