# Hold-Up Play

## Overview

A **hold-up play** is a declarer technique in which declarer deliberately **refuses to win a trick immediately**, even though doing so is possible. Instead, declarer ducks one or more rounds of a suit to disrupt the defenders' communications, exhaust their entries, or improve the timing of the play.

Hold-up play is used most often in **notrump contracts**, where preserving control of the hand and preventing defenders from cashing long suits is critical.

Although similar to ducking, a hold-up play has a specific strategic purpose: **breaking the defenders' communication**.

---

# Objectives

A successful hold-up play may:

- Cut communication between defenders.
- Exhaust the dangerous opponent's suit.
- Force the defenders to switch suits.
- Prevent defenders from cashing established winners.
- Improve declarer's timing.
- Preserve control of the hand.

---

# Hold-Up vs. Ducking

A hold-up play is a **special form of ducking**.

| Ducking | Hold-Up Play |
|----------|--------------|
| Intentionally lose a trick | Intentionally lose a trick to cut defenders' communication |
| Many tactical purposes | Specifically aims to disrupt communication and timing |
| Used by declarer or defenders | Primarily a declarer technique |

Every hold-up is a duck, but not every duck is a hold-up play.

---

# Classic Notrump Example

Contract:

```
3NT
```

Opening lead:

```
♠K
```

Dummy:

```
♠ A 7 4
```

Declarer:

```
♠ Q 8 3
```

If declarer wins the first trick:

- Defenders may later regain the lead.
- They can cash the remaining spades.

Instead, declarer ducks the first two rounds of spades.

When declarer finally wins the third round, the defenders have no more spades to cash, and communication in the suit has been broken.

---

# Timing Is Critical

Holding up too few times may allow the defenders to establish and cash the suit.

Holding up too many times may leave declarer without a stopper.

The correct number of ducks depends on:

- The number of stoppers.
- The defenders' likely distribution.
- Entries to both hands.
- The contract.

---

# Hold-Up in Notrump

Typical reasons include:

- Preventing defenders from enjoying long-suit winners.
- Cutting communication.
- Keeping the dangerous opponent off lead.
- Forcing defenders to attack another suit.

This is the most common use of the technique.

---

# Hold-Up in Suit Contracts

Although less common, hold-up plays also occur in suit contracts.

Examples:

- Preserving entries.
- Preventing an opponent from gaining the lead.
- Improving timing before drawing trumps.
- Preparing an endplay.

The objectives are usually different from those in notrump.

---

# Identifying the Dangerous Opponent

A hold-up play often aims to keep one opponent from gaining the lead.

The dangerous opponent may be the defender who:

- Has the established long suit.
- Can lead through a vulnerable honor.
- Can give partner a ruff.
- Can force declarer repeatedly.

Keeping that defender off lead can determine the success of the contract.

---

# Example 1

Contract:

```
3NT
```

Opening lead:

```
♥K
```

Dummy:

```
♥ A 8 4
```

Declarer:

```
♥ Q 6 5
```

Declarer ducks twice before winning the third round.

The defenders' hearts are exhausted before they regain the lead.

---

# Example 2

Contract:

```
3NT
```

West leads:

```
♦K
```

Declarer has:

```
♦ A J 4
```

Dummy:

```
♦ 8 6 2
```

By ducking the first diamond, declarer may prevent the defenders from enjoying all of their diamond winners later.

---

# Common Situations for Hold-Up Play

Use a hold-up when:

- Defenders attack their long suit.
- You have one or more stoppers.
- Communication between defenders can be broken.
- Timing favors declarer.
- One defender is dangerous.

---

# When Not to Hold Up

Avoid a hold-up when:

- You may lose control of the suit.
- Defenders can easily switch attacks.
- Immediate winners are needed.
- Communication cannot be broken.
- The dangerous opponent already has another entry.

---

# Planning the Hold-Up

Before ducking, ask:

- How many cards do the defenders have in the suit?
- How many stoppers do I have?
- Who is the dangerous opponent?
- Can the defenders re-enter the suit later?
- Will ducking improve my timing?

A successful hold-up is usually planned at trick one.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Ducking automatically without a clear objective.
- Holding up one trick too many.
- Forgetting about defenders' side entries.
- Ignoring the dangerous opponent.
- Failing to plan the entire hand before ducking.

# Key Principles

- A hold-up play is a deliberate duck designed to disrupt the defenders' communication.
- It is most effective in notrump contracts.
- The technique often prevents defenders from cashing their long suit.
- Correct timing is essential—duck too few times and the defense retains communication; duck too many times and you may lose control of the suit.
- Always identify the dangerous opponent before deciding whether to hold up.
