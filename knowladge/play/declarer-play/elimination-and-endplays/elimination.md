# Elimination Play Technique

## Overview

**Elimination Play** (also called **Strip Play**) is a declarer-play technique in which declarer removes ("strips") the defenders of their safe exit cards before forcing a defender to win a trick. The defender is then unable to make a safe return and must concede an extra trick.

Elimination Play is frequently used together with an **Endplay (Throw-In)**, but it is a separate technique. The elimination removes the defenders' safe exit cards; the endplay exploits the resulting position.

---

# Purpose

Elimination Play allows declarer to:

- Remove defenders' safe exit cards.
- Force a favorable return.
- Avoid risky finesses.
- Create extra tricks without establishing new winners.
- Improve the chances of making close contracts.

---

# Basic Principle

The technique follows three stages:

1. Eliminate the defenders' safe exit suits.
2. Force a defender to win a trick.
3. Benefit from the defender's forced return.

Unlike a squeeze, no defender is forced to discard. Instead, the defender is forced to **lead**.

---

# When to Use

Elimination Play is appropriate when:

- A defender has a dangerous exit suit.
- Declarer can remove that exit suit.
- One defender must eventually win a trick.
- A forced return will benefit declarer.

---

# Requirements

A successful Elimination Play usually requires:

- Removal of one or more side suits.
- Accurate counting.
- Proper timing.
- A defender who will eventually gain the lead.
- A favorable position after the forced return.

---

# Relationship to Endplay

The techniques are closely connected.

### Elimination

Removes safe exit cards.

### Endplay

Forces the defender to make the return.

Most successful endplays begin with an elimination.

---

# Example 1 – Lead into a Tenace

## Contract

```
4♠
```

Dummy

```
♠ AQ
```

Declarer

```
♠ J10
```

West

```
♠ Kx
```

Declarer first eliminates clubs and hearts.

Then exits with a diamond.

West wins.

West has only spades remaining.

Any spade lead gives declarer two tricks.

---

# Example 2 – Ruff and Discard

Trump = Hearts

Declarer strips:

- Clubs.
- Diamonds.

West eventually wins a spade.

West must return:

- A club.
- A diamond.

Both produce a ruff-and-discard.

---

# Example 3 – Forced Trump Lead

Trump = Spades

After eliminating side suits:

West wins a diamond.

West's only remaining cards are trumps.

Any trump return removes one of West's own trumps and benefits declarer.

---

# Planning the Play

Before Trick One, identify:

- Safe exit suits.
- Which defender should be endplayed.
- Which suits must be eliminated.
- Entries.
- Remaining trump control.

---

# Eliminating Suits

Typical elimination sequence:

1. Cash side-suit winners.
2. Ruff remaining cards.
3. Draw trumps if appropriate.
4. Leave the defender with only dangerous return cards.

---

# Timing

Timing is critical.

Usually:

1. Eliminate first.
2. Preserve entries.
3. Throw in the defender.
4. Take advantage of the forced return.

Attempting the throw-in before eliminating exits usually fails.

---

# Common Situations

Elimination Play frequently occurs:

- Before an Endplay.
- Before a Ruff-and-Discard.
- To avoid a finesse.
- In suit contracts.
- Occasionally in notrump contracts.

---

# Hand Example

Dummy

```
♠ AQ4
♥ K7
♦ —
♣ —
```

Declarer

```
♠ J10
♥ A
♦ A
♣ —
```

West

```
♠ K8
♦ K
```

Declarer:

- Eliminates hearts and clubs.
- Throws West in with a diamond.

West must lead spades into the AQ-J10 tenace.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Forgetting to eliminate one side suit.
- Throwing in the wrong defender.
- Losing communication.
- Drawing trumps at the wrong time.
- Miscounting the defenders' remaining cards.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Preserve safe exit cards.
- Avoid taking winners too early.
- Recognize elimination positions.
- Maintain communication.
- Avoid being thrown in.

---

# Advantages

- Avoids risky finesses.
- Forces favorable returns.
- Creates extra tricks.
- Highly effective in close contracts.
- Excellent preparation for an Endplay.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires precise planning.
- Accurate counting is essential.
- Timing errors can ruin the play.
- Not every deal offers an elimination opportunity.

# Declarer's Checklist

Before attempting Elimination Play:

- Which defender should win the trick?
- Which suits must be stripped?
- Have I preserved the necessary entries?
- Is the dangerous defender on lead?
- What return will the defender be forced to make?

---

# Memory Guide

2. Preserve communication.
3. Throw in the defender.
4. Profit from the forced return.

---

# Memory Aid

**Elimination Play = "Strip Before You Throw In."**

Remember:

- **Remove the defenders' safe exit suits first.**
- **Preserve entries throughout the play.**
- **Force the chosen defender to win a trick.**
- **Take advantage of the inevitable favorable return.**
- **Often used together with an Endplay.**

Elimination Play is one of the cornerstones of expert declarer play. By stripping the defenders of safe exit cards before conceding the lead, declarer creates positions where the defender has no favorable return. Combined with Endplays, Loser-on-Loser plays, and Squeezes, it is an essential technique for making difficult contracts.
