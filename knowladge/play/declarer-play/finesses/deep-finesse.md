# Deep Finesse Technique

## Overview

A **Deep Finesse** is an advanced form of finesse in which declarer attempts to win a trick with a **lower honor** against an intermediate missing honor rather than against the highest missing honor. Instead of finessing the Queen against the King, declarer may finesse the **10 against the Jack**, or the **9 against the 10**.

A Deep Finesse is often available when declarer holds a strong sequence of honors and wishes to maximize the number of tricks in a suit.

---

# Purpose

The Deep Finesse allows declarer to:

- Win additional tricks against intermediate honors.
- Maximize tricks in a suit.
- Preserve higher honors for later tricks.
- Improve long-suit development.
- Increase the chances of making the contract.

---

# Basic Principle

Instead of finessing against the highest missing honor, declarer finesses against a **lower missing honor**.

Example:

```
Dummy
♠ AQ10

Declarer
♠ 543
```

Missing:

```
♠ KJ
```

Rather than playing the Queen, declarer may play the **10**, hoping East holds the Jack.

If successful, both the Ace and Queen remain to capture the King later.

---

# When to Use

A Deep Finesse is appropriate when:

- Intermediate honors are missing.
- Declarer holds a strong sequence.
- The position allows preservation of higher honors.
- The contract benefits from maximizing tricks.

---

# Requirements

A successful Deep Finesse usually requires:

- A touching honor sequence.
- One or more intermediate honors missing.
- Correct entries.
- Favorable honor position.
- Accurate timing.

---

# Example 1 – Classic Deep Finesse

Holding

```
Dummy
♠ AQ10

Declarer
♠ 543
```

Missing:

```
♠ KJ
```

Lead a small spade toward dummy.

If East plays low:

Play the **10**.

If East holds the Jack:

The 10 wins.

Later:

The Queen captures the King.

Declarer scores three tricks.

# Deep Finesse vs. Simple Finesse

## Simple Finesse

Attempts to win against the **highest missing honor**.

Example:

```
AQ
```

against

```
K
```

---

## Deep Finesse

Attempts to win against an **intermediate honor**.

Example:

```
AQ10
```

against

```
KJ
```

---

# Probability

The success of a Deep Finesse depends on:

- The location of the missing intermediate honor.
- Available entries.
- Information from the auction.
- Defenders' signals.

As with all finesses, additional information can change the theoretical odds.

---

# Planning the Play

Before attempting a Deep Finesse, ask:

- Which honor am I finessing against?
- Can I preserve the higher honor?
- Are sufficient entries available?
- Is there a safer alternative?
- Will the Deep Finesse gain an extra trick?

---

# Timing

Timing is essential.

Generally:

1. Preserve entries.
2. Delay the finesse if additional information may become available.
3. Avoid blocking the suit.
4. Cash established winners only after the finesse.

---

# Common Situations

Deep Finesses occur:

- In notrump contracts.
- During slam play.
- While establishing long suits.
- In combination with repeated finesses.
- When holding touching honors.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Playing the wrong honor.
- Losing necessary entries.
- Blocking the suit.
- Forgetting a simpler winning line.
- Attempting a Deep Finesse unnecessarily.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Conceal intermediate honors.
- Falsecard when appropriate.
- Preserve communication.
- Avoid revealing the honor position.
- Force declarer into guessing.

---

# Advantages

- Produces extra tricks.
- Preserves higher honors.
- Maximizes long-suit potential.
- Useful in slam contracts.
- Combines naturally with repeated finesses.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires careful timing.
- Depends on favorable honor position.
- Entry management is essential.
- Often more difficult than a simple finesse.

Before taking a Deep Finesse:

- Which intermediate honor is missing?
- Can I preserve my higher honors?
- Do I have enough entries?
- Is another line safer?
- Will the finesse create an extra trick?

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Identify the missing intermediate honor.
2. Preserve entries.
3. Lead toward the touching honors.
4. Play the lower honor.
5. Cash the remaining winners.

# Memory Aid

**Deep Finesse = "Finesse the Lower Honor First."**

Remember:

- **Target an intermediate honor.**
- **Preserve the higher honors for later.**
- **Requires accurate timing and entries.**
- **Often gains an extra trick over a simple finesse.**
- **Always compare with alternative percentage and safety plays.**

The Deep Finesse is an elegant declarer-play technique that allows declarer to maximize tricks by attacking an intermediate missing honor rather than the highest one. Although less common than the ordinary finesse, it is an important weapon in expert play, especially in notrump contracts and slams where every extra trick counts.
