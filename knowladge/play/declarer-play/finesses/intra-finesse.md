# Discovery Play Technique

## Overview

**Discovery Play** is a declarer-play technique in which declarer intentionally gathers information about the defenders' hands **before committing to a critical decision** such as a finesse, safety play, or endplay.

Rather than guessing immediately, declarer first "discovers" the defenders' distributions, honor locations, or remaining cards by cashing winners, observing signals, counting the hand, and analyzing the auction.

Discovery Play is not a separate card-play maneuver like a finesse or squeeze. Instead, it is a **method of information gathering** that leads to better decisions throughout the hand.

---

# Purpose

Discovery Play allows declarer to:

- Reduce guessing.
- Locate missing honors.
- Determine defenders' distributions.
- Improve percentage plays.
- Choose the correct finesse.
- Identify squeeze or endplay opportunities.

---

# Basic Principle

The guiding principle is:

> **Gather information before making an important decision.**

Whenever possible, postpone a guess until additional evidence has been collected.

---

# Sources of Information

Declarer can obtain information from:

- The auction.
- The opening lead.
- Defenders' signals.
- Defenders' discards.
- Suit distributions.
- Count of high-card points.
- Cards already played.

Every trick provides new information.

---

# When to Use

Discovery Play is especially useful when:

- Choosing between two finesses.
- Deciding whether to finesse or play for the drop.
- Planning a squeeze.
- Planning an endplay.
- Determining suit distribution.
- Selecting a safety play.

---

# Example 1 – Two Possible Finesses

Contract

```
3NT
```

Declarer can finesse:

- Hearts.
- Clubs.

Rather than guessing immediately, declarer first cashes side-suit winners.

The defenders' discards reveal that West is short in clubs.

Declarer now knows the club finesse is more likely to succeed.

---

# Example 2 – Finesse or Drop

Holding

```
Dummy
♠ AQJ

Declarer
♠ 432
```

Missing:

```
♠ K
```

Instead of taking the finesse immediately, declarer first cashes winners in other suits.

West shows an unexpected distribution during the play.

The new information helps determine whether the finesse or playing for the drop is more attractive.

---

# Example 3 – Locating a Missing Queen

Holding

```
Dummy
♦ AKJ

Declarer
♦ 654
```

Missing:

```
♦ Q
```

After counting the defenders' distributions from earlier tricks, declarer concludes that East is far more likely to hold the Queen and adjusts the line of play accordingly.

---

# Planning the Play

Before Trick One, ask:

- Which decisions can be postponed?
- What information do I still need?
- Which winners can I cash safely first?
- Can I discover defenders' distributions?
- Will additional information improve my percentage play?

---

# Counting the Hand

Discovery Play relies heavily on counting.

Declarer should count:

- Distribution.
- High-card points.
- Remaining trumps.
- Winners.
- Losers.
- Entries.

Accurate counting often eliminates the need for guessing.

---

# Using the Auction

The auction frequently provides clues.

For example:

- An opening bidder is more likely to hold missing honors.
- A preempt may reveal distribution.
- A takeout double often indicates shortness in opener's suit.
- A notrump opening limits high-card strength.

These clues should be combined with information gained during the play.

---

# Timing

Timing is important.

General guideline:

1. Gather information.
2. Delay critical guesses.
3. Make the key decision only after sufficient evidence is available.

Do not postpone so long that necessary entries disappear.

---

# Common Situations

Discovery Play frequently occurs:

- Before a finesse.
- Before a safety play.
- Before a squeeze.
- Before an endplay.
- During slam play.
- In notrump contracts.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Guessing too early.
- Ignoring information from the auction.
- Forgetting defenders' discards.
- Failing to count distribution.
- Losing entries while collecting information.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Conceal distribution.
- Falsecard when appropriate.
- Avoid revealing honor locations.
- Coordinate signals carefully.
- Preserve ambiguity whenever possible.

---

# Advantages

- Reduces unnecessary guesses.
- Improves percentage decisions.
- Supports accurate planning.
- Increases contract success.
- Essential for expert declarer play.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires patience.
- Depends on accurate counting.
- Information may still be incomplete.
- Delaying too long may create entry problems.

# Declarer's Checklist

Before making a critical decision:

- What information do I already have?
- What additional information can I obtain?
- Can I safely postpone the decision?
- Have I counted the defenders' distributions?
- Does the auction suggest where the missing honors are?

# Memory Aid

**Discovery Play = "Don't Guess Until You Must."**

Remember:

- **Gather information before committing to a key line of play.**
- **Use the auction, opening lead, signals, and discards to locate missing cards.**
- **Count distributions continuously throughout the hand.**
- **Delay finesses and other critical decisions when safe to do so.**
- **Every card played tells a story—use it.**

Discovery Play is one of the defining habits of expert declarers. Rather than relying on instinct or luck, skilled players gather as much information as possible before making important decisions. By combining careful counting, observation, and logical inference, declarer can transform many difficult guesses into high-percentage plays.
