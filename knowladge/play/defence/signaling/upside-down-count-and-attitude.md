# Upside-Down Count and Attitude (UDCA)

## Purpose

**Upside-Down Count and Attitude (UDCA)** is a defensive signaling method in which the meanings of **high** and **low** cards are reversed from Standard Carding.

In UDCA:

- **Low cards encourage.**
- **High cards discourage.**
- **Low-High shows an even count.**
- **High-Low shows an odd count.**

Many expert partnerships prefer UDCA because it allows defenders to encourage while preserving higher cards for later use.

---

# Signal Priority

Most partnerships use the following priority:

1. **Attitude**
2. **Count**
3. **Suit Preference**

Always agree with partner when each signal applies.

---

# UDCA Attitude Signals

## Purpose

Attitude tells partner whether to continue the suit.

### Low Card

Shows:

- Encouragement
- "Please continue."

Example:

Partner leads:

```
♠A
```

Dummy:

```
♠K74
```

Your holding:

```
♠Q52
```

Play:

```
♠2
```

Low card encourages.

---

### High Card

Shows:

- Discouragement
- "Please switch."

Example:

Holding:

```
♥J83
```

Play:

```
♥8
```

High card discourages.

---

# UDCA Count Signals

## Purpose

Count tells partner how many cards you hold in the suit.

### Low-High

Shows:

- Even number of cards

Example:

Holding:

```
♦84
```

Play:

```
4 then 8
```

Even count.

---

### High-Low

Shows:

- Odd number of cards

Example:

Holding:

```
♣742
```

Play:

```
7 then 2
```

Odd count.

---

# UDCA Suit Preference

Suit Preference is **not** reversed.

It remains:

| Card Played | Meaning |
|-------------|---------|
| High | Higher-ranking side suit |
| Low | Lower-ranking side suit |

Only **attitude** and **count** are upside down.

---

# Typical Situations

## Partner Leads an Ace

Usually:

Give **Attitude**.

Low = Encourage.

High = Discourage.

---

## Declarer Leads the Suit

Usually:

Give **Count**.

Low-High = Even.

High-Low = Odd.

---

## Giving Partner a Ruff

Usually:

Give **Suit Preference**.

High requests higher-ranking side suit.

Low requests lower-ranking side suit.

# Typical Defensive Priorities

1. Attitude.
2. Count.
3. Suit Preference.

Not every opportunity allows all three.

---

# Standard Opening Leads

UDCA is usually combined with standard opening leads such as:

| Lead | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Ace | AK or asks for attitude |
| King | AK or KQ (partnership agreement) |
| Queen | QJ |
| Jack | J10 or interior sequence |
| Fourth-best | Long suit |
| Top of doubleton | Doubleton |

Lead agreements are independent of UDCA.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Agree on:

- Ace asks for attitude or count.
- King lead style.
- Smith Echo.
- Lavinthal/McKenney.
- Suit-preference situations.
- Whether UDCA applies against both suit and notrump contracts.
- Opening lead style.

---

# Advantages

- Encouraging with a low card preserves higher cards.
- Frequently provides more flexible defense.
- Popular among expert partnerships.
- Compatible with modern lead agreements.
- Works well with Smith Echo and Lavinthal.

---

# Disadvantages

- Opposite of Standard Carding.
- Easy to forget with unfamiliar partners.
- Requires complete partnership agreement.

---

# Typical Defensive Flow

1. Partner leads.
2. Give attitude.
3. Later give count.
4. Finally give suit preference if appropriate.

Always follow partnership priorities.

---

# Common Partnership Style

Most expert partnerships play:

- **UDCA**
- Standard opening leads
- Lavinthal suit preference
- Smith Echo against notrump
- Count when declarer leads the suit

# System Summary

| Signal Type | Low Card | High Card |
|--------------|----------|-----------|
| Attitude | Encourage | Discourage |
| Count | First of even count | First of odd count |
| Suit Preference | Lower side suit | Higher side suit |

UDCA is one of the most popular defensive signaling methods among experienced bridge players. By reversing the meanings of attitude and count while keeping suit-preference signals unchanged, it provides efficient defensive communication and preserves higher cards for later play.
