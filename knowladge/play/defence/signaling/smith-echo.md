# Smith Echo

## Overview

The **Smith Echo** (also called the **Smith Peter**) is a defensive signaling convention used primarily in **Notrump contracts**. It allows defenders to communicate their attitude toward the **opening lead** rather than the suit currently being played.

Unlike standard attitude signals, the Smith Echo is given **while following suit to declarer's first lead**, not when the opening lead is played. This enables defenders to tell partner whether they like the opening lead and whether it should be continued when the opportunity arises.

The guiding principle is:

> **Encourage or discourage the opening lead by the card you play to declarer's first suit.**

---

# Purpose

Smith Echo helps defenders to:

- Indicate whether the opening lead should be continued.
- Improve defensive communication in Notrump contracts.
- Distinguish between attitude toward the opening lead and attitude toward the current suit.
- Coordinate defensive plans more accurately.

---

# When to Use

Smith Echo is most commonly used:

- In **Notrump contracts**.
- After partner has made the opening lead.
- On declarer's first lead from dummy or hand.
- Before ordinary count or suit-preference signals become important.

Some partnerships extend Smith Echo to suit contracts, but this is less common.

---

# Standard Smith Echo

The most common agreement is:

```
High-Low

Encourage continuation of the opening lead suit.
```

```
Low-High

Discourage continuation.
```

---

# Reverse Smith Echo

Some partnerships play the reverse method:

```
Low-High

Encourage.
```

```
High-Low

Discourage.
```

The partnership must agree which method is used.

---

# Example 1

Contract:

```
3NT
```

Opening lead:

```
♠4
```

Dummy wins the trick and leads a diamond.

Partner follows:

```
♦8 then ♦3
```

The high-low sequence is the Smith Echo.

Meaning:

```
Please continue spades when you regain the lead.
```

---

# Example 2

Opening lead:

```
♥5
```

Declarer wins and immediately attacks clubs.

Partner follows:

```
♣2 then ♣9
```

Low-high indicates:

```
I do not want a heart continuation.
```

(assuming Standard Smith Echo)

---

# Why Smith Echo?

Without Smith Echo:

- Partner may not know whether the opening lead was productive.
- Defensive communication may be delayed.

Smith Echo provides an early opportunity to communicate.

---

# Smith Echo vs. Ordinary Attitude

Ordinary attitude:

```
Signal about the suit currently being played.
```

Smith Echo:

```
Signal about the opening lead suit.
```

This distinction is the defining feature of the convention.

---

# Advantages

Smith Echo:

- Improves communication.
- Helps defenders return the correct suit.
- Works especially well in Notrump.
- Provides useful information early in the play.

---

# Limitations

Smith Echo:

- Uses one early signal that cannot simultaneously show count.
- Requires partnership agreement.
- May occasionally reveal information to declarer.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Signaling about the current suit instead of the opening lead.
- Forgetting that the signal applies only after declarer's first lead.
- Confusing Standard and Reverse Smith Echo.
- Using Smith Echo without partnership agreement.

---

# Comparison

| Method | Signals About |
|---------|---------------|
| Attitude | Current suit |
| Count | Number of cards |
| Suit Preference | Desired side suit |
| **Smith Echo** | Opening lead suit |

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should agree:

- Standard or Reverse Smith Echo.
- Whether it applies only in Notrump.
- Whether it is used in suit contracts.
- Signal priority when another signal is needed.

These agreements should appear on the partnership's convention card.

# Player's Checklist

Before using Smith Echo:

- Confirm the partnership agreement.
- Remember that the signal refers to the opening lead.
- Consider whether another signal has priority.
- Maintain consistent carding.

---

# Memory Guide

## Four Steps

1. Partner makes the opening lead.
2. Declarer wins and attacks another suit.
3. Give the agreed Smith Echo while following suit.
4. Partner interprets the signal when deciding whether to return the opening lead suit.

---

# Memory Aid

**Smith Echo = "Signal About the Opening Lead."**

Remember:

- **Smith Echo is primarily a Notrump defensive convention.**
- **The signal is given while following to declarer's first lead, not on the opening lead itself.**
- **Its purpose is to tell partner whether the opening lead should be continued.**
- **Partnerships must agree whether they play Standard or Reverse Smith Echo.**
- **Smith Echo improves defensive communication by separating attitude toward the opening lead from attitude toward the current suit.**

Smith Echo is one of the most effective modern defensive signaling methods for Notrump contracts. By allowing defenders to communicate their attitude toward the opening lead at the earliest practical opportunity, it enhances partnership cooperation and increases the chances of defeating the contract.
