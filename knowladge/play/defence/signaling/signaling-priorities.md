# Signaling Priorities

## Overview

**Signaling Priorities** define **which type of defensive signal has the highest priority in a particular situation**. Since a single played card can usually communicate only one message, defenders must know whether partner needs **Attitude**, **Count**, or **Suit Preference** information.

Without clear priorities, even expert partnerships can misinterpret signals and lose tricks. Most successful partnerships agree on a signaling hierarchy before play begins.

The guiding principle is:

> **Give partner the information that is most useful at this moment.**

---

# Purpose

Signaling Priorities help defenders to:

- Avoid conflicting signals.
- Improve partnership communication.
- Select the correct defensive continuation.
- Prevent misunderstandings.
- Coordinate complex defensive plans.

---

# The Three Basic Signal Types

## 1. Attitude Signal

Answers the question:

> **"Do I want this suit continued?"**

Typical agreement:

- High card = Encourage.
- Low card = Discourage.

Use when partner needs to know whether to continue the current suit.

---

## 2. Count Signal

Answers the question:

> **"How many cards do I have in this suit?"**

Typical agreement:

- Standard Count:
  - High-Low = Even.
  - Low-High = Odd.

- UDCA (Upside-Down Count):
  - High-Low = Odd.
  - Low-High = Even.

Use when distribution is more important than attitude.

---

## 3. Suit Preference Signal

Answers the question:

> **"Which side suit should partner play?"**

Typical agreement:

- High = Higher-ranking side suit.
- Low = Lower-ranking side suit.
- Middle = No preference.

Use when partner has a choice of return suits.

---

# General Priority Order

Most partnerships use the following order:

| Priority | Signal |
|-----------|--------|
| 1 | Attitude |
| 2 | Count |
| 3 | Suit Preference |

However, the actual priority depends on the position.

---

# Priority by Situation

## Situation 1 – Opening Lead

Usually:

**Attitude** has priority.

Partner wants to know:

- Should the suit be continued?

---

## Situation 2 – Declarer Is Establishing a Suit

Usually:

**Count** becomes more important.

Partner wants to know:

- How many cards remain?

---

## Situation 3 – Partner Is Cashing Winners

Usually:

**Suit Preference** becomes the priority.

Partner wants to know:

- Which suit should be played next?

---

## Situation 4 – Giving Partner a Ruff

Usually:

**Suit Preference** is the highest priority.

Tell partner which suit to return after the ruff.

---

## Situation 5 – Discarding

Priority depends on partnership agreements.

Common methods:

- Attitude discard.
- Suit Preference discard (e.g., Lavinthal).
- Count discard.

Partnership agreement is essential.

---

# Typical Priority Chart

| Situation | Highest Priority |
|------------|------------------|
| Opening lead | Attitude |
| Partner leads a suit | Attitude |
| Declarer running a long suit | Count |
| Cashing winners | Suit Preference |
| Giving a ruff | Suit Preference |
| Endgame defense | Suit Preference |
| Squeeze defense | Suit Preference |

---

# Example 1 – Attitude

West leads:

```
♠K
```

East holds:

```
♠Q74
```

East plays:

```
♠7
```

High encourages.

West continues spades.

---

# Example 2 – Count

Declarer establishes diamonds.

Partner needs to know:

- How many diamonds remain?

East gives a count signal.

---

# Example 3 – Suit Preference

West cashes the ♥A.

East plays:

```
♥9
```

The high card requests the higher-ranking side suit.

West switches accordingly.

---

# Planning the Defense

Before signaling, ask:

- What decision will partner face next?
- Does partner need attitude?
- Does partner need count?
- Does partner need a suit recommendation?
- Which signal has priority here?

---

# Common Mistakes

- Giving count when partner needs attitude.
- Giving Suit Preference too early.
- Ignoring partnership agreements.
- Sending conflicting messages.
- Forgetting the context of the hand.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Before playing together, partners should agree on:

- Standard or UDCA count.
- Attitude style.
- Suit Preference methods.
- Lavinthal usage.
- Smith Echo usage.
- Discard priorities.
- Trump signaling agreements.

---

# Advantages

- Eliminates ambiguity.
- Improves defensive cooperation.
- Produces better switches.
- Reduces misunderstandings.
- Essential for expert partnerships.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires partnership discipline.
- Situations may overlap.
- Some spot-card combinations limit signaling.
- Priorities vary between partnerships.

---

# Related Techniques

| Technique | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|
| Attitude Signals | Encourage or discourage a suit |
| Count Signals | Show distribution |
| Suit Preference | Recommend a side suit |
| Lavinthal | Suit Preference through discards |
| Smith Echo | Attitude toward the opening lead |
| False Carding | Deceive declarer while communicating legally |

---

# Defender's Checklist

Before signaling:

- What information does partner need most?
- Which signal has priority?
- Can my chosen card communicate clearly?
- Will partner recognize the priority?
- Does the signal fit our partnership agreements?

---

# Memory Guide

## Four Questions

Before every signal, ask:

1. Does partner need attitude?
2. Does partner need count?
3. Does partner need Suit Preference?
4. Which answer helps partner the most?

# Typical Decision Process

```
Partner Wins a Trick
↓

What Decision Must Partner Make?

↓

Need Attitude?
↓

Yes → Give Attitude

No
↓

Need Count?
↓

Yes → Give Count

No
↓

Give Suit Preference
```

---

# Memory Aid

**Signaling Priorities = "Tell Partner What Matters Most."**

Remember:

- **One card should communicate one clear message.**
- **The correct signal depends on the situation, not just the carding system.**
- **Attitude usually comes first, Count second, and Suit Preference third—but context can change the priority.**
- **Partnership agreements are essential for consistent interpretation.**
- **Ask yourself what decision partner is about to make, and provide the information that best helps with that decision.**

Signaling Priorities are the foundation of effective defensive communication. Even the best signaling methods fail if defenders send the wrong type of message at the wrong time. By understanding when Attitude, Count, or Suit Preference has priority, partnerships can defend accurately, avoid misunderstandings, and maximize their chances of defeating the contract.
