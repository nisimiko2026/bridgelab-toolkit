# Lavinthal (McKenney) Suit Preference Signals

## Purpose

The **Lavinthal** (also called **McKenney**) convention is a **Suit Preference Signal** used by defenders to tell partner **which side suit to lead or continue**.

Unlike **Attitude** or **Count** signals, Lavinthal does **not** express encouragement or the number of cards held. Instead, it asks partner to switch to either the higher-ranking or lower-ranking of the remaining suits.

Lavinthal is one of the most widely played defensive signaling methods in modern bridge.

---

# When It Applies

Lavinthal is used when:

- Attitude is no longer relevant.
- Count is no longer useful.
- Defender is discarding.
- Defender is giving partner a ruff.
- Defender knows partner must switch suits.

Typical situations include:

- Declarer is running a long suit.
- Partner wins a trick and must choose a new lead.
- A defender is forced to discard.

---

# Basic Principle

When a defender plays a card in one suit:

- A **high card** requests the **higher-ranking** of the two remaining side suits.
- A **low card** requests the **lower-ranking** of the two remaining side suits.

The suit being played and the trump suit are **ignored**.

---

# Determining the Remaining Suits

Only the two side suits **other than**:

- The suit currently being played.
- The trump suit (in suit contracts).

are considered.

---

# Example 1 – Suit Contract

Trump = ♠

Declarer leads:

```
♥
```

Remaining side suits are:

- ♣
- ♦

Since:

```
♦ ranks higher than ♣
```

Signal:

| Card Played | Requests |
|--------------|----------|
| High Heart | Diamonds |
| Low Heart | Clubs |

---

Declarer leads:

```

```

- ♣

- ♠

Usually the signal concerns the two realistic continuation suits.

Most partnerships apply Lavinthal only when partner clearly has a choice.

# Typical Uses

## Discarding

Partner leads:

```
♠A
```

Dummy wins later and runs hearts.

You discard:

```
♣9
```

High club asks partner:

Lead **Spades** (higher remaining suit).

---

## Giving a Ruff

Partner cashes:

```
♦A
```

You know partner will continue diamonds.

You play:

```
♦9
```

High diamond requests the higher remaining side suit.

---

## Returning Partner's Lead

Partner leads:

```
♣A
```

You encourage.

Later partner gains the lead.

Your signal has already indicated which suit to switch to.

# Common Defensive Situations

Lavinthal is frequently used:

- On discards.
- When giving partner a ruff.
- When declarer has established the current suit.
- After attitude and count are already known.
- Whenever partner obviously needs help choosing a switch.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Agree on:

- Whether Lavinthal applies to all discards.
- Whether Smith Echo takes precedence.
- Whether attitude overrides suit preference.
- Whether count overrides suit preference.
- Use against notrump contracts.
- Priority of signals.

---

# Signal Priority

Most expert partnerships use:

1. Attitude
2. Count
3. Suit Preference (Lavinthal)

Suit Preference is used only when attitude and count are unnecessary.

---

# Advantages

- Gives partner precise lead guidance.
- Extremely useful during discards.
- Helps locate killing switches.
- Widely played by expert partnerships.
- Easy to combine with Standard or UDCA carding.

---

# Disadvantages

- Not available on every trick.
- Requires partnership judgment.
- Declarer may occasionally benefit from the information.

---

# Comparison

| Signal | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| Attitude | Continue or switch current suit |
| Count | Number of cards held |
| Lavinthal | Which side suit to lead |
| Smith Echo | Attitude about partner's opening lead in notrump |

---

# Common Partnership Style

Most expert partnerships play:

- Standard or UDCA attitude.
- Standard or UDCA count.
- Lavinthal suit preference.
- Smith Echo versus notrump.

Lavinthal is generally used only when attitude and count are no longer important.

# Memory Aid

**Lavinthal = "Tell partner where to switch."**

Remember:

- Ignore the suit being played.
- Ignore trumps.
- Look only at the **remaining side suits**.
- **High = Higher remaining suit**
- **Low = Lower remaining suit**

Lavinthal (McKenney) Suit Preference Signals are among the most popular defensive signaling methods in bridge. Combined with **Attitude**, **Count**, and **Smith Echo**, they provide a complete and highly effective defensive communication system.
