# Attitude Signals

## Overview

**Attitude Signals** are the most fundamental defensive signals in bridge. They tell partner whether you **want the current suit continued** or **prefer a switch**. Unlike count signals, which describe the number of cards you hold, attitude signals express your opinion about the suit being played.

Attitude is usually the first signal exchanged between defenders and forms the foundation of effective defensive communication.

The guiding principle is:

> **Tell partner whether to continue the suit.**

---

# Purpose

Attitude signals help defenders to:

- Encourage continuation of a suit.
- Discourage continuation.
- Locate missing honors.
- Coordinate the defense.
- Establish defensive tricks efficiently.

---

# When to Use

Attitude signals are most commonly used:

- When partner leads a suit.
- When partner wins a trick and may continue the suit.
- On the opening lead.
- Whenever partner needs to know whether the suit is promising.

Attitude generally has the highest priority unless partnership agreements specify otherwise.

---

# Standard Attitude

The most common agreement is:

```
High Card = Encourage

Low Card = Discourage
```

Example:

Partner leads:

```
♠K
```

You hold:

```
♠Q 7 4
```

Playing the **7** encourages continuation because you have the Queen.

---

# Upside-Down Attitude (UDCA)

Some partnerships use:

```
Low Card = Encourage

High Card = Discourage
```

This method is called **Upside-Down Count and Attitude (UDCA)**.

The partnership must agree which method is being used.

---

# What Does "Encourage" Mean?

Encouraging partner usually means:

- You hold an honor.
- You believe the suit can produce tricks.
- You would like partner to continue the suit when appropriate.

It does **not** guarantee possession of a specific honor.

---

# What Does "Discourage" Mean?

Discouraging partner usually means:

- You have little or no value in the suit.
- Another suit may be more productive.
- Continuing the suit is unlikely to help the defense.

---

# Example 1 – Encouraging

Contract:

```
4♠
```

Opening lead:

```
♦K
```

Dummy:

```
♦Q 8 5
```

You hold:

```
♦J 7 2
```

Playing the **7** (standard attitude) encourages partner to continue diamonds because your Jack can become a winner.

---

# Example 2 – Discouraging

Partner leads:

```
♣A
```

You hold:

```
♣6 4 2
```

Playing the **2** discourages continuation because the suit is unlikely to produce additional tricks.

---

# Attitude vs. Count

Attitude answers:

> **Should partner continue this suit?**

Count answers:

> **How many cards do I hold in this suit?**

These are different messages and should not be confused.

---

# Attitude vs. Suit Preference

Attitude:

```
Continue this suit?
```

Suit Preference:

```
If you switch, which suit should you choose?
```

Suit preference usually becomes relevant only after attitude and count are no longer important.

---

# Signal Priority

A common priority is:

1. Attitude
2. Count
3. Suit Preference

Partnership agreements should clearly define these priorities.

---

# Advantages

Attitude signals:

- Improve partnership communication.
- Help defenders establish suits.
- Prevent unnecessary suit continuations.
- Support accurate defensive planning.

---

# Limitations

Attitude signals:

- Cannot communicate count at the same time.
- May occasionally reveal information to declarer.
- Must be interpreted in context.

The auction, dummy, and declarer's play should always be considered.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Giving the wrong signal.
- Forgetting the partnership agreement.
- Confusing Standard and UDCA.
- Signaling automatically without considering the entire hand.
- Ignoring signal priorities.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should agree on:

- Standard or Upside-Down attitude.
- Signal priorities.
- Whether exceptions apply in special situations.
- How attitude interacts with Smith Echo and other signaling methods.

These agreements should be recorded on the convention card.

# Player's Checklist

When partner leads a suit:

- Decide whether continuation is desirable.
- Follow the partnership agreement.
- Give the correct attitude signal.
- Consider whether another signal has priority.
- Continue counting the entire hand.

---

# Memory Guide

## Four Steps

1. Partner leads a suit.
2. Decide whether you want it continued.
3. Give the agreed attitude signal.
4. Continue communicating as the hand develops.

---

---

# Memory Aid

**Attitude Signals = "Continue or Switch?"**

Remember:

- **Attitude is the defenders' first and most common message.**
- **Its purpose is to tell partner whether continuing the current suit is likely to be productive.**
- **Partnerships must agree whether they use Standard or Upside-Down (UDCA) attitude.**
- **Attitude should always be interpreted together with the auction, dummy, and declarer's play.**
- **Effective attitude signaling is the foundation of successful defensive communication.**

Attitude Signals are the cornerstone of defensive partnership play. By clearly expressing whether a suit should be continued, defenders can coordinate their efforts, establish tricks efficiently, and maximize their chances of defeating the contract.
