# Italian Discard Convention

## Purpose

The **Italian Discard Convention** is a defensive signaling method used when a defender is **unable to follow suit** and must discard. Rather than simply throwing an unwanted card, the discard conveys information about another suit.

Italian Discards help defenders communicate:

- Which suit they prefer partner to lead.
- Which suits they do not want led.
- Where they hold strength.
- Defensive priorities during the play.

The convention is most commonly used in **Notrump contracts**, but many partnerships also use it in suit contracts.

---

# Basic Principle

The rank of the discard determines the message.

| Card Discarded | Meaning |
|----------------|---------|
| **Low (2–4)** | Encourages the discarded suit |
| **Middle (5–7)** | Neutral / No message |
| **High (8–A)** | Discourages the discarded suit and requests another suit |

---

# High Card Discards

A **high discard** says:

> "I do **not** want this suit led."

It also gives suit-preference information:

- **Odd high card** → Requests the **higher-ranking** of the remaining suits.
- **Even high card** → Requests the **lower-ranking** of the remaining suits.

This combines a discouraging signal with Lavinthal-style suit preference.

---

# Low Card Discards

A low discard encourages the discarded suit.

Example:

```
Discard:
♣3
```

Meaning:

> "If possible, lead clubs."

---

# Middle Card Discards

Middle cards are generally:

- Neutral.
- No strong preference.
- Used when no useful message is available.

Some partnerships use middle cards to preserve future signaling opportunities.

---

# Suit Preference with High Discards

Assume:

- Hearts are being played.
- Trumps are spades.

The remaining side suits are:

- Clubs
- Diamonds

Examples:

### Discard ♦9 (odd high)

Requests:

- The **higher-ranking** remaining suit.

### Discard ♦8 (even high)

Requests:

- The **lower-ranking** remaining suit.

Partnership agreement is essential regarding suit-ranking interpretation.

---

# Typical Situations

Italian Discards are commonly used:

- When declarer runs a long suit.
- During forced discards.
- When partner has the lead later.
- Against Notrump contracts.
- When attitude and count are no longer relevant.

---

# Example 1

Contract:

```
3NT
```

Declarer runs hearts.

You discard:

```
♣2
```

Meaning:

Encourages clubs.

---

# Example 2

Declarer continues hearts.

You discard:

```
♦9
```

Meaning:

Do **not** lead diamonds.

Request the higher-ranking remaining suit.

---

# Example 3

Declarer runs spades.

You discard:

```
♥6
```

Meaning:

Neutral.

No specific message.

---

# Hand Example

You hold:

```
♠ 84
♥ 73
♦ AQ5
♣ J962
```

Declarer cashes several heart winners.

You discard:

```
♣2
```

Meaning:

Please lead clubs if possible.

---

# Priority of Signals

Most partnerships use:

1. Attitude.
2. Count.
3. Suit Preference.

Italian Discards are generally used only when ordinary attitude or count signals are no longer useful.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Agree on:

- Whether Italian Discards apply in both suit and notrump contracts.
- Exact definition of low, middle, and high cards.
- Whether high-card discards include Lavinthal-style suit preference.
- Priority versus Smith Echo.
- Priority versus ordinary suit-preference signals.
- Treatment of honor discards.

---

# Advantages

- Simple and logical.
- Conveys useful information on every discard.
- Combines attitude and suit preference.
- Popular in European bridge.
- Complements Standard Signals or UDCA.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires careful partnership agreement.
- Gives declarer some information.
- Less common than pure Lavinthal discards in some regions.

---

# Comparison

| Discard Method | Meaning |
|----------------|---------|
| Standard | Usually attitude only |
| Lavinthal | Suit preference only |
| Italian | Low = encourage, High = discourage + suit preference, Middle = neutral |

---

# Common Partnership Style

Most expert partnerships using Italian Discards play:

- **Low cards (2–4)** = Encourage the discarded suit.
- **Middle cards (5–7)** = Neutral.
- **High cards (8–A)** = Discourage the discarded suit.
- High odd/even cards also indicate suit preference between the remaining suits.

---

# Memory Guide

| Card Range | Meaning |
|------------|---------|
| 2–4 | Encourage |
| 5–7 | Neutral |
| 8–A | Discourage |

---

# Memory Aid

**Italian Discards = "Low Like, Middle Maybe, High Hate."**

Remember:

- **Low (2–4)** → "Lead this suit."
- **Middle (5–7)** → "No message."
- **High (8–A)** → "Don't lead this suit."

Many partnerships also use **high odd/even discards** to indicate **which of the other suits** they prefer partner to lead.

Italian Discards are a practical and efficient defensive signaling method that combines attitude and suit-preference information, making them particularly effective when declarer is running a long suit and defenders have only discard opportunities to communicate.
