# Defensive Planning

## Overview

**Defensive Planning** is the process by which defenders develop and execute a coordinated strategy to defeat the contract. Rather than playing each trick independently, successful defenders analyze the auction, opening lead, dummy, and subsequent play to create a long-term defensive plan.

Expert defenders constantly ask:

> **"How can our partnership take enough tricks to defeat this contract?"**

Good defensive planning combines counting, signaling, communication, and tactical card play.

---

# Purpose

Defensive Planning allows defenders to:

- Develop a coordinated strategy.
- Maximize partnership communication.
- Identify declarer's plan.
- Select the best defensive attack.
- Prevent squeezes, endplays, and crossruffs.
- Defeat contracts consistently.

---

# The Planning Process

Effective defense follows these basic steps:

1. Analyze the auction.
2. Choose the correct opening lead.
3. Evaluate dummy.
4. Count declarer's hand.
5. Count defensive winners.
6. Identify declarer's likely plan.
7. Choose the best defense.
8. Reevaluate after every trick.

---

# Step 1 – Analyze the Auction

The auction provides valuable information.

Look for:

- Declarer's strength.
- Distribution.
- Trump fit.
- Side suits.
- Partner's bidding.
- Lead-directing doubles.

Ask:

- Which suits are likely to be strong?
- Which suits are weak?
- What contract is declarer trying to make?

---

# Step 2 – Select the Opening Lead

Choose the lead that best supports the defensive plan.

Typical options:

- Partner's suit.
- Longest and strongest suit (against Notrump).
- Trump lead.
- Singleton lead.
- Top of a sequence.

The opening lead often determines the tempo of the defense.

---

# Step 3 – Evaluate Dummy

When dummy appears, immediately examine:

- High-card strength.
- Suit lengths.
- Shortages.
- Entries.
- Trump support.
- Potential winners.

Then revise the original defensive plan.

---

# Step 4 – Count Declarer's Hand

Estimate:

- Distribution.
- High-card points.
- Remaining trumps.
- Likely entries.

Continue updating this information throughout the hand.

---

# Step 5 – Count Defensive Winners

Determine:

- Immediate winners.
- Potential winners.
- Promotable tricks.
- Trump tricks.

Ask:

- How many tricks do we already have?
- Where will additional tricks come from?

---

# Step 6 – Identify Declarer's Plan

Look for clues that declarer intends to:

- Draw trumps.
- Ruff losers.
- Establish a long suit.
- Take a finesse.
- Execute a squeeze.
- Perform an endplay.
- Cross Ruff.
- Dummy Reversal.

Recognizing declarer's objective allows defenders to interfere.

---

# Step 7 – Choose the Best Defense

Possible strategies include:

## Active Defense

Attack immediately.

Best when:

- Declarer has many winners.
- Tempo is critical.

---

## Passive Defense

Avoid giving declarer extra tricks.

Best when:

- Declarer must guess correctly.
- Defensive tricks already exist.

---

## Forcing Defense

Force declarer to ruff repeatedly.

Often creates:

- Trump Promotion.
- Uppercut opportunities.

---

## Trump Attack

Lead trumps to reduce ruffing.

Especially effective against:

- Cross Ruff.
- Dummy Reversal.

---

# Step 8 – Reevaluate After Every Trick

Every trick provides information.

Ask:

- What have we learned?
- Has declarer's plan changed?
- Should we switch suits?
- Are communications intact?
- Is a squeeze or endplay developing?

Expert defenders constantly update their plan.

---

# Example 1 – Notrump Defense

Contract

```
3NT
```

Opening lead establishes four defensive winners.

Declarer begins establishing clubs.

Defense immediately switches to cash its long suit before declarer gains control.

---

# Example 2 – Suit Contract

Contract

```
4♠
```

Dummy has a singleton diamond.

Defense immediately recognizes a possible Cross Ruff.

A trump lead prevents repeated ruffs.

---

# Example 3 – Forcing Defense

Contract

```
4♥
```

Declarer has weak trumps.

Defense repeatedly forces declarer to ruff side suits.

Eventually:

Trump Promotion creates the setting trick.

---

# Communication

Successful defense requires partnership cooperation.

Use:

- Attitude signals.
- Count signals.
- Suit-preference signals.
- Smith Echo.
- Lavinthal.

Clear communication improves defensive planning.

---

# Timing

Timing is critical.

Examples:

- Cash winners before declarer establishes theirs.
- Switch before communication is lost.
- Force declarer before trumps disappear.
- Lead trumps before declarer begins a Cross Ruff.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Defending one trick at a time.
- Ignoring declarer's plan.
- Failing to count distribution.
- Breaking partnership communication.
- Continuing the wrong suit.
- Missing the correct switch.

---

# Defensive Priorities

Generally:

1. Take immediate winners.
2. Establish future winners.
3. Prevent declarer's plan.
4. Preserve communication.
5. Count every suit.

---

# Advantages

- Improves partnership cooperation.
- Produces better defensive switches.
- Prevents declarer's advanced techniques.
- Maximizes defensive tricks.
- Leads to more defeated contracts.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires concentration.
- Accurate counting is essential.
- New information may require changing the plan.
- Poor communication reduces effectiveness.

# Memory Aid

**Defensive Planning = "Defend the Hand, Not Just the Trick."**

Remember:

- **Use the auction and opening lead to build your plan.**
- **Count declarer's distribution continuously.**
- **Recognize declarer's objective early.**
- **Coordinate with partner through signals and carding.**
- **Reevaluate your strategy after every trick.**

Defensive Planning is the foundation of expert defense. The strongest defenders do not simply react to declarer's play—they anticipate it. By combining counting, communication, signaling, and strategic thinking, a defensive partnership can consistently find the winning line and defeat contracts that appear to be unbeatable.
