# Defensive Communication

## Overview

Successful defense is built on **partnership communication**. Unlike declarer, who can see both hands, defenders each see only 13 cards and must cooperate using only the legal methods allowed by the Laws of Bridge.

Defensive communication consists of:

- Opening leads
- Carding methods
- Attitude signals
- Count signals
- Suit preference signals
- Partnership agreements
- Logical inferences

The purpose is not to exchange secret information, but to communicate legally through the cards played.

The guiding principle is:

> **Two defenders become one partnership through legal communication.**

---

# Purpose

Defensive communication helps partners to:

- Coordinate the defense.
- Identify the correct continuation.
- Locate honors.
- Determine suit lengths.
- Choose the correct switch.
- Defeat contracts through cooperation.

---

# Sources of Defensive Communication

Legal communication comes from:

- The opening lead.
- The order in which cards are played.
- Attitude signals.
- Count signals.
- Suit preference signals.
- Partnership agreements.
- The auction.
- Declarer's play.

Everything else is unauthorized information and must be ignored.

---

# Opening Lead

The opening lead is often the defenders' first message.

It may communicate:

- Suit length.
- Honor sequence.
- Interior sequence.
- Partnership lead agreements.

Example:

```
♠K
```

may indicate different holdings depending on partnership agreements.

---

# Attitude Signals

Attitude answers one question:

> **Should partner continue this suit?**

Typical agreement:

```
High = Encourage

Low = Discourage
```

Attitude is usually the first priority when partner leads a suit.

---

# Count Signals

Count answers the question:

> **How many cards do you hold?**

Standard agreement:

```
High-Low = Even

Low-High = Odd
```

Count becomes important when partner needs to know the distribution of a suit.

---

# Suit Preference Signals

Suit preference answers:

> **If you switch, which side suit should you choose?**

Typical agreement:

```
High card

Higher-ranking suit.

Low card

Lower-ranking suit.
```

Suit preference is commonly used when attitude and count are no longer relevant.

---

# Smith Echo

Smith Echo is a special agreement used primarily in Notrump contracts.

Rather than signaling about the current suit, it communicates whether partner should continue the **opening lead suit** when the opportunity arises.

---

# Opening Lead Agreements

Partnerships should agree on:

- Fourth-best leads.
- Honor sequence leads.
- Interior sequence leads.
- Ace and King lead agreements.
- Journalist or Rusinow leads (if used).

Clear agreements prevent misunderstandings.

---

# Communication Priorities

Not every signal can be given at the same time.

A common priority is:

1. Attitude
2. Count
3. Suit Preference

Partnerships should clearly define these priorities.

---

# Communication Through Tempo

Players should strive for a consistent tempo.

Changes in tempo:

- Are **not** legal communication.
- Create Unauthorized Information (UI).
- Must be ignored by partner.

Only the played cards may communicate.

---

# Communication Through Inference

Defenders also communicate indirectly through:

- The auction.
- Dummy.
- Declarer's line of play.
- Previously revealed information.

Inference complements, but never replaces, agreed signaling methods.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Before play, partners should discuss:

- Opening leads.
- Attitude signals.
- Count signals.
- Suit preference.
- Smith Echo.
- False carding philosophy.
- False signaling philosophy.

These agreements should be recorded on the convention card when appropriate.

---

# Example

Contract:

```
4♠
```

Partner leads:

```
♦K
```

You:

- Encourage with your attitude signal.
- Later give count.
- Finally use suit preference to suggest a club switch.

Three different messages are communicated during the same hand.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Giving the wrong priority signal.
- Mixing signaling methods.
- Forgetting partnership agreements.
- Revealing unauthorized information.
- Using false signals when partner genuinely needs information.

---

# Best Practices

Strong defensive partnerships:

- Agree on every signaling method.
- Keep communication consistent.
- Signal only when useful.
- Avoid unnecessary information.
- Place partnership understanding above deceptive play.

---

# Advantages

Effective communication:

- Improves opening lead accuracy.
- Increases defensive coordination.
- Helps locate missing honors.
- Produces better defensive switches.
- Defeats more contracts.

# Player's Checklist

During every hand:

- Interpret the opening lead.
- Give the highest-priority signal.
- Follow partnership agreements.
- Observe declarer's play.
- Count the hand.
- Ignore unauthorized information.
- Cooperate with partner throughout the defense.

# Memory Guide

1. Opening Lead
2. Attitude
3. Count
4. Suit Preference
5. Smith Echo
6. Inference

Together they form the complete language of defensive communication.

# Memory Aid

**Defensive Communication = "The Language of the Defenders."**

Remember:

- **Every card can carry information when played according to partnership agreements.**
- **Opening leads, signals, and inferences work together to create a complete defensive picture.**
- **Only authorized methods of communication are legal; tempo, gestures, and mannerisms must never influence partner's decisions.**
- **Successful defense depends on partnership cooperation, not individual brilliance.**
- **The strongest defensive partnerships communicate clearly, consistently, and ethically throughout every hand.**

Defensive Communication is the foundation of expert defense. By combining agreed opening leads, signaling methods, logical inferences, and ethical partnership practices, two defenders can function as a coordinated team, overcoming the advantage declarer gains from seeing both declarer hands. Mastering this language is one of the most important steps toward becoming an accomplished bridge defender.
