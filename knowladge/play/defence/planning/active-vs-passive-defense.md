# Active vs. Passive Defense

## Overview

One of the most important decisions defenders make is whether to adopt an **Active Defense** or a **Passive Defense**. This choice often determines whether the defense defeats the contract or gives declarer the tempo needed to succeed.

- **Active Defense** attempts to establish defensive tricks immediately by attacking declarer's weaknesses.
- **Passive Defense** avoids giving declarer extra tricks and waits for declarer to make the first move.

Expert defenders constantly evaluate which style is appropriate based on the auction, dummy, and the opening lead.

---

# Active Defense

## Purpose

An **Active Defense** attempts to:

- Establish defensive winners quickly.
- Attack declarer's weak suits.
- Force declarer to make difficult decisions.
- Prevent declarer from setting up long suits.
- Defeat the contract before declarer can develop extra tricks.

---

## Typical Situations

Choose Active Defense when:

- Declarer has many immediate winners.
- The defense must establish tricks quickly.
- You have an attractive attacking lead.
- Dummy appears weak.
- Declarer is likely to discard losers on long suits.
- Time is working against the defense.

---

## Typical Active Leads

Examples include:

- Leading an unsupported ace (by partnership agreement).
- Attacking an unbid suit.
- Leading through declarer's announced strength.
- Switching aggressively after trick one.
- Leading partner's bid suit.

---

## Example

Auction

```
West      North      East      South
1NT       Pass       3NT       All Pass
```

You hold:

```
♠ KJ74
♥ 82
♦ AQ64
♣ 743
```

Leading the **♦4** (fourth-best) actively attacks your strongest suit before declarer can establish theirs.

---

# Passive Defense

## Purpose

A **Passive Defense** attempts to:

- Avoid giving declarer free tricks.
- Prevent establishing declarer's honors.
- Wait for declarer to guess correctly.
- Force declarer to solve difficult problems independently.

---

## Typical Situations

Choose Passive Defense when:

- Declarer lacks sufficient winners.
- The contract depends on successful finesses.
- Your hand contains dangerous unsupported honors.
- Any aggressive lead might establish declarer's suit.
- The defense already has enough tricks if it avoids mistakes.

---

## Typical Passive Leads

Examples include:

- Safe trump leads.
- Leading from weak suits.
- Avoiding unsupported honors.
- Avoiding new suits late in the play.
- Returning partner's safe suit.

---

## Example

Contract

```
4♠
```

You hold:

```
♠ 3
♥ K84
♦ Q72
♣ KJ943
```

A passive trump lead may prevent declarer from obtaining useful ruffs.

---

# Comparing the Two Styles

| Active Defense | Passive Defense |
|----------------|-----------------|
| Attack immediately | Wait for declarer |
| Develop defensive winners | Avoid giving tricks away |
| Higher risk | Lower risk |
| Often best against Notrump | Often best against Suit contracts |
| Creates pressure | Applies patience |

---

# Factors to Consider

Before choosing a defensive style, consider:

- The auction.
- Vulnerability.
- Contract level.
- Dummy's strength.
- Declarer's likely distribution.
- Your partner's bidding.
- Your own holding.

---

# Against Notrump Contracts

Active Defense is usually preferred because:

- Declarer has time to establish long suits.
- Defenders must establish their own long suit first.
- Tempo is critical.

Typical strategy:

- Attack the longest and strongest suit.
- Establish winners before declarer does.

---

# Against Suit Contracts

Passive Defense is often preferable because:

- Aggressive leads may establish declarer's honors.
- Declarer often has ruffing opportunities.
- Safe defense may force declarer into difficult guesses.

Typical strategy:

- Avoid leading away from unsupported honors.
- Consider a trump lead to reduce ruffing potential.

---

# Example 1 – Active Defense

Contract

```
3NT
```

You hold:

```
♠ AQ85
♥ 74
♦ J965
♣ KQ2
```

Lead:

```
♠5
```

Attack the long suit immediately.

---

# Example 2 – Passive Defense

Contract

```
4♥
```

You hold:

```
♠ KJ4
♥ 83
♦ K964
♣ J763
```

Rather than leading a diamond away from the king, choose a passive club or trump lead, depending on the auction.

---

# Defensive Planning

After seeing dummy:

Ask:

- Does declarer have enough winners already?
- Must I attack immediately?
- Can declarer benefit from my lead?
- Should I wait for declarer to commit?

Reevaluate after every trick.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Active Defense

- Attacking the wrong suit.
- Leading away from unsupported honors unnecessarily.
- Establishing declarer's winners.
- Ignoring partner's bidding.

---

## Passive Defense

- Waiting too long.
- Missing the only chance to establish winners.
- Giving declarer unlimited time.
- Failing to attack before declarer's long suit is established.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Discuss:

- Opening lead style.
- Signals.
- Carding methods.
- Suit preference.
- Attitude priorities.
- When to switch from passive to active defense.

---

# Advantages

## Active Defense

- May defeat contracts immediately.
- Establishes winners early.
- Creates pressure on declarer.

---

## Passive Defense

- Reduces gifts to declarer.
- Forces declarer to solve the hand.
- Avoids unnecessary risk.

---

# Disadvantages

## Active Defense

- Can establish declarer's honors.
- Higher risk.

---

## Passive Defense

- May be too slow.
- Allows declarer to develop extra winners.

# Defender's Checklist

Before choosing your defense:

- Is declarer racing to establish winners?
- Do we need tricks immediately?
- Is there a safe lead?
- Which suit should be attacked?
- Will patience gain more than aggression?

---

# Memory Guide

## Active Defense

- Attack.
- Develop winners.
- Apply pressure.

---

## Passive Defense

- Wait.
- Avoid gifts.
- Let declarer guess.

---

# Comparison

| Situation | Preferred Style |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Notrump contracts | Usually Active |
| Suit contracts | Often Passive |
| Declarer already has many winners | Active |
| Declarer must guess correctly | Passive |

---

# Memory Aid

**Active Defense = "Attack Before It's Too Late."**

- Develop tricks immediately.
- Pressure declarer.
- Race to establish winners.

**Passive Defense = "Don't Help Declarer."**

- Avoid risky leads.
- Force declarer to do the work.
- Wait for declarer's mistakes.

Successful defenders know **when to attack and when to wait**. The ability to switch between Active and Passive Defense based on the auction, dummy, and developing play is one of the defining skills of expert bridge defense.
