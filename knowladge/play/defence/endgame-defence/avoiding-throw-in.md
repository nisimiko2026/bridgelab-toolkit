# Avoiding the Throw-In

## Overview

A **throw-in** (or **endplay**) occurs when declarer deliberately gives a defender the lead at a moment when that defender has **no safe exit**. Forced to lead from an unfavorable holding, the defender concedes one or more extra tricks.

**Avoiding the throw-in** is the defensive skill of recognizing this danger early and taking preventive action before declarer can execute the endplay.

Strong defenders constantly ask:

> *"If declarer puts me on lead later, will I have a safe card to return?"*

If the answer is no, the defense should adjust its play before it is too late.

---

# Objectives

Avoiding a throw-in aims to:

- Prevent declarer from forcing a favorable lead.
- Preserve safe exit cards.
- Avoid leading away from honors.
- Eliminate declarer's endplay opportunities.
- Maintain defensive control in the endgame.

---

# Basic Principle

A defender who expects to lose the lead later should prepare for that moment in advance.

This may involve:

- cashing winners early,
- exiting safely while it is still possible,
- eliminating dangerous suits,
- preserving an otherwise unimportant low card,
- or changing the timing of the defense.

Planning ahead is the key.

---

# Recognizing a Possible Throw-In

Warning signs include:

- Declarer begins eliminating side suits.
- Dummy contains dangerous tenaces.
- One defender appears to be guarding multiple suits.
- Declarer repeatedly ducks tricks.
- The number of remaining exit cards is shrinking.

These are classic indicators that declarer may be preparing an endplay.

---

# Typical Throw-In Position

Suppose West holds:

```
♠ Kx
♥ Jx
```

Dummy has:

```
♠ AQ
♥ AQ
```

If declarer throws West in with a club, West must lead:

- a spade into AQ,
- or a heart into AQ.

Either exit gives declarer an extra trick.

The defense should recognize this possibility before the position develops.

---

# Preserving Safe Exit Cards

A safe exit is any card that:

- does not lead away from an honor,
- does not establish declarer's suit,
- does not concede a finesse,
- does not give declarer a ruff-and-sluff.

Good defenders avoid using these cards unnecessarily.

---

# Cash Winners Before They Disappear

Sometimes the best defense is to take winners immediately.

If a defender delays cashing an ace, declarer may later throw that defender in, forcing the ace to become an entry that leads to disaster.

Cashing the winner first removes the danger.

---

# Exit While It Is Still Safe

Instead of waiting until the endgame, defenders often benefit from making a harmless exit early.

Examples include:

- returning declarer's suit,
- leading a completely safe suit,
- switching to trumps before the endplay develops.

A timely exit may eliminate declarer's entire plan.

---

# Avoid Becoming the Target

Declarer usually chooses the defender with the weakest exit cards.

To avoid becoming the target:

- retain flexible exit cards,
- avoid stripping every safe suit,
- keep communication with partner,
- recognize when declarer is eliminating suits.

Sometimes preserving a seemingly insignificant low card prevents an endplay.

---

# Avoid Opening New Suits

Late in the hand, opening a new suit often helps declarer.

Whenever possible:

- return an already established suit,
- avoid leading away from unsupported honors,
- force declarer to open critical suits personally.

Passive exits are usually safest.

---

# Recognizing Elimination Play

Many throw-ins begin with an elimination.

Declarer removes every safe exit by:

1. drawing trumps,
2. cashing side suits,
3. eliminating defenders' exit cards,
4. throwing a defender in.

Recognizing this sequence early allows defenders to interrupt it.

---

# Common Defensive Techniques

To avoid a throw-in, defenders may:

- cash side winners immediately,
- exit safely before the danger arises,
- preserve low cards in safe suits,
- avoid unnecessary switches,
- force declarer to open key suits,
- retain communication between defenders.

---

# Common Errors

## Using Every Safe Exit

Discarding or leading every harmless card leaves only dangerous exits.

---

## Delaying Winners

Holding back an immediate winner may give declarer the opportunity to create an endplay.

---

## Opening Declarer's Suit

Leading into declarer's tenace often gives away the contract.

---

## Ignoring Declarer's Plan

When declarer repeatedly strips side suits, defenders should immediately suspect an elimination and throw-in.

---

## Forgetting the Endgame

Many defenders focus only on the current trick rather than the final position.

Throw-ins are often prevented several tricks before they occur.

---

# Practical Questions

Before surrendering the lead, ask:

- If declarer wins now, can I be thrown in later?
- Do I still have a safe exit?
- Should I cash my winner immediately?
- Can I safely exit now rather than later?
- Is declarer eliminating suits?
- Which defender is most vulnerable to an endplay?

These questions often reveal the correct defense.

# Practical Tips

- Think several tricks ahead whenever declarer begins stripping suits.
- Preserve at least one safe exit whenever possible.
- Cash winners before they become liabilities.
- Return declarer's suit if it provides the safest exit.
- Avoid leading away from unsupported honors in the endgame.
- Watch declarer's discards—they often reveal an elimination plan.
- Remember that preventing an endplay usually starts long before the final few tricks.

---

# Key Takeaways

- A throw-in forces a defender to make a damaging lead.
- The best defense is to recognize the danger before the endgame develops.
- Preserve safe exit cards and avoid exhausting harmless leads.
- Cash winners and exit safely while the opportunity still exists.
- Anticipating declarer's endplay plan is an essential advanced defensive skill.
