# Safe Exit Cards

## Overview

A **Safe Exit Card** is a card that allows a player to **lose the lead without giving the opponents an advantage**. Safe exits are especially important in **endplays, elimination plays, defensive play, and endgame strategy**. By choosing the correct exit card, a player avoids leading into an opponent's tenace, conceding a ruff-and-discard, or providing an extra trick.

Conversely, **eliminating an opponent's safe exit cards** is often the first step in creating a successful **Throw-In (Endplay)** or **Strip Squeeze**.

The guiding principle is:

> **When you must lose the lead, choose a card that gives nothing away.**

---

# Purpose

Safe Exit Cards allow a player to:

- Lose the lead safely.
- Avoid giving declarer or defenders a free trick.
- Maintain defensive control.
- Prevent endplays.
- Preserve communication.
- Escape dangerous positions.

---

# Basic Principle

A safe exit card:

- Does not lead into an opponent's tenace.
- Does not concede a ruff-and-discard.
- Does not establish an opponent's suit.
- Does not give away an unnecessary trick.

A player with no safe exit is vulnerable to a **Throw-In**.

---

# Why Safe Exit Cards Matter

In many endgames, the battle is not over **who wins the next trick**, but **who is forced to lead next**.

If you have a safe exit, you can safely surrender the lead.

If you do not, your opponent may force you to:

- Lead into a finesse.
- Lead away from a guarded honor.
- Give a ruff-and-discard.
- Break a critical suit.

---

# Types of Safe Exit Cards

## 1. Passive Exit

Lead a suit that gives away nothing.

Example:

Lead from:

```
♣ 8 4
```

into:

```
Dummy
♣ A J 3
```

This is often safer than leading away from a king.

---

## 2. Trump Exit

In suit contracts, a trump return is often the safest exit because it rarely gives declarer an extra trick.

However, this is not always true if declarer is planning a **Trump Coup**.

---

## 3. Long Suit Exit

Returning an exhausted long suit may be completely safe because no honors remain.

---

## 4. Forced Exit

Sometimes every possible lead is dangerous.

The defender has **no safe exit**, making them vulnerable to an endplay.

---

# Example 1 – Safe Exit

Contract

```
3NT
```

West holds:

```
♣ 7
♦ K4
```

Leading the singleton club is safe.

Leading the diamond gives declarer a finesse.

---

# Example 2 – No Safe Exit

Contract

```
4♠
```

West holds:

```
♥ K3
♦ Q4
```

Declarer has stripped clubs and trumps.

Whichever suit West leads:

- Declarer gains a trick.

West has no safe exit.

---

# Example 3 – Creating a Throw-In

Declarer eliminates:

- Clubs.
- Diamonds.
- Trumps.

West is left with:

```
♥ KJ
```

Declarer throws West in.

West must lead hearts into declarer's tenace.

The lack of a safe exit gives declarer the contract.

---

# Planning the Play

Before playing to Trick One, ask:

- Which exits are safe?
- Which exits are dangerous?
- Can I eliminate the opponents' safe exits?
- Can I preserve one of my own?
- Which defender is vulnerable?

---

# Eliminating Safe Exit Cards

Declarer often removes defenders' safe exits by:

- Cashing side-suit winners.
- Ruffing side suits.
- Drawing trumps.
- Eliminating entire suits.

Only dangerous suits remain.

The defender can then be thrown in successfully.

---

# Defensive Planning

Defenders should try to:

- Preserve at least one safe exit.
- Avoid unnecessary discards in passive suits.
- Recognize elimination play early.
- Keep communication intact.

A preserved safe exit may defeat an otherwise successful endplay.

---

# Timing

Timing is essential.

Safe exits should be:

- Preserved early.
- Used only when necessary.
- Counted throughout the hand.

Losing the last safe exit too early can be fatal.

---

# Common Situations

Safe Exit Cards are especially important in:

- Endplays.
- Throw-Ins.
- Strip Squeezes.
- Elimination Plays.
- Endgame defense.
- Avoidance Play.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Cashing the last safe exit too early.
- Leading away from an honor unnecessarily.
- Ignoring elimination play.
- Forgetting to preserve passive suits.
- Miscounting remaining exits.

---

# Advantages

- Prevents endplays.
- Preserves defensive flexibility.
- Improves endgame planning.
- Helps avoid unnecessary tricks.
- Supports accurate defensive strategy.

---

# Disadvantages

- May require passive defense.
- Sometimes impossible to preserve.
- Accurate counting is essential.
- Often overlooked until too late.

---

# Related Techniques

| Technique | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|
| Throw-In (Endplay) | Exploit the lack of safe exits |
| Elimination Play | Remove defenders' safe exits |
| Strip Squeeze | Combine elimination and endplay |
| Avoidance Play | Keep the dangerous opponent off lead |
| Defensive Planning | Preserve safe exits whenever possible |
| Entry Killing | Remove communication between hands |

---

# Player's Checklist

Before giving up the lead:

- Do I have a safe exit?
- Which suit is safest?
- Can I preserve a passive return?
- Has my opponent eliminated my exits?
- Will this lead give away a trick?

---

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Identify all possible exit cards.
2. Determine which exits are safe.
3. Preserve safe exits whenever possible.
4. Eliminate opponents' safe exits.
5. Use the safest exit when forced to surrender the lead.

---

# Comparison

| Technique | Main Idea |
|-----------|-----------|
| Safe Exit Cards | Lose the lead without giving away a trick |
| Throw-In | Force an opponent with no safe exit to lead |
| Elimination Play | Remove opponents' safe exits |
| Avoidance Play | Keep the dangerous opponent off lead |
| Entry Killing | Destroy communication rather than exit cards |

---

# Typical Sequence

```
Identify Safe Exit Cards
↓

Preserve Your Own

↓

Eliminate Opponents' Safe Exits

↓

Force Opponent to Win a Trick

↓

Opponent Has No Safe Return

↓

Gain an Extra Trick
```

---

# Memory Aid

**Safe Exit Cards = "Always Leave Yourself a Safe Way Out."**

Remember:

- **A safe exit loses the lead without conceding an extra trick.**
- **The absence of a safe exit is the foundation of most endplays.**
- **Declarer tries to eliminate defenders' safe exits; defenders try to preserve them.**
- **Passive exits are often stronger than active ones.**
- **Recognizing and preserving safe exit cards is a hallmark of expert endgame play.**

Safe Exit Cards are a fundamental concept in advanced bridge strategy. They are the key to successful defensive planning and the target of many declarer techniques such as Elimination Play, Throw-Ins, and Strip Squeezes. Expert players constantly evaluate which exits remain safe and shape the play to preserve their own while eliminating those of their opponents.
