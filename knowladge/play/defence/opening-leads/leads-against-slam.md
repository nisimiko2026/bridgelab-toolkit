# Leads Against Slam Contracts

## Overview

Opening leads against **slam contracts** differ significantly from leads against part-score, game, or notrump contracts. At the slam level, declarer usually has abundant strength and only one or two potential losers. The defenders rarely have time to establish a long suit, so the emphasis shifts from **creating tricks** to **avoiding giving one away**.

The opening lead is often the defenders' only opportunity to defeat the contract. A successful lead may immediately establish the setting trick, while an ill-chosen lead can hand declarer an otherwise unavailable winner.

---

# Defensive Objectives

Against slams, defenders aim to:

- Avoid conceding a trick.
- Cash immediate winners when appropriate.
- Find partner's strength.
- Create or obtain a ruff when possible.
- Prevent declarer from eliminating losers.
- Make declarer guess rather than solve the hand.

Unlike lower-level contracts, the priority is usually **not** to establish a long suit.

---

# General Principles

When choosing an opening lead against a slam, consider:

1. The auction.
2. Partner's bidding.
3. Declarer's likely distribution.
4. Dummy's expected strength.
5. Whether an immediate trick must be taken.
6. Whether a passive lead is safest.

If uncertain, choose the lead least likely to give declarer a free trick.

---

# Passive Leads

Passive leads are the default against many slams.

Typical passive leads include:

- A trump.
- A low card from a weak suit.
- A small card from three or four small cards.

Example:

```
♠ 9 6 3
```

Lead:

```
♠3
```

This avoids leading away from an unsupported honor.

---

# Avoid Unsupported Honor Leads

One of the most common errors is leading away from an unsupported honor.

Example:

```
♥ K 7 4
```

A heart lead may establish declarer's queen or jack.

Unless the auction strongly suggests otherwise, choose a safer alternative.

---

# When to Lead an Ace

Leading an ace can be correct when:

- Holding **AK**.
- The partnership uses ace leads to request attitude.
- A quick cashing trick may disappear.
- Partner may hold the setting trick.

Example:

```
♣ A K 8 4
```

Leading the ace allows partner to encourage or discourage continuation according to partnership agreement.

Avoid leading a bare ace unless the auction clearly justifies it.

---

# Looking for a Ruff

A singleton lead may be the only chance to defeat a slam.

Requirements:

- Partner is likely to gain the lead.
- Partner is expected to hold an ace or entry.
- Declarer may not immediately draw trumps.

Example:

Auction:

```
1♠  Pass
3♠  Pass
6♠
```

Holding:

```
♦7
```

A singleton diamond lead may allow partner to win an ace and return a diamond for a ruff.

Because this strategy is risky, it should be based on strong evidence from the auction.

---

# Trump Leads

Trump leads are often excellent against slams.

Benefits include:

- Preventing dummy from ruffing losers.
- Reducing crossruff opportunities.
- Removing declarer's flexibility.
- Giving away very little information.

Trump leads are especially attractive when:

- Declarer has shown two suits.
- Dummy is expected to be short in a side suit.
- No attacking lead is obvious.

---

# Partner's Suit

If partner has overcalled or doubled and clearly shown a suit, leading that suit is frequently the best choice.

Partner's bidding provides more reliable information than speculative leads from your own hand.

---

# Reading the Auction

The auction often determines the correct lead.

### Strong Cue-Bidding Auction

```
1♥
2♦
2♥
3♣
3♠
4NT
...
6♥
```

The opponents have described most of their controls.

A passive lead is usually best.

---

### Competitive Auction

```
1♠
2♦
4♠
6♠
```

If partner bid diamonds, leading a diamond may be the only realistic chance to defeat the slam.

---

# Small Slam vs. Grand Slam

## Small Slam (Level 6)

One defensive trick defeats the contract.

Avoid speculative leads that may establish declarer's twelfth trick.

---

## Grand Slam (Level 7)

The defenders need two tricks.

More aggressive leads may be justified, since a single trick is not enough.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Before defending slams, discuss:

- Ace asks for attitude or count.
- King asks for count or unblock.
- Rusinow or standard honor leads.
- Singleton leads.
- Trump lead philosophy.
- Top of Nothing.
- Interior sequence leads.

Good partnership agreements are especially important because there is rarely a second chance after the opening lead.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Leading away from unsupported honors.
- Automatically leading partner's suit without considering the auction.
- Trying to establish a long suit instead of taking immediate tricks.
- Ignoring the possibility of a trump lead.
- Failing to recognize when a singleton lead is the only winning defense.
- Giving declarer a free finesse at trick one.

---

# Practical Example 1

Auction:

```
West   North   East   South
1NT    Pass    6NT    All Pass
```

West holds:

```
♠ K 7 4
♥ 9 6 3
♦ A 10 7 5
♣ J 8 2
```

A passive diamond lead is usually superior to leading away from the unsupported king of spades.

---

# Practical Example 2

Auction:

```
West   North   East   South
1♠     Pass    6♠     All Pass
```

West holds:

```
♠ 8 4
♥ K 8 5
♦ 7
♣ Q 10 9 6 3
```

If the auction suggests partner holds the ♦A, the singleton diamond lead may be the only chance to score a ruff and defeat the slam.

---

# Checklist Before Leading

Ask yourself:

1. Can I take an immediate winner?
2. Am I leading away from an unsupported honor?
3. Has partner shown a suit?
4. Would a trump lead be safer?
5. Is a singleton lead likely to produce a ruff?
6. What has the auction revealed about declarer's shape?
7. Which lead gives declarer the fewest free options?

# Key Principles

- Against slams, **do not give declarer a trick**.
- Passive leads are often superior to aggressive attacks.
- Lead partner's suit when the auction clearly supports it.
- Trump leads frequently disrupt declarer's plans with minimal risk.
- Singleton leads are powerful but should be based on strong evidence from the auction.
- Let the auction guide your opening lead more than your own hand.
