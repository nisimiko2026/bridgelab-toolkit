# Opening Leads from AQ

## Overview

The holding **Ace-Queen (AQ)** is a **broken honor combination**, not a touching sequence. Unlike **AK**, **KQ**, or **QJ**, the missing King means that leading an honor requires careful judgment.

In standard bridge methods:

- **Against Notrump:** Lead the **fourth-best spot card** from length.
- **Against Suit Contracts:** The **Ace** may be led by partnership agreement to obtain count or look for a ruff.
- **Do not normally lead the Queen** from AQ in standard methods.

The guiding principle is:

> **AQ is not a sequence—treat it as a strong holding, not a sequence lead.**

---

# Purpose

Opening leads from AQ aim to:

- Establish a long suit safely.
- Preserve the Queen.
- Obtain information from partner.
- Avoid giving declarer a free finesse.
- Develop additional winners later.

---

# Understanding the Holding

Example:

```
♠ A Q 8 6 4
```

Strengths:

- One immediate winner.
- Good long-suit potential.
- Flexible defensive holding.

Weaknesses:

- Missing the King.
- No touching honors.
- The Queen may be vulnerable if led prematurely.

---

# Standard Expert Treatment

## Against Notrump

The standard lead is:

```
Fourth-best spot card
```

Example

Holding

```
♣ A Q 8 6 4
```

Lead

```
♣4
```

Reason:

- Preserve the Ace.
- Conceal the Queen.
- Maximize the chance of establishing the suit.

---

## Against Suit Contracts

The Ace may be led when:

- The partnership leads Ace from strong holdings.
- You want partner's count.
- You are looking for a possible ruff.

Otherwise, another lead may be more effective.

---

# Why Not Lead the Queen?

Leading the Queen from AQ:

- Does **not** promise the Jack.
- May mislead partner.
- Can give declarer useful information.
- Is contrary to standard honor-lead methods.

Therefore, the Queen is **not** normally led from AQ.

---

# Partner's Signals

When the Ace is led, partner usually gives:

## Count

### Standard Count

```
High-Low = Even

Low-High = Odd
```

### UDCA

```
High-Low = Odd

Low-High = Even
```

---

## Attitude

Some partnerships instead use:

- High = Encourage.
- Low = Discourage.

---

## Suit Preference

When appropriate, partner may later use Suit Preference to recommend a switch.

---

# Example 1 – Standard Notrump Lead

Holding

```
♦ A Q 9 7 5
```

Contract

```
3NT
```

Lead

```
♦5
```

This preserves the honor structure while beginning to establish the suit.

---

# Example 2 – Suit Contract

Holding

```
♥ A Q 8 4
```

Contract

```
4♠
```

Lead

```
♥A
```

Partner gives count.

If partner shows a doubleton, continue hearts for a possible ruff.

---

# Example 3 – Passive Alternative

Holding

```
♠ A Q 7 3
```

No attractive lead is available.

A passive lead from another suit may be safer than leading from AQ.

---

# Planning the Defense

Before leading AQ, ask:

- Is this a Notrump or suit contract?
- Do we lead fourth-best from length?
- Do I need information from partner?
- Am I looking for a ruff?
- Is another suit a better opening lead?

---

# Special Considerations

### AQ Is Not a Sequence

AQ differs from:

```
AK
KQ
QJ
```

because there are **no touching honors**.

Do not use sequence-lead principles.

---

### Long Suit

AQ becomes much stronger when accompanied by length.

Example:

```
AQ8765
```

A fourth-best lead is often ideal against Notrump.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Leading the Queen from AQ.
- Treating AQ as a touching sequence.
- Cashing the Ace unnecessarily.
- Ignoring the auction.
- Forgetting partnership agreements.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Before play, discuss:

- Does the Ace ask for count or attitude?
- Do we lead fourth-best from AQ against Notrump?
- When should the Ace be led from AQ?
- How do our methods differ in suit contracts?
- When is Suit Preference used?

---

- Fits naturally into fourth-best lead methods.

# Disadvantages

2. Apply your partnership's lead agreements.
3. Lead fourth-best (or the Ace by agreement).
4. Interpret partner's signal.
5. Continue or switch based on the full picture.

# Memory Aid

**AQ = "Strong Holding, Not a Sequence."**

Remember:

- **AQ is a broken honor combination.**
- **Against Notrump, fourth-best from length is usually the standard lead.**
- **Against suit contracts, the Ace may be led by agreement to obtain count or seek a ruff.**
- **Do not lead the Queen in standard methods—it does not promise the Jack.**
- **Always combine your lead with the auction, dummy, and your partnership's agreed methods.**

The **AQ** holding requires sound judgment rather than automatic honor leads. By recognizing that it is **not** a touching sequence and by following standard fourth-best and Ace-lead agreements, defenders preserve communication, avoid giving declarer unnecessary information, and maximize the long-term value of the suit.
