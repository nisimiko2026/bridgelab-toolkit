# Rusinow Leads

## Overview

**Rusinow Leads** are an opening lead convention in which the leader **does not lead the top honor from a touching sequence**. Instead, the **second-highest honor** is led.

The primary objective is to provide partner with more precise information about the leader's honor holding, reducing ambiguity compared with standard honor leads.

Rusinow leads are most commonly used **against suit contracts**, although some expert partnerships extend them to notrump contracts.

Because Rusinow leads differ significantly from standard methods, they require explicit partnership agreement.

---

# Basic Principle

From a touching honor sequence, lead the **second-highest honor**.

Examples:

| Holding | Standard Lead | Rusinow Lead |
|---------|---------------|--------------|
| AK | A | K |
| AKQ | A | K |
| AKJ | A | K |
| KQ | K | Q |
| KQJ | K | Q |
| KQ10 | K | Q |
| QJ | Q | J |
| QJ10 | Q | J |
| J10 | J | 10 |

The lead therefore identifies both the presence of a sequence and often its exact upper honor.

---

# Why Use Rusinow Leads?

Standard leads sometimes leave partner uncertain.

Example:

Standard lead:

```
Lead: ♠K
```

Could come from:

- KQ
- KQJ
- AK
- AKQ
- AKJ

Under Rusinow:

```
Lead: ♠K
```

almost always promises an **Ace above** (AK, AKQ, AKJ, etc.), while a lead of the **Queen** promises a **King above**.

Partner can therefore place the unseen honors much more accurately.

# Standard Rusinow Table

| Holding | Lead |
|---------|------|
| AK | K |
| AKQ | K |
| AKJ | K |
| AKQJ | K |
| KQ | Q |
| KQJ | Q |
| KQ10 | Q |
| QJ | J |
| QJ10 | J |
| J10 | 10 |

---

# Advantages

## More Accurate Defensive Inference

Partner can identify:

- Which honor is missing.
- Whether the ace is held.
- Whether an unblock may be necessary.
- Whether to encourage continuation.

---

## Better Communication

Because the top honor is retained, later defensive play often becomes clearer.

Example:

Leader:

```
♠ A K 8 4
```

Lead:

```
♠K
```

Partner knows the ace is also present.

---

## Improved Slam Defense

Against slams, knowing exactly where the missing honors lie can be critical for deciding whether to signal encouragement, count, or suit preference.

---

# Example 1

Holding:

```
♠ A K 7 4
```

Against 4♥

Lead:

```
♠K
```

Partner infers:

- Leader also has the ace.
- The king is not from KQ.

---

# Example 2

Holding:

```
♥ K Q 9 4
```

Lead:

```
♥Q
```

Partner knows:

- King is above.
- This is a genuine sequence.

---

# Example 3

Holding:

```
♦ Q J 10 6
```

Lead:

```
♦J
```

Partner immediately places the queen above.

# Ace and King Leads

Partnerships must agree how ace and king leads work outside touching sequences.

Common treatments include:

### Ace

- Requests attitude.
- Requests count.
- Shows AK only.

### King

- From AK (Rusinow standard).
- May request count or unblock.

These agreements vary considerably and must be discussed.

---

# Against Suit Contracts

Rusinow is most frequently played against suit contracts because:

- Precise honor location is valuable.
- Dummy's trumps reduce the importance of preserving spot-card ambiguity.
- Defensive communication outweighs the information given to declarer.

---

# Against Notrump

Some expert partnerships extend Rusinow to notrump contracts.

Advantages:

- Better honor identification.
- Improved unblocking.

Disadvantages:

- Gives declarer more information.
- Many partnerships prefer traditional fourth-best methods.

Most club partnerships restrict Rusinow to suit contracts only.

---

# Interior Sequences

Partnership agreement is required.

Examples:

| Holding | Possible Lead |
|---------|---------------|
| K J 10 | J |
| Q 10 9 | 10 |
| J 9 8 | 9 |

Some partnerships use Rusinow only for **true touching sequences** and treat interior sequences separately.

---

# Comparison with Standard Leads

| Holding | Standard | Rusinow |
|---------|----------|----------|
| AK | A | K |
| AKQ | A | K |
| KQ | K | Q |
| KQJ | K | Q |
| QJ | Q | J |
| J10 | J | 10 |

The difference is simple:

- **Standard:** lead the highest honor.
- **Rusinow:** lead the second-highest honor.

---

# Advantages

- Greater precision.
- Better defensive communication.
- Easier unblocking.
- Better continuation decisions.
- Strong expert support.

---

# Disadvantages

- Non-standard.
- Requires complete partnership agreement.
- Can reveal more information to declarer.
- Must fit with ace/king signaling agreements.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Before adopting Rusinow, discuss:

- Suit contracts only or all contracts?
- Ace asks for attitude or count?
- King asks for unblock or count?
- Treatment of AK versus AKQ.
- Interior sequences.
- Top of Nothing.
- Rusinow versus standard honor leads.
- Third- and fifth-best or fourth-best from length.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Forgetting to lead the second honor.
- Mixing Rusinow with standard methods.
- Applying Rusinow to unsupported honors.
- Using Rusinow without partnership agreement.
- Confusing Rusinow leads with carding signals during play.

# Key Principles

- Rusinow leads use the **second-highest card from a touching honor sequence**.
- They provide partner with more precise information about honor location.
- They are most commonly played against suit contracts.
- They require detailed partnership agreements, especially for ace and king leads.
- Consistency is essential—both defenders must interpret the lead using the same methods.
