# Opening Leads from AQ10

## Overview

The holding **Ace-Queen-Ten (AQ10)** is an **interior sequence**, not a touching sequence. Unlike **AKQ** or **KQJ**, the honors are separated by the missing King and Jack, making the opening lead more dependent on partnership agreements.

In **standard opening leads**, the recommended lead is usually:

- **Fourth-best spot card** against Notrump.
- **Ace** only when the partnership specifically leads Ace from strong holdings or when an immediate cash-out is required.
- **Do not normally lead the Queen** in standard methods.

In systems such as **Journalist Leads**, however, the **Ten** may be led to show an interior sequence like AQ10x.

The guiding principle is:

> **AQ10 is an interior sequence—treat it differently from a touching sequence.**

---

# Purpose

Opening leads from AQ10 aim to:

- Develop the suit safely.
- Avoid giving declarer a free finesse.
- Obtain information from partner.
- Preserve communication.
- Maximize long-term defensive tricks.

---

# Understanding the Holding

Example:

```
♠ A Q 10 7 4
```

Strengths:

- One immediate winner.
- Strong intermediate cards.
- Potential to establish additional tricks.

Weaknesses:

- Missing the King.
- Missing the Jack.
- Honors are not touching.

---

# Standard Expert Treatment

## Against Notrump

The normal lead is:

```
Fourth-best spot card
```

Example

Holding

```
♣ A Q 10 7 4
```

Lead

```
♣4
```

Reason:

- Preserve the Ace.
- Keep the Queen protected.
- Allow partner to contribute honors if possible.

---

## Against Suit Contracts

Leading the Ace is appropriate when:

- The partnership leads Ace from strong holdings.
- You want count from partner.
- You are looking for a possible ruff.

Otherwise, a passive lead from another suit is often superior.

---

# Alternative Methods

## Journalist Leads

Some partnerships using **Journalist Leads** lead the:

```
10
```

from

```
AQ10x
```

to identify an interior sequence.

This treatment is **not** part of standard opening leads and must be agreed in advance.

---

# Partner's Signals

When the Ace is led, partner usually gives:

## Count

Standard

```
High-Low = Even

Low-High = Odd
```

or

UDCA

```
High-Low = Odd

Low-High = Even
```

---

## Attitude

Some partnerships instead request:

- High = Encourage.
- Low = Discourage.

---

## Suit Preference

Later in the suit, partner may use Suit Preference when appropriate.

---

# Example 1 – Standard Notrump Lead

Holding

```
♦ A Q 10 7 5
```

Contract

```
3NT
```

Lead:

```
♦5
```

This preserves the honor structure while beginning to establish the suit.

---

# Example 2 – Suit Contract

Holding

```
♥ A Q 10 4
```

Contract

```
4♠
```

Lead:

```
♥A
```

Partner gives count.

If partner has a doubleton, continue hearts for a possible ruff.

---

# Example 3 – Journalist Method

Holding

```
♣ A Q 10 8
```

Agreement:

Journalist Leads.

Lead:

```
♣10
```

Partner immediately recognizes an interior sequence.

---

# Planning the Defense

Before leading AQ10, ask:

- Are we defending a Notrump or suit contract?
- Do we play standard or Journalist Leads?
- Am I trying to establish the suit?
- Do I need count or attitude?
- Is another opening lead more attractive?

---

# Common Mistakes

- Treating AQ10 like KQJ.
- Leading the Queen in standard methods.
- Forgetting partnership agreements.
- Automatically cashing the Ace.
- Ignoring the auction.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Before play, discuss:

- Does the Ace ask for count or attitude?
- Do we use fourth-best leads from AQ10?
- Do we play Journalist Leads?
- When should the Ace be led from AQ10?
- How do our methods differ in Notrump and suit contracts?

---

# Advantages

- Flexible defensive holding.
- Good long-suit potential.
- Fits naturally into fourth-best lead methods.
- Can identify an interior sequence in Journalist systems.

---

# Disadvantages

- No touching sequence.
- Easy to misplay if treated like AKQ or KQJ.
- Requires clear partnership agreements.

# Memory Aid

**AQ10 = "Interior Sequence—Not a Touching Sequence."**

Remember:

- **AQ10 is not led like KQJ or KQ10.**
- **In standard methods, fourth-best is usually preferred against Notrump.**
- **Against suit contracts, the Ace may be led to obtain information or seek a ruff.**
- **In Journalist Leads, the Ten may identify the interior sequence.**
- **Always follow your partnership's agreed opening lead methods.**

The **AQ10** holding requires judgment rather than automatic action. Because it is an interior sequence, the correct opening lead depends on the contract type and your partnership's agreements. Expert defenders recognize that preserving the honor structure and obtaining the right information from partner are usually more important than cashing an immediate trick.
