# Opening Leads from AKQ

## Overview

Leading from **Ace-King-Queen (AKQ)** is one of the strongest opening lead holdings in bridge. Because the leader holds the top three honors, the partnership's agreements focus less on **which honor to lead** and more on **what information partner should provide** after the lead.

Most expert partnerships lead the **Ace** from AKQ, but some use the **King** depending on their overall opening lead agreements (e.g., Ace asks for attitude, King asks for count).

The guiding principle is:

> **Cash your winners efficiently while obtaining the information needed to decide whether to continue the suit or switch.**

---

# Purpose

Opening leads from AKQ are used to:

- Cash immediate winners.
- Establish a long suit.
- Obtain a ruff in suit contracts.
- Learn partner's distribution.
- Decide whether to continue or switch.
- Prevent declarer from establishing winners.

---

# Typical Expert Agreements

The most common treatments are:

| Lead | Typical Meaning |
|------|-----------------|
| **Ace** | AKQ or AK; asks for **attitude** or **count**, depending on partnership agreement |
| **King** | AKQ in partnerships where the King asks for **count** |
| **Queen** | Usually **not** led from AKQ as an opening lead; normally shows a QJ sequence or equivalent |

**Important:** The exact treatment depends on partnership agreements.

---

# Against Notrump Contracts

## Objectives

- Establish the long suit.
- Cash winners before declarer develops theirs.
- Learn whether the suit should be continued.

### Example

Holding

```
♠ A K Q 7 5
```

Contract

```
3NT
```

Lead:

```
♠A
```

Partner's signal:

- Encourage if holding useful honors or length.
- Give count if that is the partnership agreement.

Continue or switch based on the information received.

---

# Against Suit Contracts

## Objectives

- Cash immediate winners.
- Discover whether partner has a doubleton.
- Give partner a ruff if appropriate.

### Example

Holding

```
♥ A K Q 8
```

Contract

```
4♠
```

Lead:

```
♥A
```

Partner gives count.

If partner shows a doubleton, continue hearts to obtain a ruff.

---

# Partner's Signals

## Count

Many expert partnerships request **count** from partner after leading the Ace from AKQ.

### Standard Count

```
High-Low = Even

Low-High = Odd
```

### UDCA Count

```
High-Low = Odd

Low-High = Even
```

Count helps determine whether the suit should be continued.

---

## Attitude

Some partnerships prefer **attitude**.

Typical agreement:

- High = Encourage.
- Low = Discourage.

Useful when deciding whether to continue cashing the suit.

---

## Suit Preference

After the first or second honor has been cashed, some partnerships switch to **Suit Preference**.

Examples:

- Partner is known to have a doubleton.
- A ruff is likely.
- Count is already known.

---

# Example 1 – Cashing Three Winners

Holding

```
♣ A K Q J 5
```

Contract

```
3NT
```

Lead:

```
♣A
```

Partner encourages.

Cash:

```
♣K
♣Q
```

The defense establishes enough tricks before declarer gains control.

---

# Example 2 – Giving a Ruff

Holding

```
♦ A K Q 6
```

Contract

```
4♥
```

Lead:

```
♦A
```

Partner plays:

```
♦2
♦7
```

The count shows a doubleton.

Continue diamonds.

Partner scores a ruff.

---

# Example 3 – Switching

Holding

```
♠ A K Q 4
```

Partner discourages after the Ace.

Rather than cashing all three honors, switch to another suit.

Correct timing prevents declarer from gaining extra tricks.

# Planning the Defense

Before leading AKQ, ask:

- Am I trying to establish a long suit?
- Do I need count or attitude?
- Is a ruff possible?
- Should I continue after the first honor?
- Will cashing all three honors help or hurt the defense?

---

# Common Mistakes

- Automatically cashing all three honors.
- Ignoring partner's signal.
- Forgetting partnership agreements.
- Continuing after a discouraging signal.
- Missing a ruff opportunity.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Before play, discuss:

- Ace or King from AKQ?
- Does the lead ask for count or attitude?
- Different agreements in Notrump and suit contracts.
- When to switch to Suit Preference.
- Whether to continue after a discouraging signal.

---

# Advantages

- Establishes immediate winners.
- Creates strong defensive communication.
- Helps identify ruff opportunities.
- Provides flexibility after partner's signal.
- One of the strongest possible opening lead holdings.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires precise partnership agreements.
- Incorrect interpretation may waste winners.
- Automatic continuation may give declarer tempo.

Before leading AKQ:

- Which honor does our partnership lead?
- What information do I want from partner?
- Is a ruff possible?
- Should I continue or switch after the signal?
- Will cashing all three honors benefit the defense?

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Lead the agreed honor.
2. Read partner's signal.
3. Decide whether to continue.
4. Look for ruff opportunities.
5. Switch if continuing no longer helps.

---

# Comparison

| Holding | Typical Treatment |
|----------|-------------------|
| AK | Lead Ace (modern) or King (traditional), depending on partnership agreement |
| AKQ | Usually lead Ace, then use partner's signal to decide whether to continue |
| KQ | Often lead the King from the sequence |
| QJ | Often lead the Queen from the sequence |

# Memory Aid

**AKQ Leads = "Strong Holding, Smart Continuation."**

Remember:

- **AKQ is a powerful opening lead, but the continuation depends on partner's information.**
- **Most expert partnerships lead the Ace, though some use the King by agreement.**
- **Do not cash all three honors automatically—listen to partner's signal first.**
- **Against suit contracts, always consider whether partner can score a ruff.**
- **Clear partnership agreements are more important than the specific honor led.**

Opening leads from **AKQ** provide the defense with immediate strength and excellent communication opportunities. By combining a strong honor holding with clear agreements about count, attitude, and suit preference, defenders can establish winners efficiently, create ruffs, and avoid unnecessary continuation when a switch offers a better chance of defeating the contract.
