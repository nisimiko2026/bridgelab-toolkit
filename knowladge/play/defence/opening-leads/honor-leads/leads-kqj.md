# Opening Leads from KQJ

## Overview

The holding **King-Queen-Jack (KQJ)** is one of the strongest honor sequences in bridge. Since it consists of **three touching honors**, it provides an excellent opportunity to establish a long suit or cash immediate winners.

In virtually all standard opening lead methods, the correct lead is:

```
King
```

The King is the top of the sequence and clearly identifies a powerful honor holding. Partner can often infer that the Queen and Jack are also present.

The guiding principle is:

> **Lead the top of a strong touching honor sequence.**

---

# Purpose

Opening leads from KQJ aim to:

- Establish a long suit.
- Force declarer's Ace.
- Promote additional winners.
- Help partner identify the honor holding.
- Coordinate the defense efficiently.

---

# Standard Expert Treatment

| Holding | Opening Lead |
|----------|--------------|
| KQ | King |
| KQ10 | King |
| **KQJ** | **King** |
| KQJ10 | King |

The King promises the Queen and usually the Jack.

---

# Why Lead the King?

Holding

```
♠ K Q J 6 4
```

Advantages:

- Identifies a strong sequence.
- Forces declarer's Ace if present.
- Leaves the Queen and Jack ready to become winners.
- Fits virtually all standard lead agreements.

---

# Against Notrump Contracts

## Objectives

- Establish the long suit.
- Force out declarer's Ace.
- Cash developed winners before declarer establishes theirs.

### Example

Holding

```
♣ K Q J 8 5
```

Contract

```
3NT
```

Lead:

```
♣K
```

If declarer wins the Ace, the remaining honors usually establish the suit.

---

# Against Suit Contracts

## Objectives

- Cash immediate tricks.
- Force declarer's Ace.
- Prevent declarer from discarding losers.
- Decide whether to continue based on partner's signal.

Example

Holding

```
♥ K Q J 7
```

Contract

```
4♠
```

Lead:

```
♥K
```

Partner's signal determines whether to continue or switch.

---

# Partner's Signals

The exact signal depends on partnership agreements.

## Attitude

Many partnerships use:

- High = Encourage.
- Low = Discourage.

This is especially common against Notrump.

---

## Count

Some partnerships prefer count after a King lead.

### Standard Count

```
High-Low = Even

Low-High = Odd
```

### UDCA

```
High-Low = Odd

Low-High = Even
```

---

## Suit Preference

Once count and attitude are known, partner may use Suit Preference to suggest the best continuation or switch.

---

# Example 1 – Establishing the Suit

Holding

```
♦ K Q J 9
```

Lead:

```
♦K
```

Declarer wins the Ace.

The Queen and Jack later become winners.

---

# Example 2 – Continue

Holding

```
♠ K Q J 5
```

Partner encourages.

Continue spades.

The defense develops multiple winners.

---

# Example 3 – Switch

Holding

```
♣ K Q J 4
```

Partner discourages.

Switch to another suit instead of automatically cashing additional honors.

Correct timing may defeat the contract.

---

# Planning the Defense

Before leading KQJ, ask:

- Is this my strongest attacking suit?
- Am I trying to establish the suit?
- Does the auction suggest another lead?
- What information do I want from partner?
- Should I continue after the first trick?

---

# Special Considerations

### Missing the Ace

The King lead usually indicates:

- The Ace is not held by the leader.
- Declarer or partner is likely to hold it.

---

### Three Touching Honors

KQJ is considerably stronger than:

```
KQ10
```

because the Jack increases the likelihood of establishing the entire suit.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Leading the Queen instead of the King.
- Automatically cashing all honors.
- Ignoring partner's signal.
- Continuing after a discouraging signal.
- Forgetting the auction.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Before play, discuss:

- Does the King always promise the Queen?
- What signal is expected after a King lead?
- Different methods against Notrump and suit contracts.
- When Suit Preference applies.
- Whether Journalist or Rusinow Leads are used.

---

# Advantages

- Strong touching sequence.
- Easy for partner to recognize.
- Frequently establishes the suit.
- Excellent attacking lead.
- Standard treatment worldwide.

---

# Disadvantages

- Continuing automatically may help declarer.
- Requires accurate partnership signaling.
- Timing still matters.

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Identify the touching sequence.
2. Lead the King.
3. Read partner's signal.
4. Continue or switch.
5. Reevaluate after each trick.

# Memory Aid

**KQJ Leads = "Top of Three Touching Honors."**

Remember:

- **Lead the King from KQJ in virtually all standard methods.**
- **The King promises the Queen and usually the Jack.**
- **KQJ is stronger than KQ10 because the three touching honors are more likely to establish the suit.**
- **Partner's signal determines whether continuing the suit is best.**
- **Do not continue automatically—consider the auction, dummy, and the overall defensive plan.**

Opening leads from **KQJ** are among the most effective attacking leads in bridge. The strong touching sequence allows defenders to establish long-suit winners, communicate their holding clearly, and coordinate the defense through partner's signals. Like all honor-sequence leads, success depends not only on the opening lead itself but also on accurate continuation and sound partnership agreements.
