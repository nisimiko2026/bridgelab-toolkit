# Opening Leads from QJ10

## Overview

The holding **Queen-Jack-Ten (QJ10)** is one of the strongest **touching honor sequences** below the King. Because it contains **three consecutive honors**, it is an excellent opening lead combination and is treated consistently in virtually all standard bridge systems.

The standard opening lead is:

```
Queen
```

The Queen is the **top of the touching sequence**, clearly identifying the honor structure to partner while helping establish the suit.

The guiding principle is:

> **Lead the top of a touching honor sequence.**

---

# Purpose

Opening leads from QJ10 aim to:

- Establish the suit.
- Force declarer's higher honors.
- Promote additional winners.
- Communicate the honor holding to partner.
- Coordinate the defense.

---

# Understanding the Holding

Example:

```
♠ Q J 10 7 4
```

Strengths:

- Three touching honors.
- Excellent attacking lead.
- Easy for partner to recognize.

Weakness:

- Missing the Ace and King.
- Declarer may have immediate control if holding both.

---

# Standard Expert Treatment

| Holding | Opening Lead |
|----------|--------------|
| QJ | Queen |
| **QJ10** | **Queen** |
| QJ109 | Queen |
| QJ108 | Queen |

The Queen promises the Jack and generally the Ten.

---

# Against Notrump Contracts

## Objectives

- Establish the long suit.
- Force declarer's Ace or King.
- Develop additional tricks before declarer establishes winners.

### Example

Holding

```
♣ Q J 10 8 5
```

Contract

```
3NT
```

Lead:

```
♣Q
```

If declarer wins with the King or Ace, the remaining honors may become winners later.

---

# Against Suit Contracts

## Objectives

- Cash immediate tricks if possible.
- Force declarer's honors.
- Develop the suit.
- Obtain information from partner.

Example

Holding

```
♥ Q J 10 6
```

Contract

```
4♠
```

Lead:

```
♥Q
```

Partner's signal determines whether to continue or switch.

---

# Partner's Signals

The exact signal depends on partnership agreements.

## Attitude

Common agreement:

- High = Encourage.
- Low = Discourage.

Especially useful against Notrump.

---

## Count

Some partnerships request count after a Queen lead.

### Standard Count

```
High-Low = Even

Low-High = Odd
```

### UDCA

```
High-Low = Odd

Low-High = Even
```

---

## Suit Preference

Later in the defense, partner may use Suit Preference if appropriate.

---

# Example 1 – Establishing the Suit

Holding

```
♦ Q J 10 5
```

Lead:

```
♦Q
```

Declarer wins with the Ace.

The Jack and Ten later become winners.

---

# Example 2 – Continue

Holding

```
♠ Q J 10 4
```

Partner encourages.

Continue spades.

The defense develops additional tricks.

---

# Example 3 – Switch

Holding

```
♣ Q J 10 6
```

Partner discourages.

Switch to another suit rather than continuing automatically.

---

# Planning the Defense

Before leading QJ10, ask:

- Is this my strongest attacking suit?
- Does the auction suggest another lead?
- What information do I want from partner?
- Should I continue after the first trick?
- Can the suit be established quickly?

---

# Special Considerations

### Missing the Ace and King

The Queen lead normally denies both higher honors.

Partner should not assume the leader has the King.

---

### Strong Sequence

QJ10 is significantly stronger than:

```
Q109
```

because the Jack fills the sequence and greatly improves the suit's trick-taking potential.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Leading the Jack instead of the Queen.
- Automatically continuing after a discouraging signal.
- Ignoring the auction.
- Misreading partner's signal.
- Forgetting partnership agreements.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Before play, discuss:

- Does the Queen always promise the Jack?
- What signal is expected after a Queen lead?
- Differences between Notrump and suit contracts.
- When Suit Preference applies.
- Whether Journalist or Rusinow Leads are used.

---

# Advantages

- Strong touching sequence.
- Easy for partner to recognize.
- Frequently establishes long-suit winners.
- Standard treatment worldwide.
- Flexible continuation.

---

# Disadvantages

- May lose tempo if continued incorrectly.
- Requires accurate partnership signaling.
- Declarer may immediately control the suit with the Ace and King.

Before leading QJ10:

- What information do I need from partner?

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Identify the touching sequence.
2. Lead the Queen.
3. Read partner's signal.
4. Continue or switch.
5. Reevaluate after every trick.

# Memory Aid

**QJ10 Leads = "Top of Three Touching Honors."**

Remember:

- **Lead the Queen from QJ10 in standard opening-lead methods.**
- **The Queen promises the Jack and usually the Ten.**
- **QJ10 is one of the strongest non-top honor sequences.**
- **Partner's signal determines whether to continue the suit.**
- **Do not continue automatically—consider the auction, dummy, and the overall defensive plan.**

The **QJ10** holding is an ideal opening-lead sequence. By leading the Queen, defenders accurately describe their honor structure, begin establishing the suit, and provide partner with valuable information. Combined with sound signaling and thoughtful continuation, QJ10 leads are among the most reliable and effective attacking leads in bridge.
