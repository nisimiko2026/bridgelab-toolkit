# Standard Opening Leads

## Purpose

**Standard Opening Leads** are the agreed methods a partnership uses to select the opening lead against both **Suit Contracts** and **Notrump Contracts**.

A consistent opening lead style helps defenders:

- Establish long-suit tricks.
- Develop partner's honors.
- Give accurate attitude and count signals.
- Improve defensive communication.

This document describes the **Standard American** opening lead style, the most widely used natural lead system.

---

# General Principles

### Against Suit Contracts

Priority is usually:

1. Lead a supported honor sequence.
2. Lead a singleton (when seeking a ruff).
3. Lead partner's bid suit.
4. Lead an unbid suit.
5. Avoid leading away from unsupported honors.

---

### Against Notrump Contracts

Priority is usually:

1. Longest and strongest suit.
2. Fourth-best lead.
3. Partner's suit.
4. Avoid unsupported aces.

---

# Honor Leads

## Ace

Lead from:

```
AK
```

or

```
AKx
```

or

```
AKQ
```

Many partnerships use the Ace to request **Attitude** or **Count**.

---

## King

Lead from:

```
KQ
```

or

```
AK
```

depending on partnership agreement.

Many expert partnerships play:

- Ace from AK
- King from KQ

---

## Queen

Lead from:

```
QJ
```

or

```
QJ10
```

---

## Jack

Lead from:

```
J10
```

or

```
KJ10
```

or interior sequences.

---

## Ten

Lead from:

```
109
```

or

```
K109
```

or

```
Q109
```

---

# Sequence Leads

Lead the top card from a sequence.

Examples:

| Holding | Lead |
|----------|------|
| AKQ | Ace |
| KQJ | King |
| QJ10 | Queen |
| J109 | Jack |
| 1098 | Ten |

---

# Long Suit Leads

Against Notrump:

Lead:

**Fourth-best**

Example:

Holding:

```
KJ742
```

Lead:

```
4
```

Fourth-best.

```

```

```
4
```

## Third Best

Some partnerships use:

Third-best from even-length suits.

Requires partnership agreement.

---

# Short Suit Leads

Against Suit Contracts

## Singleton

Lead singleton to obtain a ruff later.

Example:

```
♣7
```

Singleton.

---

## Doubleton

Lead:

Highest card.

Example:

Holding:

```
84
```

Lead:

```
8
```

Then:

```
4
```

---

# Interior Sequences

Lead the top of the interior sequence.

Examples:

| Holding | Lead |
|----------|------|
| KJ10x | Jack |
| Q109x | Ten |
| AJ10x | Jack |

---

# Leading Partner's Suit

General rule:

Lead:

- Top of a doubleton.
- Highest from three small.
- Lowest from four or more small.

Example:

Holding:

```
8732
```

Lead:

```
2
```

---

# Notrump Opening Leads

Preferred:

- Fourth-best.
- Top of honor sequence.
- Interior sequence.

Avoid:

- Unsupported ace.
- Unsupported king.

---

# Suit Contract Opening Leads

Preferred:

- Singleton.
- Honor sequence.
- Partner's suit.
- Trump (occasionally).

# Signals After Opening Lead

Most partnerships combine Standard Leads with:

- Standard Attitude.
- Standard Count.
- Lavinthal Suit Preference.
- Smith Echo versus Notrump.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Agree on:

- Ace asks for Attitude or Count.
- King from AK or KQ.
- Fourth-best or third/fifth leads.
- Lead style against Notrump.
- Lead style against Suit Contracts.
- Lead from interior sequences.
- Rusinow leads (if played).
- Journalist leads (if played).

---

# Advantages

- Simple and widely understood.
- Compatible with Standard Carding.
- Works well with UDCA.
- Easy for new partnerships.
- Used in most bridge clubs.

---

# Disadvantages

- Less descriptive than Rusinow or Journalist leads.
- Some holdings are ambiguous.
- Requires partnership agreements for Ace and King leads.

# Common Partnership Style

Most expert partnerships using Standard Leads play:

- Fourth-best against Notrump.
- Top of sequences.
- High from doubleton.
- Singleton against suit contracts.
- Standard or UDCA signals.
- Lavinthal suit preference.
- Smith Echo versus Notrump.
