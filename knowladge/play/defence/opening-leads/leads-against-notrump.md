# Leads Against Notrump Contracts

## Overview

The opening lead against a **notrump contract** is often the most important defensive decision of the entire hand. Unlike suit contracts, there is no trump suit to shorten declarer's winners, so the defense usually succeeds by **establishing long-suit tricks before declarer develops enough winners**.

The opening leader should use information from the auction, partnership agreements, and hand evaluation to select the lead most likely to develop tricks while preserving communication with partner.

---

# Objectives

A successful opening lead against notrump aims to:

- Establish long-suit winners.
- Attack declarer's weak suit.
- Knock out declarer's stoppers.
- Preserve entries to cash established tricks.
- Give partner the best opportunity to contribute.
- Avoid giving declarer unnecessary tricks.

---

# General Priorities

In most situations, the preferred opening leads are:

1. **Top of an honor sequence**
2. **Fourth-highest from the longest and strongest suit**
3. **Partner's bid suit**
4. **Safe passive lead** (when no attacking lead is attractive)

The auction may justify exceptions.

---

# Leading from Honor Sequences

Honor sequences provide the safest and most informative attacking leads.

Typical leads include:

| Holding | Lead |
|---------|------|
| AKQx | Ace |
| KQJx | King |
| QJ10x | Queen |
| J109x | Jack |
| 1098x | Ten (if agreed) |

Advantages:

- Establishes tricks quickly.
- Gives partner valuable information.
- Usually avoids giving away a trick.

---

# Fourth-Highest from the Longest Suit

A classic notrump lead is the **fourth-highest card** from your longest and strongest suit.

Example:

```
♠ K J 8 5 3
```

Lead:

```
♠5
```

Objectives:

- Establish long-suit winners.
- Remove declarer's stopper.
- Allow partner to gain the lead and cash the suit later.

This approach works best when partnership agreements include fourth-best leads.

---

# The Rule of 11

When fourth-best leads are used, defenders may apply the **Rule of 11**.

Formula:

```
11 − opening lead =
number of higher cards
held by dummy, declarer, and partner.
```

The Rule of 11 helps estimate partner's holding and plan the defense, but should always be combined with the auction and later play.

---

# Leading Partner's Suit

If partner has bid a suit, that suit is often the best opening lead.

Partner's bidding suggests:

- length,
- honors,
- potential entries.

Unless there is a compelling reason otherwise, supporting partner's suit is usually sound strategy.

---

# Passive Leads

Sometimes the best lead is a passive one.

Passive leads are appropriate when:

- declarer has shown a balanced hand,
- no attacking suit is obvious,
- breaking another suit is dangerous,
- the defense already appears to have sufficient tricks.

Examples include:

- a low card from a weak suit,
- an unsupported honor only when partnership methods recommend it,
- avoiding dangerous tenaces.

---

# Avoid Leading Away from Unsupported Honors

Be cautious about leading from:

```
Kx
Qx
Jx
KJx
QJx
```

Such leads may:

- establish declarer's honors,
- solve declarer's guessing problems,
- concede an immediate trick.

Unless the auction strongly suggests otherwise, these are generally poor opening leads.

---

# Using the Auction

The bidding often determines the best lead.

Consider:

- declarer's strength,
- dummy's likely distribution,
- partner's overcall,
- unbid suits,
- possible stoppers.

Example:

```
1NT — Pass — 3NT
```

The unbid major may offer the strongest attacking prospects.

---

# Leading Against Strong Notrump

Against a strong notrump opening (typically 15–17 HCP):

- attack your longest suit,
- seek immediate trick development,
- preserve entries,
- avoid speculative honor leads.

The defense must establish tricks before declarer develops nine winners.

---

# Leading Against Weak Notrump

Against a weak notrump opening:

- aggressive leads become more attractive,
- declarer is more likely to lack stoppers,
- partner is more likely to hold useful honors.

However, normal partnership agreements still apply.

---

# Communication

Entries are critical in notrump defense.

Before choosing the lead, consider:

- Can partner gain the lead?
- Can established tricks be cashed later?
- Will I still have an entry after the suit is developed?

Without communication, even a correctly chosen suit may never produce tricks.

---

# Common Errors

## Choosing the Wrong Suit

Leading a short or weak suit often helps declarer establish winners.

---

## Ignoring the Auction

The bidding frequently identifies the best attacking suit.

---

## Breaking Unsupported Honors

Leading away from isolated honors often gifts declarer an extra trick.

---

## Forgetting Entries

A long suit is useless if the defenders cannot regain the lead.

---

## Misapplying the Rule of 11

The Rule of 11 is helpful only when the partnership actually uses fourth-best leads.

---

# Practical Questions

Before making the opening lead, ask:

- Which suit is most likely to produce tricks?
- Has partner suggested a suit?
- Can we establish this suit before declarer gains control?
- Do I have entries to cash later winners?
- Would a passive lead be safer?
- What does the auction suggest about declarer's weak suits?

# Practical Tips

- Lead the top of an honor sequence whenever possible.
- Otherwise, prefer fourth-highest from your longest and strongest suit.
- Support partner's bid suit unless another lead is clearly superior.
- Count entries before attacking a long suit.
- Use the auction to identify declarer's likely weaknesses.
- Avoid speculative leads from unsupported honors.
- Begin counting declarer's winners as soon as dummy appears.

---

# Key Takeaways

- Against notrump, the defense usually wins by establishing long-suit tricks before declarer develops enough winners.
- The best opening leads are typically honor sequences, fourth-highest from the longest suit, or partner's bid suit.
- The auction, partnership agreements, and communication all influence the correct lead.
- Successful notrump defense depends on both **choosing the right suit** and **having the entries to cash it later**.
- A well-chosen opening lead often determines the outcome of the entire contract.
