# Third-and-Fifth Leads

## Overview

**Third-and-Fifth Leads** are an opening lead agreement in which the leader uses:

- **Third-highest** card from an odd number of cards in a suit.
- **Fifth-highest** card from an even number of cards in a suit.

The convention is designed to help partner estimate the leader's suit length more accurately than with standard fourth-best leads.

Third-and-Fifth is used primarily against **notrump contracts**, although some partnerships also apply it against suit contracts.

---

# Basic Principle

From a suit headed by an honor or with no attractive honor sequence:

- **Odd number of cards → Lead the third-highest.**
- **Even number of cards → Lead the fifth-highest.**

Examples:

| Holding | Lead |
|---------|------|
| A J 8 | 8 |
| K J 9 | 9 |
| Q 10 7 | 7 |
| K 10 8 4 | 4 |
| A J 9 6 | 6 |
| 9 7 5 3 2 | 5 |

The lead gives partner clues about whether the suit length is odd or even.

---

# Why Use Third-and-Fifth?

Compared with fourth-best leads, Third-and-Fifth helps partner:

- Estimate suit length.
- Count the suit more accurately.
- Determine whether the leader started with an odd or even number of cards.
- Plan defensive communications.

---

# Comparison with Fourth-Best Leads

Consider:

```
♠ K 9 7 4
```

### Fourth-Best

Lead:

```
♠4
```

### Third-and-Fifth

Lead:

```
♠4
```

No difference.

Now consider:

```
♠ K 10 7
```

### Fourth-Best

Not applicable (only three cards).

Many partnerships would lead the lowest card.

### Third-and-Fifth

Lead:

```
♠7
```

Partner immediately knows the suit contains an odd number of cards.

# Typical Lead Table

| Holding | Lead |
|---------|------|
| A J 8 | 8 |
| K J 9 | 9 |
| Q 8 6 | 6 |
| K 10 8 4 | 4 |
| A 9 7 5 | 5 |
| J 9 8 6 2 | 2 |

From touching honor sequences, partnerships normally continue to use their agreed honor leads (standard, Rusinow, etc.).

---

# Against Notrump Contracts

Third-and-Fifth is most valuable against notrump because:

- Long suits often produce defensive tricks.
- Suit length is critical.
- Partner needs accurate count information.
- Declarer cannot ruff established winners.

---

# Against Suit Contracts

Some partnerships continue using Third-and-Fifth.

Others prefer:

- Standard leads.
- Top of Nothing.
- Rusinow.
- Passive leads.
- Trump leads.

Partnership agreement is essential.

---

# Example 1

Holding:

```
♣ A J 8
```

Against 3NT

Lead:

```
♣8
```

Partner infers:

- Odd number of cards.
- Likely three-card holding.

---

# Example 2

Holding:

```
♦ K 10 8 4
```

Lead:

```
♦4
```

Partner infers:

- Even number of cards.
- Probably four cards.

---

# Example 3

Holding:

```
♥ 9 7 5 3 2
```

Lead:

```
♥5
```

The 5 is the third-highest card, showing an odd-length suit.

---

# Advantages

- Better suit-length estimation.
- Improved defensive counting.
- Easier continuation decisions.
- Strong partnership communication.
- Particularly useful in notrump defense.

---

# Disadvantages

- Less familiar than fourth-best leads.
- Can reveal more information to declarer.
- Requires accurate partnership memory.
- May complicate mixed lead agreements.

---

# Interaction with Honor Leads

Third-and-Fifth applies only to leads **from length without a touching honor sequence**.

Example:

```
♠ K Q 8 5
```

Lead:

```
♠K
```

not:

```
♠8
```

Honor-sequence agreements always take precedence.

# Common Mistakes

- Leading fourth-best instead of third-highest from odd-length suits.
- Forgetting to use the fifth-highest from even-length suits.
- Applying Third-and-Fifth to honor sequences.
- Mixing Third-and-Fifth with fourth-best methods.
- Assuming all partnerships use this agreement.

# Comparison of Lead Styles

| Method | Main Principle | Best Use |
|---------|----------------|----------|
| Fourth-Best | Fourth-highest from length | Standard worldwide |
| Third-and-Fifth | Third from odd, fifth from even | Accurate suit-length inference |
| Top of Nothing | Highest from weak suits | Suit contracts |
| Rusinow | Second-highest from honor sequence | Expert partnerships |

---

# Key Principles

- Lead the **third-highest** card from an odd-length suit.
- Lead the **fifth-highest** card from an even-length suit.
- Continue to lead the agreed honor card from touching honor sequences.
- Third-and-Fifth is primarily a notrump opening-lead agreement.
- Use it only when both partners understand and apply the method consistently.
