# Opening Leads from KQ

## Overview

The holding **King-Queen (KQ)** is one of the classic **touching honor sequences** used for opening leads. Because the honors are consecutive, leading the **King** is standard in most bridge systems.

A lead from KQ is intended to:

- Establish the suit.
- Help partner identify the honor holding.
- Develop tricks before declarer can establish winners.

The exact continuation depends on the contract type, partner's signal, and the partnership's opening lead agreements.

The guiding principle is:

> **Lead the top of a touching honor sequence.**

---

# Purpose

Opening leads from KQ aim to:

- Establish a long suit.
- Force declarer's Ace.
- Promote additional winners.
- Help partner identify the holding.
- Improve defensive communication.

---

# Standard Expert Treatment

Most expert partnerships agree:

| Holding | Lead |
|---------|------|
| KQ | **King** |
| KQJ | **King** |
| KQ10 | **King** |
| KQJ10 | **King** |

The King promises at least the Queen and usually denies the Ace.

---

# Against Notrump Contracts

## Objectives

- Establish the suit quickly.
- Force out declarer's Ace.
- Create winners before declarer establishes a long suit.

### Example

Holding

```
♠ K Q 8 5 2
```

Contract

```
3NT
```

Lead:

```
♠K
```

If declarer wins with the Ace, the remaining spades may become established winners.

---

# Against Suit Contracts

## Objectives

- Cash immediate tricks when appropriate.
- Force declarer's Ace.
- Avoid helping declarer establish side suits.

Example

Holding

```
♥ K Q 7 4
```

Contract

```
4♠
```

Lead:

```
♥K
```

Partner's signal helps determine whether to continue.

---

# Partner's Signals

The exact signal depends on partnership agreements.

## Attitude

Many partnerships use:

- High = Encourage.
- Low = Discourage.

Useful against Notrump when deciding whether to continue.

---

## Count

Some partnerships request count after a King lead.

### Standard Count

```
High-Low = Even

Low-High = Odd
```

### UDCA

```
High-Low = Odd

Low-High = Even
```

Count is especially useful in suit contracts.

---

## Suit Preference

When count and attitude are already known, partner may use Suit Preference to recommend the best switch.

---

# Example 1 – Force the Ace

Holding

```
♦ K Q 10 5
```

Lead:

```
♦K
```

Declarer wins the Ace.

The remaining diamonds later become winners.

---

# Example 2 – Continue the Suit

Holding

```
♣ K Q 9 4
```

Partner encourages.

Continue clubs.

The defense establishes the suit.

---

# Example 3 – Switch

Holding

```
♠ K Q 8 4
```

Partner discourages.

Rather than continuing spades, switch to another suit.

Correct timing may defeat the contract.

---

# Planning the Defense

Before leading KQ, ask:

- Is this my strongest suit?
- Am I trying to establish the suit?
- Does the auction suggest another lead?
- Will partner recognize the King as a sequence lead?
- Should I continue after Trick One?

---

# Special Considerations

### Missing the Ace

A KQ lead normally suggests the Ace is elsewhere.

Partner should avoid assuming the leader holds AK unless the partnership has special agreements.

---

### Broken Sequences

KQ differs from:

```
KJ
```

or

```
K10
```

because KQ is a true touching sequence.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Leading the Queen instead of the King.
- Continuing after a discouraging signal.
- Forgetting partnership agreements.
- Automatically cashing multiple honors.
- Ignoring the auction.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Before play, discuss:

- Does the King always promise the Queen?
- What signal is requested after the King lead?
- Differences between Notrump and suit contracts.
- When Suit Preference applies.
- Whether special honor-lead methods (e.g., Journalist or Rusinow) are used.

---

# Advantages

- Clearly identifies a touching sequence.
- Helps establish long suits.
- Improves defensive communication.
- Standard treatment recognized by most players.
- Flexible continuation based on partner's signal.

---

# Disadvantages

- May lose tempo if continued incorrectly.
- Requires partnership agreement on follow-up signals.
- Can be affected by deceptive carding.

# Defender's Checklist

Before leading KQ:

- Am I leading from a true touching sequence?
- Does our partnership lead the King from KQ?
- What information do I want from partner?
- Should I continue after the signal?
- Is another suit a better opening lead?

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Identify the touching sequence.
2. Lead the King.
3. Read partner's signal.
4. Continue or switch.
5. Reevaluate after each trick.

# Memory Aid

**KQ Leads = "Top of the Touching Sequence."**

Remember:

- **Lead the King from KQ and longer touching sequences in standard methods.**
- **The King normally promises the Queen and usually denies the Ace.**
- **Partner's signal determines whether the suit should be continued.**
- **Do not continue automatically—consider the auction, dummy, and partner's information.**
- **Strong partnership agreements make KQ leads more effective.**

Opening leads from **KQ** are among the most fundamental defensive treatments in bridge. By leading the top of the touching honor sequence and interpreting partner's signal correctly, defenders can establish long suits efficiently, coordinate their defense, and maximize their chances of defeating the contract.
