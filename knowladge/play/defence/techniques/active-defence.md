# Active Defence

## Overview

**Active defence** is a defensive strategy that seeks to **create defensive tricks before declarer can establish additional winners**. Rather than waiting for declarer to make mistakes, the defenders take the initiative by attacking vulnerable suits, forcing declarer, promoting trump tricks, or disrupting declarer's timing.

Active defence is often essential when passive play would allow declarer to complete the contract comfortably.

The guiding principle is:

> **When time favors declarer, the defense must seize the initiative.**

---

# Objectives

An active defence aims to:

- Establish defensive winners.
- Attack declarer's weak suits.
- Disrupt declarer's timing and communication.
- Force declarer into difficult decisions.
- Prevent declarer from developing extra tricks.
- Defeat the contract before declarer gains control.

---

# Basic Principle

Active defence is based on **tempo**.

If declarer is about to establish a long suit, execute a squeeze, complete a crossruff, or eliminate losers, the defenders often cannot afford to wait.

Immediate action is required to create tricks while the opportunity still exists.

---

# When to Choose Active Defence

Active defence is usually correct when:

- Declarer already has many immediate winners.
- The defense must establish tricks before entries disappear.
- Declarer threatens to establish a long suit.
- Trump promotion is possible.
- Declarer is delaying drawing trumps.
- Waiting clearly benefits declarer.

In these situations, passive defence often loses by giving declarer too much time.

---

# Characteristics of Active Defence

An active defence commonly involves:

- attacking an unestablished suit,
- switching aggressively,
- forcing declarer to ruff,
- continuing partner's attacking suit,
- promoting trump tricks,
- knocking out declarer's entries.

The defenders are willing to accept some risk in exchange for creating opportunities.

---

# Typical Active Plays

## Attacking a Weak Suit

If declarer appears vulnerable in a side suit, switching immediately may establish defensive winners before declarer can protect the position.

---

## Continuing Partner's Suit

Partner's opening lead often identifies the best attacking suit.

Persisting with that attack may establish long-suit winners before declarer gains control.

---

## Forcing Declarer

Repeatedly leading a suit that declarer must ruff shortens declarer's trump holding and reduces control.

---

## Trump Promotion

Active defence frequently seeks to promote intermediate trumps by forcing declarer to ruff high or by creating uppercut positions.

---

## Knocking Out Entries

Against notrump contracts, attacking declarer's communication may prevent established long suits from becoming winners.

---

# Active Defence in Notrump Contracts

In notrump, active defence often means:

- attacking the defenders' long suit,
- removing declarer's stopper,
- establishing winners before entries disappear,
- forcing declarer to use key honors early.

Because tempo is critical, delaying the attack is often fatal.

---

# Active Defence in Trump Contracts

In suit contracts, active defence commonly includes:

- forcing declarer,
- promoting trumps,
- attacking before trumps are drawn,
- preventing crossruffs,
- cashing side winners before they disappear.

Correct timing is essential.

---

# Auction Clues

The auction often indicates when active defence is required.

Examples include:

- Declarer has shown a strong balanced hand in notrump.
- Opponents have bid confidently toward game or slam.
- Partner has bid or overcalled a suit.
- Declarer has described a source of extra tricks.

These clues help identify the most promising attacking plan.

---

# Risks of Active Defence

Because active defence is aggressive, it carries risks.

A poorly chosen attack may:

- establish declarer's suit,
- give away a finesse,
- concede a trick,
- open a previously safe suit.

Active defence should therefore be based on evidence from the auction and play—not guesswork.

---

# Active vs. Passive Defence

## Active Defence

Focuses on:

- creating tricks,
- attacking immediately,
- disrupting declarer,
- accepting calculated risk.

Best when:

- the defense must act before declarer develops winners.

---

## Passive Defence

Focuses on:

- avoiding gifts,
- preserving winners,
- forcing declarer to solve problems,
- minimizing risk.

Best when:

- declarer still has difficult decisions and time favors the defense.

Expert defenders know when to shift from one style to the other as the hand develops.

---

# Common Defensive Errors

## Waiting Too Long

The greatest mistake in active defence is failing to attack before declarer establishes the contract.

---

## Attacking the Wrong Suit

An aggressive switch without evidence often helps declarer instead of the defense.

---

## Ignoring Partner's Information

Signals, bidding, and the opening lead frequently identify the correct attacking suit.

---

## Cashing Winners Prematurely

Sometimes preserving an entry is more valuable than taking an immediate trick.

---

## Continuing an Unsuccessful Attack

If the original attacking plan no longer works, the defense should reassess rather than persist automatically.

---

# Practical Questions

Before choosing an active defence, ask:

- Does declarer already have enough winners?
- Must the defense establish tricks immediately?
- Which suit offers the best attacking prospects?
- Can partner regain the lead?
- Will waiting improve declarer's position?
- Is the potential gain worth the risk?

# Practical Tips

- Develop an attacking plan as soon as dummy appears.
- Use the auction and opening lead to identify the best suit to attack.
- Preserve entries needed to continue the attack.
- Force declarer before trumps are drawn whenever appropriate.
- Switch aggressively only when there is a clear objective.
- Continuously reassess whether active defence is still required.
- Remember that tempo is often the defense's greatest weapon.

---

# Key Takeaways

- Active defence seeks to create tricks before declarer can establish additional winners.
- It is driven by tempo and the need to seize the initiative.
- Successful active defence combines sound judgment, communication, and accurate timing.
- Aggression should be based on evidence rather than speculation.
- Knowing when to attack is one of the defining skills of expert defenders.
