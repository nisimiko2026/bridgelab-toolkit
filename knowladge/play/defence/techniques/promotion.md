# Promotion Plays

## Overview

**Promotion Plays** are techniques in which a player deliberately creates a new winning card that was **not originally a winner**. Through careful timing, forcing plays, or tactical card combinations, declarer or the defenders "promote" an otherwise losing card into a winning trick.

Promotion is most commonly associated with **trump promotion** on defense, but promotion can also occur in side suits when intermediate cards become established after higher cards are removed.

Understanding promotion opportunities is essential for both declarers and defenders.

---

# Purpose

Promotion Plays allow players to:

- Create extra tricks.
- Force opponents to use high cards.
- Promote intermediate cards into winners.
- Improve trick-taking efficiency.
- Defeat or make close contracts.

---

# Basic Principle

The guiding principle is:

> **Force higher cards to disappear so lower cards become winners.**

Promotion usually results from:

- Forcing plays.
- Overruffs.
- Honor exchanges.
- Elimination of higher cards.
- Tactical timing.

---

# Types of Promotion

## 1. Trump Promotion

The most common type.

The defense forces declarer to use high trumps, allowing a defender's lower trump to become a winner.

Example:

```
Declarer
♠ AQJ

West
♠ 109

East
♠ K4
```

Repeated forcing eventually promotes West's ♠10 into a winning trump.

---

## 2. Uppercut

A special form of Trump Promotion.

One defender overruffs declarer, promoting partner's intermediate trump into a winner.

Example:

```
Declarer ruffs with ♠Q

East overruffs with ♠K

West's ♠10 later becomes a winner.
```

---

## 3. Honor Promotion

An intermediate honor becomes a winner after higher honors are forced out.

Example:

```
Dummy
♦ AQ

Declarer
♦ J10

Missing
♦ K
```

After the King is driven out, the Jack becomes a promoted winner.

---

## 4. Long Suit Promotion

Small cards become winners after all higher cards have been played.

Example:

```
♣ AKQJ765
```

Once defenders' clubs are exhausted, the remaining small clubs are promoted into winners.

---

## 5. Entry Promotion

A card that was not originally an entry becomes one after higher cards are removed.

This often occurs in squeeze endings and long-suit play.

---

# Planning the Play

Before Trick One, ask:

- Which cards might become winners later?
- Can higher cards be forced out?
- Can a trump be promoted?
- Can I establish a long suit?
- Is an Uppercut possible?

---

# Timing

Timing is critical.

General sequence:

1. Count winners or losers.
2. Identify promotion opportunities.
3. Preserve entries.
4. Force higher cards.
5. Cash promoted winners.

---

# Common Situations

Promotion frequently occurs:

- During forcing defense.
- In Uppercuts.
- In long-suit establishment.
- During squeezes.
- In end positions.
- During trump battles.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Missing a forcing opportunity.
- Drawing trumps too early.
- Failing to establish the long suit.
- Wasting intermediate cards.
- Ignoring promotion possibilities.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Against declarer's promotion attempts, defenders should:

- Preserve higher cards.
- Attack entries.
- Prevent long-suit establishment.
- Force declarer to spend entries.
- Count remaining honors.

When seeking defensive promotion, defenders should:

- Continue forcing.
- Coordinate Uppercuts.
- Preserve intermediate trumps.

---

# Advantages

- Creates tricks that did not originally exist.
- Improves efficiency.
- Supports advanced techniques.
- Useful for both declarer and defenders.
- Frequently determines close contracts.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires careful planning.
- Timing is essential.
- Easy to overlook.
- Often depends on accurate counting.

# Declarer's and Defender's Checklist

Before attempting a Promotion Play:

- Which cards can become winners?
- Can higher cards be removed?
- Are sufficient entries available?
- Is timing favorable?
- Will the promotion create an additional trick?

# Memory Aid

**Promotion Plays = "Create Winners That Didn't Exist."**

Remember:

- **Promotion is about creating new winners, not simply cashing existing ones.**
- **Force higher cards to disappear.**
- **Look for promotion opportunities in both trumps and side suits.**
- **Preserve entries to enjoy the promoted trick.**
- **Good timing is the key to successful promotion.**

Promotion Plays are fundamental to expert bridge. Whether promoting a defender's trump through an Uppercut, establishing a long suit, or turning an intermediate honor into a winner, the ability to recognize and exploit promotion opportunities often makes the difference between making and defeating a contract.
