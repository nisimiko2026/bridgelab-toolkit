# Counting Winners Technique

## Overview

**Counting Winners** is one of the first and most important skills in declarer play. Before playing to the first trick, declarer should determine **how many sure winners** are immediately available and how many additional winners must be developed to make the contract.

Counting winners is used primarily in **Notrump contracts**, where declarer cannot normally ruff losers. It forms the foundation of declarer's planning and often determines the entire line of play.

---

# Purpose

Counting Winners allows declarer to:

- Determine whether the contract can be made.
- Identify which suits require development.
- Plan the order of play.
- Preserve entries to established winners.
- Avoid unnecessary risks.

---

# Basic Principle

Before playing from dummy, ask:

> **"How many immediate winners do I have?"**

Then compare that number with the number of tricks required.

For example:

- Contract: **3NT**
- Required tricks: **9**
- Immediate winners: **7**

Declarer must develop **2 additional winners**.

---

# When to Use

Counting Winners is essential in:

- Notrump contracts.
- Slam contracts.
- Suit contracts after trumps have been drawn.
- Any hand requiring careful planning.

---

# What Is a Winner?

A **winner** is a card that will take a trick immediately if played.

Examples:

```
♠ A
♥ AK
♦ KQ (after the Ace has been played)
♣ Q (after higher honors are gone)
```

Potential winners are **not** counted until they have been established.

---

# Immediate Winners

Example:

```
Dummy
♠ AQ
♥ KJ
♦ A42
♣ KQ5

Declarer
♠ K3
♥ AQ
♦ K53
♣ A74
```

Immediate winners:

- Spades: 2
- Hearts: 3
- Diamonds: 2
- Clubs: 3

Total:

**10 winners**

No further development is required.

---

# Example 1 – 3NT Contract

Contract

```
3NT
```

Required:

```
9 tricks
```

Immediate winners:

- Spades: 2
- Hearts: 2
- Diamonds: 2
- Clubs: 1

Total:

**7 winners**

Declarer must develop **2 more winners**, perhaps by establishing a long suit or taking a finesse.

---

# Example 2 – Establishing a Long Suit

Dummy

```
♣ AKQJ7
```

Declarer

```
♣ 43
```

Initially:

Only the top honors are immediate winners.

After driving out the opponents' remaining club honors, the small clubs become established winners.

---

# Potential Winners

Potential winners are tricks that can be created by:

- Driving out higher honors.
- Establishing a long suit.
- Taking a finesse.
- Ruffing losers.
- Executing a squeeze.

Do **not** count them until you have a plan to establish them.

---

# Counting in Suit Contracts

Although **Counting Losers** is usually preferred in suit contracts, counting winners is still valuable after:

- Drawing trumps.
- Establishing side suits.
- Planning a slam.

---

# Planning the Play

Before Trick One:

1. Count immediate winners.
2. Count required tricks.
3. Identify additional winners needed.
4. Decide which suit to develop.
5. Preserve entries to cash established winners.

---

# Entry Management

Counting winners must be combined with good **Entry Management**.

Example:

Dummy

```
♣ AKQJ7
```

Declarer

```
♣ 32
```

If declarer has no entry to dummy after establishing clubs, the winners cannot be cashed.

An established winner is useless if it cannot be reached.

---

# Choosing the Right Suit

If additional winners are required, declarer should usually develop the suit that:

- Has the highest probability of success.
- Requires the fewest tricks to establish.
- Preserves communication.
- Minimizes risk.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Counting potential winners as immediate winners.
- Forgetting entries.
- Developing the wrong suit.
- Failing to plan before Trick One.
- Cashing winners in the wrong order.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Attack declarer's entries.
- Prevent long suits from being established.
- Force declarer to guess.
- Count declarer's likely winners.
- Switch suits when necessary.

---

# Advantages

- Provides a clear plan.
- Simplifies declarer play.
- Prevents unnecessary risks.
- Essential in notrump contracts.
- Improves timing.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires accurate counting.
- Entries must also be considered.
- Suit development may fail.
- New information during play may change the plan.

# Declarer's Checklist

Before Trick One:

- How many immediate winners do I have?
- How many tricks are required?
- Which suit should I develop?
- Do I have enough entries?
- Which line gives me the greatest chance of success?

---

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Count immediate winners.
2. Count required tricks.
3. Identify missing winners.
4. Develop the best suit.
5. Preserve entries.

---

# Comparison

| Counting Losers | Count unavoidable losers |

---

# Typical Planning Process

```
Count Winners
↓

Need More Tricks?

↓

Yes

↓

Choose Best Suit

↓

Develop Winners

↓

Cash Established Tricks
```

---

# Memory Aid

**Counting Winners = "Count First, Play Second."**

Remember:

- **Count only immediate winners.**
- **Do not count potential winners until they are established.**
- **Develop the easiest additional tricks.**
- **Protect your entries to established winners.**
- **Recount after every important trick.**

Counting Winners is the cornerstone of declarer play in notrump contracts. Expert players always begin by determining exactly how many tricks they already have, then create a logical plan for developing the remaining winners needed to fulfill the contract.
