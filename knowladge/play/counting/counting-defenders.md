# Counting Defenders

## Overview

Counting the defenders' hands is one of the most powerful skills in bridge. By combining information from the auction, opening lead, signals, and previous tricks, declarer can often determine the exact distribution and location of key cards before making critical decisions.

## Why Count the Defenders?

- Improve finesse decisions
- Locate missing honors
- Choose the correct line of play
- Plan squeezes and endplays
- Avoid guesswork

## Sources of Information

### The Auction

- Length shown
- Strength shown
- Suit preferences
- Denials

### Opening Lead

- Fourth-best
- Top of sequence
- Singleton or doubleton
- Lead agreements

### Defender's Play

- Following suit
- Discards
- Signals
- Tempo (where appropriate)

## Counting Distribution

Determine:

- Number of cards in each suit
- Remaining trumps
- Likely shortages

Example:
- West has shown five spades.
- East followed twice then discarded.
- Therefore...

## Counting High-Card Points

Estimate missing HCP using:

- Auction
- Opening lead
- Revealed honors

Example:
West opened 1♥ (12–21 HCP).
Dummy has 14 HCP.
Declarer has 12 HCP.
Therefore East has at most...

## Placing Individual Cards

Once enough information is available:

- Locate the Queen
- Determine which defender holds a stopper
- Decide whether a finesse is necessary

## Updating the Count

After every trick:

- Recount distribution
- Update HCP
- Revise assumptions

## Common Mistakes

- Counting only one defender
- Ignoring auction information
- Forgetting previously played cards
- Drawing conclusions too early
