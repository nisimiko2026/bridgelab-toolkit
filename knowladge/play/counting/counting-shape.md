# Counting Shape

## Overview

Counting shape is the process of determining the exact or probable distribution of an unseen hand by combining information from the auction and the play.

Unlike counting high-card points, counting shape focuses only on the number of cards held in each suit.

## Why Count Shape?

- Locate shortages
- Determine remaining trumps
- Improve finesse decisions
- Plan squeezes and endplays
- Predict discards
- Place individual cards

## Sources of Information

### The Auction

- Opening bids
- Responses
- Rebids
- Preempts
- Doubles
- Cue bids

### The Play

- Opening lead
- Following suit
- Ruffs
- Discards
- Signals

## Counting Distribution

Determine:

- Known suit lengths
- Remaining unknown cards
- Possible distributions

Example:

West opens 1♠.

Later shows a sixth spade.

East follows twice then discards.

West's shape is becoming known.

## Typical Shapes

- 4-4-3-2
- 5-3-3-2
- 5-4-3-1
- 6-3-2-2
- Balanced hands
- Unbalanced hands

## Using Shape in Declarer Play

Examples:

- Choosing a finesse
- Planning an endplay
- Timing a squeeze
- Deciding whether to play for the drop

## Using Shape in Defence

Examples:

- Finding partner's shortage
- Determining declarer's side suit
- Choosing a switch
- Planning a forcing defence

## Updating the Count

After every trick:

- Confirm known lengths
- Eliminate impossible shapes
- Refine the remaining possibilities

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming balanced shape too early
- Ignoring auction information
- Forgetting previous discards
- Not revising the count
