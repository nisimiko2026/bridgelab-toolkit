# Preservation of Entries

## Overview

**Preservation of entries** is a fundamental card-play technique in which declarer or a defender carefully manages access between hands. The objective is to ensure that high cards, established winners, or long suits can be reached when needed.

Many otherwise successful contracts fail because a player establishes winners but cannot reach them. Expert players plan their entries before playing to the first trick.

Entry preservation is important in both **declarer play** and **defense**.

---

# What Is an Entry?

An **entry** is any card that allows a player to gain the lead and reach a particular hand.

Examples include:

- An ace.
- A guarded king.
- A high trump.
- An established winner.
- Occasionally a finesse that succeeds.

Without an entry, winners in the opposite hand may become stranded.

---

# Why Entries Matter

Proper entry management allows you to:

- Cash established winners.
- Reach long suits.
- Take finesses.
- Execute squeezes.
- Perform endplays.
- Maintain communication between hands.

Poor entry management can make even perfectly established tricks worthless.

---

# Planning Before Trick One

Before playing from dummy at trick one, ask:

- Where are my winners?
- Which hand contains the long suit?
- How many entries does each hand have?
- Which entries are essential?
- Which entries can be spent safely?

Expert declarers often identify entry problems before choosing their first play.

---

# Preserving Entries to Dummy

A common mistake is using dummy's entries too early.

Example:

```
Dummy
♠ A 4
♥ K 8 5
♦ Q 7
♣ A J 10 8 5

Declarer
♠ K Q
♥ A Q 4
♦ A K 6
♣ 7 6 4 2
```

If declarer unnecessarily cashes the ♣A early, the remaining club winners may become unreachable.

Instead, preserve the ace until the club suit has been established.

---

# Preserving Entries to Hand

Sometimes declarer must preserve entries to the closed hand instead.

Example:

```
Dummy
♦ A Q J 10

Declarer
♦ K 6
```

If declarer wins an early trick with the ♦A instead of the ♦K, later access to dummy's remaining diamond winners may be lost.

Choosing the correct honor to win with can preserve valuable communication.

---

# Overtaking to Preserve Entries

Sometimes the correct play is to overtake partner's winner or your own honor.

Example:

```
Dummy
♣ A Q 10

Declarer
♣ K J 9
```

If communication requires declarer to remain in hand, overtaking may preserve a later entry to dummy.

The decision depends on the overall plan.

---

# Entries and Long Suits

Long suits often require entries after they are established.

Typical sequence:

1. Lose a trick to establish the suit.
2. Regain the lead.
3. Use a preserved entry.
4. Cash the long-suit winners.

Without Step 3, the established winners may never be collected.

---

# Entries and Finesses

Many finesses require repeated access to one hand.

Example:

```
Dummy
♠ A J 8 4

Declarer
♠ K 7 5
```

If declarer wastes the only entry to dummy before taking the finesse, the opportunity is lost.

---

# Entries in Notrump Contracts

Entry preservation is particularly important in notrump because:

- Long suits produce extra tricks.
- Communication cannot be restored by ruffing.
- Timing is critical.
- Hold-up plays often depend on preserved entries.

---

# Entries in Suit Contracts

Suit contracts provide additional flexibility because trumps can sometimes create new entries.

However, declarer must still avoid:

- Drawing all the trumps too early.
- Ruffing away valuable entries.
- Using high trumps unnecessarily.

---

# Defensive Entry Preservation

Defenders also manage entries carefully.

Typical objectives include:

- Reaching partner's established suit.
- Returning partner's lead.
- Giving partner a ruff.
- Continuing an attack after winning an honor.

Sometimes a defender ducks a trick specifically to preserve a later entry.

---

# Example 1

Contract:

```
3NT
```

Dummy:

```
♣ A K 10 8 6
```

Declarer:

```
♣ 7 5 4
```

Declarer first establishes the club suit, then later uses the preserved ♣A as the entry to cash the remaining club winners.

---

# Example 2

Dummy:

```
♥ A J
```

Declarer:

```
♥ K Q 4
```

Winning an early heart trick with the ♥K instead of the ♥A preserves the ace as a later entry to dummy.

---

# Example 3

Declarer has one remaining entry to dummy.

Instead of cashing an immediate side-suit winner, declarer first establishes a long suit, then uses the preserved entry to reach the newly established winners.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Using entries too early.
- Establishing a suit without a way to reach it later.
- Winning with the wrong honor.
- Forgetting that a finesse may require multiple entries.
- Drawing trumps before creating necessary communications.
- Failing to plan the entire hand before playing to trick one.

# Key Principles

- Winners are valuable only if you can reach them.
- Count your entries before playing to the first trick.
- Preserve essential entries until they are needed.
- Choose carefully which honor wins a trick to maintain communication.
- Entry preservation is a critical skill for both declarer and defenders.
