# Matchpoints vs. IMPs

## Overview

The two primary scoring methods in duplicate bridge are **Matchpoints (Pairs)** and **International Match Points (IMPs) (Teams)**. Although the laws of bridge are identical, the **best strategy can be very different** depending on the scoring method.

At **Matchpoints**, every board is a separate contest against the entire field. Small score differences matter, and every overtrick can be valuable.

At **IMPs**, the objective is to gain or avoid large swings. Making the contract is usually more important than collecting overtricks.

The guiding principle is:

> **At Matchpoints, maximize your score. At IMPs, maximize your expected gain while minimizing risk.**

---

# Overview Comparison

| Feature | Matchpoints | IMPs |
|----------|-------------|------|
| Competition | Pairs | Teams |
| Goal | Beat as many pairs as possible | Win by the largest point margin |
| Importance of Overtricks | Very high | Usually low |
| Importance of Making the Contract | High | Critical |
| Risk Tolerance | Higher | Lower |
| Safety Plays | Less common | Very common |

---

# How Scoring Works

## Matchpoints

Each board is compared with every other pair that played the same deal.

- Beat another score → 2 Matchpoints
- Tie another score → 1 Matchpoint
- Lose to another score → 0 Matchpoints

Every additional 10 or 20 points may move a pair from the bottom of the field to the top.

---

## IMPs

Scores from two tables are compared.

The point difference is converted into **International Match Points (IMPs)** using the standard IMP conversion table.

Small score differences produce small IMP gains, while large swings produce larger IMP gains.

---

# Bidding Strategy

## Matchpoints

Generally:

- Bid close games aggressively.
- Compete actively for part-scores.
- Stretch slightly for thin games when favorable.
- Fight for every extra score.

Example:

```
Part-score battle

110 vs 140

Winning the contract may produce a top board.
```

---

## IMPs

Generally:

- Bid games that have a reasonable chance.
- Avoid speculative contracts.
- Bid sound slams.
- Protect against large penalties.

The emphasis is on expected value, not small gains.

---

# Declarer Play

## Matchpoints

Priorities:

- Make the contract.
- Maximize overtricks.
- Accept small risks for extra tricks.
- Choose aggressive lines when appropriate.

Example:

```
3NT

Nine tricks guaranteed.

An extra finesse may produce ten tricks.

Often correct.
```

---

## IMPs

Priorities:

- Secure the contract.
- Protect against bad breaks.
- Use safety plays.
- Avoid unnecessary risk.

Example:

```
3NT

Nine tricks guaranteed.

A risky finesse for an overtrick is usually rejected.
```

---

# Defensive Strategy

## Matchpoints

Defenders often:

- Attack aggressively.
- Try to defeat contracts.
- Prevent overtricks.
- Seek every extra trick.

---

## IMPs

Defenders often:

- Focus on defeating the contract.
- Avoid giving declarer unnecessary gifts.
- Prefer passive defense when attacking is dangerous.
- Protect against large swings.

---

# Overtricks

## Matchpoints

Overtricks are often worth nearly as much as bidding an extra contract.

Example:

```
3NT +1

430
```

versus

```
3NT

400
```

Those 30 points may move a pair from average to top.

---

## IMPs

The same 30-point difference is:

```
1 IMP
```

Usually not worth taking significant risk.

---

# Safety Plays

## Matchpoints

Sometimes rejected.

Example:

```
95% chance of making
with an overtrick possibility.

Preferred.
```

---

## IMPs

Safety plays are frequently correct.

Example:

```
100% chance of making

No overtrick.

Preferred.
```

Making the contract is the priority.

---

# Slam Bidding

## Matchpoints

Thin slams are often avoided.

Missing a slam usually costs less than going down.

---

## IMPs

Sound slams should be bid.

Making a vulnerable slam often produces a major IMP swing.

---

# Competitive Auctions

## Matchpoints

Compete aggressively for:

- Part-scores.
- Overcalls.
- Balancing actions.

Winning a part-score battle may be worth a top board.

---

## IMPs

Competition should remain disciplined.

Avoid expensive sacrifices unless clearly profitable.

---

# Common Decisions

| Situation | Matchpoints | IMPs |
|-----------|-------------|------|
| Take a finesse for an overtrick? | Usually Yes | Usually No |
| Safety play? | Sometimes No | Usually Yes |
| Stretch for game? | Sometimes Yes | Only with reasonable odds |
| Bid a close slam? | Rarely | More often if percentages justify it |
| Fight for a part-score? | Aggressively | Selectively |
| Double a marginal contract? | More aggressively | More cautiously |

---

# Example 1 – Overtrick

Contract:

```
4♠
```

Safe line:

```
10 tricks
```

Aggressive line:

```
11 tricks if successful.

Down one if unsuccessful.
```

### Matchpoints

Often choose the aggressive line.

### IMPs

Take the safe line.

---

# Example 2 – Thin Game

Contract options:

```
2♥

or

4♥
```

### Matchpoints

Game may be worth bidding if chances are close.

### IMPs

Bid only if the game is a good favorite.

---

# Example 3 – Safety Play

Holding:

```
A Q 10 x

opposite

K x x x
```

One line guarantees the contract.

Another gains an overtrick if successful.

### Matchpoints

Consider the aggressive line.

### IMPs

Choose the guaranteed contract.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Playing Matchpoint strategy at IMPs.
- Taking unnecessary risks at IMPs.
- Rejecting safety plays.
- Ignoring overtricks at Matchpoints.
- Forgetting the scoring method before the first trick.

---

# Advantages

## Matchpoints

- Rewards precise technique.
- Every trick matters.
- Encourages active competition.

## IMPs

- Rewards sound judgment.
- Encourages percentage bridge.
- Reduces the importance of small swings.

# Player's Checklist

Before every board, ask:

- Is this Matchpoints or IMPs?
- Should I maximize overtricks or secure the contract?
- Is this the right time for a safety play?
- Should I compete aggressively?
- What line gives the best expected result under this scoring method?

## Five Rules

2. Adjust your bidding.
3. Adjust your declarer play.
4. Adjust your defense.
5. Reevaluate every close decision through the lens of the scoring format.

# Memory Aid

**Matchpoints = "Every Trick Counts."**

**IMPs = "Every Contract Counts."**

Remember:

- **At Matchpoints, maximize your score—even one overtrick can be decisive.**
- **At IMPs, protect the contract and avoid unnecessary swings.**
- **Safety plays are usually correct at IMPs but may cost valuable matchpoints.**
- **Aggressive competition is rewarded more often at Matchpoints.**
- **Expert players adjust every bidding and play decision to the scoring method before the first card is played.**

The ability to switch between **Matchpoint** and **IMP** strategy is one of the hallmarks of expert duplicate bridge. While the cards remain the same, the optimal decisions often change dramatically. Understanding these differences enables players to maximize their results, whether competing in a local pairs game or a world-class team championship.
