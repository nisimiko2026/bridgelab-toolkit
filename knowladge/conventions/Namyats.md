# Namyats Convention

## Purpose

The **Namyats Convention** is an artificial opening system that transfers strong four-level preemptive openings into the opposite minor. It allows the partnership to distinguish between **strong one-suited major hands** and **weak preemptive major openings**.

With Namyats:

- **4♣** shows a strong **4♥** opening.
- **4♦** shows a strong **4♠** opening.

This preserves the natural **4♥** and **4♠** openings as weak preempts.

---

# Origin

"Namyats" is **Stayman** spelled backwards.

The convention was developed as a way to describe powerful one-suited major hands while retaining the usefulness of natural preemptive major openings.

---

# Basic Structure

| Opening | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **4♣** | Artificial, strong heart hand |
| **4♦** | Artificial, strong spade hand |
| **4♥** | Natural weak preempt |
| **4♠** | Natural weak preempt |

---

# 4♣ Opening

Artificial.

Shows:

- A strong one-suited heart hand.
- Usually **8+ hearts** (sometimes a very strong 7-card suit).
- Approximately **8–9 playing tricks**.
- Too strong for a normal preempt but not suitable for a one-level opening followed by repeated jumps.

Equivalent to a strong natural **4♥** opening.

---

# 4♦ Opening

Artificial.

Shows:

- A strong one-suited spade hand.
- Usually **8+ spades** (sometimes a very strong 7-card suit).
- Approximately **8–9 playing tricks**.

Equivalent to a strong natural **4♠** opening.

---

# Typical Requirements

Most partnerships require:

- Excellent 7- or 8-card major suit.
- Approximately **15–18 HCP**, or equivalent playing strength.
- Few defensive values outside the long suit.
- Little slam interest unless partner has unexpected values.

Playing tricks are generally more important than HCP.

---

# Natural Four-Level Openings

With Namyats:

| Opening | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **4♥** | Weak preempt |
| **4♠** | Weak preempt |

Typical requirements:

- 8-card suit.
- Approximately 6–10 HCP.
- Purely preemptive.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Agree on:

- Minimum suit length (7 or 8 cards).
- Required playing tricks.
- Minimum HCP.
- Whether slam interest is expected.
- Continuations after interference.
- Whether transfers are mandatory.
- Roman Key Card style after transfer completion.

---

# Advantages

- Distinguishes strong and weak four-level major openings.
- Preserves natural weak preempts.
- Improves slam exploration.
- Gives responder immediate information about opener's strength.
- Widely used by advanced partnerships.

---

# Disadvantages

- Uses two artificial four-level openings.
- Requires partnership memory.
- Gives opponents information about the long suit.

---

# Comparison

| Opening | Without Namyats | With Namyats |
|---------|-----------------|--------------|
| 4♣ | Natural clubs | Strong hearts |
| 4♦ | Natural diamonds | Strong spades |
| 4♥ | Strong or weak (partnership style) | Weak preempt |
| 4♠ | Strong or weak (partnership style) | Weak preempt |

---

# Common Partnership Style

Most expert partnerships play:

- **4♣ = Strong hearts**
- **4♦ = Strong spades**
- **4♥ / 4♠ = Weak preempts**
- **4♥ and 4♠ responses are forced completions**
- RKCB after the transfer is completed.

Some partnerships require **8½ playing tricks** rather than a specific HCP range.

# Memory Aid

**Namyats = "Strong Majors Through the Opposite Minor."**

Remember:

- **4♣ = Strong Hearts**
- **4♦ = Strong Spades**
- **4♥ = Weak Hearts**
- **4♠ = Weak Spades**

Think of Namyats as a transfer convention at the four level. By using the opposite minor to show a powerful one-suited major hand, the partnership preserves natural weak preemptive openings while gaining a useful method for describing strong major-suit hands.
