# Fourth Suit Forcing (FSF)

## Purpose

The **Fourth Suit Forcing (FSF)** convention is used when opener and responder have bid **three different suits** naturally. A bid of the **only unbid suit** is **artificial** and asks partner for additional information rather than showing length in that suit.

FSF helps the partnership:

- Keep the auction low while exploring for game.
- Clarify hand strength and distribution.
- Search for a notrump contract.
- Investigate major-suit fits.
- Explore slam when appropriate.

---

# When It Applies

Typical auction:

```
Opener    Responder
1♣        1♥
1♠        2♦
```

The bid of **2♦** is the **fourth suit** (clubs, hearts, and spades have already been bid), so it is **artificial**.

The fourth suit bid does **not** promise diamonds.

---

# Basic Principle

A bid of the only unbid suit:

- Is **artificial**.
- Is **forcing for one round** (or to game, by partnership agreement).
- Requests additional information from opener.

It says:

> "Describe your hand more completely."

# Strength Requirements

Typical requirements:

- Invitational or better values.
- Usually **11+ HCP**.
- Interest in game.
- No clear natural rebid.

Some partnerships require enough strength for game opposite a normal opening.

---

# Opener's Priorities

After FSF, opener should:

1. Show **three-card support** for responder's major.
2. Bid **notrump** with a stopper in the fourth suit.
3. Rebid the original suit with extra length.
4. Show a second suit.
5. Make the most descriptive rebid.

---

# Opener's Rebids

## Raise Responder's Suit

Example:

```
1♣
1♥
1♠
2♦
2♥
```

Shows:

- Exactly three-card heart support.

---

## Notrump

Example:

```
1♣
1♥
1♠
2♦
2NT
```

Shows:

- Stopper in diamonds.
- Balanced hand.

---

## Rebid Original Suit

Example:

```
1♦
1♠
2♣
2♥
3♦
```

Shows:

- Extra diamond length.

---

## Bid Another Suit

Natural.

Shows additional distribution.

---

# Responder's Continuations

After opener clarifies:

- Pass (rare if only one-round forcing).
- Invite game.
- Bid game.
- Begin slam exploration.

# Typical Meanings

FSF often denies:

- A natural rebid.
- Immediate support raise.
- Comfortable notrump bid.

Responder is asking opener to clarify.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Agree on:

- Is FSF forcing **one round** or **to game**?
- Minimum HCP required.
- Whether opener promises a stopper before bidding notrump.
- Whether three-card support is shown immediately.
- Whether FSF can conceal a real suit.
- Continuations after interference.

---

# Advantages

- Conserves bidding space.
- Finds 3-card major fits.
- Locates notrump stoppers.
- Improves slam exploration.
- Very common in modern bidding systems.

---

# Disadvantages

- Artificial bid requires partnership agreement.
- Exact forcing level varies.
- Some auctions are partnership-style dependent.

---

# Common Partnership Style

Most expert partnerships play:

- Fourth Suit Forcing is **forcing for one round**.
- A bid of **2NT** or **3NT** promises a stopper in the fourth suit.
- Opener first shows **three-card support** if available.
- With no suitable natural rebid, opener makes the least descriptive legal bid.

Some partnerships play **Fourth Suit Game Force**, in which the fourth-suit bid commits the partnership to game.

---

# Comparison

| Bid | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Natural new suit | Shows length in the suit |
| Fourth Suit | Artificial request for more information |

# Memory Guide

When three suits have already been bid naturally:

| Fourth Suit Bid | Meaning |
|-----------------|---------|
| Artificial | "Tell me more." |
| Not natural | Does **not** promise the suit |

---

# Typical Opener Priorities

1. Support responder with **3 cards**.
2. Bid notrump with a **stopper**.
3. Rebid own suit.
4. Show another feature.

---

# Memory Aid

**Fourth Suit Forcing = "Describe your hand."**

Remember:

- **The fourth suit is almost never natural.**
- It asks partner to clarify strength, shape, support, or stoppers.
- It is usually **forcing for one round**, though some partnerships play it as **game forcing**.

FSF is one of the cornerstone conventions of modern natural systems, including **SAYC**, **2/1 Game Force**, **Acol**, and many other standard partnerships.
