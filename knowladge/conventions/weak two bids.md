# Weak Two Bids Convention

## Purpose

The **Weak Two Bid** is a preemptive opening bid at the two level showing a weak hand with a long six-card suit. Its primary objectives are to:

- Make life difficult for the opponents.
- Compete for the part-score.
- Suggest an opening lead if the opponents declare.
- Accurately describe a weak one-suited hand.

Most modern systems use **2♦, 2♥, and 2♠** as Weak Two openings. The **2♣** opening is reserved for strong artificial hands.

---

# When It Applies

Opening bids:

```
2♦
2♥
2♠
```

Natural and preemptive.

---

# Basic Requirements

Typical Weak Two requirements:

- **6-card suit**
- **5–10 HCP**
- Good suit quality
- No outside four-card major (partnership style)
- No hand strong enough for a one-level opening followed by a rebid

Distribution is generally more important than high-card points.

---

# Suit Quality

Typical acceptable suits:

```
AKJxxx
```

```
KQJxxx
```

```
AQ10xxx
```

Minimum quality:

- Two of the top three honors, or
- Three of the top five honors

Suit quality should improve when vulnerable.

# Responses

## Pass

Most common.

Responder has no game interest.

---

## Raise

Competitive.

Example:

```
2♥
3♥
```

Invitational or competitive depending on partnership agreement.

---

## Jump to Game

Responder believes game is likely.

Example:

```
2♠
4♠
```

---

## New Suit

Natural.

Usually forcing for one round.

Shows strong values.

---

## 2NT Inquiry

Artificial.

Asks opener to describe:

- Strength
- Suit quality

Most partnerships use the **Ogust Convention**.

---

# Ogust Responses

After:

```
2♥
2NT
```

Typical responses:

| Rebid | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| 3♣ | Weak hand, poor suit |
| 3♦ | Weak hand, good suit |
| 3♥ | Good hand, poor suit |
| 3♠ | Good hand, good suit |
| 3NT | Maximum with excellent suit (partnership agreement) |

---

# Opener's Rebids

Without a 2NT inquiry:

- Pass
- Raise
- Bid game with exceptional fit

After Ogust:

Describe hand according to agreed structure.

# When NOT to Open a Weak Two

Avoid with:

- Strong opening values.
- Seven-card suit (consider a three-level preempt).
- Two-suited hands.
- Poor suit quality when vulnerable.
- Four-card major outside a minor Weak Two (partnership agreement).

---

# Partnership Agreements

Agree on:

- Minimum HCP.
- Suit quality requirements.
- Whether 2NT is Ogust.
- Responses after interference.
- Vulnerability style.
- Whether a side four-card major is permitted.
- Whether 2♦ is Weak Two or Multi 2♦.

---

# Advantages

- Highly disruptive.
- Easy to learn.
- Consumes bidding space.
- Suggests opening lead.
- Excellent competitive tool.

---

# Disadvantages

- Gives opponents information.
- Poor discipline can lead to penalties.
- Requires responder to judge game accurately.

---

# Comparison

| Opening | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| 2♣ | Strong artificial |
| 2♦ | Weak Two (or Multi by agreement) |
| 2♥ | Weak Two |
| 2♠ | Weak Two |
| 3-level | Seven-card preempt |

---

# Common Partnership Style

Most expert partnerships play:

- Six-card suit.
- 5–10 HCP.
- 2NT = Ogust inquiry.
- Better suits when vulnerable.
- Good offensive potential.

Some partnerships replace the Weak **2♦** with **Multi 2♦**.

# Memory Aid

**Weak Two = "Weak Hand, Strong Suit."**

Remember:

- **6-card suit**
- **5–10 HCP**
- **Preemptive**
- **2NT asks about strength and suit quality (Ogust)**
- **2♣ remains strong and artificial**

Weak Two openings are among the most effective preemptive tools in modern bridge, combining immediate description of a long suit with obstruction of the opponents' auction while helping partner judge competitive and game decisions.
