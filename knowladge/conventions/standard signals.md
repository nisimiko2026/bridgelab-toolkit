# Standard Signals (Standard Carding)

## Purpose

**Standard Signals** are defensive agreements that allow partners to exchange information during the play of the hand. They help defenders communicate about:

- Attitude toward the suit being played.
- Count (number of cards held).
- Suit preference (Lavinthal/McKenney if played).
- Declarer's distribution.

Good defensive signaling improves defense, prevents misunderstandings, and helps defenders choose the correct continuation.

---

# Standard Signal Priority

Most partnerships use the following order of priority:

1. **Attitude**
2. **Count**
3. **Suit Preference** (when attitude and count are not relevant)

Always agree on priorities with partner.

---

# Standard Attitude Signals

## Purpose

Attitude signals tell partner whether you **like** or **dislike** the suit being played.

### High Card

Shows:

- Encouragement
- "Continue this suit."

Example:

```
Lead: ♠K

Dummy
♠ 842

You
♠ Q73

Play:
♠7
```

The relatively high card encourages.

---

### Low Card

Shows:

- Discouragement
- "Please switch."

Example:

```
Lead: ♥A

Dummy
♥ J74

You
♥ 952

Play:
♥2
```

Low card discourages.

---

# Standard Count Signals

## Purpose

Count signals tell partner how many cards you hold in the suit.

### High-Low

Shows:

- Even number

Example:

Holding:

```
♦84
```

Play:

```
8 then 4
```

Even count.

---

### Low-High

Shows:

- Odd number

Example:

Holding:

```
♣742
```

Play:

```
2 then 7
```

Odd count.

---

# Standard Suit Preference Signals

## Purpose

Suit Preference indicates which side suit partner should lead next.

Usually used when:

- Attitude is already known.
- Count is irrelevant.
- Declarer is ruffing.
- Giving partner a ruff.

---

### High Card

Requests:

Higher-ranking side suit.

Example:

```
Clubs
Diamonds
Hearts
Spades
```

Playing a high club asks for:

- Hearts or Spades.

---

### Low Card

Requests:

Lower-ranking side suit.

Example:

Playing a low club asks for:

- Diamonds.

Signal:

Attitude.

Signal:

# Common Agreements

Most partnerships use:

| Situation | Signal |
|-----------|--------|
| Partner leads honor | Attitude |
| Declarer leads suit | Count |
| Known count/attitude | Suit Preference |

---

# Standard Opening Leads

Most Standard partnerships also play:

| Lead | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Ace | AK or asks for attitude |
| King | AK or KQ (partnership agreement) |
| Queen | QJ |
| Jack | J10 or interior sequence |
| Ten | 109 or stronger |
| Fourth-best | Long suit |
| Top of doubleton | Doubleton |
| High from three small | Standard agreement |
| Low from four small | Fourth-best |

Lead agreements should be discussed separately.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Agree on:

- Ace asks for attitude or count.
- King lead style.
- Standard or UDCA.
- Suit preference situations.
- Signals versus notrump.
- Signals versus suit contracts.
- Smith Echo.
- Lavinthal or McKenney.

---

# Advantages

- Simple and widely understood.
- Easy for new partnerships.
- Used throughout the world.
- Works well with standard lead methods.
- Foundation for advanced defensive agreements.

---

# Disadvantages

- Sometimes gives declarer information.
- Less efficient than UDCA for many experts.
- Requires judgment when signaling honestly is dangerous.

---

# Comparison

| Signal | Standard | UDCA |
|---------|----------|------|
| High card | Encourage | Discourage |
| Low card | Discourage | Encourage |
| High-Low | Even count | Odd count |
| Low-High | Odd count | Even count |

# Typical Defensive Flow

1. Partner leads.
2. Give attitude.
3. Later give count.
4. Finally give suit preference if appropriate.

Not every signal is available on every trick.

---

# Memory Aid

## Standard Carding

### Attitude

**High = I Like It**

**Low = I Don't**

---

### Count

**High-Low = Even**

**Low-High = Odd**

---

### Suit Preference

**High = Higher Suit**

**Low = Lower Suit**

# System Summary

| Signal Type | High Card | Low Card |
|--------------|-----------|----------|
| Attitude | Encourage | Discourage |
| Count | First of even count | First of odd count |
| Suit Preference | Higher side suit | Lower side suit |

Standard Signals form the foundation of defensive communication in bridge. Most partnerships combine them with agreed opening leads and optional treatments such as **Lavinthal**, **Smith Echo**, or **Upside-Down Count and Attitude (UDCA)** for a complete defensive carding system.
