# Michaels Cue Bid Convention

## Purpose

The **Michaels Cue Bid** is an overcall convention used after an opponent opens a suit. By cue bidding the opponent's suit, overcaller shows a **two-suited hand** with at least **5-5 distribution** (occasionally 5-4 by partnership agreement).

The convention enables the partnership to:

- Show both suits with one bid.
- Compete effectively for the partscore.
- Find major-suit fits quickly.
- Make life difficult for the opponents.

---

# When It Applies

After an opponent opens a natural suit:

```
(1♣)   ?
```

or

```
(1♦)   ?
```

or

```
(1♥)   ?
```

or

```
(1♠)   ?
```

The overcaller may bid the opponent's suit.

---

# Basic Structure

| Opponent Opens | Cue Bid Shows |
|----------------|---------------|
| 1♣ | Hearts and Spades |
| 1♦ | Hearts and Spades |
| 1♥ | Spades + an unspecified minor |
| 1♠ | Hearts + an unspecified minor |

---

# Cue Bid Over a Minor Opening

## (1♣) – 2♣

Shows:

- 5+ Hearts
- 5+ Spades

Usually:

- 8+ HCP (distribution more important than points)

---

## (1♦) – 2♦

Shows:

- 5+ Hearts
- 5+ Spades

---

# Cue Bid Over a Major Opening

## (1♥) – 2♥

Shows:

- 5+ Spades
- 5+ Clubs or Diamonds

---

## (1♠) – 2♠

Shows:

- 5+ Hearts
- 5+ Clubs or Diamonds

---

# Strength

Typical ranges:

| Hand Type | Strength |
|------------|----------|
| Minimum | 8–12 HCP |
| Intermediate | 13–16 HCP |
| Strong | 17+ HCP |

Distribution is more important than high-card points.

---

# Responses

## After Michaels Over a Minor

Example:

```
(1♣)
2♣
Pass
?
```

Partner chooses:

```
2♥
```

or

```
2♠
```

Preference for the better major.

---

## After Michaels Over a Major

Example:

```
(1♠)
2♠
Pass
?
```

Partner may:

- Raise hearts
- Bid **2NT** to ask for the minor
- Bid game with sufficient values

---

# The 2NT Inquiry

After a Michaels cue bid over a major opening:

```
(1♠)
2♠
Pass
2NT
```

Artificial.

Asks:

**Which minor do you have?**

Responses:

| Rebid | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| 3♣ | Clubs |
| 3♦ | Diamonds |

---

# Strong Michaels Hands

With a very strong two-suited hand (typically 17+ HCP), some partnerships:

- Bid Michaels first and continue aggressively.
- Double first, then show the second suit.
- Use another strong two-suit treatment by partnership agreement.

# Partnership Agreements

Agree on:

- Minimum distribution (5-5 or 5-4).
- Minimum HCP.
- Maximum HCP before using another method.
- Whether 2NT asks for the minor.
- Responses after interference.
- Vulnerability style.
- Whether unusual 2NT is played together with Michaels.

---

# Relationship to Unusual 2NT

Most partnerships play:

| Bid | Shows |
|------|--------|
| Michaels Cue Bid | Highest two suits (or both majors) |
| Unusual 2NT | Lowest two unbid suits |

Together they allow every two-suited hand to be shown efficiently.

---

# Advantages

- Shows two suits immediately.
- Helps locate major-suit fits.
- Consumes bidding space.
- Difficult for opponents to compete.
- Simple and widely played.

---

# Disadvantages

- Minor suit is unknown after cue bidding a major.
- Requires partnership agreements on strength.
- Strong two-suited hands may need special treatment.

---

# Common Partnership Style

Most expert partnerships play:

- At least **5-5** shape.
- 8+ HCP (distribution emphasized).
- **2NT** asks for the unknown minor.
- Michaels together with **Unusual 2NT**.

Some aggressive partnerships allow vulnerable **5-4** shapes.

| Cue Bid | Meaning |
|----------|---------|

| 2♦ over 1♦ | Both majors |
| 2♥ over 1♥ | Spades + Minor |
| 2♠ over 1♠ | Hearts + Minor |

---

# Memory Aid

**Think "Michaels = Two Suits."**

Remember:

- **Over a minor opening → Both majors**
- **Over a major opening → Other major + an unknown minor**
- **2NT asks for the minor**

The Michaels Cue Bid is one of the most effective and widely used conventions for showing two-suited overcalls and is commonly paired with **Unusual 2NT** to cover all two-suited hands.
