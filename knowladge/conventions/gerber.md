# Gerber Convention

## Purpose

The **Gerber Convention** is an ace-asking convention that uses **4♣** to inquire about aces after a **Notrump bid**. It is primarily used to investigate slam when the partnership has agreed that notrump is the likely contract.

Gerber enables the partnership to:

- Determine the number of aces held.
- Explore small and grand slams.
- Preserve bidding space below Blackwood.
- Avoid unnecessary five-level bidding.

Most modern partnerships use **Roman Key Card Blackwood (RKCB)** for suit contracts and reserve **Gerber** for notrump auctions.

---

# When It Applies

Gerber is typically used after:

- **1NT**
- **2NT**
- A natural **3NT**
- Occasionally after a quantitative 4NT sequence (partnership agreement)

Example:

```
1NT
4♣
```

4♣ is **Gerber**, not a natural club bid.

---

# Basic Meaning

The bid of:

```
4♣
```

asks:

> **"How many aces do you have?"**

---

# Standard Responses

| Response | Aces |
|----------|------|
| **4♦** | 0 or 4 |
| **4♥** | 1 |
| **4♠** | 2 |
| **4NT** | 3 |

---

# Asking for Kings

After the ace response, the original asker may bid:

```
5♣
```

This asks:

> **"How many kings do you have?"**

---

# Standard King Responses

| Response | Kings |
|----------|-------|
| **5♦** | 0 or 4 |
| **5♥** | 1 |
| **5♠** | 2 |
| **5NT** | 3 |

---

# Typical Auction

```
1NT
4♣
4♥
```

Meaning:

- Gerber.
- One ace.

---

Continuation:

```
5♣
5♠
```

Meaning:

- King inquiry.
- Two kings.

# Continuations

After learning the number of aces:

Responder may:

- Sign off in game.
- Ask for kings.
- Bid six notrump.
- Explore grand slam.

---

# What Gerber Does NOT Show

Gerber does **not**:

- Agree a trump suit.
- Ask for key cards.
- Replace Roman Key Card Blackwood.
- Show clubs.

It is purely an ace-asking convention.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Agree on:

- Which notrump auctions permit Gerber.
- Whether 4♣ remains natural in some auctions.
- Whether 5♣ always asks for kings.
- Relationship with quantitative 4NT.
- Whether Gerber applies after rebid notrumps.
- Responses after interference.

---

# Advantages

- Uses lower bidding space than Blackwood.
- Excellent after notrump openings.
- Easy to remember.
- Widely played.
- Simple continuation structure.

---

# Disadvantages

- Can conflict with natural club bids.
- Less useful after a trump suit has been agreed.
- Most experts prefer RKCB for suit slams.

---

# Gerber vs. Blackwood

| Convention | Ask | Best Used After |
|------------|-----|-----------------|
| **Gerber (4♣)** | Aces | Notrump auctions |
| **Blackwood (4NT)** | Aces | Suit auctions |
| **RKCB (4NT)** | Key cards | Suit auctions |

---

# Common Partnership Style

Most expert partnerships play:

- **4♣ = Gerber only after natural notrump bids.**
- **4NT = Quantitative** over a notrump opening (unless a suit has been agreed).
- **RKCB** after suit agreement.
- **5♣** asks for kings.

Some partnerships play **Super Gerber**, but this is uncommon.

# Typical Sequence

```
1NT
4♣
4♥
5♣
5♠
6NT
```

Meaning:

- Gerber.
- One ace.
- King inquiry.
- Two kings.
- Small slam.

---

# Memory Aid

**Gerber = "Clubs Ask for Aces."**

Remember:

- **4♣ = Ace Ask**
- **5♣ = King Ask**
- **Used after Notrump auctions**
- **Not used after a trump suit has been agreed**
- **RKCB is generally preferred for suit contracts**

Gerber remains one of the classic slam conventions in bridge. Although Roman Key Card Blackwood has largely replaced it in suit auctions, Gerber continues to be an effective and widely played method for investigating slams after natural notrump openings and rebids.
