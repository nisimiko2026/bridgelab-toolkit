# Reopening Double Convention

## Purpose

The **Reopening Double** (also called a **Balancing Double**) is made after the opponents appear ready to play a low-level contract because partner has passed. It allows the partnership to compete when there may still be a part-score available.

A Reopening Double:

- Keeps the auction alive.
- Shows values and interest in competing.
- Asks partner to bid unless holding defensive strength.
- Is usually a form of **takeout**, not a penalty double.

---

# When It Applies

A Reopening Double occurs when the auction has reached the balancing seat.

Example:

```
West    North    East    South
1♥      Pass     Pass     Double
```

South is in the balancing seat.

The Double reopens the bidding.

---

# Basic Principle

A Reopening Double says:

> "Partner, if you have anything useful, let's compete."

It is generally:

- Takeout-oriented.
- Slightly lighter than a direct takeout double.
- Based on the expectation that partner has some values.

# Strength Requirements

Unlike a direct Takeout Double, balancing allows lighter values.

Typical strength:

| Situation | Typical HCP |
|-----------|-------------|
| Direct Takeout Double | 12+ HCP |
| Reopening Double | 8–11 HCP (sometimes less with excellent shape) |

The expectation is that partner has some of the missing high cards.

---

# Shape Requirements

Ideal:

- Support for the unbid suits.
- Shortness in opener's suit.

Typical shapes:

```
4-4-4-1
```

```
5-4-3-1
```

```
4-5-3-1
```

Balanced hands with suitable strength may also reopen.

---

# Partner's Responsibilities

Partner should normally:

- Bid the best unbid suit.
- Pass only with genuine penalty interest.
- Raise with support.
- Bid notrump with a stopper and appropriate values.

---

# Responses

Shows:

- Defensive strength.
- Willingness to defend.

# Partnership Agreements

Agree on:

- Minimum HCP.
- Whether balanced hands qualify.
- Maximum level for reopening doubles.
- Meaning over 1NT.
- Responses with weak hands.
- Whether reopening doubles are always takeout.
- Style when vulnerable.

---

# Advantages

- Prevents opponents from buying the contract cheaply.
- Uses the "borrowed king" principle.
- Allows lighter competitive actions.
- Frequently wins part-score battles.
- Fundamental competitive tool.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires sound judgment.
- Poor distribution can be costly.
- Must distinguish from penalty doubles.

---

# The Borrowed King Principle

When balancing, assume partner holds approximately:

```
3–5 HCP
```

that would have been enough for the opponents to invite game if they had held them.

Therefore you may balance with fewer high-card points than in direct seat.

---

# Comparison

| Double | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| Takeout Double | Direct action showing opening values |
| Reopening Double | Balancing action with lighter values |
| Responsive Double | Advancer competes after partner's takeout double |
| Negative Double | Responder shows unbid major(s) |
| Penalty Double | Defend the contract |

---

# Common Partnership Style

Most expert partnerships play:

- **8–11 HCP** in balancing seat.
- Support for the unbid suits.
- Shortness in opener's suit when possible.
- Pass converts the double to penalties.
- Balanced hands are acceptable with suitable strength.

# Typical Priorities

1. Compete if a fit is likely.
2. Show support for unbid suits.
3. Keep the auction alive.
4. Allow partner to convert to penalties when appropriate.

---

# Memory Aid

**Reopening Double = "Don't let them play there."**

Remember:

- **Balancing seat only.**
- **Usually takeout, not penalty.**
- **Can be lighter than a direct takeout double.**
- **Partner may pass with defensive strength.**

The Reopening Double is a cornerstone of competitive bridge, allowing the partnership to compete intelligently for part-scores while using the "borrowed king" principle to justify lighter balancing actions.
