# Blackwood Convention

## Purpose

The **Blackwood Convention** is a slam-investigation convention that uses **4NT** to ask partner for the number of **aces** held. It is used after the partnership has determined that slam may be possible.

Blackwood helps the partnership:

- Avoid missing two quick aces.
- Decide between game, small slam, and grand slam.
- Explore slam efficiently.
- Evaluate combined strength before committing to slam.

Today, many expert partnerships prefer **Roman Key Card Blackwood (RKCB)**, but standard Blackwood remains widely taught and played.

---

# When It Applies

Blackwood is typically used:

- After a trump suit has been agreed.
- When the partnership is considering slam.

Example:

```
1♠
3♠
4NT
```

The bid of **4NT** is **Blackwood**, not a natural notrump bid.

---

# Basic Meaning

The bid:

```
4NT
```

asks:

> **"How many aces do you hold?"**

---

# Standard Ace Responses

| Response | Aces |
|----------|------|
| **5♣** | 0 or 4 |
| **5♦** | 1 |
| **5♥** | 2 |
| **5♠** | 3 |

---

# King Ask

After learning the number of aces, the original asker may bid:

```
5NT
```

This asks:

> **"How many kings do you have?"**

---

# Standard King Responses

| Response | Kings |
|----------|--------|
| **6♣** | 0 or 4 |
| **6♦** | 1 |
| **6♥** | 2 |
| **6♠** | 3 |

---

# Typical Auction

```
1♥
3♥
4NT
5♦
```

Meaning:

- Blackwood.
- One ace.

---

Continuation:

```
5NT
6♥
```

Meaning:

- King ask.
- Two kings.

# Continuations

After learning the number of aces:

The partnership may:

- Sign off in game.
- Ask for kings.
- Bid six of the agreed suit.
- Investigate a grand slam.

---

# When NOT to Use Blackwood

Avoid Blackwood when:

- A trump suit has not been agreed.
- You have a void that may distort ace values.
- You need to know about key cards or the trump queen.
- You lack enough strength to bid slam regardless of partner's response.

Roman Key Card Blackwood is often preferable.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Agree on:

- Whether Blackwood or RKCB is standard.
- Whether 4NT is quantitative in some auctions.
- Whether Gerber is used after notrump openings.
- Responses after interference.
- Whether 5NT always asks for kings.
- Treatment after opponents double or overcall (DOPI/ROPI).

---

# Advantages

- Very easy to learn.
- Simple response structure.
- Effective for basic slam investigation.
- Universally recognized.
- Compatible with many bidding systems.

---

# Disadvantages

- Does not identify the trump king as a key card.
- Does not ask about the trump queen.
- Poor when a partnership has a void.
- Less accurate than Roman Key Card Blackwood.

---

# Blackwood vs. RKCB

| Feature | Blackwood | RKCB |
|----------|-----------|------|
| Asks for | Aces | Five key cards |
| Trump King Included | No | Yes |
| Trump Queen | No | Yes |
| Accuracy | Good | Excellent |

---

# Common Partnership Style

Most modern partnerships play:

- **Roman Key Card Blackwood (1430 or 0314)** after a trump suit has been agreed.
- **Gerber** after natural notrump openings.
- Standard Blackwood only in limited situations or by partnership agreement.

# Typical Sequence

```
1♠
3♠
4NT
5♦
5NT
6♥
6♠
```

Meaning:

- Blackwood.
- One ace.
- King ask.
- Two kings.
- Small slam.

---

# Relationship with Other Slam Conventions

Typical expert sequence:

1. Agree trump.
2. Cue Bid controls.
3. Serious 3NT (if played).
4. Last Train (if needed).
5. **Blackwood or RKCB**.
6. Grand slam investigation.

---

# Memory Aid

**Blackwood = "4NT Asks for Aces."**

Remember:

- **4NT = Ace Ask**
- **5NT = King Ask**
- **Best after a trump suit has been agreed**
- **Simple four-step response structure**
- **RKCB is the modern enhancement**

Blackwood is one of the oldest and most influential bridge conventions. Although many expert partnerships now use Roman Key Card Blackwood for greater precision, standard Blackwood remains an essential convention and a cornerstone of slam bidding.
