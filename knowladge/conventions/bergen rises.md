# Bergen Raises Convention

## Purpose

**Bergen Raises** are used after partner opens **1♥** or **1♠** to distinguish between weak, invitational, and limit raises while making it harder for the opponents to enter the auction.

The convention uses **3♣** and **3♦** as artificial raises, allowing direct raises to retain preemptive meanings.

---

# When It Applies

After partner opens:

```
1♥
```

or

```
1♠
```

with no interference.

Example:

```
1♠   Pass
?
```

---

# Standard Bergen Structure

| Response | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| 2M | Simple raise (6–9 HCP, 3-card support) |
| 3♣ | Limit raise or better, 4-card support |
| 3♦ | Weak preemptive raise, 4-card support |
| 3M | Mixed raise (approximately 7–9 HCP, 4-card support) |
| 4M | Shutout raise |

**Note:** Several Bergen variants exist. This document describes the most commonly played modern version.

---

# 2M – Simple Raise

Example:

```
1♠
2♠
```

Shows:

- 6–9 HCP
- Usually 3-card support
- No game interest

---

# 3♣ – Limit Raise or Better

Artificial.

Shows:

- 10–12 HCP (or stronger)
- 4-card trump support
- Invitational or better

Opener evaluates whether to invite, bid game, or explore slam.

---

# 3♦ – Weak Bergen Raise

Artificial.

Shows:

- About 0–6 HCP
- 4-card trump support
- Distributional hand
- Preemptive

The purpose is to consume bidding space while showing the fit.

---

# 3M – Mixed Raise

Example:

```
1♥
3♥
```

Shows:

- Approximately 7–9 HCP
- 4-card support
- Some offensive values
- Too strong for the weak raise but not enough for a limit raise

---

# 4M – Shutout Raise

Example:

```
1♠
4♠
```

Shows:

- Weak hand
- Usually five or more trumps
- Highly distributional
- Little defensive strength

Purely preemptive.

---

# Opener's Rebids After 3♣

Possible continuations:

### Pass

Minimum opening.

Invitation declined.

---

### 3M

Minimum with no game interest.

---

### Game

Accepts invitation.

Believes game has good chances.

---

### New Suit

Natural.

Shows extra values and shape.

May begin slam investigation.

# Partnership Agreements

Agree on:

- Whether 3♣ is **limit raise** or **limit raise or better**.
- Exact HCP ranges for each response.
- Whether 2M promises exactly three-card support.
- Minimum trump length for weak and mixed raises.
- Whether Bergen Raises remain on after interference.
- Slam continuations after 3♣.

---

# Advantages

- Clearly distinguishes different strengths of raises.
- Makes competitive bidding more difficult for opponents.
- Helps opener judge whether to compete, invite, or bid game.
- Improves game and slam decisions.
- Widely used in Standard American and 2/1 systems.

---

# Disadvantages

- Artificial responses require partnership agreement.
- Multiple Bergen styles are in common use.
- Continuations after interference vary.

# Memory Aid

**Think "Bergen separates the raises."**

- **2M** = Simple
- **3♣** = Strong (limit or better)
- **3♦** = Weak
- **3M** = Mixed
- **4M** = Shutout

By assigning different strength ranges to different raises, opener can judge the partnership's combined strength much more accurately.

---

# Typical Partnership Style

Most modern expert partnerships play:

- **2M** = 6–9 HCP, 3-card support
- **3♣** = 10+ HCP, 4-card support, limit raise or better
- **3♦** = 0–6 HCP, 4-card support, preemptive
- **3M** = 7–9 HCP, 4-card support (mixed raise)
- **4M** = Weak, highly distributional, 5+ trumps
