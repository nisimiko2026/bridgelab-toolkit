# Takeout Double Convention

## Purpose

The **Takeout Double** is a conventional double made after an opponent opens the bidding. It asks partner to bid one of the **unbid suits** and is used to compete with hands that are too strong or too shapely for a simple overcall.

The convention helps the partnership:

- Compete for the contract.
- Find major-suit fits.
- Show opening values with support for the unbid suits.
- Describe distribution more accurately than a natural overcall.

---

# When It Applies

After an opponent makes a natural opening bid:

```
West   North
1♦     Double
```

The double is **for takeout**, not for penalties.

---

# Basic Requirements

A Takeout Double normally shows:

- **Opening strength** (about 12+ HCP)
- **Support for the unbid suits**
- **Shortness in the opponent's suit** (preferably a singleton or void)

Distribution is usually more important than high-card points.

---

# Typical Shape

Ideal shapes include:

```
4-4-4-1
```

```
4-4-5-0
```

```
5-4-4-0
```

Balanced hands are generally unsuitable unless very strong.

# Strength Guidelines

| Hand Type | Typical HCP |
|------------|-------------|
| Minimum | 12–14 HCP |
| Intermediate | 15–17 HCP |
| Strong | 18+ HCP |

Hands with exceptional distribution may double with fewer points.

---

# Partner's Responsibilities

Partner should normally **bid**.

Even with:

```
0–5 HCP
```

partner is expected to respond unless holding unusual defensive strength in the opponent's suit.

---

# When NOT to Double

Avoid a Takeout Double with:

- Long opponent's suit.
- Balanced minimum hands without support for the unbid suits.
- Weak hands.
- Strong one-suited hands better shown by an overcall.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Agree on:

- Minimum HCP.
- Shape requirements.
- Whether equal-length suits are required.
- Responses with very weak hands.
- Style when vulnerable.
- Continuations after interference.
- Whether cue bids are forcing.

---

# Balancing Takeout Double

In the balancing seat:

```
1♥
Pass
Pass
Double
```

The strength requirement is reduced.

Typical:

- 9–11 HCP

Distribution remains important.

---

# Advantages

- Finds major-suit fits.
- Shows opening values.
- Flexible description.
- Allows partner to choose the best suit.
- Fundamental convention in every bidding system.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires shortness in opponent's suit.
- Partner must usually respond even with weak hands.
- Poor doubles can be expensive.

---

# Comparison

| Action | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| Takeout Double | Support for unbid suits |
| Negative Double | Responder shows unbid major(s) |
| Support Double | Exactly three-card support |
| Penalty Double | Defend the contract |

---

# Common Partnership Style

Most expert partnerships play:

- **Opening values** (approximately 12+ HCP)
- **Support for all unbid suits**
- **Shortness in opener's suit**
- Lighter balancing doubles
- Responsive doubles in later auctions

# Memory Aid

**Takeout Double = "Partner, please choose an unbid suit."**

Remember:

- **Opening values**
- **Support for the unbid suits**
- **Shortness in opponent's suit**
- **Not for penalties**

The Takeout Double is one of the most fundamental conventions in bridge and forms the foundation of modern competitive bidding.
