
# Smith Echo Convention

## Purpose

The **Smith Echo** (also known as the **Smith Peter**) is a defensive signaling convention used primarily **against Notrump contracts**. It allows the defenders to communicate their attitude toward **partner's opening lead**, rather than toward the suit currently being played.

Unlike standard attitude signals, the Smith Echo tells partner whether you **liked the opening lead** and would like that suit continued when partner regains the lead.

---

# When It Applies

The Smith Echo is normally used:

- Against **Notrump contracts**
- After partner has made the opening lead
- When declarer wins the trick and later leads another suit

Example:

```
West    North    East    South
1NT     Pass     3NT     All Pass
```

Partner leads:

```
♠4
```

Declarer wins the first trick and later leads another suit.

Your card in that new suit is the Smith Echo.

---

# Basic Principle

The signal is given **while following suit to declarer's lead**, not while following to partner's lead.

It answers the question:

> "Did I like partner's opening lead?"

---

# Standard Smith Echo

Most partnerships play:

| Card Played | Meaning |
|-------------|---------|
| High card | I like partner's opening lead |
| Low card | I do not like partner's opening lead |

This version is called **Standard Smith Echo**.

---

# Reverse Smith Echo

Some partnerships reverse the meanings:

| Card Played | Meaning |
|-------------|---------|
| Low card | I like partner's opening lead |
| High card | I do not like partner's opening lead |

This is known as **Reverse Smith Echo**.

Partnership agreement is essential.

---

# What Smith Echo Does NOT Show

Smith Echo does **not** show:

- Attitude toward the suit currently being played.
- Count in the suit being played.
- Suit preference.

Its only purpose is to comment on **partner's opening lead**.

---

# Typical Defensive Priority

Most expert partnerships use:

1. Smith Echo
2. Count
3. Suit Preference

The exact priority depends on the position and partnership agreement.

# Partnership Agreements

Agree on:

- Standard or Reverse Smith Echo.
- Whether Smith applies only against Notrump.
- Whether Smith is used after every opening lead.
- Priority versus count.
- Priority versus suit preference.
- Whether opening lead must be an honor.

---

# Advantages

- Helps partner know whether to continue the opening lead.
- Improves defensive communication.
- Frequently finds extra defensive tricks.
- Works well with both Standard Carding and UDCA.
- Widely played by experienced partnerships.

---

# Disadvantages

- Usually limited to Notrump contracts.
- Can occasionally provide information to declarer.
- Requires clear partnership agreements.

---

# Comparison

| Signal | Purpose |
|---------|---------|
| Attitude | Continue current suit |
| Count | Number of cards held |
| Lavinthal | Which side suit to switch to |
| Smith Echo | Whether partner should continue the opening lead |

---

# Common Partnership Style

Most expert partnerships play:

- **Standard Smith Echo**
- Only against **Notrump contracts**
- Standard or UDCA attitude and count
- Lavinthal suit preference

Some partnerships also use Smith Echo in suit contracts, but this is much less common.

# Typical Defensive Sequence

1. Partner makes the opening lead.
2. Declarer wins the trick.
3. Declarer attacks another suit.
4. Defender gives the Smith Echo.
5. Partner later decides whether to continue the opening lead.

---

# Relationship to Other Signals

Smith Echo does **not** replace:

- Attitude
- Count
- Suit Preference (Lavinthal)

Instead, it supplements them by providing information specifically about the opening lead.

---

# Memory Aid

**Smith Echo = "Should partner continue the opening lead?"**

Remember:

- Used primarily **against Notrump contracts**.
- Signal is given **on declarer's lead**, not partner's lead.
- **Standard Smith:** High = Yes, Low = No.
- **Reverse Smith:** Low = Yes, High = No.

Smith Echo is one of the most effective defensive signaling methods in modern bridge, helping defenders judge whether the opening lead remains the best source of tricks while complementing Attitude, Count, and Lavinthal signals.
