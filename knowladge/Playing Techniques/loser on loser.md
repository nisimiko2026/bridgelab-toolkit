# Loser-on-Loser Technique

## Overview

The **Loser-on-Loser** technique is a declarer-play strategy in which declarer deliberately discards one losing card on another losing card. Although one trick is still lost, two potential losers are eliminated, often creating an extra trick or avoiding a later loser.

This technique is particularly useful in suit contracts but also appears in notrump play.

---

# Purpose

The Loser-on-Loser technique allows declarer to:

- Eliminate two losers for the loss of one trick.
- Avoid later losing tricks.
- Preserve trump control.
- Improve communication between the hands.
- Increase the chances of making the contract.

---

# Basic Principle

Instead of ruffing or trying to win a hopeless trick, declarer discards one loser on another.

For example:

- Dummy has a losing club.
- Declarer has a losing diamond.
- When opponents win the diamond trick, declarer discards the losing club.

Only one trick is lost instead of two.

---

# When to Use

Loser-on-Loser is appropriate when:

- Both hands contain unavoidable losers.
- Ruffing is impossible or undesirable.
- Discarding preserves trump control.
- One losing trick can eliminate another.

---

# Typical Situations

Common situations include:

- Discarding a losing side-suit card on another losing side suit.
- Throwing a loser on a long established suit.
- Discarding during an opponent's winner.
- Eliminating a future loser before drawing trumps.

---

# Example 1 – Basic Loser-on-Loser

## Contract

```
4♠
```

Dummy

```
♠ KQ83
♥ A72
♦ Q43
♣ 765
```

Declarer

```
♠ A7642
♥ K53
♦ A76
♣ 842
```

Suppose declarer has:

- One club loser.
- One diamond loser.

Instead of trying to save both, declarer allows the opponents to win the diamond and discards the losing club.

Result:

Only one trick is lost.

---

# Example 2 – Established Side Suit

Dummy

```
♣ AKQJ
```

Declarer

```
♦ 742
```

After cashing the club winners, declarer discards losing diamonds.

The losers disappear without needing to ruff.

---

# Example 3 – Preserving Trump Control

Contract:

```
4♥
```

Dummy

```
♥ QJ87
♣ KQJ
```

Declarer

```
♥ AK64
♠ 843
```

Instead of ruffing a spade loser, declarer discards it on a losing club.

Dummy's trumps remain available for later use.

---

# Planning the Play

Before Trick One, identify:

- Sure winners.
- Sure losers.
- Losers that can be discarded.
- Losers that must be ruffed.
- Opponents' likely winners.

Look for opportunities where one losing trick can eliminate another.

---

# Timing

Timing is critical.

Usually:

1. Establish the discard suit.
2. Preserve entries.
3. Discard the loser at the earliest safe opportunity.
4. Draw trumps only when appropriate.

---

# Common Situations

Loser-on-Loser frequently occurs:

- After establishing a long side suit.
- During elimination plays.
- Before executing an endplay.
- Before a squeeze.
- In combination with cross-ruff techniques.

---

# Combination with Other Techniques

Loser-on-Loser often works together with:

- Elimination Play.
- Endplay (Throw-In).
- Dummy Reversal.
- Cross Ruff.
- Squeeze.
- Safety Play.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Discarding the wrong loser.
- Losing communication between hands.
- Drawing trumps too early.
- Missing a better ruffing opportunity.
- Failing to count the remaining losers.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Force declarer to ruff rather than discard.
- Attack entries.
- Prevent the establishment of long suits.
- Avoid giving declarer free discard opportunities.
- Count declarer's remaining losers.

---

# Advantages

- Converts two losers into one.
- Preserves valuable trump control.
- Simplifies declarer's play.
- Frequently creates an additional trick.
- Often avoids difficult guesses.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires careful planning.
- Timing is essential.
- Poor communication can ruin the play.
- Not always available.

---

# Related Techniques

| Technique | Purpose |

| Endplay | Force a favorable return |
| Elimination | Remove safe exit cards |

| Safety Play | Maximize the chance of making the contract |

---

# Declarer's Checklist

Before using Loser-on-Loser:

- Can one loser be discarded on another?
- Is ruffing unnecessary?
- Will I preserve trump control?
- Are my entries secure?
- Is the timing correct?

---

# Memory Guide

## Four Steps

1. Identify two losers.
2. Find a losing trick or established winner that allows a discard.
3. Discard one loser on the other.
4. Continue with the remaining plan.

---

# Comparison

| Technique | Main Idea |
|-----------|-----------|
| Ruffing | Use a trump to eliminate a loser |
| Loser-on-Loser | Discard one loser on another losing trick |
| Dummy Reversal | Ruff losers in dummy |
| Cross Ruff | Ruff losers in both hands |
| Elimination | Remove safe exits before an endplay |

---

# Memory Aid

**Loser-on-Loser = "Lose One Trick, Eliminate Two Losers."**

Remember:

- **Discard a loser on another losing trick.**
- **Preserve trumps whenever possible.**
- **Plan before drawing trumps.**
- **Look for established long suits or unavoidable losing tricks.**
- **Often combines with endplays and elimination plays.**

The Loser-on-Loser technique is a fundamental declarer-play weapon that transforms two potential losers into a single lost trick. Expert declarers constantly look for opportunities to exchange one unavoidable loser for the elimination of another, improving their chances of making the contract.
