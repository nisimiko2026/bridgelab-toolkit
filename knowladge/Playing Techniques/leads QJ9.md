# Opening Leads from QJ9

## Overview

The holding **Queen-Jack-Nine (QJ9)** is a **broken honor sequence**. While the **Queen and Jack** form a touching sequence, the missing Ten means the holding is not as strong as **QJ10**. Nevertheless, **QJ9** is still treated as a standard sequence for opening leads.

In virtually all standard bridge methods, the correct opening lead is:

```
Queen
```

The Queen is the top of the touching honor sequence and promises at least the Jack.

The guiding principle is:

> **Lead the top of the touching honor sequence, even when the sequence is incomplete.**

---

# Purpose

Opening leads from QJ9 aim to:

- Establish the suit.
- Force declarer's higher honors.
- Promote the Nine into a winner if possible.
- Help partner identify the honor holding.
- Coordinate the defense.

---

# Understanding the Holding

Example:

```
♠ Q J 9 6 4
```

Strengths:

- Two touching honors.
- Good attacking lead.
- May establish additional winners.

Weaknesses:

- Missing the Ten.
- Missing the Ace and King.
- Slightly less powerful than QJ10.

---

# Standard Expert Treatment

| Holding | Opening Lead |
|----------|--------------|
| QJ | Queen |
| **QJ9** | **Queen** |
| QJ10 | Queen |
| QJ109 | Queen |

The Queen promises the Jack and may also include the Nine.

---

# Against Notrump Contracts

## Objectives

- Establish the long suit.
- Force declarer's Ace or King.
- Develop additional tricks before declarer establishes winners.

### Example

Holding

```
♣ Q J 9 8 4
```

Contract

```
3NT
```

Lead:

```
♣Q
```

Declarer may win with the King or Ace, allowing the remaining honors to become winners later.

---

# Against Suit Contracts

## Objectives

- Force declarer's honors.
- Develop the suit.
- Learn from partner's signal.
- Avoid helping declarer establish side suits.

Example

Holding

```
♥ Q J 9 6
```

Contract

```
4♠
```

Lead:

```
♥Q
```

Partner's signal determines whether continuing the suit is correct.

---

# Partner's Signals

The meaning depends on partnership agreement.

## Attitude

Common agreement:

- High = Encourage.
- Low = Discourage.

Useful when deciding whether to continue.

---

## Count

Some partnerships prefer count after a Queen lead.

### Standard Count

```
High-Low = Even

Low-High = Odd
```

### UDCA

```
High-Low = Odd

Low-High = Even
```

---

## Suit Preference

When appropriate, partner may later use Suit Preference to recommend the best continuation.

---

# Example 1 – Establish the Suit

Holding

```
♦ Q J 9 5
```

Lead:

```
♦Q
```

Declarer wins with the Ace.

The Jack and Nine may later become winners.

---

# Example 2 – Continue

Holding

```
♠ Q J 9 4
```

Partner encourages.

Continue spades.

The defense develops additional tricks.

---

# Example 3 – Switch

Holding

```
♣ Q J 9 6
```

Partner discourages.

Switch to another suit.

Correct timing avoids helping declarer.

---

# Planning the Defense

Before leading QJ9, ask:

- Is this my strongest attacking suit?
- Does the auction suggest another lead?
- What information do I need from partner?
- Should I continue after Trick One?
- Can the Nine become a useful card later?

---

# Special Considerations

### Missing the Ten

The missing Ten makes QJ9 weaker than QJ10.

Do not assume the Nine will become a winner.

---

### Missing the Ace and King

The Queen lead generally indicates the leader does not hold either higher honor.

Partner should interpret the lead accordingly.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Leading the Jack instead of the Queen.
- Treating QJ9 like QJ10.
- Continuing after a discouraging signal.
- Ignoring the auction.
- Forgetting partnership agreements.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Before play, discuss:

- Does the Queen always promise the Jack?
- What signal follows a Queen lead?
- Differences between Notrump and suit contracts.
- When Suit Preference applies.
- Whether Journalist or Rusinow Leads are used.

---

# Advantages

- Standard sequence lead.
- Easy for partner to recognize.
- Helps establish long suits.
- Strong attacking lead.
- Compatible with most expert systems.

---

# Disadvantages

- Weaker than QJ10.
- Missing the Ten may reduce trick-taking potential.
- Incorrect continuation may help declarer.

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Identify the sequence.
2. Lead the Queen.
3. Read partner's signal.
4. Continue or switch.
5. Reevaluate after each trick.

# Memory Aid

**QJ9 Leads = "Lead the Top of the Sequence."**

Remember:

- **Lead the Queen from QJ9 in standard opening-lead methods.**
- **The Queen promises the Jack but not necessarily the Ten.**
- **QJ9 is a broken sequence, slightly weaker than QJ10.**
- **Partner's signal helps determine whether to continue the suit.**
- **Always combine the opening lead with the auction, dummy, and your partnership agreements.**

The **QJ9** holding is a standard honor-sequence opening lead. Although weaker than QJ10 because of the missing Ten, it still provides a clear message to partner and offers good potential for establishing long-suit winners. Successful defense depends on interpreting partner's signal correctly and choosing the right moment to continue or switch.
