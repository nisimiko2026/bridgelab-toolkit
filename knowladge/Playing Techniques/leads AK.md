# Opening Leads from AK

## Overview

Leading from **Ace-King (AK)** is one of the most important partnership agreements in bridge. Although every partnership agrees that AK is a powerful holding, there are several treatments regarding **whether to lead the Ace or the King**, and **what partner's signal should mean**.

Modern expert partnerships usually agree on the meaning of both the **Ace lead** and the **King lead**, since the continuation often depends on partner's signal.

---

# Purpose

Opening leads from AK aim to:

- Cash immediate winners.
- Set up defensive tricks.
- Obtain a ruff.
- Collect count or attitude information.
- Prevent declarer from establishing winners.

---

# Common Partnership Styles

## Style 1 – Ace from AK (Most Common)

Lead:

```
A
```

Promises:

```
AK or AKx
```

Partner usually gives:

- Count
- Attitude

depending on partnership agreement.

This is the most popular treatment in modern bridge.

---

## Style 2 – King from AK (Traditional)

Lead:

```
K
```

Promises:

```
AK
```

The Ace is retained for the second round.

Some older partnerships still use this method.

---

## Style 3 – Different Agreements

Some expert partnerships play:

- Ace asks for attitude.
- King asks for count.
- Reverse signals against Notrump.
- Different treatments against suit contracts.

Always discuss these agreements before play.

---

# Against Notrump Contracts

Objectives:

- Establish a long suit.
- Cash winners before declarer gains control.
- Maintain communication.

### Example

Holding

```
♠ AK763
```

Contract

```
3NT
```

Lead:

```
♠A
```

Partner signals:

- Encourage if holding the Queen or useful spot cards.
- Count if agreed.

Continue according to the signal.

---

# Against Suit Contracts

Objectives:

- Cash immediate winners.
- Discover whether a ruff is available.
- Prevent declarer from discarding losers.

Example

Holding

```
♥ AK84
```

Contract

```
4♠
```

Lead:

```
♥A
```

Partner gives count.

If partner shows a doubleton heart, continue hearts to give partner a ruff.

---

# Partner's Signals

## Count

Typical agreement:

Standard Count

```
High-Low = Even

Low-High = Odd
```

or

UDCA

```
High-Low = Odd

Low-High = Even
```

Count often helps determine whether to continue the suit.

---

## Attitude

Some partnerships use:

- High = Encourage.
- Low = Discourage.

Especially useful when the leader is deciding whether to continue the suit.

---

## Suit Preference

Occasionally used when:

- Count is already known.
- Declarer is void.
- A ruff is imminent.

The spot card requests the preferred return suit.

---

# Example 1 – Giving a Ruff

Contract

```
4♥
```

West leads:

```
♣A
```

Holding

```
♣AK95
```

East follows:

```
♣2
♣7
```

The count indicates a doubleton.

West continues clubs.

East ruffs the third club.

---

# Example 2 – Continue or Switch

West leads:

```
♦A
```

East discourages.

West switches immediately.

The defense avoids giving declarer an extra trick.

---

# Example 3 – Cashing Winners

Holding

```
♠AKQJ
```

Against

```
6NT
```

West leads the Ace.

East encourages.

The defense cashes enough spade tricks before declarer establishes winners.

---

# Planning the Defense

Before leading from AK, ask:

- Is there an immediate need to cash winners?
- Am I trying to establish the suit?
- Do I need count or attitude?
- Is partner likely to have a ruff?
- Should I continue after the first trick?

---

# Common Mistakes

- Forgetting partnership agreements.
- Asking for count when attitude is needed.
- Cashing both honors automatically.
- Ignoring partner's signal.
- Continuing the suit after a discouraging signal.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Discuss before play:

- Ace or King from AK?
- Does Ace ask for count or attitude?
- Does King ask for count or attitude?
- Different agreements in Notrump and suit contracts?
- When is Suit Preference used?

---

# Advantages

- Establishes immediate winners.
- Helps obtain ruffs.
- Improves defensive communication.
- Coordinates partnership defense.
- Essential for successful opening leads.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires precise partnership agreements.
- Incorrect interpretation may cost a trick.
- Different expert pairs use different methods.

# Defender's Checklist

Before leading AK:

- Am I leading the agreed honor?
- What information do I need from partner?
- Does partner know which signal to give?
- Is a ruff possible?
- Should I continue or switch after Trick One?

---

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Choose the correct honor.
2. Know what information you need.
3. Read partner's signal.
4. Decide whether to continue.
5. Reevaluate after each trick.

---

```

↓

↓

```

---

# Memory Aid

**AK Leads = "Lead, Listen, Then Decide."**

Remember:

- **Agree with partner whether the Ace or King is led from AK.**
- **Know whether the lead asks for count, attitude, or suit preference.**
- **Do not cash the second honor automatically—partner's signal may tell you to switch.**
- **Against suit contracts, look for ruff opportunities.**
- **Against Notrump, focus on establishing and cashing your long suit before declarer does.**

Modern expert partnerships most commonly lead the **Ace from AK**, using partner's signal to determine whether to continue the suit. However, several expert methods exist, and the most important factor is a clear partnership agreement on both the opening lead and the meaning of the follow-up signals.
