# Coup en Passant Technique

## Overview

**Coup en Passant** (French for **"passing stroke"**) is an advanced declarer-play technique in which declarer deliberately allows one defender to ruff a trick, knowing that the defender's trump is **not high enough** to beat declarer's remaining trump winner.

Unlike an ordinary finesse or endplay, declarer **benefits from being overruffed** because the opponent's trump trick would have been a winner anyway. By allowing the defender to ruff first, declarer promotes a trump trick in the opposite hand.

The Coup en Passant is closely related to the **Trump Coup**, but the two techniques are different.

---

# Purpose

The Coup en Passant allows declarer to:

- Promote a trump trick.
- Neutralize a defender's high trump.
- Avoid an otherwise unavoidable trump loser.
- Create an additional trick through timing.
- Make contracts that appear impossible.

---

# Basic Principle

The guiding principle is:

> **Let the defender ruff first, then win with your remaining trump.**

Instead of trying to overruff immediately, declarer allows the defender to take the ruff because doing so improves the remaining trump position.

---

# When to Use

A Coup en Passant is appropriate when:

- A defender has a high but isolated trump.
- Declarer has a remaining trump winner.
- Declarer can force the defender to ruff.
- Ruffing immediately would not gain anything.
- Timing favors declarer.

---

# Requirements

A successful Coup en Passant usually requires:

- A suit contract.
- Accurate counting of the remaining trumps.
- A forced ruff by the defender.
- Proper entries.
- Correct timing.

---

# Relationship to Other Techniques

The Coup en Passant is related to:

- Trump Coup
- Trump Promotion
- Uppercut
- Endplay
- Elimination Play

Unlike a **Trump Coup**, declarer does **not** shorten their own trumps to equal the defender's length. Instead, declarer benefits by allowing the defender to ruff first.

---

# Example 1 – Basic Coup en Passant

## Contract

```
4♠
```

Remaining trumps:

Dummy

```
♠ K
```

Declarer

```
♠ A
```

West

```
♠ Q
```

Declarer leads a side suit.

West is forced to ruff with the ♠Q.

Declarer later wins the remaining trump trick with the ♠A.

West's trump winner has been neutralized without costing declarer an extra trick.

# Example 2 – Forced Ruff

Trump:

```
♥
```

Declarer leads a losing club.

East ruffs with the ♥J.

Declarer still holds the ♥A.

The defender's trump trick is taken at a moment favorable to declarer, leaving declarer in control.

# Planning the Play

Before Trick One, ask:

- Can a defender be forced to ruff?
- Would that trump winner score anyway?
- Do I gain by letting the defender ruff first?
- Are my entries sufficient?
- Is there a better alternative?

---

# Timing

Timing is critical.

Typical sequence:

1. Count remaining trumps.
2. Preserve entries.
3. Force the defender to ruff.
4. Win the remaining trump later.
5. Cash the rest of the winners.

Allowing the defender to ruff too early or too late may destroy the position.

---

# Common Situations

Coup en Passant frequently occurs:

- In suit contracts.
- In end positions.
- After elimination plays.
- Together with endplays.
- In trump control battles.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Trying to overruff unnecessarily.
- Miscounting the remaining trumps.
- Forcing the ruff too early.
- Losing a vital entry.
- Confusing the Coup en Passant with a Trump Coup.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Avoid being forced to ruff prematurely.
- Preserve trump communication.
- Count declarer's remaining trumps.
- Recognize potential Coup en Passant positions.
- Avoid unnecessary trump expenditure.

---

# Advantages

- Converts an inevitable defensive trump trick into a harmless one.
- Promotes declarer's remaining trump winner.
- Improves trump control.
- Frequently avoids an extra loser.
- Elegant and efficient endgame technique.

---

# Disadvantages

- Rarely available.
- Requires accurate trump counting.
- Highly dependent on timing.
- Easy to overlook.

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Count the remaining trumps.
2. Preserve communication.
3. Force the defender to ruff.
4. Retain your winning trump.
5. Cash the remaining winners.

# Memory Aid

**Coup en Passant = "Let the Defender Ruff First."**

Remember:

- **Do not overruff automatically.**
- **If the defender's trump trick is inevitable, let it happen at the right time.**
- **Retain control of the remaining trumps.**
- **Count the trump position carefully.**
- **Do not confuse this technique with the Trump Coup—they are related but distinct.**

The **Coup en Passant** is a subtle but powerful declarer-play technique. By allowing a defender to score an inevitable trump trick at the moment most favorable to declarer, the remaining trump position is improved and an otherwise difficult contract can often be brought home. Mastery of this technique requires excellent visualization, precise timing, and accurate trump counting.
