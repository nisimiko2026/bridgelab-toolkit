# Throw-In (Endplay) Technique

## Overview

A **Throw-In**, also known as an **Endplay**, is an advanced declarer-play technique in which declarer deliberately gives the lead to a defender at a carefully chosen moment, forcing that defender to make a favorable return.

Instead of trying to avoid losing a trick, declarer intentionally **"throws in"** a defender who has no safe exit. The defender must then lead into declarer's tenace, concede a ruff-and-discard, or otherwise give declarer an extra trick.

The Throw-In is one of the most elegant and powerful techniques in bridge and is frequently combined with **Elimination Play**, **Avoidance Play**, and **Strip Squeeze**.

---

# Purpose

A Throw-In allows declarer to:

- Force a defender to make a favorable lead.
- Avoid risky finesses.
- Create an extra trick.
- Produce a ruff-and-discard.
- Exploit defenders who have no safe exit cards.

---

# Basic Principle

The guiding principle is:

> **Force the defender to lead for you.**

Declarer first removes the defender's safe exit cards, then deliberately loses a trick to that defender.

Because the defender has no safe return, any lead benefits declarer.

---

# Requirements

A successful Throw-In usually requires:

- A defender who must eventually win a trick.
- Elimination of the defender's safe exit suits.
- Proper timing.
- Accurate counting.
- Sufficient entries.

---

# Relationship to Other Techniques

A Throw-In is closely related to:

- **Endplay** (another name for the same technique)
- **Elimination Play**
- **Strip Squeeze**
- **Avoidance Play**

In many hands:

1. Elimination creates the position.
2. Throw-In exploits it.

---

# Example 1 – Lead into a Tenace

Contract

```
4♠
```

Dummy

```
♠ AQ
```

Declarer

```
♠ J10
```

West

```
♠ Kx
```

Declarer first eliminates clubs and hearts.

Then declarer exits with a diamond.

West wins.

West has only spades remaining.

Any spade return gives declarer two spade tricks.

---

# Example 2 – Ruff-and-Discard

Trump:

```
♥
```

Declarer strips clubs and diamonds.

West wins a spade.

West must return:

- A club.
- Or a diamond.

Either allows declarer to ruff in one hand and discard a loser from the other.

---

# Example 3 – Forced Trump Lead

Trump:

```
♠
```

Declarer strips all side suits.

West wins a diamond.

West's only remaining cards are trumps.

The forced trump lead removes one of West's own trumps and helps declarer avoid a loser.

---

# Planning the Play

Before Trick One, ask:

- Which defender should be thrown in?
- Which suits must be eliminated?
- Can I force that defender to win a trick?
- What return will the defender be forced to make?
- Have I preserved the necessary entries?

---

# Eliminating Safe Exit Cards

Before the Throw-In:

Declarer should:

- Cash side-suit winners.
- Ruff remaining side-suit losers.
- Remove defenders' safe exit cards.

The fewer safe exits a defender has, the stronger the Throw-In becomes.

---

# Timing

Timing is critical.

Typical order:

1. Count losers.
2. Eliminate side suits.
3. Preserve entries.
4. Throw the defender in.
5. Benefit from the forced return.

Throwing the defender in too early usually fails.

---

# Common Situations

Throw-Ins frequently occur:

- In suit contracts.
- In Notrump contracts.
- After Elimination Play.
- Before or after a Strip Squeeze.
- To avoid a finesse.
- To create a ruff-and-discard.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Forgetting to eliminate one exit suit.
- Throwing in the wrong defender.
- Losing a vital entry.
- Miscounting the defenders' remaining cards.
- Giving the defender a safe exit.

---

# Defensive Countermeasures

Defenders should:

- Preserve safe exit cards.
- Avoid winning tricks too early.
- Exchange guarding responsibilities.
- Count declarer's elimination plan.
- Recognize developing Throw-In positions.

---

# Advantages

- Avoids risky finesses.
- Forces favorable leads.
- Creates extra tricks.
- Produces ruff-and-discard opportunities.
- Extremely effective in close contracts.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires careful planning.
- Accurate counting is essential.
- Timing errors can destroy the position.
- Not every hand contains a Throw-In opportunity.

---

# Declarer's Checklist

Before attempting a Throw-In:

- Which defender should win the trick?
- Have I removed all safe exits?
- Which return will the defender be forced to make?
- Are my entries intact?
- Can I improve the position before conceding the lead?

---

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Identify the target defender.
2. Eliminate safe exit suits.
3. Preserve entries.
4. Throw the defender in.
5. Profit from the forced return.

# Memory Aid

**Throw-In = "Make the Defender Play for You."**

Remember:

- **A Throw-In and an Endplay are the same technique.**
- **Remove the defender's safe exits before conceding the lead.**
- **Choose the correct defender to throw in.**
- **The defender's forced return should benefit declarer.**
- **Throw-Ins often avoid risky finesses and create ruff-and-discard opportunities.**

The Throw-In is one of the classic techniques of expert declarer play. By carefully stripping the defenders of safe exit cards and forcing the right defender to win a trick, declarer turns the defenders' lead into an advantage. Mastering Throw-Ins requires accurate counting, precise timing, and careful planning, making it a cornerstone of advanced bridge technique.
