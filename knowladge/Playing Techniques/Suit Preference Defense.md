# Suit Preference Defense

## Overview

**Suit Preference Defense** is a defensive signaling method in which a defender uses the **rank of a played card** to indicate which side suit partner should lead or continue. Unlike **Attitude Signals**, which indicate whether a suit is desirable, or **Count Signals**, which show the number of cards held, a Suit Preference Signal identifies **which suit** partner should prefer.

Suit Preference signals are widely used by experienced partnerships and are particularly valuable when **attitude and count are already known or irrelevant**.

The guiding principle is:

> **Use the size of the card to tell partner which suit you prefer.**

---

# Purpose

Suit Preference Defense allows defenders to:

- Direct partner to the correct suit.
- Improve partnership communication.
- Avoid costly defensive switches.
- Coordinate ruffs and cashing winners.
- Defend complex contracts more accurately.

---

# Basic Principle

A Suit Preference Signal gives information about **the other two suits**, excluding:

- The suit currently being played.
- The trump suit (in suit contracts).

The signal is made using the rank of the card played.

Typical agreement:

- **High card** → Prefer the **higher-ranking** side suit.
- **Low card** → Prefer the **lower-ranking** side suit.
- **Middle card** → No preference or neutral.

*(Partnership agreements may vary.)*

---

# When to Use

Suit Preference signals are commonly used:

- When giving partner a ruff.
- After cashing an Ace.
- When following to partner's winning trick.
- When discarding.
- In squeeze defense.
- In endgame positions.
- When attitude and count are no longer relevant.

---

# Example 1 – Giving a Ruff

## Contract

```
4♠
```

West leads the ♦A.

East follows with:

```
♦9
```

Partnership agreement:

- High = higher side suit.
- Low = lower side suit.

The ♦9 requests a **club** return (the higher-ranking side suit between clubs and hearts).

West later leads a club, allowing East to score a ruff.

---

# Example 2 – Discard Signal

West wins the ♥A.

East discards:

```
♣2
```

The low discard requests the **lower-ranking** remaining suit.

West switches accordingly.

---

# Example 3 – Cashing Winners

West cashes the ♠A against a Notrump contract.

East plays:

```
♠9
```

The high card requests a switch to the higher-ranking remaining suit.

West changes the defense successfully.

---

# Common Situations

Suit Preference signals are especially valuable:

## After Giving Partner a Ruff

The defender indicates which suit should be returned after the ruff.

---

## On Partner's Cashing Winner

A defender indicates the preferred continuation.

---

## During Discards

A discard may indicate:

- Which suit to attack.
- Which suit to avoid.

---

## Squeeze Defense

Suit Preference may help partner preserve the correct guard.

---

# Planning the Defense

Before signaling, ask:

- Does partner already know my attitude?
- Does partner already know my count?
- Which remaining suit should partner attack?
- Can I safely give a Suit Preference Signal?
- Will partner recognize it?

---

# Timing

Timing is important.

Suit Preference signals are most effective:

- After attitude has been established.
- After count has become known.
- When partner faces a choice of return.

They are generally **not** used when attitude or count information is more important.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Giving Suit Preference when partner needs attitude.
- Giving Suit Preference when count is more important.
- Forgetting partnership agreements.
- Sending ambiguous signals.
- Misreading partner's signal.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Before using Suit Preference, partners should agree:

- Which situations call for Suit Preference.
- Whether high means higher-ranking suit.
- Whether middle cards are neutral.
- Priority between attitude, count, and Suit Preference.

---

# Advantages

- Improves partnership communication.
- Helps select the correct defensive switch.
- Essential for advanced defensive play.
- Particularly useful in end positions.
- Reduces guesswork.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires partnership agreement.
- Can be confused with count or attitude.
- May be impossible with awkward spot cards.
- Incorrect interpretation may cost a trick.

---

# Related Techniques

| Technique | Purpose |

| False Carding | Mislead declarer while communicating with partner |

---

# Defender's Checklist

Before giving a Suit Preference Signal:

- Does partner need attitude instead?
- Does partner need count instead?
- Which side suit do I want returned?
- Can my spot card clearly show the preference?
- Will partner interpret the signal correctly?

---

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Determine whether Suit Preference is appropriate.
2. Identify the preferred return suit.
3. Choose the correct spot card.
4. Confirm partnership agreement.
5. Watch for partner's response.

---

# Comparison

| Signal Type | Communicates |
|--------------|--------------|
| Attitude | Do I like this suit? |
| Count | How many cards do I have? |
| Suit Preference | Which suit should partner play next? |
| Lavinthal | Suit preference given by a discard after partner wins a trick |
| Smith Echo | Attitude toward the opening lead |

---

# Typical Signaling Process

```
Partner Wins a Trick
↓

Partner Has a Choice of Return

↓

Play High or Low Spot Card

↓

Partner Reads Suit Preference

↓

Correct Defensive Switch
```

---

# Memory Aid

**Suit Preference = "Tell Partner Where to Go Next."**

Remember:

- **Suit Preference chooses a suit, not attitude or count.**
- **High usually requests the higher-ranking side suit.**
- **Low usually requests the lower-ranking side suit.**
- **Use Suit Preference only when it has priority over attitude and count.**
- **Clear partnership agreements are essential.**

Suit Preference Defense is one of the cornerstones of expert partnership defense. By using the rank of a spot card to indicate the preferred return suit, defenders can coordinate switches, obtain ruffs, and avoid costly misunderstandings. When used at the correct time and with clear partnership agreements, Suit Preference signaling becomes a powerful communication tool in every level of bridge.
