# Timing

## Overview

**Timing** is the art of playing cards in the **correct order** to maximize the chances of making or defeating a contract. While **Tempo** is about *who has the initiative* or *who wins the race*, **Timing** is about *when* to perform each action.

Many bridge techniques fail not because the idea is wrong, but because they are executed **too early or too late**. Expert players understand that the sequence of plays is often as important as the plays themselves.

The guiding principle is:

> **Do the right thing at the right time.**

---

# Purpose

Good Timing allows players to:

- Preserve entries.
- Develop winners efficiently.
- Execute squeezes and endplays.
- Prevent communication problems.
- Maximize trick-taking.
- Avoid blocking suits.
- Control the flow of the hand.

---

# Timing vs. Tempo

Although closely related, the concepts are different.

| Timing | Tempo |
|---------|-------|
| **When** to play a card | **Who** controls the race |
| Order of play | Speed of developing tricks |
| Sequence of actions | Initiative and time |
| Internal hand management | Overall race between declarer and defenders |

Both concepts work together throughout the play.

---

# Basic Principle

Before playing a card, ask:

- Should I play this **now**?
- Should I wait?
- What happens if I delay?
- What happens if I act immediately?

Correct timing often determines the success of the contract.

---

# Timing in Declarer Play

Declarer must decide:

- When to draw trumps.
- When to establish a long suit.
- When to lose the lead.
- When to preserve entries.
- When to cash winners.
- When to execute a squeeze or endplay.

---

# Example 1 – Drawing Trumps

Contract

```
4♠
```

Declarer has a club loser that can be ruffed in dummy.

If declarer draws all the trumps first:

The ruff disappears.

Correct timing:

1. Ruff the club.
2. Then draw trumps.

---

# Example 2 – Establishing a Long Suit

Contract

```
3NT
```

Declarer needs to establish clubs.

Correct timing:

Lose a club trick immediately.

Waiting too long may allow the defenders to establish and cash their own winners first.

---

# Example 3 – Cashing Winners

Declarer has:

```
♦AKQ
```

and only one entry to dummy.

If the entries are used before cashing diamonds:

The winners become stranded.

Timing requires preserving the entry until the diamonds are ready to cash.

---

# Timing in Defensive Play

Defenders also rely on timing.

Examples:

- Cash winners before declarer discards losers.
- Switch suits before declarer establishes a long suit.
- Force declarer before trumps are drawn.
- Preserve entries for partner.

A delayed switch often costs the contract.

---

# Timing and Entries

Many timing problems are actually entry problems.

Always ask:

- Which hand should I be in?
- Will this play destroy an entry?
- Should I preserve an honor as an entry?

Poor timing often leaves established winners unreachable.

---

# Timing and Trumps

The timing of drawing trumps is critical.

Draw trumps early when:

- Preventing Cross Ruff.
- Protecting side-suit winners.
- Maintaining control.

Delay drawing trumps when:

- Ruffing losers.
- Executing Dummy Reversal.
- Preparing a Trump Coup.

---

# Timing and Squeezes

Successful squeezes require precise timing.

Declarer must:

1. Rectify the count.
2. Preserve entries.
3. Cash winners in the correct order.
4. Play the squeeze card last.

One premature play can destroy the squeeze.

---

# Timing and Endplays

Endplays require:

- Eliminating side suits.
- Preserving the throw-in card.
- Waiting until the defender has no safe exit.

Acting too early allows the defender to escape.

---

# Planning the Play

Before Trick One, ask:

- What is my overall plan?
- Which plays must come first?
- Which entries are critical?
- Can I afford to lose the lead?
- Which tricks should be delayed?

---

# Common Situations

Timing is critical in:

- Notrump contracts.
- Suit contracts.
- Long-suit establishment.
- Entry Management.
- Safety Plays.
- Hold-Up Plays.
- Squeezes.
- Endplays.
- Trump Coups.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Drawing trumps too early.
- Cashing winners too soon.
- Losing entries.
- Delaying suit establishment.
- Executing a squeeze before rectifying the count.
- Playing automatically instead of planning.

---

# Advantages

Good timing:

- Preserves communication.
- Maximizes winners.
- Supports advanced techniques.
- Prevents blocked suits.
- Increases contract success.

---

# Disadvantages

Poor timing:

- Wastes entries.
- Loses tempo.
- Blocks suits.
- Prevents squeezes.
- Creates unnecessary losers.

---

# Player's Checklist

Before every important play:

- Is this the right time?
- Should I preserve an entry?
- Should I draw trumps now?
- Can I afford to lose the lead?
- Will delaying improve my position?

---

# Memory Guide

## Five Questions

1. What is my overall plan?
2. Which action must happen first?
3. Which entries are essential?
4. Can I safely delay this play?
5. What happens if I play this card now?

---

# Comparison

| Concept | Main Idea |
|-----------|-----------|
| Timing | Perform each action in the correct order |
| Tempo | Win the race to establish tricks |
| Entry Management | Preserve communication |
| Planning the Play | Organize the complete strategy |
| Hold-Up Play | Delay intentionally to improve timing |

# Typical Planning Process

```
Count Winners or Losers
↓

Develop the Overall Plan

↓

Identify Critical Entries

↓

Determine the Correct Order

↓

Execute Each Step

↓

Adjust as New Information Appears
```

---

# Memory Aid

**Timing = "The Right Play at the Right Moment."**

Remember:

- **Good timing is about sequence, not speed.**
- **Many correct plays fail because they are made at the wrong time.**
- **Always plan the entire hand before playing to Trick One.**
- **Protect entries and avoid blocking your own winners.**
- **Expert bridge is often won by superior timing rather than superior cards.**

Timing is one of the most important strategic skills in bridge. Whether deciding when to draw trumps, establish a long suit, lose the lead, or execute an advanced technique such as a squeeze or endplay, the order of play is often the deciding factor. Mastering timing allows players to transform sound ideas into successful contracts and accurate defenses.
