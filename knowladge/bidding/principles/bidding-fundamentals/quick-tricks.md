# Quick Tricks

## Purpose

**Quick Tricks (QT)** measure a hand's **immediate trick-taking potential** from high honors. Unlike High Card Points (HCP), Quick Tricks emphasize **controls** (Aces and protected Kings) rather than overall honor strength.

Quick Tricks are commonly used for:

- Opening hand evaluation
- Rule of 22
- Game and slam investigation
- Defensive strength assessment
- Comparing borderline opening hands

Quick Tricks are a supplement to HCP and distribution, not a replacement.

---

# Standard Quick Trick Values

Evaluate each suit independently.

| Holding | Quick Tricks |
|---------|-------------:|
| AK | 2.0 |
| AQ | 1.5 |
| A | 1.0 |
| KQ | 1.0 |
| K | 0.5 |
| Other holdings | 0 |

Examples:

```
AKxx      = 2
AQxx      = 1.5
Axxx      = 1
KQxx      = 1
Kxxx      = 0.5
QJxx      = 0
```

# Using Quick Tricks

## Opening Bids

Quick Tricks help distinguish between hands with the same HCP.

Example:

### Hand A

```
♠ AK943
♥ K74
♦ 862
♣ J4
```

- HCP = 10
- QT = 2.5

### Hand B

```
♠ QJ943
♥ Q74
♦ K62
♣ J4
```

- HCP = 10
- QT = 0.5

Although both hands contain the same HCP, **Hand A is far stronger** because it has much greater immediate trick-taking power.

---

## Rule of 22

The Rule of 22 uses Quick Tricks directly.

Open when:

```
HCP
+ Length of two longest suits
+ Quick Tricks
≥ 22
```

This rewards hands with controls rather than scattered queens and jacks.

---

## Slam Bidding

Hands rich in Quick Tricks generally produce:

- Better slam potential
- More reliable controls
- Better chances opposite minimum hands

---

## Defensive Evaluation

Quick Tricks estimate how many immediate defensive tricks your side may take before declarer establishes winners.

For example:

```
AK
```

normally produces two immediate tricks.

```
A
```

normally produces one.

```
KQ
```

usually produces one if partner has the Ace or declarer cannot immediately attack the suit.

---

# Strengths

Quick Tricks:

- Value controls more accurately than HCP
- Distinguish between good and poor 12-point hands
- Improve borderline opening decisions
- Useful for slam judgment
- Simple to calculate

---

# Limitations

Quick Tricks:

- Ignore suit length
- Ignore intermediates (10s, 9s, 8s)
- Ignore distribution
- Ignore suit quality beyond top honors
- Should never replace complete hand evaluation

Always consider:

- HCP
- Distribution
- Suit quality
- Controls
- Vulnerability
- Partnership style

# Summary

Quick Tricks measure the **immediate trick-taking strength** of a hand by emphasizing Aces and protected Kings. They are widely used in opening decisions, especially in the **Rule of 22**, and provide a more realistic assessment of a hand's offensive and defensive potential than HCP alone.
