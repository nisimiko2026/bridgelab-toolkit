# Playing Tricks

## Purpose

**Playing Tricks (PT)** estimate the number of tricks a hand is expected to take **as declarer with little or no help from partner**, assuming a suitable trump fit or an appropriate contract.

Unlike **High Card Points (HCP)**, which measure honor strength, or **Quick Tricks**, which measure immediate winners, Playing Tricks evaluate a hand's **offensive trick-taking potential** based on:

- Suit length
- Suit quality
- Distribution
- Controls

Playing Tricks are particularly useful for:

- Evaluating opening bids
- Weak-two and preemptive openings
- Competitive bidding
- Sacrifice decisions
- Assessing offensive versus defensive strength

---

# Basic Concept

Estimate how many tricks your hand can produce if your long suits become winners.

Hands with:

- Long, solid suits
- Shortages
- Controls
- Good intermediates

typically contain **more Playing Tricks** than their HCP alone would suggest.

# Estimating Playing Tricks

There is no universally accepted formula, but experienced players use the following guidelines.

## Major Suit Examples

### AKQJxx

```
AKQJxx
```

Approximately **6 Playing Tricks**

### AKQxx

```
AKQxx
```

Approximately **5 Playing Tricks**

### AQJxxx

```
AQJxxx
```

Approximately **5 Playing Tricks**

---

### KQJxxx

```
KQJxxx
```

Approximately **4½ Playing Tricks**

---

### QJ109xx

```
QJ109xx
```

Approximately **3½–4 Playing Tricks**

## Side Suit Values

| Holding | Approximate PT |
|---------|---------------:|
| AK | 2 |
| AQ | 1½–2 |
| A | 1 |
| KQ | 1 |
| K | ½ |
| QJ | 0–½ |
| Small cards | 0 |

These values are approximate and depend on suit length and texture.

Although only 13 HCP, this hand is highly offensive.

# Applications

## Opening Bids

Hands with many Playing Tricks often justify opening despite modest HCP.

Example:

```
♠ AKQJ96
♥ 74
♦ 95
♣ 863
```

Only 10 HCP but approximately **6 Playing Tricks**, making it an easy opening bid.

---

## Weak Two Openings

A typical Weak Two opening usually promises:

- Around **6 Playing Tricks**
- Good six-card suit
- Limited defensive values

---

## Preemptive Openings

Three-level preempts generally promise:

- About **7 Playing Tricks**
- Excellent suit quality
- Highly offensive distribution

---

## Competitive Auctions

Playing Tricks help determine:

- Whether to compete further
- Whether to sacrifice
- Whether to double opponents

More Playing Tricks generally indicate a more offensive hand.

# Playing Tricks vs Quick Tricks

| Playing Tricks | Quick Tricks |
|----------------|--------------|
| Long-term winners | Immediate winners |
| Offensive evaluation | Defensive evaluation |
| Emphasize suit quality | Emphasize controls |
| Used for preempts | Used for opening decisions |
| Depend on distribution | Depend on top honors |

---

# Playing Tricks vs Losing Trick Count

| Playing Tricks | Losing Trick Count |
|----------------|--------------------|
| Estimates winners | Estimates losers |
| Used for offensive evaluation | Used after a fit is found |
| Can be applied before finding a fit | Best with trump support |

---

# Advantages

Playing Tricks:

- Reward long, strong suits
- Better evaluate offensive hands
- Useful in competitive auctions
- Improve preemptive bidding
- Complement HCP and distribution

---

# Limitations

Playing Tricks:

- Are estimates rather than exact calculations
- Depend on finding a suitable trump fit
- Ignore partner's contribution
- May overvalue unsupported long suits
- Should not replace complete hand evaluation

Always consider:

- HCP
- Quick Tricks
- Distribution
- Suit quality
- Vulnerability
- Partnership style

# Summary

Playing Tricks estimate a hand's **offensive trick-taking potential**, emphasizing long, strong suits and distribution rather than raw HCP. They are especially valuable when evaluating weak-two openings, preempts, competitive auctions, and highly distributional hands. Used together with HCP, Quick Tricks, and Losing Trick Count, Playing Tricks provide a more complete assessment of a hand's true playing strength.
