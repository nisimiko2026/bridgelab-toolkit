# Chinese Precision

## Overview

**Chinese Precision** is a strong club bidding system developed and refined by the Chinese national bridge program during the 1980s and 1990s. It is based on the Precision family of systems but incorporates several modifications designed to improve bidding accuracy, particularly in competitive auctions and slam investigations.

Like other Precision systems, the cornerstone is a **strong artificial 1♣ opening**, while the remaining one-level openings are limited and highly descriptive. Chinese Precision has been used successfully by Chinese national teams in international competition and remains one of the best-known Precision variants.

---

# System Characteristics

- Strong artificial **1♣**
- Five-card major openings
- Limited one-level openings
- Strong notrump
- Frequent relay sequences
- Early disclosure of hand strength
- Excellent slam bidding structure
- Modern competitive methods

| Opening | Meaning |
|----------|---------|

| 1♦ | Natural, 11–15 HCP |
| 1♥ | Natural, 5+ hearts, 11–15 HCP |
| 1♠ | Natural, 5+ spades, 11–15 HCP |

| 2♣ | Natural, clubs, limited |
| 2♦ | Weak Two |
| 2♥ | Weak Two |
| 2♠ | Weak Two |
| 2NT | 20–21 balanced |
| 3-level | Preemptive |

# The Strong Club

## 1♣

Artificial.

Shows:

- 16+ HCP
- Any distribution
- Game interest opposite most responding hands

Unlike natural systems, the 1♣ opening says **nothing about clubs**.

Example:

♠ AKQ7  
♥ A94  
♦ AK6  
♣ K85

Open:

**1♣**

---

# Responses to 1♣

A common response structure is:

| Response | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| 1♦ | Negative (0–7 HCP) |
| 1♥ | Positive, hearts |
| 1♠ | Positive, spades |
| 1NT | Balanced positive |
| 2♣ | Clubs |
| 2♦ | Diamonds |

Many partnerships use relay or step responses after the initial reply to describe shape and strength more precisely.

---

# Limited One-Level Openings

After any natural one-level opening, opener is normally limited to **11–15 HCP**.

This allows responder to judge quickly whether game is possible.

Example:

1♠

shows:

- 5+ spades
- 11–15 HCP

---

# Minor Openings

## 1♦

Natural.

Shows:

- 3+ diamonds
- 11–15 HCP

## 2♣

Natural.

Typically shows:

- Long clubs
- Approximately 11–15 HCP
- Unsuitable for a 1♣ opening

Exact treatment varies by partnership.

---

# Major Openings

## 1♥

- 5+ hearts
- 11–15 HCP

## 1♠

- 5+ spades
- 11–15 HCP

---

# Notrump

## 1NT

Shows:

- 15–17 HCP
- Balanced

Typical shapes:

- 4-3-3-3
- 4-4-3-2
- 5-3-3-2

---

# Weak Two Openings

## 2♦ / 2♥ / 2♠

Show:

- Six-card suit
- Approximately 6–10 HCP
- Preemptive intent

---

# Responses to 1NT

Most Chinese Precision partnerships use:

- Stayman
- Jacoby Transfers
- Texas Transfers
- Smolen (optional)
- Minor-suit Stayman (optional)

---

# Responses to Major Openings

Common agreements include:

- Jacoby 2NT
- Splinter Raises
- Bergen Raises
- Drury (passed hand)
- Fourth Suit Forcing

---

# Competitive Bidding

Frequently employed conventions include:

- Negative Doubles
- Support Doubles
- Responsive Doubles
- Michaels Cue Bid
- Unusual 2NT
- Lebensohl
- Rubensohl
- Weak Jump Overcalls

---

# Slam Methods

Typically include:

- Roman Key Card Blackwood (1430 or 3014)
- Cue Bidding
- Control Bids
- Serious 3NT
- Last Train
- Exclusion Key Card Blackwood (optional)

# Advantages

- Excellent hand description
- Efficient slam bidding
- Early definition of strength
- Limited natural openings simplify responder's decisions
- Strong competitive framework

---

# Disadvantages

- Artificial 1♣ requires partnership experience
- More memory-intensive than natural systems
- Relay sequences can be complex
- Opponents often prepare specialized defenses

# Summary

Chinese Precision is a refined member of the Precision family that combines a **strong artificial 1♣ opening** with **limited natural one-level openings**, **five-card majors**, and a **strong notrump**. Its emphasis on early hand description, relay methods, and efficient slam investigation has made it a successful system at the highest levels of international bridge, particularly among Chinese national teams.
