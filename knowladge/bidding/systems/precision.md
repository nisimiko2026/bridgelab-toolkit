# Precision Club System

# Part 1 — History, Philosophy and System Overview

---

# Overview

The **Precision Club System** is one of the world's best-known **strong club bidding systems**. Developed by **C. C. Wei** during the 1960s, Precision revolutionized competitive bridge by introducing an artificial **strong 1♣ opening** while making the remaining one-level openings highly descriptive and limited in strength.

Unlike natural systems such as **SAYC** or **2/1 Game Force**, Precision separates **strong hands** from **limited hands** immediately.

This provides several important advantages:

- Earlier identification of game and slam.
- More accurate hand description.
- Efficient relay bidding.
- Greater competitive pressure.
- Reduced ambiguity.

Today Precision remains one of the most influential artificial bidding systems ever developed.

---

# History

## Origins

The Precision Club System was created by the Taiwanese businessman and world-class bridge player **C. C. Wei**.

During the early 1960s he believed that traditional natural systems suffered from one major weakness:

> Strong hands consumed too much bidding space before their true strength became known.

His solution was elegant:

Reserve one opening bid—

```
1♣
```

—for **all very strong hands**.

This allowed every other opening bid to become:

- Limited
- More descriptive
- Easier to judge

---

## International Success

Precision became famous after the **Taiwan Precision Team** won the:

- 1969 Bermuda Bowl
- 1970 Bermuda Bowl

The system immediately attracted worldwide attention.

Many countries developed their own Precision variants.

---

# Evolution

Original Precision eventually produced numerous descendants.

Major descendants include:

- Super Precision
- Precision Today
- Swedish Precision
- Chinese Precision
- Ultimate Club
- Magic Diamond
- Meckwell Precision

Although these systems differ in detail, they all preserve the central idea:

> Strong hands begin with an artificial club opening.

---

# Philosophy

Precision is based upon three simple ideas.

## Principle 1

Strong hands should immediately identify themselves.

Instead of:

```
1♠
```

followed by several strong rebids,

Precision begins:

```
1♣
```

Partner immediately knows:

Game is likely.

---

## Principle 2

Limited openings are easier to judge.

Example

```
1♦
```

may promise:

11–15 HCP.

Responder immediately knows opener cannot hold:

18 HCP.

---

## Principle 3

Relay bidding is more efficient.

Because strength is already known, bidding space is used to describe:

- Shape
- Controls
- Suit length
- Slam potential

---

# Artificial vs Natural Systems

Natural systems attempt to make every bid resemble the actual hand.

Example

```
1♥
```

shows:

Hearts.

Precision often uses bids whose primary purpose is to describe:

Strength.

Not suit.

---

# Strong Club Principle

Every strong club system follows one fundamental rule:

```
1♣
```

does **not** promise clubs.

Instead it promises:

Strong hand.

Everything else follows from this idea.

# Summary

The Precision Club System revolutionized bridge bidding by introducing the concept of a **strong artificial 1♣ opening** and limiting the strength of all other one-level openings. Developed by **C. Although more demanding than natural systems such as SAYC, Precision has become the foundation of an entire family of strong club systems and remains one of the most influential bidding methods in tournament bridge.

# Introduction

The defining characteristic of Precision is that the **opening bid immediately classifies the hand**.

Unlike natural systems, where an opening may cover a wide strength range (for example 12–21 HCP), most Precision openings are **limited**, allowing responder to judge the partnership's combined assets much more accurately.

The auction usually proceeds in three stages:

1. Classify the hand.
2. Describe shape.
3. Explore game or slam.

# Part 3 — Competitive Bidding, Interference, Defensive Methods and Balancing

# Introduction

Competitive bidding in Precision differs significantly from natural systems because **one opening bid (1♣) immediately identifies a strong hand**. This changes the objectives for both sides.

For the Precision partnership, the goals are:

- Preserve constructive auctions whenever possible.
- Distinguish between relay and natural continuations.
- Compete efficiently with limited hands.
- Penalize over-aggressive interference.
- Continue slam investigation despite competition.

For the opponents, the goals are often the opposite:

- Disrupt relay sequences.
- Consume bidding space.
- Force the strong club partnership to guess.
- Prevent accurate slam exploration.

Consequently, competition is more frequent and often more aggressive against Precision than against natural systems.

---

# Philosophy of Competitive Bidding

Precision partnerships generally follow these principles:

1. Limited hands compete aggressively.
2. Strong hands remain descriptive.
3. Preserve relay auctions whenever practical.
4. Penalty opportunities increase after a Strong Club opening.
5. Shape is more important than HCP.

# Precision Club System

# Part 4 — Slam Bidding, Relay Development, Partnership Agreements and Advanced Treatments

---

# Introduction

The greatest strength of the Precision Club System is not its artificial **1♣** opening—it is the **accuracy with which it reaches games and slams**.

Because the partnership identifies strong hands immediately and frequently uses relay sequences to exchange precise information, Precision often reaches slams that natural systems either miss or bid with much less certainty.

Unlike SAYC or even 2/1 Game Force, Precision is designed to answer a series of questions systematically:

1. Is game certain?
2. What is opener's exact shape?
3. Where is the trump fit?
4. How many controls do we have?
5. Is slam likely?
6. Which slam is best?

---

# Philosophy of Slam Bidding

Precision follows one fundamental principle:

> **Do not ask for key cards until you know enough about the hand.**

The auction should gradually establish:

- Shape
- Strength
- Fit
- Controls
- Shortness
- Source of tricks

Only then should Blackwood or Roman Key Card Blackwood be considered.

---

# Information Before Action

Natural systems often begin slam exploration after only limited hand description.

Precision attempts to identify:

- Exact distribution.
- Approximate HCP.
- Number of controls.
- Suit quality.

before committing to slam.

# Final Summary

The Precision Club System transformed modern bridge by introducing the concept of a **strong artificial 1♣ opening** combined with **limited natural openings**. This simple but powerful idea allows partnerships to classify hands immediately, exchange information with remarkable efficiency, and investigate games and slams using relay bidding and control-showing methods.

Over time, Precision has become the foundation for an entire family of strong club systems, influencing **Blue Club**, **Super Precision**, **Chinese Precision**, **Ultimate Club**, **Magic Diamond**, and many other expert methods. While its complexity demands discipline, preparation, and detailed partnership agreements, Precision rewards serious partnerships with one of the most accurate and sophisticated bidding frameworks ever developed.

For players willing to invest the effort, Precision remains one of the finest systems available for duplicate and tournament bridge.
