# Acol Bidding System

## Overview

**Acol** is a natural bidding system originating in the United Kingdom and is one of the world's most widely played bridge systems. It is characterized by **four-card majors**, **weak notrump**, and natural suit bidding.

The modern Acol system has many partnership variations. This document describes a standard modern Acol structure.

# Responses to 1NT

## Stayman

```
1NT
2♣
```

Asks for a four-card major.

Responses:

| Response | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| 2♦ | No four-card major |
| 2♥ | Four hearts |
| 2♠ | Four spades |
| 2NT | Both majors (optional agreement) |

---

# Advantages

- Natural and easy to learn.
- Excellent major-suit discovery.
- Flexible hand evaluation.
- Strong competitive methods.
- Popular throughout the UK and Commonwealth countries.

---

# Disadvantages

- Four-card majors sometimes miss 5-3 fits.
- Weak NT may be vulnerable to penalties.
- Many partnership variations exist.

# System Characteristics

- **Natural bidding**
- **Four-card majors**
- **Weak 1NT (12–14 HCP)**
- **Strong artificial 2♣**
- **Weak Two openings**
- **Natural competitive bidding**
- **Stayman and Transfers after 1NT**
- **Rule of 20 for opening decisions**

---

# Memory Aid

Think of **Acol** as the classic natural system:

- **4-card majors**
- **12–14 weak notrump**
- **Strong 2♣**
- **Weak Twos**
- **Natural bidding first, conventions second**

This combination makes Acol one of the simplest and most effective natural bridge systems.
