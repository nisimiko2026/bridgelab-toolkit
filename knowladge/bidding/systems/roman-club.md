# Roman Club

## Overview

**Roman Club** is one of the classic **strong club bidding systems**, developed in Italy during the late 1950s and early 1960s by members of the famous **Italian Blue Team**, particularly **Walter Avarelli** and **Giorgio Belladonna**. It evolved from the earlier **Neapolitan Club** and became one of the most influential strong club systems in bridge history.

Roman Club combines a **strong artificial 1♣ opening**, **four-card major openings**, and **limited natural one-level openings**. The system places particular emphasis on **scientific bidding**, **control-showing methods**, and **slam investigation**, laying much of the groundwork for later systems such as **Blue Club**, **Precision**, and numerous relay-based methods.

Although no longer widely played in its original form, Roman Club remains an important milestone in the evolution of strong club bidding.

---

# Historical Background

During the 1950s, the Italian Blue Team sought increasingly accurate methods for describing strong hands and investigating slams. Building on the Neapolitan Club, the Roman Club introduced a more refined strong-club structure and improved continuations.

Its success in international competition helped establish artificial strong-club systems as a viable alternative to natural bidding. Many later innovations in Precision and other expert systems trace their origins to Roman Club principles.

# The Strong Club

## 1♣

Artificial.

Shows:

- Approximately **17+ HCP**
- Any distribution
- Hand too strong for the limited natural openings

The opening does **not** promise clubs.

Example:

♠ AKQ7  
♥ AQ6  
♦ AK5  
♣ K94

Open:

**1♣**

---

# Responses to 1♣

A typical response structure was:

| Response | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| **1♦** | Negative (0–7 HCP) |
| **1♥** | Positive with hearts |
| **1♠** | Positive with spades |
| **1NT** | Positive balanced |
| **2♣** | Positive with clubs |
| **2♦** | Positive with diamonds |

After the initial response, opener clarified:

- Distribution
- Strength
- Controls
- Suit quality

Although less relay-oriented than later systems, Roman Club introduced more scientific continuations than earlier methods.

---

# Limited Natural Openings

Natural one-level openings generally showed:

- Approximately **11–16 HCP**
- Defined suit
- Limited strength

This limitation allowed responder to judge game prospects accurately.

---

# Major Openings

## 1♥

Shows:

- Four or more hearts
- Approximately 11–16 HCP

## 1♠

Shows:

- Four or more spades
- Approximately 11–16 HCP

Roman Club retained **four-card major openings**, consistent with Italian practice of the period.

---

# Minor Openings

## 1♦

Natural.

Shows:

- Diamonds
- Limited opening values

## 2♣

Natural.

Shows:

- Long clubs
- Limited strength

# Notrump Opening

## 1NT

Shows:

- Balanced hand
- Approximately **16–18 HCP**

Typical balanced shapes:

- 4-3-3-3
- 4-4-3-2
- 5-3-3-2

---

# Strong Two-Bids

Many versions retained strong natural two-level openings in diamonds, hearts, and spades, although treatments differed between partnerships.

---

# Competitive Methods

Compared with modern systems, competitive methods were relatively straightforward:

- Natural overcalls
- Takeout Doubles
- Penalty Doubles
- Limited cue-bid usage

Most modern conventions had not yet been developed.

---

# Slam Methods

Roman Club placed exceptional emphasis on slam bidding through:

- Cue Bidding
- Control-showing bids
- Blackwood
- Accurate hand limitation
- Scientific control evaluation

These methods strongly influenced later expert systems.

# Disadvantages

- Artificial methods require partnership experience
- Four-card majors may miss some 5–3 fits
- Less descriptive than modern relay systems
- Historical methods vary among sources

---

# Historical Importance

Roman Club helped establish the dominance of the Italian Blue Team and demonstrated the effectiveness of strong-club bidding. Many concepts introduced or refined in the system—including limited openings, artificial strength-showing bids, and scientific slam investigation—became standard features of later expert systems.

# Legacy

Roman Club occupies an important place in bridge history as one of the major milestones in the evolution of strong-club bidding. While largely replaced by more modern systems, its ideas continue to influence expert bidding through Precision, Blue Club, and other scientific methods.
