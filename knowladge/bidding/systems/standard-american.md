# Standard American

## Overview

**Standard American (SA)** is one of the most influential natural bidding
systems ever developed. It serves as the foundation for many modern systems,
including **Standard American Yellow Card (SAYC)** and **Two-over-One Game
Force (2/1 GF)**.

The system emphasizes

- natural bidding,
- accurate hand description,
- balanced hand evaluation,
- simple partnership agreements,
- logical auction development.

Unlike club systems such as Precision, Standard American relies primarily on
natural opening bids and progressively describes the hand through responses and
rebids.

Today, Standard American remains one of the world's most widely played systems
and forms the basis of bridge education in many countries.

---

# Objectives

Standard American aims to

- identify the best strain,
- locate major-suit fits,
- evaluate game and slam,
- describe strength naturally,
- simplify partnership agreements,
- provide a logical bidding framework.

---

# Fundamental Principles

The system is based on

- natural opening bids,
- five-card majors,
- longer minor openings,
- high-card point evaluation,
- balanced hand patterns,
- constructive bidding.

---

# Basic Philosophy

The partnership attempts to answer three questions:

1. Do we have enough strength for game?
2. Do we have a major-suit fit?
3. If not, is notrump or a minor suit the best contract?

---

# Hand Evaluation

Primary evaluation uses

- High Card Points (HCP)
- Distribution
- Playing Tricks
- Losing Trick Count (later auctions)
- Controls (slam auctions)

Standard HCP values

| Honor | Value |
|--------|------:|
| Ace | 4 |
| King | 3 |
| Queen | 2 |
| Jack | 1 |

---

# Opening Requirements

A normal opening requires

- approximately 12–21 HCP,
- or equivalent playing strength.

Distribution may justify lighter third-seat openings.

---

# Major Suit Openings

## 1♥

Promises

- five or more hearts,
- opening values.

---

## 1♠

Promises

- five or more spades,
- opening values.

---

# Minor Suit Openings

## 1♣

Shows

- clubs,
- opening values,
- usually three or more clubs.

When holding

```
3-3
```

many partnerships open

1♣.

---

## 1♦

Shows

- diamonds,
- opening values,
- usually three or more diamonds.

Longer minor preferred.

---

# Balanced Notrump Opening

## 1NT

Shows

- 15–17 HCP,
- balanced hand,
- no five-card major (partnership style).

Typical patterns

```
4-3-3-3

4-4-3-2

5-3-3-2
```

---

# Strong Opening

## 2♣

Artificial.

Shows

- very strong hand,
- game force or nearly so.

May be balanced or unbalanced.

---

# Weak Two Openings

Typical openings

```
2♦

2♥

2♠
```

show

- weak hand,
- six-card suit,
- approximately 5–10 HCP.

---

# Three-Level Openings

Natural preempts.

Usually

- seven-card suit,
- weak hand,
- obstructive purpose.

---

# Gambling 3NT

Optional.

Artificial.

Shows

- solid minor,
- little outside strength.

---

# Responses

Responder first determines

- game interest,
- fit,
- strain,
- forcing level.

Standard American uses

- natural responses,
- limit raises,
- invitational bids,
- game-forcing sequences.

---

# Rebids

Opener's rebid clarifies

- strength,
- distribution,
- suit quality,
- support,
- notrump suitability.

Priority is

1. Support responder.
2. Rebid own suit.
3. Introduce second suit.
4. Bid notrump.

---

# Modern Expert Perspective

At the highest level of competition, **pure Standard American is rarely played**
without significant enhancements. However, its natural opening structure
remains the foundation for many world-class partnerships.

Most experts today play a "Standard American Plus" style, incorporating modern
conventions while retaining the familiar natural opening bids.

This evolution has produced systems that are more accurate in game and slam
bidding while preserving the accessibility and logical structure of the
original system.

# Historical Perspective

Standard American evolved during the mid-20th century through the work of
leading American bridge theorists, including **Charles Goren**, **Edgar
Kaplan**, **Alfred Sheinwold**, and others. It gradually replaced the
Culbertson system as the dominant natural bidding method in North America.

The introduction of five-card majors, stronger notrump structures, and later
enhancements such as SAYC and Two-over-One Game Force transformed Standard
American into the basis of most modern natural bidding systems.

---

# Summary

**Standard American** is a natural bidding system built around **five-card
majors**, **natural minor openings**, and logical hand description through
responses and rebids. It emphasizes simplicity, flexibility, and accurate game
and slam evaluation while providing an excellent framework for integrating
modern conventions.

Although many expert partnerships now play enhanced versions such as
**Two-over-One Game Force**, the principles of Standard American remain the
foundation of contemporary natural bidding and continue to influence bridge
players around the world.
