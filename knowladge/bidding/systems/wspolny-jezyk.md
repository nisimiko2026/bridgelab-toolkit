# Wspólny Język (Polish Standard)

## Overview

**Wspólny Język** ("Common Language") is the traditional **natural bidding system** taught and widely played in Poland. Before the widespread adoption of the **Polish Club** system among expert partnerships, Wspólny Język served as the national standard and remains popular in clubs, teaching programs, and among many recreational players.

The system combines **five-card major openings**, a **strong notrump**, and **natural one-level openings**. It is broadly comparable to **Standard American**, but incorporates several conventions and bidding styles that have become standard in Polish bridge.

---

# System Characteristics

- Natural bidding
- Five-card major openings
- Strong notrump
- Natural minor openings
- High Card Point (HCP) evaluation
- Modern competitive methods
- Standardized teaching methods
- Flexible partnership agreements

---

# Basic Opening Structure

| Opening | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| **1♣** | Natural, 3+ clubs |
| **1♦** | Natural, 3+ diamonds |
| **1♥** | Natural, 5+ hearts |
| **1♠** | Natural, 5+ spades |
| **1NT** | Balanced, 15–17 HCP |
| **2♣** | Strong artificial |
| **2♦** | Weak Two (or partnership agreement) |
| **2♥** | Weak Two |
| **2♠** | Weak Two |
| **2NT** | Balanced, 20–21 HCP |
| **3-level** | Preemptive |

> **Note:** Individual Polish partnerships often incorporate modern conventions and treatments. The structure above reflects the commonly taught natural version rather than every local variation.

---

# Hand Evaluation

Wspólny Język uses the standard **High Card Point (HCP)** method.

| Honor | HCP |
|--------|----:|
| Ace | 4 |
| King | 3 |
| Queen | 2 |
| Jack | 1 |

Distributional values are added once a trump fit has been established.

# Notrump Opening

## 1NT

Shows:

- Balanced hand
- **15–17 HCP**

Typical shapes:

- 4-3-3-3
- 4-4-3-2
- 5-3-3-2

Balanced hands with 18–19 HCP normally begin with a suit and rebid **2NT**.

---

# Strong Opening

## 2♣

Artificial.

Shows:

- Very strong hand
- Usually game forcing
- Approximately 22+ HCP or equivalent playing strength

---

# Weak Two Openings

## 2♦ / 2♥ / 2♠

Typically show:

- Six-card suit
- Approximately 6–10 HCP
- Preemptive intent

Some partnerships use different agreements for the **2♦** opening.

# Advantages

- Natural and intuitive
- Easy to learn
- Well supported by Polish teaching materials
- Compatible with modern conventions
- Flexible partnership style
- Good balance between simplicity and accuracy

---

# Disadvantages

- Less descriptive than strong-club systems
- Expert partnerships often migrate to **Polish Club**
- Partnership agreements vary for advanced treatments

---

# Historical Importance

For many years, Wspólny Język served as the standard teaching system in Poland. Although the **Polish Club** system became dominant among tournament experts, Wspólny Język remains an important foundation for bridge education and recreational play.

# Legacy

Wspólny Język played a major role in the development of modern Polish bridge. Its natural structure provided a common framework for generations of players before many advanced partnerships adopted the more sophisticated Polish Club system.

# Summary

**Wspólny Język** ("Common Language") is the traditional Polish natural bidding system based on **five-card major openings**, a **15–17 HCP notrump**, and **natural one-level openings**. Although many expert Polish partnerships now play **Polish Club**, Wspólny Język remains an important teaching system and a solid, modern natural framework for club and tournament bridge.

---

# Notes

- Wspólny Język is **not a single rigid system**. The official teaching framework has evolved over time, and many partnerships add modern conventions while retaining its natural philosophy.
- In contemporary Polish bridge, **Polish Club** is the dominant expert system, while **Wspólny Język** continues to be widely used for instruction and by many club players.
