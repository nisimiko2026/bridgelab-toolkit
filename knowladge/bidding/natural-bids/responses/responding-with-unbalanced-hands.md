# Responding with Unbalanced Hands

## Overview

Unbalanced hands are among the most powerful and flexible hands in bridge.

Unlike balanced hands, whose value is largely determined by high-card points,
unbalanced hands derive much of their strength from

- long suits,
- shortness,
- ruffing potential,
- controls,
- extra playing tricks.

Consequently, responder frequently upgrades unbalanced hands and emphasizes
distribution before pure point count.

Natural bidding systems are designed to uncover these distributional assets as
early as possible.

---

# Objectives

When holding an unbalanced hand, responder should

- describe distribution accurately,
- show long suits,
- locate a trump fit,
- emphasize playing strength,
- distinguish invitational from forcing values,
- preserve bidding space whenever possible.

---

# What Is an Unbalanced Hand?

An unbalanced hand contains

- a singleton,
- a void,
- two doubletons,
- or a long suit creating an irregular shape.

Typical patterns include

- 5-4-3-1
- 6-3-2-2
- 6-4-2-1
- 5-5-2-1
- 7-2-2-2
- 6-5-1-1
- 5-4-2-2 (semi-balanced)

The greater the distribution, the greater the potential for additional tricks.

---

# Evaluating Unbalanced Hands

Responder evaluates

- High Card Points,
- distribution,
- Losing Trick Count,
- controls,
- quick tricks,
- suit quality,
- trump support,
- ruffing potential.

Playing strength often exceeds the hand's HCP.

---

# General Priorities

Responder normally follows this order

1. Raise partner's major.
2. Show a long suit.
3. Show a second suit.
4. Reveal shortness when appropriate.
5. Begin game or slam investigation.

Unlike balanced hands, notrump is often a lower priority.

---

# Long Suits

Long suits are valuable sources of tricks.

Responder should normally show

- a five-card suit,
- a six-card suit,
- an excellent four-card suit if system permits.

Example

```
♠ KQJ984
♥ 6
♦ Q84
♣ J762
```

After

```
1♦
```

Responder bids

```
1♠
```

---

# Two-Suited Hands

Hands containing two long suits should normally describe both suits.

Example

```
♠ AQJ74
♥ K9832
♦ 5
♣ 94
```

Distribution often justifies aggressive bidding.

---

# Singleton

Singletons

- create ruffing potential,
- improve trump contracts,
- upgrade offensive strength.

Example

```
♠ AQ842
♥ K74
♦ 5
♣ QJ73
```

---

# Void

Voids are among the strongest distributional features.

Advantages include

- immediate ruffing,
- control,
- slam potential,
- elimination play.

Voids frequently justify upgrading the hand.

---

# Raising Partner

Support still has the highest priority.

With support and distribution,

responder frequently upgrades the hand.

Example

```
♠ K984
♥ AQ74
♦ 5
♣ J9842
```

After

```
1♠
```

Responder may hold significantly more playing strength than the HCP suggest.

---

# New Suit Responses

Distributional hands naturally introduce new suits.

Example

```
1♦
1♠
```

or

```
1♣
1♥
```

Natural bidding allows the partnership to discover fits early.

---

# Jump Responses

Jump responses usually indicate

- excellent suit,
- strong playing strength,
- invitational or forcing values.

Treatment varies by system.

---

# Two-over-One Responses

Distributional hands frequently qualify for

Two-over-One Game Force.

Example

```
1♠
2♦
```

Natural,

game forcing,

showing a quality suit.

---

# Splinters

A distributional hand with

- trump support,
- singleton or void,
- game-going values,

often uses a Splinter Bid.

Example

```
1♠
4♣
```

Shows

- club singleton,
- spade support,
- game force.

---

# Fit Jumps

Many partnerships use Fit Jumps to show

- support,
- side suit,
- constructive values.

These bids efficiently describe offensive hands.

---

# Losing Trick Count

Distributional hands are ideally suited for

Losing Trick Count.

Example

```
♠ AQJ74
♥ KQ84
♦ 5
♣ Q72
```

Often stronger than its HCP suggest.

---

# Controls

Unbalanced hands often contain concentrated controls.

Experts value

- aces,
- kings,
- shortness

more highly than queens and jacks.

---

# Upgrading Distribution

Upgrade with

- sixth card,
- fifth trump,
- singleton,
- void,
- excellent intermediates,
- concentrated honors.

---

# Downgrading

Downgrade when

- honors are scattered,
- long suits are weak,
- controls are missing,
- suits lack intermediates.

---

# Competitive Auctions

Distribution becomes even more valuable after interference.

Responder frequently

- competes,
- preempts,
- cue bids,
- sacrifices,
- raises aggressively.

Shape frequently outweighs HCP.

---

# Game Decisions

Distribution often upgrades invitational hands into game-going hands.

Opener should evaluate

- fit,
- trump quality,
- controls,
- side suits,
- offensive potential.

---

# Slam Potential

Distribution creates outstanding slam opportunities.

Partnerships investigate

- controls,
- cue bids,
- Roman Key Card Blackwood,
- Exclusion,
- trump quality,
- side-suit winners.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should define

- minimum suit length,
- jump responses,
- Two-over-One,
- Fit Jumps,
- Splinters,
- support style,
- Losing Trick Count,
- control evaluation,
- distributional upgrades.

---

# System Differences

## Standard American

Natural emphasis on long suits.

Distribution upgrades many hands.

---

## SAYC

Natural responses.

Traditional jump shifts.

---

## Two-over-One Game Force

Distributional hands frequently begin game-forcing auctions.

---

## Acol

Similar philosophy.

Some strength ranges differ.

---

## Precision

Artificial openings alter certain auctions.

Natural responses after natural openings remain similar.

---

## Polish Club

Artificial club opening.

Natural continuations after major and diamond openings.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Ignoring Distribution

Distribution is often more important than one or two HCP.

---

## Hiding Long Suits

Long suits should usually be shown early.

---

## Underestimating Shortness

Singletons and voids create additional tricks.

---

## Treating Every Hand as Balanced

Distributional hands require different evaluation methods.

---

## Bidding Notrump Too Quickly

Suit contracts often outperform notrump with strong distribution.

---

## Ignoring Partnership Agreements

Modern treatments differ considerably.

Consistency is essential.

---

# Expert Evaluation

Experts emphasize

- controls,
- trump fit,
- Losing Trick Count,
- suit texture,
- quick tricks,
- ruffing values,
- offensive potential,

rather than relying exclusively on HCP.

---

# Universal Principles

Responder with an unbalanced hand should

- describe shape early,
- show long suits,
- value shortness,
- support partner whenever appropriate,
- preserve bidding space,
- distinguish invitational from forcing values,
- exploit distribution to reach superior contracts.

# Summary

Unbalanced hands derive much of their strength from **distribution rather than
high-card points**. Successful responders recognize the offensive value of

- long suits,
- shortness,
- controls,
- trump support,
- ruffing potential,

and describe these features early in the auction.

Modern natural bidding systems are specifically designed to uncover
distributional strength, enabling partnerships to locate superior suit
contracts, judge game accurately, and explore slams whenever the combined
offensive potential justifies continued investigation.
