# Raising Partner's Major

## Bridge Insight

**Supporting partner's major suit is one of the most valuable actions in bridge. An eight-card fit frequently produces more tricks than a Notrump contract with the same combined strength.**

---

# Overview

When partner opens or bids a major suit, responder's first responsibility is to determine whether sufficient trump support exists. A major-suit fit often becomes the partnership's preferred strain because trumps provide additional trick-taking opportunities through ruffing and control of the play.

Raising partner's major communicates more than support—it also describes strength, offensive potential, and future intentions. The exact meaning of a raise depends on the bidding system and partnership agreements, but the underlying principles are universal.

---

# Objectives

A raise should:

- Confirm a trump fit.
- Describe responder's strength.
- Help partner judge game or slam.
- Improve hand evaluation.
- Narrow the possible final contracts.

---

# What Is a Fit?

A partnership has a fit when together it holds at least:

```
8 cards
```

in the same suit.

Examples:

```
Opener: 5 Hearts
Responder: 3 Hearts

Total = 8-card fit
```

```
Opener: 5 Spades
Responder: 4 Spades

Total = 9-card fit
```

The larger the fit, the greater the potential for ruffing and trump control.

---

# Why Raise Immediately?

Supporting partner early:

- Identifies the trump suit.
- Allows both players to re-evaluate.
- Helps judge game.
- Makes competitive decisions easier.

When holding support, raising is often more important than introducing another suit.

---

# Re-evaluating After a Fit

Once a major fit is found, responder should add value for:

- Singletons.
- Voids.
- Extra trumps.
- Controls.
- Ruffing potential.

Flat point-count evaluation is no longer sufficient.

---

# Types of Raises

### Simple Raise

Shows:

- Trump support.
- Limited values.

Leaves room for further investigation.

---

### Invitational Raise

Shows:

- Good support.
- Interest in game.
- Not enough strength to force game.

Partner decides whether to accept the invitation.

---

### Game Raise

Shows:

- Sufficient combined strength for game.
- Confidence in the trump fit.

The partnership should normally continue to game.

---

### Slam Interest

Shows:

- Excellent fit.
- Controls.
- Extra playing strength.

Begins cooperative slam exploration.

---

# Number of Trumps

General guideline:

Minimum support:

```
3-card support
```

Excellent support:

```
4-card support
```

Some partnerships distinguish between three-card and four-card raises through conventions.

---

# Distribution Matters

Two hands with identical HCP may differ greatly after a fit is found.

Example:

```
♠ Q 9 4
♥ K J 8 5
♦ 7
♣ A J 10 6
```

Often stronger than

```
♠ Q 9 4
♥ K J 8
♦ Q 10 6
♣ A J 7
```

because of the singleton.

---

# Competitive Auctions

When opponents compete, raising partner's major often becomes more valuable.

Raises may:

- Increase pressure.
- Make life difficult for opponents.
- Protect the partnership's fit.

Judgment depends upon vulnerability and scoring.

---

# System Variations

Different systems define raises differently.

Examples include:

- Bergen Raises
- Jacoby 2NT
- Limit Raises
- Mixed Raises
- Splinter Raises

These conventions are explained in separate articles.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Showing another suit instead of immediate support.
- Failing to re-evaluate distribution.
- Underestimating four-card support.
- Ignoring offensive strength.
- Confusing invitational and forcing raises.

---

# Best Practices

Experienced players:

- Support partner promptly.
- Re-evaluate after the fit is found.
- Think in tricks rather than points.
- Consider vulnerability.
- Plan the auction beyond the current bid.

# Player's Checklist

Before raising partner:

- Do we have at least an eight-card fit?
- Do I have three or four trumps?
- Have I re-evaluated my distribution?
- Am I inviting, forcing, or signing off?
- Does another bid describe my hand better?

---

# Memory Guide

## Five Priorities

1. Confirm the fit.
2. Revalue the hand.
3. Show strength.
4. Judge game.
5. Explore slam if appropriate.

---

# Expert Tip

**Once a major fit is found, stop thinking in High Card Points alone.**

Trump length, controls, and shortages often determine whether game or slam succeeds.

---

# Memory Aid

**Support First • Re-evaluate • Decide**

Remember:

- A major-suit fit is often the partnership's greatest asset.
- Distribution increases dramatically in value after support is established.
- Early support simplifies the remainder of the auction.
- Think in terms of tricks, not just points.
- Raising partner's major is one of the most descriptive bids in bridge.
