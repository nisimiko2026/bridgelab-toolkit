# Raising Partner's Major

## Overview

Finding and supporting a major-suit fit is one of the primary objectives of
natural bidding.

An eight-card (or longer) major-suit fit frequently produces more tricks than
a comparable notrump contract because of the ability to

- ruff losers,
- establish long suits,
- create extra entries,
- improve communication,
- exploit distribution.

For this reason, raising partner's major is usually the highest priority once a
fit has been discovered.

---

# Objectives

A raise should communicate

- trump support,
- hand strength,
- distribution,
- game interest,
- slam potential.

The level of the raise accurately places the hand into a strength category.

---

# Requirements

Most partnerships require

### Major Suit

- at least four-card support,
- occasionally strong three-card support,
- suitable playing strength.

The exact requirements depend upon partnership agreements.

---

# Why Major Fits Matter

Compared with notrump,

major-suit contracts allow

- ruffing,
- crossruffing,
- trump control,
- trump promotion,
- safer communication.

As a result,

finding a major fit is usually the partnership's first priority.

---

# Evaluating Support

Trump support is more than simply counting cards.

Experts also evaluate

- trump honors,
- intermediates,
- controls,
- distribution,
- side-suit shortness,
- suit quality.

Example

```
♠ KJ84
♥ A75
♦ Q62
♣ J943
```

Four-card support with useful values.

---

# Strength Categories

## Weak Raise

Approximately

6–9 HCP

Support only.

No invitation.

---

## Limit Raise

Approximately

10–12 HCP

Invites game.

---

## Game Raise

13+ HCP

or equivalent playing strength.

Game expected.

---

## Slam-Oriented Raise

Excellent controls,

distribution,

or exceptional playing strength.

---

# Simple Raise

Example

```
1♠
2♠
```

Shows

- support,
- minimum values,
- constructive hand.

Usually

6–9 HCP.

---

# Limit Raise

Example

```
1♥
3♥
```

Shows

- four-card support,
- invitational values,
- interest in game.

Typically

10–12 HCP.

---

# Direct Game Raise

Example

```
1♠
4♠
```

Shows

- enough values for game,
- no interest in investigating further,
- good trump support.

---

# Jacoby 2NT

Many partnerships use

```
1♠
2NT
```

or

```
1♥
2NT
```

as

- game forcing,
- four-card support,
- slam interest possible.

---

# Bergen Raises

Bergen Raises divide raises into several strength ranges using artificial bids.

They distinguish

- weak raises,
- constructive raises,
- limit raises,
- mixed raises.

---

# Mixed Raise

A Mixed Raise combines

- competitive values,
- four-card support,
- moderate strength.

Usually

approximately 7–9 HCP

with offensive distribution.

---

# Splinter Raise

Shows

- game force,
- singleton or void,
- excellent trump support,
- slam interest.

Example

```
1♠
4♣
```

depending upon partnership agreements.

---

# Fit Jump

Some partnerships use jump shifts to show

- fit,
- side suit,
- constructive values.

---

# Support with Three Cards

Modern partnerships occasionally raise with

excellent

three-card support

when

- distribution compensates,
- honors are well placed,
- no better descriptive bid exists.

This treatment should always be agreed in advance.

---

# Distributional Evaluation

Upgrade with

- singleton,
- void,
- fifth trump,
- sixth trump,
- side-suit source of tricks.

Downgrade with

- flat shape,
- scattered queens,
- poor trump quality.

---

# Losing Trick Count

Many expert partnerships use

Losing Trick Count

rather than HCP.

Shortness and trump length frequently outweigh one or two high-card points.

If opener later shows a major,

raising that major usually has priority over supporting the minor.

# Competitive Raises

Competition changes evaluation.

Consider

- vulnerability,
- fit length,
- offensive values,
- Law of Total Tricks,
- distribution.

Aggressive raises are common with long trump support.

---

# Opener's Evaluation

After partner raises,

opener evaluates

- HCP,
- controls,
- trump quality,
- Losing Trick Count,
- distribution,
- quick tricks.

Then chooses

- pass,
- invite,
- game,
- slam investigation.

---

# Slam Interest

Excellent raises include

- controls,
- shortness,
- good trump texture,
- source of tricks.

Modern slam exploration includes

- cue bids,
- control bids,
- Roman Key Card Blackwood,
- exclusion,
- grand slam tries.

# Common Mistakes

## Raising Too Early

Sometimes describing distribution first is preferable.

---

## Underestimating Trump Quality

Intermediate cards are valuable.

---

## Overvaluing Flat Hands

Balanced hands often produce fewer tricks.

---

## Ignoring Distribution

Singletons and voids greatly increase offensive strength.

---

## Confusing Invitational and Forcing Raises

Jacoby 2NT forces.

## Ignoring Partnership Agreements

Modern raises vary considerably.

# Expert Evaluation

Experts evaluate

- fit quality,
- controls,
- trump texture,
- Losing Trick Count,
- side-suit controls,
- offensive potential,

before choosing the level of the raise.

---

# Universal Principles

Always

- support partner whenever practical,
- value trump length highly,
- evaluate distribution,
- distinguish weak, invitational and forcing raises,
- preserve bidding space when slam is possible.

# Summary

Raising partner's major is one of the fundamental actions in constructive
bidding. A raise does far more than show support—it communicates the
partnership's offensive potential, strength, and ambitions.

By accurately distinguishing between weak, invitational, game-forcing, and
slam-oriented raises, partnerships can locate the optimum contract while making
the most effective use of bidding space. Mastering major-suit raises is
essential for successful natural bidding and forms the foundation of modern
constructive bridge.
