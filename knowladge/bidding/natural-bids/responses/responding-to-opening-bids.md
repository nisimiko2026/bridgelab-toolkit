# Responding to Opening Bids

## Bridge Insight

**The responder's first bid is not about describing every feature of the hand—it is about giving partner the most important information first while keeping the auction alive whenever game remains possible.**

---

# Overview

After partner opens the bidding, responder becomes an equal partner in describing the combined assets of the partnership. Unlike the opening bid, which describes only one hand, responder's bid begins the process of combining the strengths, distributions, and suit fits of both hands to determine the best contract.

The responder's first responsibility is **not simply to bid the longest suit**, but to evaluate the partnership's prospects and communicate the most useful information to opener.

Although bidding systems differ in the exact meanings of specific bids, the fundamental objectives of responder remain remarkably consistent across natural systems such as Standard American, SAYC, 2/1 Game Force, Acol, and many others.

---

# Objectives

Responder's first bid should help the partnership:

- Find an eight-card (or longer) trump fit.
- Determine whether game is possible.
- Exchange information efficiently.
- Describe strength.
- Describe distribution.
- Leave room for accurate future bidding.
- Reach the best contract.

---

# The Three Questions

Before making any response, responder should answer three questions.

## 1. How strong is my hand?

Evaluate:

- High Card Points (HCP)
- Distribution
- Controls
- Suit quality
- Playing strength

Hand evaluation should never rely solely on HCP.

---

## 2. Do we have a fit?

The highest priority is usually finding:

- an 8-card major suit fit.

If no major fit exists, consider:

- minor suit fits
- notrump

---

## 3. What is the most descriptive bid?

Choose the bid that communicates:

- strength
- distribution
- suit length
- future intentions

without making future bidding unnecessarily difficult.

---

# General Priorities

Most natural systems follow these priorities.

1. Support partner's major.
2. Bid your own four-card (or longer) major.
3. Show a longer minor.
4. Bid Notrump with an appropriate balanced hand.
5. Raise partner's minor when appropriate.

The exact order varies slightly among systems.

---

# Evaluating Strength

Although partnerships define exact ranges differently, responder usually thinks in four categories.

## Weak

Approximately

```
0–5 HCP
```

Usually pass unless the opening is forcing or special circumstances apply.

---

## Invitational

Approximately

```
6–9
10–11
```

depending on the auction.

Responder should invite game when appropriate.

---

## Game-going

Hands with sufficient combined strength to expect game.

Responder should avoid stopping below game.

---

## Slam Interest

Hands containing:

- controls
- excellent fit
- distribution
- extra playing strength

These hands begin investigating slam rather than simply bidding game.

---

# Balanced vs. Unbalanced Hands

Responder should immediately classify the hand.

Balanced:

- 4-3-3-3
- 4-4-3-2
- 5-3-3-2

Generally suitable for Notrump bidding.

Unbalanced:

- singleton
- void
- long suit
- two-suited shape

Usually requires natural suit bidding.

---

# Finding the Major Suit Fit

In natural bridge systems the first priority is usually:

> Find a major suit fit.

Why?

Major suit games require:

```
10 tricks
```

whereas

```
3NT
```

requires only

```
9 tricks.
```

However, major suit games are often safer because ruffing provides additional tricks.

---

# Describing Strength

Every response communicates something about strength.

Examples:

Simple raise

```
Limited strength.
```

Jump raise

```
Game values.
```

New suit

```
Usually constructive.
```

Notrump

```
Balanced hand.
```

The exact meanings depend upon partnership agreements.

---

# Describing Shape

Responder should also communicate:

- longest suit
- support
- balanced vs unbalanced
- distributional features

Good bidding paints an accurate picture of the hand.

---

# Forcing and Non-Forcing Responses

One of responder's first responsibilities is understanding whether the bid is:

- Non-forcing
- Invitational
- Forcing for one round
- Game forcing

Misunderstanding forcing level is one of the most common causes of bidding accidents.

---

# Planning Ahead

Expert responders think beyond the current bid.

Instead of asking

> What should I bid now?

they ask

> Where do I expect this auction to go?

Planning ahead avoids awkward rebids later.

---

# Partnership Communication

Good responding is a process of communication.

Every bid should answer one or more questions:

- How strong am I?
- What suits do I hold?
- Do I support partner?
- Am I interested in game?
- Am I interested in slam?

---

# System Variations

The principles discussed in this article apply to nearly all natural bidding systems.

Specific responses vary among:

- Standard American
- SAYC
- 2/1 Game Force
- Acol
- Precision
- Polish Club

For detailed treatments, see the corresponding system articles.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Responding automatically based only on HCP.
- Ignoring an eight-card major fit.
- Showing the wrong suit first.
- Underestimating distributional values.
- Failing to distinguish invitational and forcing hands.
- Planning only the current bid instead of the entire auction.

---

# Best Practices

Strong responders:

- Evaluate both strength and shape.
- Look for the major suit fit first.
- Describe the hand honestly.
- Keep the auction flexible.
- Think one or two bids ahead.
- Cooperate with partner rather than trying to describe everything immediately.

# Player's Checklist

Before making your first response, ask yourself:

- How many points do I really have?
- Is my hand balanced?
- Do we have a major fit?
- What is my longest suit?
- Is game possible?
- Is this bid forcing?
- Will my next rebid be easy?

If every answer is clear, your first response is usually straightforward.

---

# Memory Guide

## The Five Priorities

1. Evaluate the hand.
2. Look for a major fit.
3. Describe strength.
4. Describe shape.
5. Leave room for accurate future bidding.

---

# Expert Tip

**Do not try to tell partner everything with your first response.**

Your first bid should communicate the **most important information**, while leaving room to describe the rest of your hand later. Expert bidding is a conversation, not a monologue.

---

# Memory Aid

**Responding = Evaluate • Fit • Describe • Plan**

Remember:

- Evaluate both strength and distribution.
- Search for an eight-card major fit before choosing another strain.
- Tell partner the most important fact about your hand first.
- Think ahead to your rebid before selecting your first response.
- Bidding is a partnership dialogue in which each bid should make the next decision easier.

A successful response lays the foundation for the entire auction. Regardless of the bidding system being used, accurate hand evaluation, clear communication, and thoughtful planning will consistently lead the partnership toward the best contract.
