# Responding to Opening Bids

## Overview

Responder's first bid is one of the most influential decisions in the auction.

After partner opens the bidding, responder becomes responsible for describing

- strength,
- distribution,
- suit length,
- support,
- game interest,
- slam potential.

Unlike the opening bid, responder already knows approximately

- opener's strength,
- opener's first suit,
- the partnership's minimum combined assets.

The objective is to begin an efficient exchange of information that leads to
the best final contract.

---

# Objectives

Responder's first bid should communicate

- point range,
- distribution,
- longest suit,
- support,
- forcing status,
- interest in game or slam.

---

# Information Available

Responder knows

- opener's first bid,
- opener's approximate strength,
- vulnerability,
- seat position,
- partnership agreements.

Responder does **not** yet know

- opener's exact distribution,
- exact strength,
- secondary suits,
- rebid intentions.

---

# General Priorities

Responder normally follows this order.

1. Raise partner's major.
2. Bid a four-card or longer major.
3. Bid a longer minor.
4. Bid notrump.
5. Use an agreed convention.

---

# Strength Categories

## Very Weak

0–5 HCP

Normally pass unless partner's opening is forcing.

---

## Minimum Response

6–9 HCP

Constructive.

Usually not invitational.

---

## Invitational

10–12 HCP

Invites game.

---

## Game Forcing

13+ HCP

Game should normally be reached.

---

## Slam Interest

Excellent controls,
distribution,
or exceptional strength.

---

# Evaluating the Hand

Responder evaluates

- HCP,
- distribution,
- controls,
- suit quality,
- intermediates,
- Losing Trick Count,
- fit with opener.

Playing strength frequently exceeds HCP.

---

# Responding to Major Openings

After

```
1♥
```

or

```
1♠
```

priority is

- raise with support,
- bid another major,
- bid a new suit,
- bid notrump,
- use agreed conventions.

Major fits have the highest priority.

---

# Responding to Minor Openings

After

```
1♣
```

or

```
1♦
```

priority becomes

- search for a major fit,
- bid longest suit,
- bid notrump,
- support the minor when appropriate.

Minor raises are generally less urgent than major raises.

---

# Raising Partner

Support takes priority whenever practical.

Strength determines the level.

| Raise | Typical Strength |
|--------|-----------------:|
| Simple Raise | 6–9 HCP |
| Limit Raise | 10–12 HCP |
| Game Raise | 13+ HCP |
| Slam Raise | Exceptional |

Modern systems may substitute

- Bergen Raises,
- Jacoby 2NT,
- Mixed Raises,
- Splinters.

---

# New Suit Responses

A new suit generally promises

- four or more cards,
- constructive values,
- interest in further bidding.

In most natural systems

a new suit at the one level is forcing for one round.

---

# Responding in Notrump

Notrump responses describe

- balanced hands,
- stoppers,
- lack of suitable support,
- limited distribution.

Typical responses include

| Response | Typical Strength |
|-----------|-----------------:|
| 1NT | 6–9 HCP |
| 2NT | Invitational |
| 3NT | Game values |

Exact ranges depend on the system.

---

# Two-over-One Responses

In many modern partnerships

```
1♥
2♣
```

or

```
1♠
2♦
```

shows

- game-forcing values,
- natural suit,
- constructive auction.

---

# Passed-Hand Responses

Responder's previous pass limits strength.

Many bids become

- non-forcing,
- constructive,
- lighter.

Partnership agreements vary.

---

# Distributional Hands

Upgrade hands with

- singleton,
- void,
- long suit,
- strong intermediates,
- excellent controls.

Downgrade

- flat hands,
- scattered honors,
- poor suit quality.

---

# Competitive Auctions

Interference changes priorities.

Responder may

- double,
- cue bid,
- raise,
- compete,
- preempt.

Judgment becomes increasingly important.

---

# Captaincy

Initially

responder often becomes captain because opener has already described the hand.

As the auction develops,

captaincy may change depending on

- hand limitation,
- forcing status,
- conventions used.

---

# Conventional Responses

Common conventions include

- Jacoby 2NT
- Bergen Raises
- Drury
- Walsh
- Inverted Minors
- Fourth Suit Forcing
- New Minor Forcing
- Checkback Stayman
- Splinter Raises
- Two-over-One Game Force

Each convention modifies standard responses.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partnerships should define

- response ranges,
- forcing status,
- minimum suit length,
- support requirements,
- jump responses,
- inverted minors,
- Bergen Raises,
- Jacoby 2NT,
- Walsh style,
- Two-over-One methods.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Ignoring Major Fits

Always search for an eight-card major fit before settling in notrump or a
minor.

---

## Underestimating Distribution

Distribution frequently creates additional tricks.

---

## Overvaluing Flat Hands

Balanced hands generally produce fewer tricks than distributional hands.

---

## Raising Too Weakly

Differentiate between

- simple,
- invitational,
- game-forcing raises.

---

## Hiding a Four-Card Major

After a minor opening,

show a four-card major whenever appropriate.

---

## Ignoring Partnership Agreements

Modern responses vary considerably.

Consistency is more important than the particular method.

---

# Expert Evaluation

Experts evaluate

- controls,
- quick tricks,
- Losing Trick Count,
- fit quality,
- suit texture,
- offensive potential,

rather than relying solely on HCP.

---

# Universal Principles

Responder should

- describe the hand naturally,
- support partner whenever appropriate,
- search for major fits,
- distinguish invitational from forcing values,
- preserve bidding space,
- cooperate toward game and slam.

---

# Summary

Responder's first bid establishes the direction of the constructive auction.
Its purpose is to accurately describe

- strength,
- distribution,
- support,
- suit preference,
- game interest,

while allowing the partnership to locate the best strain and level.

Successful responders combine sound hand evaluation with disciplined bidding
methods and partnership agreements. By distinguishing weak, invitational,
game-forcing, and slam-oriented hands, they provide opener with the information
needed to judge the partnership's combined assets and reach the optimum
contract.
