# Response to a Major Opening

## Overview

Responding to a **major-suit opening** is one of the most important areas of
constructive bidding.

Because opener has already shown a five-card (or sometimes four-card) major,
the partnership's highest priority is to determine

- whether a trump fit exists,
- whether game is possible,
- whether notrump is preferable,
- whether slam should be explored.

Finding an eight-card major-suit fit usually takes precedence over every other
objective because major-suit games require only **10 tricks** and often produce
more tricks through ruffing than comparable notrump contracts.

---

# Meaning of a Major Opening

In most natural systems

```
1♥
```

or

```
1♠
```

shows

- opening values (approximately 12–21 HCP)
- five or more cards in the suit (four in some Acol styles)
- unlimited strength
- natural suit

---

# Responder's Priorities

Responder normally follows this order

1. Raise partner's major.
2. Show another four-card major (when appropriate).
3. Bid a new suit.
4. Bid notrump.
5. Use partnership conventions.

Support almost always takes precedence over introducing another suit.

---

# Evaluating the Hand

Responder evaluates

- High Card Points
- distribution
- controls
- trump support
- Losing Trick Count
- suit quality
- offensive potential

Modern partnerships upgrade hands with

- shortness,
- excellent trump support,
- controls,
- long suits.

---

# Strength Categories

| Strength | Typical Action |
|----------|----------------|
| 0–5 HCP | Pass |
| 6–9 HCP | Constructive response |
| 10–12 HCP | Invitational |
| 13+ HCP | Game force |
| 16+ HCP | Slam investigation |

Distribution frequently modifies these ranges.

---

# Raising Partner's Major

Supporting opener is the highest priority.

---

## Simple Raise

Example

```
1♠
2♠
```

Shows

- approximately 6–9 HCP
- three or four-card support
- constructive values

---

## Limit Raise

Example

```
1♥
3♥
```

Shows

- approximately 10–12 HCP
- four-card support
- invitational values

Many partnerships replace this treatment with Bergen Raises.

---

## Game Raise

Example

```
1♠
4♠
```

Shows

- game-going values
- excellent support
- no interest in further investigation

---

## Jacoby 2NT

Example

```
1♠
2NT
```

Artificial.

Shows

- four-card support
- game-forcing values
- possible slam interest

---

## Splinter Raise

Example

```
1♥
4♣
```

Shows

- four-card support
- singleton or void
- game-forcing values
- slam interest

---

# Responding Without Support

Without support responder usually introduces the longest suit.

Example

```
1♠
2♣
```

Natural.

Shows

- clubs
- constructive values

---

# Responding with Another Major

After

```
1♥
```

Responder may bid

```
1♠
```

showing

- four or more spades
- at least 6 HCP

Finding a spade fit remains important.

After

```
1♠
```

there is no higher-ranking major to bid.

---

# Notrump Responses

Balanced hands without suitable support often choose notrump.

---

## 1NT

Usually

- 6–9 HCP
- balanced
- no support
- no better natural response

In many modern systems

**1NT is forcing** by an unpassed hand.

---

## 2NT

Natural invitation.

Usually

- balanced
- 10–12 HCP

unless Jacoby 2NT is being played.

---

## 3NT

Natural.

Shows

- balanced game values
- no major fit

Rare after a major opening because suit contracts are often superior.

---

# New Suit Responses

A new suit generally promises

- four or more cards
- constructive values

At the one level

a new suit is normally forcing for one round.

At the two level

agreements vary.

---

# Two-over-One Game Force

After

```
1♥
2♣
```

or

```
1♠
2♦
```

most modern partnerships play

**Two-over-One Game Force**.

Shows

- 12+ HCP
- good five-card suit
- game-forcing values

This treatment greatly improves constructive bidding.

---

# Passed-Hand Responses

A passed responder has limited strength.

Many bids become

- constructive,
- non-forcing,
- lighter.

Drury is commonly used after third- or fourth-seat major openings.

---

# Distributional Hands

Upgrade with

- singleton,
- void,
- fifth trump,
- sixth-card side suit,
- concentrated honors.

Distribution frequently outweighs one or two HCP.

---

# Balanced Hands

Balanced responders evaluate

- support,
- notrump,
- invitational strength,
- game prospects.

Support generally has priority over notrump.

---

# Competitive Auctions

After interference responder may

- raise,
- double,
- cue bid,
- compete,
- preempt.

Popular conventions include

- Negative Double
- Jordan 2NT
- Support Double
- Responsive Double

---

# Game Evaluation

Responder evaluates

- combined strength,
- trump quality,
- controls,
- Losing Trick Count,
- offensive potential.

Game is usually bid aggressively with good fits.

---

# Slam Investigation

Strong responder hands begin

- cue bidding,
- control bidding,
- Roman Key Card Blackwood,
- splinters,
- serious 3NT,
- Last Train,
- Exclusion.

---

# Modern Conventions

Frequently used methods include

- Jacoby 2NT
- Bergen Raises
- Mixed Raise
- Drury
- Two-over-One Game Force
- Splinter Raises
- Help-Suit Game Tries
- Fourth Suit Forcing
- Serious 3NT
- Last Train

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should define

- minimum trump support,
- three-card raises,
- Jacoby 2NT,
- Bergen Raises,
- Mixed Raises,
- Splinters,
- Forcing 1NT,
- Two-over-One,
- Drury,
- game-try structure.

# System Differences

## Standard American

Natural responses.

Traditional raises.

---

## SAYC

Natural structure.

---

## 2/1 Game Force

Forcing 1NT.

Jacoby 2NT.

Two-over-One Game Force.

Extensive game-force methods.

---

## Acol

Four-card majors possible.

Response priorities remain similar.

---

## Precision

Artificial club system affects only unrelated auctions.

---

## Polish Club

Natural responses after major openings.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Missing a Major Fit

Support partner whenever appropriate.

---

## Bidding Notrump Too Quickly

Suit contracts frequently outperform notrump.

---

## Ignoring Distribution

Distribution creates extra tricks.

---

## Confusing Invitational and Game-Forcing Raises

Limit raises invite.

Jacoby 2NT forces.

---

## Forgetting Partnership Agreements

Modern treatments differ considerably.

---

## Underestimating Trump Quality

Excellent intermediates frequently justify upgrading.

---

# Expert Evaluation

Experts evaluate

- controls,
- trump quality,
- Losing Trick Count,
- side-suit texture,
- offensive potential,
- quick tricks,

rather than relying solely on HCP.

---

# Universal Principles

Responder should

- support partner whenever practical,
- search for the best trump fit,
- distinguish constructive, invitational and forcing hands,
- preserve bidding space,
- cooperate toward slam after game is established.

---

# Learning Progression

Study in the following order

1. Simple Raises
2. Limit Raises
3. Forcing 1NT
4. Two-over-One Game Force
5. Jacoby 2NT
6. Bergen Raises
7. Mixed Raises
8. Splinters
9. Help-Suit Game Tries
10. Slam Auctions

# Summary

Responding to a **major-suit opening** revolves around one central objective:
**identify and evaluate a major-suit fit**. Because major-suit games require
only ten tricks and allow ruffing, responder should normally support opener
before considering alternative strains.

Modern bidding methods—including **Forcing 1NT**, **Two-over-One Game Force**,
**Jacoby 2NT**, **Bergen Raises**, **Mixed Raises**, and **Splinters**—enable
partnerships to distinguish accurately between constructive, invitational,
game-forcing, and slam-oriented hands. Mastering responses to major openings is
one of the cornerstones of successful natural bidding.
