# Raising Partner's Minor

## Overview

Raising partner's minor suit is an important part of constructive bidding, but
its objectives differ significantly from raising a major.

Unlike major-suit auctions, where finding an eight-card fit is usually the
highest priority, minor-suit raises often remain exploratory because the
partnership may still discover

- a major-suit fit,
- a notrump contract,
- or a better minor contract.

Consequently, modern bidding systems treat minor raises differently from major
raises, frequently using specialized conventions to distinguish various
strength ranges.

---

# Objectives

A minor-suit raise should communicate

- trump support,
- strength,
- distribution,
- offensive potential,
- game interest,
- slam interest.

Unlike major raises, it often keeps alternative contracts available.

---

# Why Minor Raises Differ

Minor-suit games require

- **11 tricks** instead of 10.

Furthermore,

- 3NT usually scores better than 5♣ or 5♦,
- many balanced hands play better in notrump,
- the partnership continues searching for a major fit whenever possible.

Therefore, raising a minor is usually **less committal** than raising a major.

---

# Support Requirements

Typical agreements

### Clubs

Normally

- four-card support,
- sometimes five cards.

---

### Diamonds

Normally

- four-card support.

Modern partnerships often evaluate

- suit quality,
- controls,
- distribution,
- offensive strength,

rather than support length alone.

---

# Strength Categories

## Weak Raise

Approximately

6–9 HCP.

Constructive but non-invitational.

---

## Invitational Raise

Approximately

10–12 HCP.

Invites game.

---

## Game Forcing Raise

13+ HCP

or equivalent playing strength.

---

## Slam-Oriented Raise

Excellent controls,
distribution,
or exceptional fit.

---

# Simple Raise

Example

```
1♦
2♦
```

Shows

- support,
- constructive values,
- no invitation.

Usually

6–9 HCP.

---

# Limit Raise

Example

```
1♣
3♣
```

Traditional treatment

- invitational values,
- good support.

Many partnerships no longer use this treatment.

---

# Inverted Minor Raises

Modern partnerships frequently replace traditional raises with

**Inverted Minors**

Example

```
1♦
2♦
```

Shows

- 10+ HCP,
- four-card support,
- constructive or game-forcing values,
- denies a four-card major.

Meanwhile

```
1♦
3♦
```

becomes

- weak,
- preemptive,
- five-card support.

This treatment is now one of the most widely played methods.

---

# Jump Raise

Traditional

```
1♣
3♣
```

Shows

- invitational values.

Modern partnerships often redefine it as

- weak,
- preemptive,
- obstructive.

---

# Direct Game Raise

```
1♦
5♦
```

Normally shows

- sufficient values,
- no slam interest,
- confidence that a minor game is superior to 3NT.

Rare.

---

# Distributional Evaluation

Upgrade with

- singleton,
- void,
- fifth trump,
- sixth trump,
- side-suit source of tricks.

Downgrade with

- flat distribution,
- scattered queens,
- poor intermediates.

---

# Searching for Notrump

Even after supporting a minor,

the partnership frequently investigates

- 2NT,
- 3NT,
- stoppers,
- balanced hands.

Minor raises rarely eliminate notrump from consideration.

---

# Searching for a Major Fit

Responder should avoid raising a minor too early when

- a four-card major remains unshown,
- opener may also hold a major,
- a superior major-suit contract is possible.

---

# Opener's Evaluation

After partner raises the minor,

opener evaluates

- controls,
- trump quality,
- distribution,
- stoppers,
- suitability for notrump,
- slam potential.

---

# Competitive Auctions

Minor raises become more aggressive after interference.

Partnerships frequently employ

- preemptive raises,
- fit-showing jumps,
- cue bids,
- support doubles.

Evaluation depends upon

- vulnerability,
- fit length,
- offensive potential.

---

# Minor-Suit Games

Bid a minor game only when

- 3NT is unsuitable,
- no major fit exists,
- trump play offers additional tricks,
- distribution favors a suit contract.

---

# Slam Auctions

Excellent minor fits often produce outstanding slam contracts.

Important evaluation factors include

- controls,
- trump quality,
- shortness,
- side-suit winners,
- source of tricks.

---

# Inverted Minor Continuations

After an inverted raise,

opener usually

- describes minimum or extras,
- shows stoppers,
- bids notrump,
- cue bids,
- investigates slam.

These auctions are highly constructive.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partnerships should define

- traditional raises,
- Inverted Minors,
- support length,
- weak jump raises,
- forcing raises,
- fit jumps,
- cue bids,
- notrump investigations,
- slam methods.

---

# System Differences

## Standard American

Traditional raises

- simple,
- limit,
- game.

---

## SAYC

Often identical to Standard American.

Some partnerships adopt Inverted Minors.

---

## 2/1 Game Force

Inverted Minors are extremely common.

Natural constructive continuations.

---

## Acol

Natural raises.

Occasionally wider ranges.

---

## Precision

Artificial club opening changes many club auctions.

Natural diamond raises remain similar.

---

## Polish Club

Artificial club opening.

Natural diamond raises.

Constructive continuations resemble Standard American.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Ignoring Notrump

Many minor games score worse than 3NT.

---

## Missing a Major Fit

Always search for an undiscovered major fit before committing to a minor.

---

## Underestimating Distribution

Distribution frequently justifies aggressive bidding.

---

## Overvaluing Flat Hands

Balanced hands often belong in notrump.

---

## Confusing Traditional and Inverted Raises

These systems assign very different meanings to identical bids.

---

## Ignoring Partnership Agreements

Modern minor raises vary considerably.

Partnership understanding is essential.

---

# Expert Evaluation

Experts emphasize

- controls,
- trump texture,
- source of tricks,
- stoppers,
- Losing Trick Count,
- offensive potential,

rather than relying solely on HCP.

---

# Universal Principles

When raising a minor

- support accurately,
- continue searching for major fits,
- consider notrump first,
- distinguish constructive from preemptive raises,
- preserve bidding space whenever possible.

---

# Summary

Raising partner's minor differs fundamentally from raising a major because the
partnership must continue evaluating whether a **major-suit contract** or
**notrump** offers a better outcome.

Modern methods, particularly **Inverted Minor Raises**, have transformed
minor-suit auctions into highly descriptive and efficient bidding sequences.
Successful partnerships evaluate not only high-card strength but also
distribution, controls, trump quality, and the likelihood that notrump or a
major suit will produce a superior result before committing to a minor-suit
contract.
