# Raising Partner's Minor

## Bridge Insight

**Supporting a minor suit requires more judgment than supporting a major. A minor fit is valuable, but the partnership must still decide whether Notrump or a major-suit contract offers a better chance of success.**

---

# Overview

Raising partner's minor suit confirms a fit but does not automatically determine the final contract. Unlike major-suit fits, which often become the preferred trump suit, minor-suit fits compete with Notrump contracts and occasionally with delayed major-suit discoveries.

Responder must therefore evaluate not only support, but also whether the partnership should ultimately play in clubs, diamonds, or Notrump.

---

# Objectives

A minor raise should:

- Confirm support.
- Describe strength.
- Help partner evaluate game.
- Assist slam exploration.
- Distinguish weak, invitational, and forcing hands.

---

# Why Minor Raises Are Different

Major-suit games require:

```
10 tricks
```

Minor-suit games require:

```
11 tricks
```

Notrump requires only:

```
9 tricks
```

Therefore many hands with a minor fit still belong in:

```
3NT
```

---

# Showing Support

Responder usually raises with:

- Three-card support.
- Four-card support.
- Stronger support when distribution justifies.

The partnership agreement determines exact requirements.

---

# Re-evaluation

After a minor fit:

Increase the value of:

- Short suits.
- Controls.
- Long trumps.
- Good intermediates.

However, continue asking whether Notrump remains superior.

---

# Types of Raises

### Simple Raise

Shows:

- Support.
- Limited values.

---

### Invitational Raise

Shows:

- Good support.
- Interest in game.
- Uncertain game prospects.

---

### Game Raise

Shows:

- Confidence that game is correct.

Usually:

```
5♣

or

5♦
```

although many partnerships prefer 3NT whenever practical.

---

### Slam Interest

Excellent minor fits frequently produce slam opportunities because of strong controls and ruffing values.

---

# Minor Suit versus Notrump

Questions responder should ask:

- Are all suits stopped?
- Is the minor long enough to establish tricks?
- Is 3NT likely to produce more tricks?
- Will trump control be valuable?

The answer determines the preferred contract.

---

# Distribution

Minor raises become stronger with:

- Singleton.
- Void.
- Extra trumps.
- Side controls.

Flat hands frequently prefer Notrump.

---

# Competitive Auctions

Minor raises may also serve to:

- Compete.
- Obstruct the opponents.
- Show fit.

Judgment depends on vulnerability and scoring.

---

# System Variations

Treatments vary among:

- Standard American
- SAYC
- 2/1
- Precision
- Polish Club
- Acol

Conventions include:

- Inverted Minors
- Jump Raises
- Weak Raises

Each is covered separately.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Assuming every minor fit belongs in five of the minor.
- Ignoring Notrump.
- Underestimating controls.
- Overvaluing flat hands.
- Forgetting to re-evaluate after support.

---

# Best Practices

Experienced players:

- Compare the minor fit with possible Notrump contracts.
- Re-evaluate distribution.
- Think about game before raising.
- Consider slam only after confirming controls.

# Player's Checklist

Before raising a minor:

- Do we have a fit?
- Is Notrump a better contract?
- Have I re-evaluated my hand?
- Am I showing weakness, invitation, or game values?
- Does the partnership play Inverted Minors?

---

# Memory Guide

## Five Questions

1. Do we have a fit?
2. Is Notrump better?
3. How strong am I?
4. Is game realistic?
5. Could this become a slam?

---

# Expert Tip

**Never assume that finding a minor fit ends the auction.**

Expert players constantly compare the expected trick-taking potential of a minor-suit contract with the possibility of playing in Notrump.

---

# Memory Aid

**Minor Fit = Evaluate Again**

Remember:

- A minor fit is valuable but does not automatically determine the final contract.
- Always compare the minor suit with Notrump.
- Distribution becomes more important after support is found.
- Inverted Minor Raises are a partnership agreement, not a universal treatment.
- The best contract is the one that maximizes tricks, not necessarily the one that first discovers a fit.
