# Limit Raise

## Overview

A **Limit Raise** is an invitational raise of partner's suit that shows

- support,
- constructive values,
- interest in game,
- but insufficient strength to force to game.

It occupies the middle ground between

- a simple raise, and
- a game-forcing raise.

The primary objective is to allow opener to judge whether the partnership has
sufficient combined strength for game.

---

# Objectives

A Limit Raise communicates

- good support,
- invitational values,
- no forcing commitment,
- willingness to play game opposite maximum values.

---

# Typical Strength

Most natural systems use

**10–12 HCP**

or

**10–11 HCP with good support**

depending upon partnership style.

Some partnerships evaluate

- Losing Trick Count,
- distribution,
- controls,

instead of relying solely on HCP.

---

# Trump Support

Limit raises normally promise

### Major Suit

Four-card support.

Some partnerships allow excellent three-card support.

---

### Minor Suit

Usually five-card support,
although partnership agreements vary.

---

# Typical Auction

```
1♠
3♠
```

Responder shows

- invitational values,
- four-card support,
- no singleton or void,
- no game force.

---

Another example

```
1♥
3♥
```

---

# Hand Requirements

Typical hand

- 10–12 HCP
- four-card support
- balanced or semi-balanced
- no singleton suitable for a splinter
- no game-forcing strength

Example

```
♠ Q842
♥ K73
♦ AJ5
♣ QJ84
```

Auction

```
1♠
3♠
```

---

# Opener's Evaluation

After a Limit Raise opener evaluates

- HCP,
- controls,
- trump quality,
- distribution,
- side-suit texture,
- Losing Trick Count.

Then chooses

- pass,
- game,
- occasionally slam investigation.

---

# Passing

Pass with

- minimum opening,
- poor controls,
- flat distribution,
- weak trump suit.

Example

```
♠ AK964
♥ J73
♦ K82
♣ 954
```

---

# Accepting the Invitation

Bid game with

- maximum opening,
- useful controls,
- excellent trump suit,
- distributional extras.

Example

```
♠ AKQ76
♥ AQ4
♦ J7
♣ K85
```

---

# Borderline Hands

Modern experts evaluate

- controls,
- shortness,
- trump intermediates,
- playing tricks,

rather than HCP alone.

Many 13-HCP hands are worth game.

Some 15-HCP hands are not.

---

# Distributional Upgrades

Upgrade with

- singleton,
- void,
- fifth trump,
- sixth trump,
- side-suit source of tricks.

Example

```
♠ AKQ86
♥ K2
♦ A97
♣ J952
```

Worth accepting opposite many limit raises.

---

# Downgrades

Downgrade with

- flat 4-3-3-3,
- unsupported honors,
- wasted values,
- poor trumps.

---

# Limit Raise Versus Simple Raise

### Simple Raise

Shows approximately

6–9 HCP.

No invitation.

---

### Limit Raise

Shows

10–12 HCP.

Invites game.

---

### Direct Game Raise

Shows

13+ HCP

or equivalent playing strength.

No invitation.

---

# Major-Suit Limit Raise

Most common treatment.

Auction

```
1♥
3♥
```

or

```
1♠
3♠
```

Shows

- four-card support,
- invitational values.

---

# Minor-Suit Limit Raise

Example

```
1♦
3♦
```

Shows

- invitational values,
- strong diamond support.

Many partnerships instead prefer

- 2NT,
- Inverted Minors,
- or other constructive raises.

---

# Modern Treatments

Many partnerships replace the traditional limit raise with

- Jacoby 2NT,
- Bergen Raises,
- Mixed Raise,
- Jordan 2NT,
- Fit Jumps.

Traditional limit raises remain widely played.

---

# Bergen Raises

In Bergen Raises

the direct jump raise no longer promises a traditional limit raise.

Instead,

specialized artificial bids distinguish

- weak raises,
- constructive raises,
- limit raises.

---

# Two-over-One Systems

Most 2/1 partnerships retain

```
1♥
3♥
```

and

```
1♠
3♠
```

as natural limit raises.

Jacoby 2NT becomes the primary game-forcing raise.

---

# Splinter Versus Limit Raise

Splinter

- singleton or void,
- game forcing,
- slam interest.

Limit Raise

- balanced,
- invitational,
- no shortage shown.

---

# Losing Trick Count

Many partnerships evaluate

- LTC,
- controls,
- trump length,

rather than HCP.

Example

```
♠ KQJ7
♥ A85
♦ J74
♣ Q982
```

Often a textbook limit raise.

---

# Opener's Continuations

After a limit raise opener may

## Pass

Minimum.

---

## Bid Game

Maximum.

---

## Make a Game Try

Some partnerships permit further investigation.

Examples include

- cue bids,
- 3NT,
- control bids,
- help-suit game tries.

System dependent.

---

# Slam Interest

Occasionally opener continues toward slam with

- excellent controls,
- exceptional distribution,
- strong trumps.

Responder cooperates naturally.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should define

- HCP range,
- three-card support?,
- four-card support?,
- Bergen Raises,
- Jacoby 2NT interaction,
- mixed raises,
- game-try structure,
- evaluation method,
- LTC usage.

# Common Mistakes

## Raising Too Light

Weak raises belong at the two level.

---

## Raising Too Strong

Game-forcing hands should not use a limit raise.

---

## Ignoring Distribution

Playing strength often matters more than HCP.

---

## Accepting Automatically

Maximum HCP alone does not guarantee game.

---

## Passing Automatically

Excellent shape frequently upgrades a minimum opening.

---

# Expert Evaluation

Experts consider

- controls,
- trump texture,
- Losing Trick Count,
- quick tricks,
- side-suit source of tricks,
- fit quality,

before deciding whether to accept.

---

# Universal Principles

A Limit Raise should

- show genuine invitational strength,
- promise useful trump support,
- allow opener to judge game,
- leave captaincy with opener.

# Summary

The **Limit Raise** is one of the most important constructive tools in natural
bidding. It accurately describes a hand with

- invitational strength,
- solid trump support,
- interest in game,
- but insufficient values to force.

By clearly separating invitational hands from weak raises and game-forcing
raises, the Limit Raise enables opener to evaluate the combined assets and
choose confidently between partscore and game. It remains a cornerstone of
modern constructive bidding across virtually every natural bidding system.
