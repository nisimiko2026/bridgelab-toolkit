# Responding with Balanced Hands

## Overview

Balanced hands are among the easiest hands to describe accurately in natural
bidding.

Without long suits or significant distributional features, responder's primary
responsibility is to communicate

- strength,
- stoppers,
- support,
- notrump suitability.

Because balanced hands rarely produce additional playing strength through
ruffing, accurate point-count evaluation becomes especially important.

---

# Objectives

When holding a balanced hand, responder should

- accurately describe strength,
- support partner whenever appropriate,
- search for a major-suit fit,
- choose notrump when suitable,
- preserve bidding space,
- distinguish invitational hands from game-forcing hands.

---

# What Is a Balanced Hand?

A balanced hand contains

- no void,
- no singleton,
- at most one doubleton.

Typical patterns include

- 4-3-3-3
- 4-4-3-2
- 5-3-3-2

Semi-balanced hands such as

- 5-4-2-2
- 6-3-2-2

are treated similarly in many auctions.

---

# Evaluating Balanced Hands

Primary factors include

- High Card Points,
- honor location,
- suit texture,
- stoppers,
- fit with partner,
- controls.

Distribution contributes less than with unbalanced hands.

---

# General Priorities

Responder normally follows this order

1. Raise partner's major.
2. Show a four-card major.
3. Bid notrump.
4. Support a minor.
5. Introduce a new suit only when appropriate.

---

# After a Major Opening

Example

```
1♠
?
```

Responder first asks

- Do I have support?
- Do I have another four-card major?
- Is notrump appropriate?

---

## With Support

Raise according to strength.

| Strength | Typical Action |
|----------|----------------|
| 6–9 HCP | Simple Raise |
| 10–12 HCP | Limit Raise |
| 13+ HCP | Game Force |
| Slam Values | Jacoby 2NT / Splinter / Control Bid |

---

## Without Support

Balanced hands usually bid

```
1NT
```

or

```
2NT
```

depending on strength.

---

# After a Minor Opening

Example

```
1♦
?
```

Priority becomes

1. Show a four-card major.
2. Bid notrump.
3. Support the minor.

Finding a major fit remains more important than immediately bidding notrump.

---

# One Notrump Response

Typical meaning

- 6–9 HCP
- balanced
- no suitable support
- no higher-priority natural response

Example

```
1♠
1NT
```

Exact range varies by system.

---

# Two Notrump Response

Usually

- 10–12 HCP
- balanced
- invitational

Example

```
1♦
2NT
```

Invites opener to bid game with a maximum.

---

# Three Notrump Response

Shows

- balanced game values,
- approximately 13–15 HCP,
- confidence in notrump,
- no major fit.

Example

```
1♣
3NT
```

---

# Showing a Four-Card Major

Balanced hands should normally show a four-card major after a minor opening.

Example

```
1♣
1♥
```

even with a balanced hand.

Locating an eight-card major fit usually outweighs immediate notrump bidding.

---

# Balanced Hands with Support

Support generally takes priority over notrump.

Example

```
♠ K842
♥ Q84
♦ AJ7
♣ KJ53
```

Auction

```
1♠
2♠
```

rather than

```
1NT
```

---

# Invitational Balanced Hands

Typical methods

- 2NT
- Limit Raise
- New Minor Forcing
- Checkback Stayman

depending on the previous auction.

---

# Game-Forcing Balanced Hands

Responder should

- establish game force,
- preserve bidding space,
- investigate the best strain,
- consider slam.

Common methods include

- Jacoby 2NT,
- Two-over-One,
- Fourth Suit Forcing,
- Checkback,
- Cue Bidding.

---

# Stoppers

Balanced hands often seek notrump.

Responder evaluates

- stopper quality,
- partially stopped suits,
- double stoppers,
- communication.

Missing stoppers may favor a suit contract.

---

# Upgrading Balanced Hands

Upgrade with

- excellent controls,
- working honors,
- strong intermediates,
- excellent suit texture,
- fitting honors.

---

# Downgrading Balanced Hands

Downgrade with

- unsupported queens,
- scattered honors,
- poor intermediates,
- duplicated values,
- bad controls.

---

# Captaincy

Balanced responders often become captain once opener limits the hand.

Captain chooses

- strain,
- level,
- game,
- slam investigation.

# Summary

Balanced hands emphasize **accurate strength evaluation** rather than
distribution. Responder's primary goals are to

- locate a major-suit fit,
- determine whether notrump is appropriate,
- communicate the correct strength range,
- distinguish invitational from game-forcing values.

Successful partnerships combine disciplined point-count evaluation with careful
assessment of stoppers, controls, and fit quality, allowing them to reach the
best partscore, game, or slam while preserving maximum bidding efficiency.
