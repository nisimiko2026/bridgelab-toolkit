# Rebids After Preempts

## Overview

Unlike constructive one-level openings, **preemptive openings** are designed to
describe the hand almost completely with the opening bid itself.

After opening

- a Weak Two,
- a Three-Level Preempt,
- or a Four-Level Preempt,

opener has already shown

- limited strength,
- a long suit,
- offensive distribution,
- preemptive intent.

Consequently, opener's rebids are generally **descriptive rather than
exploratory**, and occur only when responder invites further bidding.

---

# Objectives

After a preemptive opening, opener should

- remain within the limited range already shown,
- accurately answer responder's inquiries,
- cooperate when game is possible,
- avoid introducing unnecessary information,
- preserve the partnership's understanding.

---

# General Philosophy

The opening bid has already communicated

- hand strength,
- suit length,
- offensive nature.

Therefore,

opener should **not** attempt to describe every feature of the hand.

Responder normally becomes the **captain** of the auction.

---

# Rebids After a Weak Two

Weak Two openings leave sufficient bidding space for constructive continuations.

Typical rebids include

- Feature responses,
- Ogust responses,
- accepting game invitations,
- signing off.

---

# After a Raise

Example

```
2♥
3♥
Pass
```

Responder has made a constructive raise.

Unless opener has unexpected extra playing strength,

opener normally passes.

---

# After a Game Raise

Example

```
2♠
4♠
```

Responder has placed the contract.

Opener should pass.

---

# After a New Suit

Example

```
2♥
3♣
```

A new suit is generally forcing.

Opener normally

- supports responder when appropriate,
- rebids the original suit,
- shows useful features if requested.

Natural bidding is preferred.

---

# After a 2NT Feature Ask

One of the most common rebids.

Example

```
2♠
2NT
```

Responder asks for additional information.

Typical rebids show

- outside ace,
- outside king,
- useful feature,
- minimum,
- maximum.

The exact structure depends upon partnership agreement.

---

# Ogust Responses

Many partnerships employ **Ogust**.

Example

```
2♥
2NT
```

Typical responses

| Rebid | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| 3♣ | Minimum, poor suit |
| 3♦ | Minimum, good suit |
| 3♥ | Maximum, poor suit |
| 3♠ | Maximum, good suit |

Other response structures are common.

---

# Rebids After Three-Level Preempts

Three-level preempts consume much more bidding space.

Accordingly,

opener rarely rebids.

Typical situations include

- responder raises,
- responder bids game,
- responder introduces a forcing new suit.

---

# After a Raise

Example

```
3♦
4♦
```

Responder is competing or inviting game.

Opener normally passes.

---

# After a New Suit

Example

```
3♠
4♣
```

Responder shows significant strength.

Opener should

- cooperate naturally,
- support responder when appropriate,
- avoid over-describing the hand.

---

# After 3NT

Example

```
3♣
3NT
```

Responder believes notrump is superior.

Opener normally passes.

---

# Rebids After Four-Level Preempts

Four-level openings leave almost no bidding space.

Consequently,

opener almost never makes a descriptive rebid.

Most auctions end immediately.

---

# After a Minor Raise

Example

```
4♦
5♦
```

Opener passes.

---

# After a Competitive Auction

Example

```
4♠
Double
Pass
```

or

```
4♠
5♥
```

Opener should evaluate

- offensive potential,
- expected tricks,
- vulnerability,
- sacrifice possibilities.

Judgment is more important than point count.

---

# Opener's Priorities

After any preempt,

opener should

1. Remember the hand is already limited.
2. Avoid speculative bidding.
3. Answer artificial inquiries accurately.
4. Cooperate only when responder shows strength.
5. Respect partnership agreements.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should define

- Feature Ask responses,
- Ogust,
- forcing new suits,
- competitive methods,
- doubles,
- cue bids,
- sacrifice philosophy.

---

# Competitive Auctions

When opponents intervene,

opener evaluates

- fit,
- distribution,
- vulnerability,
- expected tricks.

Common actions include

- Pass,
- Raise,
- Double,
- Cue Bid (agreement).

Because opener has already described the hand, unnecessary competition should be
avoided.

---

# Hand Evaluation

Rebids after preempts depend primarily on

- playing tricks,
- controls,
- offensive potential,
- suit quality,
- vulnerability.

High Card Points are of secondary importance.

---

# Modern Expert Style

Experts emphasize

- disciplined rebids,
- practical judgment,
- efficient communication,
- preserving the limited nature of the opening.

Responder directs the auction.

Natural continuations.

## SAYC

Very similar.

## Two-over-One Game Force

Little difference.

---

## Acol

Natural methods.

Minor partnership variations.

---

## Precision

Natural continuations after natural preempts.

---

## Polish Club

Similar philosophy.

Specific agreements vary.

# Common Mistakes

## Rebidding Without Extra Information

The opening bid has already described the hand.

---

## Forgetting the Limited Nature of the Opening

Do not introduce extra strength that was never promised.

---

## Ignoring Partnership Agreements

Feature Ask and Ogust require precise agreements.

---

## Competing Too Aggressively

Limited hands should avoid speculative bidding.

---

## Overvaluing High Card Points

Playing tricks and suit quality remain more important.

---

# Expert Evaluation

Experts emphasize

- accurate responses,
- practical judgment,
- offensive potential,
- suit quality,
- partnership discipline,

rather than detailed hand description.

---

# Universal Principles

After any preemptive opening,

opener should

- remain within the limited range,
- answer responder accurately,
- cooperate when appropriate,
- avoid unnecessary bidding,
- preserve the partnership's understanding.

# Summary

Rebids after **preemptive openings** differ fundamentally from rebids after
constructive openings. Because the opening bid has already shown a **limited
hand with a long suit**, opener rarely needs to describe the hand further.

After a **Weak Two**, opener may answer **Feature Ask** or **Ogust** inquiries.
After **Three-Level** and **Four-Level Preempts**, opener usually makes only
practical, natural rebids in response to responder's strength. Successful
preemptive continuations rely on **discipline**, **partnership agreements**,
and the understanding that responder—not opener—normally directs the remainder
of the auction.
