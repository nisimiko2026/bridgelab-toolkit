# Opening Rebids

## Overview

The opener's rebid is the most descriptive bid in the auction.

After partner responds, opener has significantly more information than at the
time of the opening bid. The rebid should accurately describe the hand's

- strength,
- distribution,
- suit quality,
- support,
- game interest.

Every rebid should narrow the possible hand patterns and guide the partnership
toward the correct contract.

Although individual bidding systems differ, the underlying principles of
opening rebids are remarkably similar.

---

# Objectives

An opener's rebid attempts to answer six fundamental questions.

- How strong is opener?
- What is opener's exact distribution?
- Is there a trump fit?
- Is notrump appropriate?
- Is game likely?
- Should slam be investigated?

---

# Information Available

Before rebidding, opener knows

- responder's first bid,
- responder's approximate strength,
- responder's likely distribution,
- whether the auction is forcing,
- whether a fit exists.

This additional information allows opener to define the hand much more
precisely than the opening bid.

---

# General Priorities

The normal priority of rebids is

1. Support partner.
2. Show a second suit.
3. Rebid the original suit.
4. Bid notrump.
5. Make a jump rebid.
6. Begin slam investigation if appropriate.

---

# Strength Categories

## Minimum

12–14 HCP

Little or no extra playing strength.

---

## Medium

15–17 HCP

Useful extras but not forcing.

---

## Strong

18–19 HCP

Usually game invitational or stronger.

---

## Very Strong

20+ HCP

Normally forces game.

---

# Describing Shape

The rebid should improve partner's picture of opener's distribution.

Typical descriptions include

- balanced,
- semi-balanced,
- unbalanced,
- two-suited,
- single-suited,
- shortness.

Shape is often more important than a small difference in high-card points.

---

# Supporting Partner

Finding a fit has the highest priority.

Whenever opener holds suitable support, raising partner generally takes
precedence over introducing another suit.

Support may show

- minimum,
- invitational,
- game-going,
- slam interest,

depending on the level chosen.

---

# Showing a Second Suit

Without support, opener usually introduces another suit.

Requirements vary by partnership but typically include

- at least four cards,
- reasonable suit quality,
- natural meaning.

Showing shape early improves subsequent bidding decisions.

---

# Rebidding the Opening Suit

Rebidding the original suit generally shows

- additional length,
- lack of a better description,
- limited values unless jumping.

The exact implications depend on the auction.

---

# Reverse Bids

A reverse is one of the strongest natural rebids.

Characteristics

- extra strength,
- unbalanced hand,
- forcing for one round,
- significant distribution.

Partnership agreement determines the exact strength requirement.

---

# Notrump Rebids

Balanced hands frequently rebid notrump.

Typical meanings

| Rebid | Typical Strength |
|-------|------------------:|
| 1NT | 12–14 |
| 2NT | 18–19 |
| 3NT | Balanced game values |

Requirements normally include

- balanced shape,
- stopper(s),
- no better descriptive rebid,
- no suitable fit.

---

# Jump Rebids

Jump rebids show additional playing strength.

Common jump rebids include

- jump raise,
- jump rebid of the opening suit,
- jump into a new suit,
- jump to notrump.

Exact meanings vary by system.

---

# Rebids After Different Responses

## After a New Suit

Priority

1. Raise responder.
2. Show second suit.
3. Rebid original suit.
4. Notrump.

---

## After 1NT

Clarify

- strength,
- shape,
- suit length,
- game interest.

---

## After Raise

Evaluate

- combined strength,
- distribution,
- controls,
- trump quality.

Choose among

- pass,
- invitation,
- game,
- slam try.

---

## After Game Force

Once game is forced

priorities become

- choosing strain,
- describing controls,
- cue bidding,
- key-card investigation,
- grand slam exploration.

---

# Evaluating Distribution

Distribution frequently changes hand value.

Important features include

- six-card suit,
- second suit,
- singleton,
- void,
- excellent intermediates.

Playing strength often exceeds high-card points.

---

# Limiting the Hand

One important objective of many rebids is to limit opener's strength.

Accurate limitation allows responder to become captain of the auction.

---

# Describing Extras

Hands with

- excellent suit quality,
- controls,
- distribution,
- superior playing strength,

should make descriptive rebids that distinguish them from minimum openings.

---

# Major-Suit Auctions

Typical priorities

- support responder,
- show second suit,
- rebid major,
- notrump.

Major fits frequently become the final contract.

---

# Minor-Suit Auctions

Typical priorities

- locate major fit,
- show second suit,
- notrump,
- support responder,
- rebid minor.

Notrump often remains preferable to minor games.

---

# Game-Forcing Auctions

When game is certain

the objective changes from

"Should we bid game?"

to

"What is the best game or slam?"

Space should be preserved whenever possible.

---

# Slam-Oriented Rebids

With sufficient strength opener begins describing

- controls,
- shortages,
- trump quality,
- source of tricks.

Subsequent bidding may include

- cue bids,
- Roman Key Card Blackwood,
- control bidding,
- grand slam investigation.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Every partnership should define

- reverse strength,
- jump shifts,
- jump rebids,
- support style,
- fourth-suit forcing,
- notrump rebid ranges,
- game-force structure,
- splinter rebids,
- cue-bidding methods.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Overbidding

Do not manufacture extra values.

Describe the hand accurately.

---

## Underbidding Distribution

Distribution often outweighs one or two high-card points.

---

## Missing Fits

Support partner before introducing unnecessary descriptions.

---

## Hiding Shape

Shape is one of the most valuable pieces of information.

Describe it whenever practical.

---

## Ignoring Auction Context

The same rebid may have different meanings depending on

- vulnerability,
- forcing status,
- responder's bid,
- partnership agreements.

---

# System Differences

## Standard American

- Natural rebids
- Five-card majors
- Strong reverses

---

## SAYC

- Similar to Standard American
- Strong jump shifts
- Standard notrump structure

---

## 2/1 Game Force

- More game-forcing auctions
- Slow arrival
- Better hand definition

---

## Acol

- Four-card majors possible
- Different reverse requirements
- More frequent balanced rebids

---

## Precision

- Artificial club opening
- Similar rebid philosophy after natural openings

---

## Polish Club

- Artificial club system
- Natural rebids after natural openings
- Similar descriptive principles

---

# Universal Rebidding Principles

Always

- support before introducing another suit,
- describe shape naturally,
- distinguish minimum from extras,
- preserve bidding space,
- limit the hand whenever possible,
- cooperate after game is forced.

---

# Learning Progression

Study opener rebids in the following order

1. After Major Openings
2. After Minor Openings
3. Reverse Bids
4. Notrump Rebids
5. Jump Rebids
6. Support Rebids
7. Fourth Suit Forcing
8. Game-Forcing Auctions
9. Slam Auctions

# Summary

The opener's rebid is the partnership's primary opportunity to define the
opening hand.

A successful rebid accurately communicates

- strength,
- shape,
- support,
- suit quality,
- game interest,

while preserving sufficient bidding space for partner to judge the correct
contract.

Expert partnerships succeed because opener's rebids are disciplined,
consistent, descriptive, and fully aligned with the partnership's bidding
agreements.
