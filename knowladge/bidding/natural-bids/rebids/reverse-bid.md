# Reverse Bids

## Overview

A **reverse** is one of the most descriptive and informative bids in natural
bidding.

Unlike ordinary rebids, a reverse communicates both

- extra strength, and
- an unbalanced distribution.

Because responder is forced to bid at a higher level to return to opener's
first suit, a reverse consumes bidding space and therefore promises additional
values beyond a minimum opening.

A correctly used reverse immediately narrows opener's possible hand and often
places the partnership on the path toward game.

---

# Definition

A reverse occurs when opener

- bids a lower-ranking suit,
- responder bids,
- opener introduces a higher-ranking suit,
- responder would have to bid at the three level to return to opener's first
  suit.

Example

```
1♣   Pass   1♠   Pass
2♥
```

Responder cannot return to clubs without bidding **3♣**.

Therefore **2♥** is a reverse.

---

# Why It Is Called a Reverse

Normally suits are introduced upward.

A reverse "reverses" that order by introducing a higher-ranking suit after
opening a lower-ranking one.

Because bidding space is consumed, the bid carries extra meaning.

---

# Basic Requirements

Most partnerships require

- 16+ HCP (approximately),
- extra playing strength,
- unbalanced hand,
- at least 5 cards in the first suit,
- at least 4 cards in the second suit.

Many expert partnerships evaluate

- controls,
- suit quality,
- losing trick count,
- playing strength,

rather than relying solely on high-card points.

# Why Extra Strength Is Needed

Responder may have only

6 HCP

and now cannot stop safely below the three level.

Without extra values opener could force the partnership too high.

# Reverse After Major Opening

Major openings produce fewer reverses because

- majors rank above minors,
- bidding space differs.

Example

```
1♥
1♠
2NT
```

is **not** a reverse.

---

Possible reverse

```
1♥
2♣
2♠
```

depending on partnership agreements.

# Responder's Options

After a reverse responder may

- prefer opener's first suit,
- support the second suit,
- rebid responder's own suit,
- bid notrump,
- force to game.

Choice depends upon

- strength,
- fit,
- distribution.

# Reverse Into a Minor

Example

```
1♣
1♥
2♦
```

This is **not** a reverse because responder may return to clubs at the two
level.

---

# False Reverse

Many beginners incorrectly reverse with

- minimum values,
- balanced hands,
- weak 5-4 distribution.

These hands should usually make a simpler rebid.

---

# Reverse by Responder

Responder may also reverse.

Example

```
1♣
1♥
1♠
2♦
```

Responder's reverse generally promises

- extra values,
- forcing strength,
- additional distribution.

Partnership agreements differ more widely than opener reverses.

---

# Jump Reverse

Example

```
1♣
1♠
3♥
```

Usually shows

- very strong hand,
- excellent distribution,
- game forcing,
- possible slam interest.

---

# Reverse and Game Force

Some partnerships play

- reverse = one-round force

Others

- reverse = game force

System agreement is essential.

# Losing Trick Count

Distributional hands often justify a reverse despite moderate HCP.

Example

- two long suits,
- seven losers,

may reverse comfortably.

# Summary

A reverse is one of the strongest descriptive tools available in natural
bidding.

By combining

- extra strength,
- unbalanced distribution,
- constructive intent,

it allows opener (or occasionally responder) to describe powerful two-suited
hands while placing the partnership on the correct path toward game or slam.

Accurate reverse bidding is an essential skill for advanced partnerships and
forms a cornerstone of modern natural bidding systems.
