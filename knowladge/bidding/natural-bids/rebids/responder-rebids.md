# Responder Rebids

## Overview

Responder's rebid is one of the most important decisions in constructive
bidding.

By the time responder rebids, both partners have exchanged significant
information regarding

- strength,
- distribution,
- suit length,
- possible fits,
- game potential.

Responder now becomes responsible for determining whether the partnership
should

- stop in partscore,
- invite game,
- bid game,
- investigate slam.

Unlike responder's first bid, the rebid frequently establishes the final
direction of the auction.

---

# Objectives

Responder's rebid attempts to answer several questions.

- Is game likely?
- Has a trump fit been found?
- Is notrump the best contract?
- Does responder have additional distribution to show?
- Should the partnership investigate slam?
- Who should become captain?

---

# Information Available

Before rebidding, responder knows

- opener's opening bid,
- opener's rebid,
- opener's approximate strength,
- opener's distribution,
- whether opener has limited the hand,
- whether a fit exists.

This information allows responder to make much more accurate decisions.

---

# General Priorities

Responder normally follows this order.

1. Support opener.
2. Clarify strength.
3. Describe additional distribution.
4. Choose the final strain.
5. Invite or force game.
6. Begin slam investigation.

---

# Strength Categories

## Weak

0–5 HCP

Normally passes unless forced.

---

## Minimum

6–9 HCP

Competes for partscore.

---

## Invitational

10–12 HCP

Invites game.

---

## Game Forcing

13+ HCP

Game is virtually certain.

---

## Slam Interest

Excellent controls,
distribution,
or approximately 15+ HCP opposite an opening hand.

---

# Supporting Opener

When opener rebids a suit that responder can support,
support usually has priority.

---

## Simple Raise

Shows

- support,
- minimum values,
- preference for that strain.

Example

```
1♦
1♠
2♠
3♠
```

Usually invitational.

---

## Jump Raise

Shows

- excellent support,
- game-going values,
- sometimes slam interest.

Partnership agreements vary.

---

## Game Raise

Shows

- enough combined values for game,
- no further investigation needed.

---

# Rebidding Responder's Suit

Responder may repeat the original suit to show

- additional length,
- suit quality,
- preference for that denomination.

Example

```
1♣
1♠
2♦
2♠
```

Usually shows

- six-card suit,
- limited values.

---

## Jump Rebid

Shows

- strong suit,
- extra values,
- invitational or stronger.

---

# Introducing a New Suit

Responder occasionally introduces a second suit.

Usually shows

- additional distribution,
- sufficient strength,
- natural meaning.

Example

```
1♦
1♠
2♣
2♥
```

Partnership agreements determine whether this is forcing.

---

# Preference Bids

A preference bid returns to opener's first suit without necessarily promising
support.

Example

```
1♣
1♥
1♠
2♣
```

Usually indicates

- minimum hand,
- no better descriptive action.

---

# Notrump Rebids

Responder may rebid notrump with

- balanced shape,
- stoppers,
- no major fit,
- suitable strength.

---

## 1NT

Usually limited.

---

## 2NT

Typically invitational.

---

## 3NT

Shows

- game values,
- balanced hand,
- confidence in notrump.

---

# Invitational Rebids

Responder invites game by

- raising,
- bidding notrump,
- rebidding a suit,
- making a help-suit game try,
- using partnership-specific invitations.

---

# Game-Forcing Rebids

Once responder establishes a game force,

the partnership should concentrate on

- choosing the best strain,
- describing controls,
- investigating slam.

---

# Rebids After Opener Supports

Once opener raises responder's suit,

responder evaluates

- trump quality,
- controls,
- shortness,
- distribution,
- losing trick count.

Responder then chooses

- pass,
- invitation,
- game,
- slam investigation.

---

# Rebids After Opener Rebids Notrump

Responder evaluates

- fit,
- stoppers,
- strength,
- distribution.

Typical actions

- pass,
- invite,
- bid game,
- introduce another suit,
- use conventions such as New Minor Forcing or Checkback.

---

# Rebids After Reverse

When opener reverses,

responder usually requires

- additional strength,
- good distribution,
- clear direction.

Weak hands frequently prefer.

Strong hands continue naturally.

---

# Rebids After Jump Rebids

A jump rebid from opener usually promises extra values.

Responder should

- reassess combined strength,
- determine whether game is forced,
- begin slam investigation when appropriate.

---

# Minor-Suit Auctions

Responder continues searching for

- major-suit fit,
- notrump,
- best minor contract.

Minor games are often inferior to notrump games.

---

# Major-Suit Auctions

Once a major fit is found,

responder evaluates

- trump quality,
- controls,
- shortness,
- game prospects,
- slam potential.

---

# Competitive Auctions

Competition may require responder to

- compete,
- double,
- cue bid,
- invite,
- sacrifice.

Normal rebid principles remain valid but become more judgment-oriented.

---

# Distributional Hands

Extra distribution often upgrades responder's hand.

Examples include

- six-card suit,
- second suit,
- singleton,
- void,
- excellent intermediates.

Distribution frequently outweighs HCP.

---

# Captaincy

Responder often becomes captain because opener has already limited the hand.

Captain determines

- final strain,
- final level,
- whether to investigate slam.

---

# Slam-Oriented Rebids

Responder begins slam exploration through

- cue bids,
- control bids,
- splinters,
- Roman Key Card Blackwood,
- exclusion,
- grand slam tries.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partnerships should define

- fourth-suit forcing,
- New Minor Forcing,
- Checkback Stayman,
- support doubles,
- help-suit game tries,
- cue bids,
- jump rebids,
- invitational sequences,
- game-force structure.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Ignoring Opener's Limitation

Once opener limits the hand,

responder usually becomes captain.

---

## Missing Major Fits

Major fits generally take priority over notrump.

---

## Overvaluing Balanced Hands

Balanced hands should bid naturally and avoid unnecessary aggression.

---

## Underestimating Distribution

Shape frequently creates additional tricks.

---

## Bidding Too Quickly Toward Slam

Game should be secured before beginning serious slam investigation.

---

## Rebidding Without a Purpose

Every rebid should describe

- strength,
- shape,
- support,
- or game interest.

---

# System Differences

## Standard American

- Natural rebids
- Invitational jumps
- Traditional game tries

---

## SAYC

- Similar to Standard American
- New Minor Forcing available
- Standard invitational methods

---

## 2/1 Game Force

- More game-forcing auctions
- Slow arrival
- Extensive control bidding

---

## Acol

- Different strength ranges
- Four-card majors possible
- Similar rebid philosophy

---

## Precision

- Artificial openings influence some sequences
- Natural responder rebids after natural auctions

---

## Polish Club

- Artificial club opening
- Natural continuation after most major and diamond auctions

---

# Universal Principles

Always

- support partner before introducing unnecessary suits,
- accurately describe strength,
- show distribution naturally,
- preserve bidding space,
- distinguish invitational hands from game-forcing hands,
- cooperate toward slam only after game is assured.

# Summary

Responder's rebid is the partnership's primary opportunity to evaluate the
combined assets after opener has described the opening hand.

A successful responder rebid accurately communicates

- strength,
- distribution,
- support,
- suit preference,
- game interest,
- slam potential.

Expert partnerships rely on disciplined responder rebids to identify the
optimal contract while preserving bidding space and maintaining clear
partnership communication.
