# Responding to a Preemptive Opening at the Three Level

## Overview

A **three-level preemptive opening** (3♣, 3♦, 3♥, or 3♠) is designed to describe a long suit while making it difficult for the opponents to enter the auction. Responder's primary task is to decide whether to **pass**, **raise**, **bid game**, **explore slam**, or **seek an alternative contract**.

Unlike responses to one-level openings, responder should place greater emphasis on **playing strength**, **fit**, and **trick-taking potential** than on High Card Points alone.

The guiding principle is:

> **Trust partner's long suit and evaluate whether the partnership has enough tricks for game or slam.**

---

# Objectives

Responder should:

- Evaluate support for opener's suit.
- Estimate combined trick-taking potential.
- Judge vulnerability and scoring.
- Decide whether to compete further or defend.
- Avoid unnecessary bidding.

---

# Typical Meaning of a Three-Level Preempt

Most partnerships use:

- Seven-card (or longer) suit.
- Weak opening strength.
- Limited defensive values.
- Highly distributional hand.

The exact requirements vary by partnership agreement.

---

# Responder's Priorities

When partner opens at the three level, ask:

1. Do we have a fit?
2. Do I have enough strength for game?
3. Is 3NT a realistic alternative?
4. Is slam possible?
5. Should I simply pass?

---

# Passing

Passing is often the correct action.

Choose Pass when:

- You have little support.
- Game is unlikely.
- The preempt has already made life difficult for the opponents.

---

# Raising Partner's Suit

Raise with:

- Strong trump support.
- Good playing strength.
- Confidence that additional tricks can be taken.

Examples:

```
3♥ — Pass — 4♥
```

```
3♠ — Pass — 4♠
```

---

# Bidding 3NT

Sometimes responder has:

- Stoppers in the unbid suits.
- Enough outside strength.
- Confidence that opener's long suit will provide sufficient tricks.

Example:

```
3♦ — Pass — 3NT
```

This often succeeds when opener's long suit can be established quickly.

---

# New Suit Response

A new suit is uncommon and usually shows:

- Strong values.
- A natural suit.
- Interest in game or slam.

Many partnerships treat a new suit as forcing.

---

# Cue Bids and Slam Exploration

Slam is rare after a weak preempt, but it can occur when responder holds exceptional strength.

Responder may:

- Cue bid controls.
- Use Key Card Blackwood if a fit is established.
- Explore only with clear slam potential.

---

# Example 1 – Raise to Game

```
West      East

3♠        4♠
```

Responder:

- Four-card support.
- Useful controls.
- Good offensive values.

Game is the practical contract.

---

# Example 2 – Bid 3NT

```
West      East

3♣        3NT
```

Responder:

- Strong balanced hand.
- Stoppers in the other suits.
- Confidence in opener's club suit.

---

# Example 3 – Pass

```
West      East

3♦        Pass
```

Responder:

- Little support.
- No realistic game.
- Allows the preempt to do its job.

---

# Evaluating the Hand

Responder should value:

- Trump support.
- Controls.
- Distribution.
- Quick tricks.
- Playing strength.

High Card Points alone may be misleading.

---

# System Variations

## Standard American

Responses are generally natural.

## SAYC

Similar to Standard American.

## 2/1 Game Force

Preemptive openings are handled naturally outside forcing auctions.

## Acol

Natural responses with partnership variations.

## Precision

Treatment depends on the partnership structure.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Overbidding with scattered values.
- Ignoring the importance of trump support.
- Looking for slam without sufficient controls.
- Competing unnecessarily.
- Forgetting that opener is limited.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should agree on:

- Minimum suit length for a preempt.
- Strength range.
- Meaning of new-suit responses.
- Whether 3NT is natural.
- Slam investigation methods.
- Competitive actions after interference.

These agreements should appear on the convention card.

# Memory Aid

**Responding to a Three-Level Preempt = "Trust the Long Suit, Evaluate the Tricks."**

Remember:

- **Partner's three-level opening describes a limited but highly distributional hand.**
- **Responder should focus on trick-taking potential rather than High Card Points alone.**
- **Passing is often the correct action when game is unlikely.**
- **Raise aggressively with strong trump support and offensive values.**
- **Explore slam only with exceptional strength and a clear fit.**

Responding to a three-level preempt is primarily an exercise in practical judgment. By combining knowledge of opener's limited strength with an evaluation of fit, controls, and playing strength, responder can accurately decide whether to stop safely, bid game, or occasionally investigate slam.
