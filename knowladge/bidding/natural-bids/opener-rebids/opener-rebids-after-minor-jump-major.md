# Opener Rebids After a Minor Opening – Jump Rebid in a Major

## Overview

A **jump rebid in a major suit** after opening **1♣** or **1♦** is an uncommon but descriptive rebid. It shows a hand with **extra strength**, a **strong second suit**, and serious interest in reaching game or slam.

Unlike a simple new-suit rebid or a reverse, a jump rebid immediately emphasizes both the quality of the second suit and the strength of the hand.

The guiding principle is:

> **A jump rebid in a major shows a powerful two-suited hand with extra values and game-going ambitions.**

---

# Purpose

The jump rebid allows opener to:

- Show a powerful second suit.
- Demonstrate extra strength.
- Describe distribution accurately.
- Force the auction.
- Begin game or slam exploration.

---

# Typical Requirements

Most natural systems require:

- Approximately 17–19+ HCP or equivalent playing strength.
- Five or more cards in the opening minor.
- Four or more cards in the major.
- An unbalanced hand.
- Excellent playing strength.

Typical distributions include:

```
5-4
5-5
6-4
6-5
```

---

# Strength

The jump rebid promises:

- Extra values.
- Strong offensive potential.
- Genuine interest in game.
- Possible slam ambitions.

It should never be made with a minimum opening.

---

# Why Jump?

Compared with a simple rebid or reverse, the jump:

- Clearly distinguishes maximum hands.
- Highlights the major suit.
- Encourages responder to cooperate aggressively.

# Responder's Priorities

Responder should evaluate:

- Support for the major.
- Fit for the minor.
- High-card strength.
- Controls.
- Distribution.
- Game and slam potential.

Possible continuations include:

- Raise the major.
- Return to the minor.
- Bid Notrump.
- Cue bid controls.
- Investigate slam.

---

# Jump Rebid vs. Reverse

### Reverse

- Shows extra strength.
- Two-suited hand.
- Forces responder to a higher level.
- More economical use of bidding space.

### Jump Rebid

- Stronger emphasis on playing strength.
- Consumes more bidding space.
- Highlights an excellent second suit.
- Often indicates greater game or slam interest.

---

# Jump Rebid vs. Simple New Suit

Simple new suit:

```
1♣
1♠
2♥
```

Shows:

- Minimum to medium opening.
- Natural second suit.

Jump rebid:

```
1♣
1♠
3♥
```

Shows:

- Extra values.
- Powerful second suit.
- Strong offensive hand.

# System Variations

## Standard American

Generally natural, showing extra strength and a genuine second suit.

## SAYC

Similar treatment.

## 2/1 Game Force

Some partnerships reserve jump rebids for particularly powerful hands because forcing sequences already describe many strong hands.

## Acol

Usually natural, but exact requirements vary.

## Precision

Treatment depends on the partnership's 1♣ structure and rebid agreements.

---

# Advantages

A jump rebid:

- Clearly distinguishes maximum hands.
- Describes both strength and distribution.
- Helps responder evaluate game and slam.
- Highlights a potentially superior trump suit.

---

# Disadvantages

A jump rebid:

- Consumes valuable bidding space.
- Leaves less room for exploration.
- Can misdescribe the hand if made too aggressively.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Jumping with a minimum opening.
- Showing a weak four-card major.
- Confusing the bid with a reverse.
- Overvaluing distribution without sufficient strength.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should agree on:

- Minimum strength.
- Required suit lengths.
- Whether the jump is forcing to game.
- Follow-up rebids.
- Treatment after interference.

These agreements should appear on the convention card.

# Player's Checklist

Before making a jump rebid:

- Do I have extra values?
- Is my second suit strong enough?
- Does the jump describe my hand better than a reverse?
- Am I genuinely interested in game or slam?

If any answer is **no**, consider a simpler rebid.
