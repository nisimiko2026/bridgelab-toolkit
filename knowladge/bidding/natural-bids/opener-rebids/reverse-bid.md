# Reverse Bid

## Overview

A **Reverse Bid** is a rebid by the opener that shows additional strength by bidding a **higher-ranking suit at the two level**, forcing responder to bid at the three level to return to opener's first suit. The reverse promises extra values and is one of the strongest natural rebids in standard bidding systems.

A reverse is based on both **strength** and **distribution**. It typically shows at least **16+ high-card points (or equivalent playing strength)** and a hand with a longer first suit than second suit.

The guiding principle is:

> **A reverse shows extra strength and an unbalanced hand that is too strong for a simple rebid.**

---

# Definition

A reverse occurs when:

- Opener bids one suit.
- Responder makes a natural response.
- Opener rebids a **higher-ranking suit**.
- Responder must bid at the **three level** to return to opener's first suit.

Example:

```
West     East

1♦       1♠
2♥
```

Responder must bid **3♦** to return to diamonds.

---

# Requirements

A reverse generally promises:

- 16+ HCP (or equivalent playing strength)
- An unbalanced hand
- At least five cards in the first suit
- Usually four or more cards in the second suit
- Longer first suit than second suit

Typical distributions include:

```
5-4
6-4
5-5
6-5
```

---

# Why Reverse?

A reverse allows opener to:

- Show extra strength.
- Describe two suits naturally.
- Force the auction higher.
- Invite or force game depending on partnership style.

---

# Example 1

```
♠ A K 4
♥ K J 8 5
♦ A Q 10 7 3
♣ 4
```

Auction:

```
West     East

1♦       1♠
2♥
```

The reverse shows:

- Extra values
- Five diamonds
- Four hearts

---

# Example 2

```
♠ A 8
♥ A K J 9
♦ K Q J 8 6
♣ 7 2
```

Auction:

```
West     East

1♦       1♠
2♥
```

Again, opener has sufficient strength to reverse.

---

# Hands That Do NOT Reverse

Minimum opening:

```
♠ K 6
♥ Q J 8 4
♦ A Q 10 8 5
♣ 9 4
```

Auction:

```
1♦
1♠
```

Correct rebid:

```
2♦
```

Not:

```
2♥
```

The hand lacks the strength required for a reverse.

---

# Reverse vs. Jump Shift

Reverse:

- Shows extra values.
- Natural.
- Usually one-round forcing.

Jump Shift:

- Much stronger.
- Often game forcing.
- Partnership agreement varies.

---

# Reverse vs. Simple New Suit

Simple new suit:

```
1♣
1♠
2♦
```

Responder may return to clubs at the two level.

Reverse:

```
1♦
1♠
2♥
```

Returning to diamonds requires bidding at the three level.

This extra level is why additional strength is required.

---

# Responder's Actions

After partner reverses, responder should evaluate:

- High-card strength.
- Fit.
- Distribution.
- Game prospects.

Possible actions include:

- Preference back to opener's first suit.
- Raise opener's second suit.
- Bid Notrump.
- Introduce a new suit.
- Explore slam.

---

# System Variations

## Standard American

Reverse promises approximately 16+ HCP and is forcing for one round.

## 2/1 Game Force

The strength requirement may be slightly flexible because game-forcing auctions already exist.

## Acol

Generally similar to Standard American.

## Precision

Requirements depend on the partnership agreements and the meaning of the 1♣ opening.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Reversing with a minimum opening.
- Reversing with equal-length suits when another rebid is preferable.
- Forgetting that a reverse shows extra values.
- Confusing a reverse with a jump shift.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should agree on:

- Minimum strength for a reverse.
- Whether all reverses are forcing.
- Treatment after interference.
- Follow-up rebids.

These agreements should appear on the convention card when applicable.

# Player's Checklist

Before making a reverse:

- Do I have extra strength?
- Is my first suit longer than my second?
- Will partner have to bid at the three level to return to my first suit?
- Is there a simpler rebid that better describes my hand?

If the answer to the last question is **yes**, a reverse is probably inappropriate.

---

# Memory Guide

## Four Requirements

1. Extra strength.
2. Longer first suit.
3. Higher-ranking second suit.
4. Forces partner higher to return to the first suit.

# Memory Aid

**Reverse Bid = "Strength Before Shape."**

Remember:

- **A reverse is not merely a two-suited hand—it is a strong two-suited hand.**
- **The defining feature is that responder must bid at the three level to return to opener's first suit.**
- **Because the auction is forced higher, opener must hold extra values.**
- **A reverse is one of the clearest natural ways to show additional strength after opening the bidding.**
- **When in doubt, choose the rebid that most accurately describes both your strength and distribution.**

The Reverse Bid is one of the fundamental natural bidding tools in bridge. By combining additional strength with a descriptive two-suited pattern, it allows opener to communicate far more information than a simple rebid, helping the partnership judge whether to stop safely, bid game, or investigate slam.
