# Opener Rebids After 1♥ or 1♠ – Jump to a Minor

## Overview

A **jump rebid in a minor suit** after opening **1♥** or **1♠** is an uncommon but descriptive rebid. By jumping in a new minor, opener shows **extra strength**, a **strong second suit**, and significant interest in reaching game or slam.

Because this rebid consumes considerable bidding space, it promises substantially more than a minimum opening and should not be used simply to show shape.

The guiding principle is:

> **A jump shift by opener shows a powerful two-suited hand with slam potential or strong game interest.**

---

# Purpose

A jump rebid in a minor allows opener to:

- Show a second strong suit.
- Describe a powerful distributional hand.
- Force the auction.
- Explore game or slam.
- Communicate both strength and shape efficiently.

---

# Typical Auction

```
West      East

1♥        1♠
3♣
```

or

```
West      East

1♠        1NT
3♦
```

The jump rebid immediately tells responder that opener has much more than a minimum opening.

---

# Typical Requirements

Most natural systems require:

- Approximately 17–19+ HCP or equivalent playing strength.
- At least five cards in the opening major.
- A strong second minor suit (usually four or more cards).
- An unbalanced hand.

Distribution often resembles:

```
5-4
5-5
6-4
6-5
```

---

# Strength

A jump rebid generally promises:

- Extra values.
- Good playing strength.
- Excellent suit quality.
- Interest in continuing toward game or slam.

A minimum opening should never make this rebid.

---

# Example 1

```
♠ A K J 8 5
♥ A 6
♦ K Q J 7
♣ A 5
```

Auction

```
1♠
1NT
3♦
```

The jump to diamonds shows:

- Strong hand.
- Powerful second suit.
- Forcing values.

---

# Example 2

```
♠ 7
♥ A K Q J 8
♦ A J 9
♣ K Q J 5
```

Auction

```
1♥
1♠
3♣
```

The jump shift describes a strong two-suited hand with excellent controls.

---

# Why Jump?

Compared with a simple rebid, the jump:

- Shows additional strength.
- Emphasizes the second suit.
- Encourages responder to cooperate toward game or slam.

---

# Responder's Priorities

Responder should evaluate:

- Fit for opener's major.
- Support for the minor.
- High-card strength.
- Controls.
- Distribution.

Possible actions include:

- Raise opener's major.
- Raise the minor.
- Bid Notrump.
- Cue bid controls.
- Begin slam investigation.

---

# System Variations

## Standard American

Generally a strong, forcing jump shift showing extra values and a genuine second suit.

## 2/1 Game Force

Many partnerships use the jump more selectively because game-forcing auctions can often describe the same hand without consuming bidding space.

## SAYC

Usually treated similarly to Standard American.

## Acol

Treatment is generally natural but may vary by partnership.

## Precision

Requirements depend on the meaning of the 1♣ opening and partnership agreements.

---

# Advantages

A jump rebid:

- Accurately describes powerful distribution.
- Helps responder evaluate slam.
- Shows extra values immediately.
- Clarifies opener's shape.

---

# Disadvantages

A jump rebid:

- Uses valuable bidding space.
- May make slam exploration more difficult if overused.
- Should not replace more descriptive natural rebids.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Jumping with a minimum opening.
- Showing a weak second suit.
- Jumping simply because the hand is distributional.
- Confusing a jump rebid with a reverse.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should agree on:

- Minimum strength.
- Whether the jump is forcing to game.
- Minimum length of the second suit.
- Follow-up rebids.
- Treatment after interference.

These agreements should be recorded on the convention card.

# Player's Checklist

Before making a jump rebid:

- Do I have extra strength?
- Is my second suit genuinely strong?
- Will the jump describe my hand better than a reverse or simple rebid?
- Am I interested in reaching game or slam?

If any answer is **no**, another rebid is probably more descriptive.

# Memory Aid

**Jump to a Minor = "Power, Shape, and Ambition."**

Remember:

- **A jump rebid in a minor is a descriptive bid, not merely a stronger version of a simple rebid.**
- **It promises extra strength and a genuine second suit.**
- **Because it consumes bidding space, it should be reserved for hands with clear game or slam aspirations.**
- **Partnership agreements determine the exact strength and forcing nature of the bid.**
- **Choose the rebid that most accurately conveys both your strength and distribution while leaving room for responder to evaluate the partnership's final contract.**

A jump rebid in a minor after opening **1♥** or **1♠** is one of the strongest natural descriptive rebids available to opener. Used with sound judgment, it enables the partnership to identify excellent game and slam contracts while providing responder with a clear picture of opener's exceptional strength and distribution.
