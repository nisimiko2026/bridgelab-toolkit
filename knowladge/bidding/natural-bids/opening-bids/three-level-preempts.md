# Three-Level Preempts

## Overview

Three-level preemptive openings are among the most powerful obstructive weapons
in bridge. They consume a significant amount of bidding space while accurately
describing a weak hand with a long suit.

The standard three-level openings are

- 3♣
- 3♦
- 3♥
- 3♠

Unlike one-level openings, these bids are **primarily preemptive rather than
constructive**. Their purpose is to make it difficult for the opponents to
exchange information while still providing partner with an accurate description
of opener's hand.

---

# Objectives

A three-level preempt aims to

- consume bidding space,
- obstruct the opponents,
- describe a long suit,
- help partner judge whether to compete,
- suggest an opening lead,
- make profitable sacrifices possible.

---

# Typical Requirements

Most partnerships require

- **5–10 HCP**
- a **good seven-card suit**
- offensive distribution
- no suitable one-level opening
- no hand appropriate for a Weak Two opening

Some partnerships occasionally preempt with an excellent six-card suit,
especially in third seat.

---

# Typical Suit Quality

A seven-card suit should normally contain

- two of the top three honors,

or

- three of the top five honors,

or equivalent playing strength.

Typical suits

```
AKQxxxx
KQJxxxx
AQJxxxx
KQTxxxx
QJTxxxx
```

Suit quality becomes more important when vulnerable.

# Hand Evaluation

Experts evaluate

- Playing Tricks
- Suit Quality
- Controls
- Distribution
- Offensive Potential
- Vulnerability
- Seat Position

Playing strength is considerably more important than High Card Points.

# Vulnerability

Vulnerability strongly influences preemptive style.

## Not Vulnerable

Aggressive preempts are common.

Slightly weaker suits may be acceptable.

---

## Vulnerable

Open conservatively.

Poor suit quality frequently leads to expensive penalties.

---

# Seat Position

## First Seat

Normal requirements.

---

## Second Seat

Disciplined style.

Avoid preempting with hands suitable for constructive bidding.

---

## Third Seat

Aggressive preempting is common.

Lead-directing value becomes especially important.

---

## Fourth Seat

Three-level preempts are uncommon.

Many partnerships require stronger hands because no opponents remain to
preempt.

---

# Responder's Responsibilities

Responder evaluates

- trump support,
- outside controls,
- game prospects,
- sacrifice potential,
- defensive strength.

Responder should remember that opener has a limited hand.

---

# Passing

The most common action.

Responder passes whenever game is unlikely.

Example

```
3♦
Pass
```

---

# Raising the Preempt

Example

```
3♥
4♥
```

Shows

- excellent trump support,
- expectation that game will succeed,
- offensive values.

Many raises are based on distribution rather than HCP.

---

# New Suit Response

A new suit is usually

- natural,
- forcing,
- strong.

Example

```
3♠
4♣
```

Shows genuine strength and interest in game.

---

# 3NT

Responder occasionally bids

```
3NT
```

when holding

- stoppers,
- strong outside values,
- confidence that opener's suit will provide enough tricks.

---

# Slam Investigation

Rare.

Only exceptional responder hands justify exploring slam.

---

# Defensive Value

Three-level preempts

- obstruct bidding,
- consume valuable space,
- suggest opening leads,
- force opponents to guess at higher levels.

This makes them among the most effective tactical weapons in bridge.

---

# Sacrifice Decisions

Three-level preempts frequently create profitable sacrifices.

Responder evaluates

- vulnerability,
- likely defensive tricks,
- offensive potential,
- expected contract.

Judgment is more important than point count.

---

# Competitive Auctions

After opponent intervention,

partnership agreements should define

- doubles,
- cue bids,
- raises,
- new suits,
- forcing actions.

Most competitive decisions depend upon fit and distribution.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should define

- minimum suit quality,
- HCP range,
- seven-card requirement,
- third-seat style,
- fourth-seat agreements,
- responses,
- forcing new suits,
- game tries.

---

# Modern Expert Style

Expert partnerships emphasize

- disciplined vulnerable preempts,
- aggressive non-vulnerable style,
- offensive distribution,
- lead-directing value,
- obstructive effectiveness.

The quality of the suit is usually more important than the exact HCP.

# Common Mistakes

## Preempting with Too Much Strength

Hands suitable for constructive bidding should not preempt.

---

## Poor Suit Quality

Weak suits often produce costly penalties.

---

## Ignoring Vulnerability

Aggressive style should be adjusted when vulnerable.

---

## Ignoring Distribution

Shape is frequently more important than HCP.

---

## Forgetting Partnership Agreements

Responses and competitive methods should be clearly defined.

# Expert Evaluation

Experts evaluate

- playing tricks,
- offensive potential,
- suit quality,
- vulnerability,
- lead-directing value,
- distribution,

rather than relying primarily on High Card Points.

---

# Universal Principles

A three-level preempt should

- promise a respectable long suit,
- describe a limited hand,
- consume bidding space,
- make life difficult for the opponents,
- help partner evaluate game and sacrifice opportunities.

# Summary

Three-level preemptive openings are among the most effective obstructive bids
in natural bridge. They combine a **weak hand**, a **long, respectable suit**,
and **offensive distribution** to consume bidding space and force opponents to
make difficult decisions at higher levels.

Successful use of three-level preempts depends on disciplined **suit quality**,
careful attention to **vulnerability** and **seat position**, and sound
partnership agreements regarding responses and competitive actions. When used
judiciously, they are a powerful tactical weapon that can disrupt the opponents
while helping the partnership compete effectively.
