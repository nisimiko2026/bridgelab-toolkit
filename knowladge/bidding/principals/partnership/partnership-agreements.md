# Partnership Agreements

## Overview

Bridge is a **partnership game**, and success depends on both players having a common understanding of how bids and card play are interpreted. **Partnership Agreements** define these shared understandings, ensuring that each bid, lead, signal, and convention conveys the same meaning to both partners.

The purpose of agreements is **not to exchange secret information**, but to ensure that both partners interpret the same actions consistently. In duplicate bridge, all partnership agreements must be fully disclosed to the opponents.

The guiding principle is:

> **Good agreements create clarity. Good judgment wins the hand.**

---

# Purpose

Partnership agreements help players to:

- Communicate accurately.
- Reduce misunderstandings.
- Improve bidding accuracy.
- Coordinate defensive play.
- Execute conventions consistently.
- Meet the disclosure requirements of duplicate bridge.

---

# Why Agreements Matter

Consider the opening lead:

```
♠A
```

Without an agreement, partner cannot know whether the Ace asks for:

- Attitude
- Count
- Suit Preference

A clear partnership agreement removes uncertainty.

---

# Areas of Partnership Agreement

A complete partnership should discuss:

- Bidding methods
- Opening leads
- Defensive signals
- Carding agreements
- Competitive bidding
- Slam conventions
- Alert procedures

---

# Bidding Agreements

Typical topics include:

## Opening Bids

- Minimum opening strength
- Five-card majors or four-card majors
- Strong club system
- Precision or natural methods

---

## Notrump Ranges

Example:

```

```

or

```
1NT = 14–16 HCP
```

- Splinters
- Drury
- Inverted Minors

---

## Competitive Auctions

Discuss:

- Overcalls
- Takeout Doubles
- Negative Doubles
- Support Doubles
- Cue Bids
- Lebensohl
- Responsive Doubles

---

# Opening Lead Agreements

Typical agreements include:

- Fourth-best from longest and strongest.
- Top of touching honor sequences.
- Ace from AK or AKQ.
- King from KQ.
- Journalist Leads (if used).
- Rusinow Leads (if used).

Example:

```
AK

Lead Ace

Requests Attitude
```

---

# Defensive Signaling

Common agreements include:

## Attitude

```
High = Encourage

Low = Discourage
```

---

## Count

Standard:

```
High-Low = Even

Low-High = Odd
```

or

UDCA:

```
High-Low = Odd

Low-High = Even
```

---

## Suit Preference

High card:

Higher-ranking suit.

Low card:

Lower-ranking suit.

---

# Carding Agreements

Discuss:

- Standard carding.
- UDCA.
- Upside-down signals.
- Smith Echo.
- Lavinthal.
- Revolving Suit Preference.

---

# Declarer Play Agreements

Although declarer play is largely individual, many partnerships discuss:

- Tempo.
- Claim procedures.
- Information management.
- Ethical communication.

---

# Convention Agreements

Every convention should have clearly defined meanings.

Examples:

- Blackwood
- Roman Key Card Blackwood
- Gerber
- Michaels Cue Bid
- Unusual Notrump
- Cappelletti
- DONT
- Multi 2♦

Every convention must be fully understood by both partners.

---

# Disclosure

Duplicate bridge requires:

- Full disclosure.
- Accurate convention cards.
- Honest explanations.
- No hidden agreements.

Partnership agreements belong to both opponents as well as the partnership.

---

# Reviewing Agreements

Strong partnerships regularly review:

- Misunderstandings.
- New conventions.
- Defensive methods.
- Competitive auctions.
- Slam bidding.

Continuous improvement is part of successful partnership development.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Assuming partner "knows."
- Playing conventions differently.
- Forgetting defensive agreements.
- Incomplete disclosure.
- Making assumptions instead of agreements.

---

# Advantages

Strong agreements:

- Improve communication.
- Reduce bidding errors.
- Strengthen defense.
- Increase partnership confidence.
- Produce more consistent results.

---

# Limitations

Even perfect agreements cannot replace:

- Good judgment.
- Accurate counting.
- Sound technique.
- Partnership trust.

Bridge remains a game of decisions, not memorization.

# Memory Aid

**Partnership Agreements = "One Partnership, One Language."**

Remember:

- **Every bid and every card should have the same meaning for both partners.**
- **Good agreements eliminate uncertainty but never replace sound judgment.**
- **All agreements must be fully disclosed to the opponents in duplicate bridge.**
- **Review and refine your agreements regularly as your partnership develops.**
- **The strongest partnerships are built on clear communication, consistency, and mutual understanding.**

Partnership Agreements form the foundation of successful bridge partnerships. They allow two players to communicate accurately within the Laws of Bridge, coordinate their bidding and defense, and make consistent decisions under pressure. A well-defined partnership system creates confidence, reduces misunderstandings, and enables both players to focus on solving the bridge problems presented by each deal.
