# Disclosure

## Purpose

**Disclosure** is the principle that opponents are entitled to know your **partnership agreements**, but **not** your private thoughts, judgments, or intentions.

Bridge is a game of **authorized information**. Players may base their decisions only on:

- Their own cards.
- The auction.
- The play.
- The partnership's disclosed agreements.

Disclosure promotes fair competition by ensuring that all players have equal access to partnership understandings.

---

# Basic Principle

Your opponents are entitled to know **everything your partner knows because of your partnership agreements**.

They are **not** entitled to know:

- What you hope partner has.
- Your personal reasoning.
- Whether you made a mistake.
- Whether you intended to deviate from an agreement.

The obligation is to disclose **agreements**, not **thought processes**.

---

# What Must Be Disclosed

Partnership agreements include both **explicit** and **implicit** understandings.

Examples include:

- Opening bid requirements
- Notrump ranges
- Responses
- Conventional bids
- Alertable calls
- Cue bids
- Preemptive style
- Overcalls
- Doubles
- Defensive methods
- Leads
- Signals
- Discard agreements
- Partnership tendencies that have become established understandings

If both partners expect a bid to have a particular meaning, that meaning must be disclosed.

---

# Explicit vs Implicit Agreements

## Explicit Agreement

The partnership has discussed and agreed upon the treatment.

Example:

> "A 2♦ response to 1NT is always a transfer to hearts."

This agreement must be disclosed.

---

## Implicit Agreement

The partnership has never discussed the treatment, but repeated experience has created a mutual expectation.

Example:

A partnership consistently opens light in third seat, and both partners expect this style.

This has become an agreement and should be disclosed if relevant.

---

# What Should NOT Be Disclosed

The following are **not** disclosed:

- Personal judgment
- Bridge skill
- Probability estimates
- Temporary speculation
- Guesses
- Psychic intentions (before they occur)
- Unauthorized information

Example:

Partner asks:

> "Why did you bid 3♠?"

Incorrect disclosure:

> "I thought partner probably had four spades."

Correct disclosure:

> "Our agreement is that 3♠ is invitational with six or more spades."

---

# Convention Cards

Convention cards provide a written summary of partnership agreements.

They help ensure:

- Accurate disclosure
- Consistent explanations
- Compliance with tournament regulations

The convention card supplements—but does not replace—the obligation to answer opponents' questions fully and accurately.

---

# Alerts and Announcements

Many bridge organizations require certain calls to be:

- **Alerted**, drawing opponents' attention to an unusual meaning.
- **Announced**, verbally describing common artificial meanings.

Requirements vary among governing organizations.

Players are responsible for following the regulations of the sponsoring organization.

---

# Explaining Agreements

When asked about a call, explain:

- The partnership agreement.
- Expected strength.
- Expected distribution.
- Whether the call is forcing.
- Whether it is artificial or natural.
- Any known continuations if necessary to understand the agreement.

Do **not** explain:

- What this particular hand contains.
- What partner probably holds.
- Whether partner has forgotten the agreement.

---

# Misunderstandings

Occasionally, one partner forgets the agreement.

Example:

The partnership agreement is:

```
2NT = Lebensohl
```

One player forgets and treats 2NT as natural.

The explanation to the opponents remains:

> "Our agreement is Lebensohl."

The incorrect bidder must continue bidding based on their own understanding until the auction ends.

After play, the director may determine whether a misunderstanding occurred and whether any adjustment is appropriate.

---

# Psychic Bids

A **psychic bid (psych)** is a deliberate and gross misstatement of strength or distribution.

A single psych is **not** a partnership agreement and therefore is **not disclosed in advance**.

However, if a partnership develops:

- Frequent psychs,
- Protected psychs,
- Expected psychs, or
- Systematic psyching,

those practices may become partnership understandings and must be disclosed according to the applicable regulations.

---

# Unauthorized Information

Players must never use information obtained from partner through:

- Hesitations
- Tempo
- Tone of voice
- Facial expressions
- Mannerisms
- Questions
- Explanations
- Other unauthorized sources

Disclosure concerns only **authorized partnership agreements**.

---

# Ethics of Disclosure

Good disclosure requires players to:

- Be honest.
- Be complete.
- Be consistent.
- Answer questions promptly.
- Avoid misleading statements.
- Correct explanations when required by the laws or regulations.

Winning through undisclosed agreements is considered unethical.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Explaining the current hand instead of the partnership agreement.
- Failing to disclose implicit understandings.
- Assuming "everyone knows" a convention.
- Forgetting to update the convention card after changing agreements.
- Describing personal style instead of partnership style.
- Giving incomplete explanations of artificial bids.

# Summary

Disclosure is a cornerstone of duplicate bridge ethics. Opponents are entitled to know **your partnership agreements**, whether explicit or implicit, but not your personal thoughts or intentions. Accurate disclosure through convention cards, alerts, announcements, and truthful explanations ensures that all players compete on equal terms and preserves the fairness and integrity of the game.
