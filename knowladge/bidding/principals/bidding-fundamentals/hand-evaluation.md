# Hand Evaluation

## Overview

**Hand Evaluation** is the process of determining the true playing strength of a bridge hand. While **High Card Points (HCP)** provide a useful starting point, expert players also evaluate:

- Distribution
- Suit quality
- Controls
- Fit
- Offensive versus defensive strength
- Vulnerability
- Position in the auction

Accurate hand evaluation leads to better opening bids, overcalls, competitive decisions, game and slam bidding, and declarer play.

The guiding principle is:

> **Evaluate the hand you have—not just the points you count.**

---

# Purpose

Hand evaluation helps players to:

- Decide whether to open the bidding.
- Judge game and slam potential.
- Evaluate competitive actions.
- Choose between declaring and defending.
- Assess offensive and defensive strength.
- Improve bidding accuracy.

---

# High Card Points (HCP)

The standard HCP scale is:

| Honor | Points |
|--------|-------:|
| Ace | 4 |
| King | 3 |
| Queen | 2 |
| Jack | 1 |

Example:

```
♠ A K 8 4
♥ Q J 6
♦ A 7 5
♣ K 9 2
```

HCP:

```
A = 4
K = 3
Q = 2
J = 1
A = 4
K = 3

Total = 17 HCP
```

HCP are the foundation—but not the whole story.

---

# Distribution

Shape greatly affects playing strength.

Typical distribution points:

| Holding | Extra Value |
|----------|------------:|
| Void | +5 |
| Singleton | +3 |
| Doubleton | +1 |

Distribution points are generally added **after a trump fit has been found**, not for balanced no-trump evaluations.

Example:

```
♠ A K J 8 6
♥ —
♦ Q 7 4
♣ A J 10 6 3
```

The void and long suits greatly increase offensive potential.

---

# Suit Quality

Long suits vary greatly in strength.

Compare:

```
♠ A K Q J 9
```

versus

```
♠ J 9 8 7 6
```

Both are five-card suits, but their trick-taking potential is very different.

Good suit quality includes:

- Top honors.
- Intermediate cards.
- Length.
- Connected honors.

---

# Controls

Controls are especially important in slam bidding.

| Card | Controls |
|------|---------:|
| Ace | 2 |
| King | 1 |

Hands with many controls often play stronger than their HCP suggest.

---

# Offensive vs. Defensive Strength

## Offensive Features

- Long suits.
- Shortages.
- Controls.
- Good fit.
- Shape.

These hands prefer declaring.

---

## Defensive Features

- Balanced distribution.
- Quick tricks.
- Honors in opponents' suits.
- Strong intermediates.

These hands defend well.

---

# Fit Evaluation

A known trump fit increases hand value.

General guidelines:

| Fit | Effect |
|------|--------|
| Eight-card fit | Normal value |
| Nine-card fit | Stronger offensive potential |
| Ten-card fit | Very powerful |

Once a fit is found:

- Reevaluate shortages.
- Upgrade useful controls.
- Downgrade unsupported queens and jacks in side suits.

---

# Losing Trick Count (LTC)

After a trump fit is established, **Losing Trick Count** often provides a better estimate of trick-taking potential than HCP alone.

Example:

```
AKQ
```

contains:

```
0 losers
```

while

```
J84
```

contains:

```
3 losers
```

LTC is especially valuable for suit contracts.

---

# The Rule of 20

For opening the bidding in first or second seat:

```
HCP

+

Length of Two Longest Suits

≥ 20
```

Example:

```
12 HCP

5-card suit

4-card suit

12 + 5 + 4 = 21

Open.
```

---

# The Rule of 15

For fourth-seat openings:

```
HCP

+

Number of Spades

≥ 15
```

This guideline reflects the importance of the spade suit in competitive auctions.

---

# Reevaluation

Hands should be reevaluated as new information appears.

Examples:

After partner supports your suit:

- Increase the value of shortages.
- Upgrade controls.
- Increase offensive strength.

After partner bids Notrump:

- Ignore shortage points.
- Emphasize balanced strength.

---

# Example 1 – Balanced Hand

```
♠ A Q 8 5
♥ K J 6
♦ A 7 4
♣ Q 9 3
```

Evaluation:

- Strong HCP.
- Balanced.
- Suitable for Notrump.

---

# Example 2 – Distributional Hand

```
♠ K Q J 10 8 5
♥ 6
♦ J 7
♣ A J 9 4
```

Only moderate HCP.

Excellent offensive potential.

Suitable for aggressive competition.

---

# Example 3 – After Finding a Fit

Original evaluation:

```
13 HCP
```

Partner raises your major.

Now:

- Singleton becomes valuable.
- Long trump suit gains value.
- Slam becomes possible.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Counting only HCP.
- Adding shortage points before a fit exists.
- Ignoring suit quality.
- Overvaluing unsupported queens.
- Forgetting to reevaluate after partner bids.

---

# Advantages

Good hand evaluation:

- Improves bidding accuracy.
- Reduces poor contracts.
- Helps judge games and slams.
- Improves competitive decisions.
- Strengthens partnership judgment.

# Player's Checklist

Before every important bid, ask:

- How many HCP do I have?
- Is my hand balanced?
- How good are my long suits?
- Do I have useful controls?
- Have we found a fit?
- Should I reevaluate?

---

# Memory Guide

## Six Steps

1. Count HCP.
2. Evaluate distribution.
3. Assess suit quality.
4. Count controls.
5. Identify offensive or defensive strengths.
6. Reevaluate after partner's bids.

---

# Comparison

| Evaluation Method | Best Used For |
|-------------------|---------------|
| HCP | Initial hand strength |
| Distribution | Suit contracts |
| Controls | Slam bidding |
| Losing Trick Count | After a fit is found |
| Rule of 20 | Opening decisions |
| Rule of 15 | Fourth-seat openings |

# Typical Evaluation Process

```
Count HCP
↓

Assess Distribution

↓

Evaluate Suit Quality

↓

Count Controls

↓

Determine Offensive vs. Defensive Strength

↓

Reevaluate After Partner's Bids

↓

Choose the Best Action
```

---

# Memory Aid

**Hand Evaluation = "Count Points, Judge Potential."**

Remember:

- **High Card Points are only the starting point.**
- **Distribution, suit quality, and controls often determine the true value of a hand.**
- **Reevaluate your hand whenever partner provides new information, especially after a fit is found.**
- **Balanced hands favor Notrump, while shapely hands favor suit contracts.**
- **Expert players bid the playing strength of the hand, not simply the HCP total.**

Hand Evaluation is one of the cornerstones of successful bridge. By combining High Card Points with distribution, suit quality, controls, and partnership information, players gain a more accurate picture of their hand's true potential. Consistent and thoughtful evaluation leads to better bidding decisions, stronger competitive judgment, and improved results in every form of duplicate bridge.
