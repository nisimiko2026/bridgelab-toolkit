# Rule of 20

## Overview

The **Rule of 20** is a widely used hand evaluation guideline that helps determine whether to **open the bidding in first or second seat**. It combines **High Card Points (HCP)** with the lengths of the hand's **two longest suits** to measure both honor strength and offensive potential.

Unlike a simple HCP requirement, the Rule of 20 recognizes that **distribution creates tricks**. A hand with moderate HCP but long suits often plays much stronger than a flat hand with the same point count.

The guiding principle is:

> **Open the bidding when your High Card Points plus the lengths of your two longest suits total 20 or more.**

---

# Purpose

The Rule of 20 helps players to:

- Decide whether to open the bidding.
- Evaluate offensive potential.
- Recognize the value of distribution.
- Distinguish between balanced and shapely hands.
- Improve opening bid judgment.

---

# When to Use

The Rule of 20 applies:

- **In first seat**
- **In second seat**

It is **not** intended for:

- Third-seat openings (which are often lighter and more tactical).
- Fourth-seat openings (use the **Rule of 15** instead).

---

# The Formula

```
High Card Points
+
Length of Longest Suit
+
Length of Second Longest Suit
=
20 or More
```

If the total is:

- **20 or more** → Usually open.
- **Less than 20** → Usually pass.

---

# Example 1 – Open

Hand:

```
♠ A Q J 8 5
♥ K 9 4
♦ 7 5
♣ Q J 6
```

High Card Points:

```
A = 4
Q = 2
J = 1
K = 3
Q = 2
J = 1

Total = 13 HCP
```

Longest suits:

```
Spades = 5
Hearts = 3
```

Calculation:

```
13 + 5 + 3 = 21
```

**Open the bidding.**

---

# Example 2 – Pass

Hand:

```
♠ A J 4
♥ Q 9 6
♦ K 10 8
♣ J 9 5 2
```

High Card Points:

```
A = 4
J = 1
Q = 2
K = 3
J = 1

Total = 11 HCP
```

Longest suits:

```
Clubs = 4
Diamonds = 3
```

Calculation:

```
11 + 4 + 3 = 18
```

**Usually pass.**

---

# Example 3 – Distribution Makes the Difference

Hand:

```
♠ K Q J 10 8
♥ A 9 7 5
♦ 8
♣ J 7 3
```

High Card Points:

```
K = 3
Q = 2
J = 1
10 = 0
A = 4
J = 1

Total = 11 HCP
```

Longest suits:

```
Spades = 5
Hearts = 4
```

Calculation:

```
11 + 5 + 4 = 20
```

Although the hand has only **11 HCP**, its excellent shape justifies an opening bid.

---

# Why It Works

The Rule of 20 recognizes that:

- Long suits create tricks.
- Distribution increases offensive potential.
- Shape often compensates for lower HCP.
- Flat hands require more honor strength.

It encourages constructive openings while avoiding many weak balanced hands.

---

# Balanced vs. Unbalanced Hands

## Balanced Hand

```
♠ A J 4
♥ K Q 6
♦ Q 9 5
♣ J 8 4
```

Even with reasonable HCP, balanced hands need sufficient strength because they lack ruffing potential.

---

## Unbalanced Hand

```
♠ A K J 10 8
♥ 8 7 5 4
♦ Q 6
♣ J 4
```

Long suits increase offensive value and often justify opening with fewer HCP.

---

# Relationship to Other Evaluation Methods

The Rule of 20 is only one evaluation tool.

Expert players also consider:

- Suit quality.
- Controls (Aces and Kings).
- Losing Trick Count (LTC).
- Position at the table.
- Vulnerability.
- Partnership style.

---

# Planning the Auction

Before opening, ask:

- How many HCP do I have?
- What are my two longest suits?
- Does the total reach 20?
- Is my hand offensive or defensive?
- Does suit quality support an opening bid?

---

# Exceptions

The Rule of 20 is a guideline, not an absolute law.

You may choose to **open with less than 20** when:

- Holding an excellent six-card suit.
- Having outstanding controls.
- Playing aggressive third-seat style (outside the Rule of 20).

You may choose to **pass despite reaching 20** when:

- Honors are poorly placed.
- Suits are weak.
- The hand is unusually defensive.

---

# Advantages

- Easy to remember.
- Recognizes the value of distribution.
- Improves opening-bid judgment.
- Widely used by tournament players.
- Prevents many marginal openings.

---

# Disadvantages

- Ignores suit quality.
- Does not account for controls.
- Less accurate for unusual distributions.
- Should not replace sound judgment.

# Opener's Checklist

Before opening in first or second seat:

- Count your High Card Points.
- Count the lengths of your two longest suits.
- Add the three numbers.
- Consider suit quality and controls.
- Make the opening decision.

---

# Memory Guide

## Five Steps

1. Count HCP.
2. Count the two longest suits.
3. Add the three values.
4. Compare with 20.
5. Apply judgment before opening.

# Typical Decision Process

```
Count HCP
↓

Count Two Longest Suits

↓

Add the Three Numbers

↓

20 or More?

↓

Yes → Usually Open

No → Usually Pass
```

---

# Memory Aid

**Rule of 20 = "Points Plus Shape."**

Remember:

- **Use the Rule of 20 only in first and second seat.**
- **Add your High Card Points to the lengths of your two longest suits.**
- **A total of 20 or more usually justifies an opening bid.**
- **Distribution often makes a hand stronger than its HCP alone suggest.**
- **Always consider suit quality, controls, vulnerability, and partnership style before making the final decision.**

The **Rule of 20** is one of the most practical opening-bid guidelines in modern bridge. By combining High Card Points with distribution, it provides a more accurate measure of offensive potential than HCP alone. Used together with sound judgment and other evaluation methods, it helps players make disciplined and effective opening decisions.
