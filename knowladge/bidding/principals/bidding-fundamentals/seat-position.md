# Seat Position

## Purpose

**Seat position** refers to your position in the bidding sequence before anyone has opened the auction. It significantly influences opening bid requirements, risk tolerance, and overall bidding strategy.

The four seat positions are:

1. First Seat
2. Second Seat
3. Third Seat
4. Fourth Seat

Each seat has different objectives because the amount of information available and the opportunity to act differ.

---

# First Seat

## Definition

You are the dealer and make the first call.

```
You   LHO   Partner   RHO
 1st
```

## Objectives

- Describe your hand accurately.
- Compete for partscore or game.
- Avoid giving away information with poor openings.

## Typical Style

Opening requirements are the most disciplined.

Use:

- Rule of 20
- Rule of 22
- Sound suit quality
- Normal opening values

Light openings are possible only with excellent distribution and partnership agreement.

---

# Second Seat

## Definition

Right-hand opponent has passed.

```
RHO(Pass)   You   LHO   Partner
            2nd
```

## Objectives

Partner has not yet had a chance to act.

Since passing may allow the board to be passed out, openings should still be reasonably sound.

## Typical Style

Requirements are similar to first seat.

Many players use:

- Rule of 20
- Rule of 22
- Good suit quality
- Reasonable defensive values

Avoid speculative openings.

---

# Third Seat

## Definition

Partner and right-hand opponent have both passed.

```
Partner(Pass)   RHO(Pass)   You
                            3rd
```

## Objectives

Third-seat openings are often tactical.

Goals include:

- Winning the partscore.
- Making life difficult for opponents.
- Suggesting a lead.
- Preventing opponents from entering the auction comfortably.

Partner is a passed hand and cannot hold opening values.

## Typical Style

Players often open lighter.

Common features include:

- Good suit quality.
- Long suits.
- Lead-directing values.
- Distribution.

High-card points become less important than suit quality.

---

### Example

```
♠ KQJ984
♥ 83
♦ 742
♣ J63
```

Only 8 HCP, but many partnerships would open **1♠** in third seat because of the excellent six-card suit.

---

# Fourth Seat

## Definition

All three previous players have passed.

```
Partner(Pass)
RHO(Pass)
LHO(Pass)
You
```

If you pass, the board is passed out.

## Objectives

Decide whether competing is likely to produce a positive score.

Unlike other seats, opening in fourth seat is not about preventing opponents from bidding—they have already declined to open.

## Rule of 15

Many players apply the Rule of 15.

```
HCP + Number of Spades ≥ 15
```

This favors opening hands containing spades, reducing the chance of allowing opponents to buy a spade partscore cheaply.

---

### Example

```
♠ KQJ84
♥ 742
♦ A74
♣ 963
```

- HCP = 10
- Spades = 5

Total = 15

Open **1♠**.

# Comparison of Seat Positions

| Seat | Typical Style | Primary Goal |
|------|---------------|--------------|
| First | Sound | Describe strength accurately |
| Second | Sound | Compete without overreaching |
| Third | Aggressive | Win the partscore, obstruct opponents, suggest a lead |
| Fourth | Selective | Decide whether bidding is profitable |

---

# Effect on Opening Requirements

| Seat | Opening Standard |
|------|------------------|
| First | Normal |
| Second | Normal |
| Third | Lightest |
| Fourth | Most selective (Rule of 15) |

---

# Influence on Preemptive Bidding

### First Seat

Require reasonable Playing Tricks and suit quality.

### Second Seat

Slightly more disciplined.

### Third Seat

Most aggressive.

Preempts are often tactical.

### Fourth Seat

Rare.

Since the auction would otherwise end, weak preempts lose much of their obstructive value.

---

# Tactical Considerations

### First and Second Seat

Prioritize:

- Accurate description
- Partnership communication
- Constructive bidding

---

### Third Seat

Prioritize:

- Interference
- Lead direction
- Winning the partscore
- Making opponents guess

---

### Fourth Seat

Prioritize:

- Expected score
- Likelihood of making the contract
- Avoiding minus scores

---

# Common Mistakes

- Opening marginal hands in first or second seat without sufficient playing strength.
- Forgetting that third-seat openings may be significantly lighter.
- Applying Rule of 20 in fourth seat instead of Rule of 15.
- Making weak fourth-seat preempts that have little obstructive value.
- Ignoring vulnerability when adjusting opening style by seat.

# Summary

Seat position is a fundamental factor in opening bid strategy. **First and second seat** emphasize sound constructive bidding, **third seat** allows tactical and aggressive openings, while **fourth seat** requires careful judgment because passing ends the auction. Successful players adjust their opening standards according to both **seat position** and **vulnerability**.
