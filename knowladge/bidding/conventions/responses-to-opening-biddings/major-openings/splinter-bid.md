# Splinter Bid Convention

## Purpose

The **Splinter Bid** is a **double-jump shift** that shows a strong hand with excellent trump support and a singleton or void in the suit bid. It is used to investigate slam while immediately identifying shortness.

A Splinter Bid allows the partnership to:

- Show a game-forcing raise.
- Identify a singleton or void.
- Help partner evaluate wasted values.
- Improve slam bidding accuracy.

---

# When It Applies

Most commonly after partner opens a major:

```
1♥
?
```

or

```
1♠
?
```

Responder makes a **double jump** in a new suit.

Example:

```
1♠
4♣
```

This is a Splinter Bid.

---

# Basic Meaning

A Splinter Bid shows:

- **4+ card support** for partner's major
- **Game-forcing values**
- **Singleton or void** in the suit bid
- No ace or king in the splinter suit (typically)

Distribution is more important than high-card points.

---

# Standard Splinter Bids

## After 1♥ Opening

| Bid | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| 4♣ | Heart support, club singleton/void |
| 4♦ | Heart support, diamond singleton/void |
| 4♠ | Heart support, spade singleton/void |

---

## After 1♠ Opening

| Bid | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| 4♣ | Spade support, club singleton/void |
| 4♦ | Spade support, diamond singleton/void |
| 4♥ | Spade support, heart singleton/void |

---

# Requirements

Typical requirements:

- **13–16 HCP** (approximately)
- Excellent distribution
- Four or more trumps
- Singleton or void
- Game-going values

Some partnerships allow very distributional hands with fewer HCP.

---

# Opener's Evaluation

After a Splinter Bid, opener should evaluate:

### Honors opposite the short suit

Example:

Responder splinters in clubs.

Opener holds:

```
♣ KQ
```

These values are less useful.

---

### Controls

Good controls increase slam chances.

---

### Trump Quality

Excellent trump support improves slam prospects.

# What a Splinter Does NOT Show

A Splinter Bid does **not** show:

- A natural suit.
- A preemptive jump.
- A weak hand.
- Interest in playing the splinter suit.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Agree on:

- Minimum HCP.
- Four or five-card support required.
- Singleton only or singleton/void.
- Whether opener may sign off immediately.
- Whether Splinters apply after interference.
- Whether mini-splinters are played.

---

# Mini-Splinters (Optional)

Some partnerships use:

```
1♠
3♥
```

Artificial.

Shows:

- Four-card support
- Singleton heart
- Invitational values

Requires partnership agreement.

---

# Advantages

- Shows support and shortness simultaneously.
- Helps partner evaluate wasted honors.
- Excellent slam tool.
- Saves bidding space.
- Widely played by experienced partnerships.

---

# Disadvantages

- Artificial jump bid.
- Requires partnership agreement.
- Can reveal useful information to opponents.

---

# Comparison

| Bid | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Jump Shift | Natural (unless agreed otherwise) |
| Splinter | Trump support + singleton/void |
| Jacoby 2NT | Strong raise without shortness shown |
| Bergen Raise | Invitational or weak raise |

---

# Common Partnership Style

Most expert partnerships play:

- **4+ trump support**
- **Singleton or void**
- **Game force**
- **13–16 HCP** (distribution may compensate for fewer points)
- No ace or king in the splinter suit

Some partnerships permit voids only with exceptional hands.

---

# Memory Guide

| Splinter Bid | Meaning |
|--------------|---------|
| Double jump in new suit | Singleton/void there |
| Agreed trump suit | Partner's major |
| Strength | Game forcing |

---

# Typical Continuations

1. Evaluate wasted values.
2. Sign off with minimum.
3. Cue bid with slam interest.
4. Use Roman Key Card Blackwood when appropriate.

---

# Memory Aid

**Splinter = "Shortness + Support."**

Remember:

- **Double jump**
- **4+ trump support**
- **Singleton or void in the bid suit**
- **Game-forcing values**
- **Slam investigation**

A Splinter Bid immediately tells partner where your shortness is, allowing accurate evaluation of honor cards and making it one of the most powerful slam-investigation tools in modern bridge.
