# Bergen Raises

## Bridge Insight

**Bergen Raises allow responder to describe both trump support and strength immediately after a major-suit opening. Instead of using a single raise for many different hands, Bergen Raises divide raises into well-defined strength ranges, making competitive and constructive auctions far more accurate.**

---

# Overview

**Bergen Raises** are conventional responses after partner opens **1♥** or **1♠**. Using artificial bids at the three level, responder immediately shows:

- Four-card (or longer) trump support.
- A defined strength range.
- Whether the raise is preemptive, constructive, or invitational.

The convention improves both competitive bidding and game evaluation by eliminating much of the ambiguity associated with traditional raises.

---

# Objectives

Bergen Raises enable the partnership to:

- Show four-card trump support immediately.
- Distinguish weak, mixed, constructive, and invitational raises.
- Increase competitive bidding accuracy.
- Make preemptive raises more effective.
- Improve game and slam evaluation.

---

# When to Use

Typical auctions:

```text
1♥      ?
```

or

```text
1♠      ?
```

The convention applies only after a **natural major-suit opening**.

---

# Requirements

### Opener

- Natural 1♥ or 1♠ opening.

### Responder

Normally:

- Four or more-card trump support.
- Defined strength according to the chosen Bergen structure.

---

# Convention Structure

Many partnerships play the following modern structure.

| Response | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| 3♣ | Constructive raise (7–9 HCP, 4+ trumps) |
| 3♦ | Limit raise (10–12 HCP, 4+ trumps) |
| 3♥ / 3♠ | Preemptive raise (usually 0–6 HCP, 4+ trumps) |

Other partnerships exchange the meanings of 3♣ and 3♦ or include a **Mixed Raise** treatment.

The exact structure is a partnership agreement.

---

# Strength Ranges

## Preemptive Raise

Typical hand:

- 0–6 HCP
- Four or more trumps

Purpose:

- Consume bidding space.
- Make life difficult for the opponents.

---

## Constructive Raise

Typical hand:

- 7–9 HCP
- Four-card support

Shows interest in competing while stopping below game.

---

## Invitational Raise

Typical hand:

- 10–12 HCP
- Four-card support

Invites opener to bid game with a maximum opening.

Opener signs off with a minimum hand.

```text

1♠        3♦

```

Opener accepts the invitation with maximum values.

# Continuations

After Bergen Raises, opener evaluates:

- Strength.
- Trump quality.
- Controls.
- Distribution.

---

## After a Preemptive Raise

With:

- Minimum opening

Responder usually passes.

With:

- Maximum opening

Opener may still bid game.

---

## After a Constructive Raise

Opener usually:

- Signs off with minimum values.
- Invites or bids game with extras.

---

## After an Invitational Raise

Opener chooses between:

- Passing below game.
- Accepting the invitation.
- Beginning slam investigation.

# Bergen Raises versus Limit Raise

| Bergen Raise | Limit Raise |
|--------------|-------------|
| Artificial | Natural |
| Four-card support emphasized | Usually three or more-card support |
| Clearly defined ranges | Broader range |
| More descriptive | Simpler |

---

# Bergen Raises versus Jacoby 2NT

| Bergen Raises | Jacoby 2NT |
|---------------|------------|
| Invitational or weaker | Game forcing |
| Four-card support | Four-card support |
| No immediate slam interest | Possible slam interest |
| Describes strength | Begins slam investigation |

---

# Bergen Raises versus Mixed Raise

| Bergen Raises | Mixed Raise |
|---------------|-------------|
| Complete raising structure | Single hand type |
| Multiple strength ranges | Intermediate values only |

# Partnership Agreements

The partnership should agree:

- Exact meanings of 3♣ and 3♦.
- HCP ranges.
- Whether Mixed Raises are included.
- Minimum trump length.
- Continuations after interference.
- Whether Bergen applies only to major openings.

These agreements should be recorded on the convention card.

# System Variations

Common treatments include:

- Original Bergen Raises.
- Reverse Bergen Raises.
- Modern Bergen.
- Bergen with Mixed Raises.
- Bergen combined with Jacoby 2NT.

Each variation should be fully defined on the convention card.

---

# Expert Thinking

Experts value Bergen Raises because they exchange **two pieces of information simultaneously**:

- Trump support.
- Hand strength.

This allows opener to make far more accurate decisions than after a simple natural raise.

The convention is especially valuable in competitive auctions, where consuming bidding space often determines the final contract.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Forgetting the partnership structure.
- Confusing Bergen Raises with Jacoby 2NT.
- Using Bergen with only three-card support.
- Ignoring vulnerability when preempting.
- Overvaluing balanced hands.

---

# Best Practices

- Agree the exact meanings before play.
- Memorize the strength ranges.
- Practice competitive auctions.
- Evaluate distribution as well as HCP.
- Record agreements on the convention card.

# Expert Tip

**Don't memorize Bergen Raises as specific bids—memorize them as a language for describing raises.**

The exact meanings of **3♣** and **3♦** vary between partnerships, but the philosophy is always the same:

1. Show four-card support immediately.
2. Tell partner how strong your raise is.
3. Leave opener with enough information to judge whether to compete, invite, bid game, or investigate slam.

---

# Memory Aid

**Bergen Raises = "Support + Strength."**

Remember:

- They apply after **1♥** and **1♠** openings.
- They promise four-card (or longer) trump support.
- Artificial bids distinguish different strength ranges.
- They improve both constructive and competitive bidding by replacing ambiguous natural raises with precise hand descriptions.
