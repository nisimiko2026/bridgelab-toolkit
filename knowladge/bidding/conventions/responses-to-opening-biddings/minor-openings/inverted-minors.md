# Inverted Minors Convention

## Purpose

The **Inverted Minors** convention changes the meanings of the single and jump raises of partner's **1♣** or **1♦** opening.

Instead of a simple raise showing a weak hand, the responses are **inverted**:

- **Single raise (2♣ or 2♦)** = Strong, invitational or better.
- **Jump raise (3♣ or 3♦)** = Weak, preemptive.

This treatment allows the partnership to explore for game while making it more difficult for the opponents to enter the auction.

---

# When It Applies

After partner opens:

```
1♣
```

or

```
1♦
```

with no interference.

Example:

```
1♣   Pass
?
```

---

# Basic Structure

| Response | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| 2♣ / 2♦ | Inverted raise (strong) |
| 3♣ / 3♦ | Weak preemptive raise |

---

# Requirements for the Inverted Raise

Typical requirements:

- **10+ HCP** (partnership agreement)
- **Five or more-card support** for opener's minor
- No four-card major suitable for a one-level response
- No stronger forcing bid available

Many partnerships require **11+ HCP** when vulnerable.

# The 2♦ Response

- Invitational or better values

# Jump Raise

Example:

```
1♣
3♣
```

Shows:

- Weak hand
- Five or more-card support
- Preemptive

Typical strength:

- 0-7 HCP

---

# Opener's Rebids

After an inverted raise, opener further describes the hand.

### Minimum Opening

Bid:

```
2NT
```

or

```
3♣ / 3♦
```

Shows minimum strength.

---

### New Suit

Natural.

Shows additional values and shape.

Example:

```
1♦
2♦
2♥
```

Natural.

---

### 3NT

Balanced maximum.

Usually denies a singleton.

---

### Jump Shift

Shows extras.

Often slam interest.

# Opener's General Priorities

After an inverted raise:

1. Show minimum or extra strength.
2. Describe distribution.
3. Show stoppers for notrump.
4. Cooperate toward slam when appropriate.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Agree on:

- Minimum HCP (10 or 11+).
- Minimum support (usually five cards).
- Whether 2NT shows balanced minimum or maximum.
- Whether inverted minors are on after interference.
- Whether opener may bypass four-card majors.
- Whether short-suit or control bids are used for slam exploration.

---

# Advantages

- Creates extra bidding space.
- Distinguishes constructive and weak raises.
- Improves slam investigation.
- Makes preemptive raises more effective.
- Widely played in Standard American and 2/1 systems.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires partnership agreements.
- Natural weak single raises are no longer available.
- Responses after interference vary by partnership.

# Memory Aid

**Think "Inverted."**

Instead of:

- Low = Weak
- High = Strong

You play:

- **Low (2-level) = Strong**
- **High (3-level) = Weak**

The single raise is constructive and game-oriented, while the jump raise is purely preemptive.

---

# Typical Partnership Style

Most modern partnerships play:

- **2♣ / 2♦**
  - 10+ HCP
  - 5+ card support
  - Invitational or better
  - Forcing for one round

- **3♣ / 3♦**
  - 0-7 HCP
  - 5+ card support
  - Preemptive
  - Little or no interest in game

Always confirm the exact strength requirements and continuations with your partner.
