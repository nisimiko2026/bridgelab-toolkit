# Smolen Convention

## Bridge Insight

**Smolen solves one of the classic problems in Notrump bidding: how responder can show 5–4 in the majors after Stayman fails to locate a four-card fit, while still keeping the strong Notrump opener as declarer.**

---

# Overview

The **Smolen Convention** is an extension of **Stayman** used after a **1NT** or **2NT** opening when:

- Responder holds **five cards in one major** and **four cards in the other**.
- Responder has **game-forcing values**.
- Opener responds **2♦**, denying a four-card major.

Responder then jumps to the **four-card major**, asking opener to bid the **five-card major**.

This unusual sequence allows opener to become declarer while accurately describing responder's shape.

---

# Objectives

Smolen enables the partnership to:

- Locate 5-3 major-suit fits.
- Keep the Notrump opener as declarer.
- Distinguish 5-4 major distributions.
- Preserve accurate game and slam bidding.
- Avoid unnecessary exposure of opener's strength.

---

# When to Use

Typical auction:

```text
1NT      2♣
2♦       3♥
```

or

```text
1NT      2♣
2♦       3♠
```

Smolen is used **only after opener denies a four-card major**.

---

# Requirements

### Opener

- Natural 1NT or 2NT opening.
- Balanced hand.
- Responds 2♦ to Stayman.

### Responder

Normally:

- Five cards in one major.
- Four cards in the other major.
- Game-forcing values.

---

# Convention Structure

Responder jumps into the **four-card major**.

This promises:

- Four cards in the bid suit.
- Five cards in the other major.
- Game-forcing strength.

Example:

| Auction | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| 1NT – 2♣ – 2♦ – 3♥ | Four Hearts, Five Spades |
| 1NT – 2♣ – 2♦ – 3♠ | Four Spades, Five Hearts |

The jump is artificial.

# Continuations

After responder's Smolen bid, opener chooses among three possibilities.

## Opener Has Three-Card Support

Example:

```text
1NT      2♣
2♦       3♥
3♠
```

Opener accepts the transfer into responder's five-card major.

Responder may:

- Pass in game.
- Continue with slam investigation.

---

## Opener Lacks Three-Card Support

Opener generally bids:

```text
3NT
```

showing no suitable major fit.

Responder normally passes unless holding slam interest.

---

## Slam Continuations

Once a fit has been established, responder may continue with:

- Cue Bidding.
- Roman Key Card Blackwood.
- Kickback.
- Control Bidding.

---

# Why Smolen Works

Without Smolen:

Responder would bid the five-card major directly.

The result:

Responder becomes declarer.

With Smolen:

Responder bids the **shorter major**, allowing opener to bid the **longer major**, keeping the strong hand hidden.

---

# Smolen versus Texas Transfers

| Smolen | Texas |
|---------|--------|
| Game forcing | Game forcing |
| Requires Stayman first | Direct transfer |
| Shows 5-4 majors | Usually six-card major |
| Searches for the best fit | Places the contract immediately |

---

# Smolen versus Jacoby Transfers

| Smolen | Jacoby |
|---------|---------|
| Requires game force | Any strength |
| Shows 5-4 majors | Shows five-card major only |
| Uses Stayman first | Direct transfer |

# Partnership Agreements

The partnership should agree:

- Is Smolen played after both 1NT and 2NT?
- Does it promise game force?
- Does opener always bid the five-card major with three-card support?
- How are continuations handled after interference?
- Are slam continuations standardized?

These agreements should appear on the convention card.

---

# System Variations

Common treatments include:

- Standard Smolen.
- Reverse Smolen.
- Smolen after Puppet Stayman.
- Smolen after Strong Club systems.

Each variation should be clearly agreed by the partnership.

---

# Expert Thinking

Smolen is an elegant example of **declarer placement**.

The convention deliberately uses an artificial jump so that opener—not responder—becomes declarer in the final major-suit contract.

Protecting the strong Notrump hand frequently prevents the defenders from finding the killing opening lead.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Forgetting Smolen applies only after a 2♦ Stayman response.
- Using Smolen without game-going values.
- Bidding the five-card major instead of the four-card major.
- Confusing Smolen with Texas Transfers.
- Forgetting opener's continuations.

---

# Best Practices

- Learn Stayman thoroughly before learning Smolen.
- Memorize the "jump into the shorter major" principle.
- Practice continuations after opener accepts or declines the fit.
- Record partnership agreements on the convention card.
- Review common auctions regularly.

# Expert Tip

**The easiest way to remember Smolen is:**

> **"Bid the short major to reach the long major."**

You intentionally bid your four-card major so that opener can become declarer in your five-card major. This preserves declarer placement while accurately describing responder's shape.
