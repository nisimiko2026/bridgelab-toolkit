# Weak Jump Overcall (WJO)

## Purpose

The **Weak Jump Overcall (WJO)** is a preemptive overcall made after an opponent opens the bidding. It shows a weak hand with a long, good-quality suit and is designed to:

- Disrupt the opponents' bidding.
- Compete for the partscore.
- Make it difficult for opponents to exchange information.
- Suggest a good lead if the opponents declare.

Unlike a strong jump overcall, a Weak Jump Overcall is primarily **preemptive**, not invitational.

---

# When It Applies

After an opponent opens a natural suit:

```
(1♣)   ?
```

or

```
(1♦)   ?
```

or

```
(1♥)   ?
```

or

```
(1♠)   ?
```

A jump overcall is made.

Example:

```
West   North
1♦     2♠
```

The jump to **2♠** is a Weak Jump Overcall.

---

# Basic Requirements

Typical requirements:

- **6-card suit** (occasionally a very strong 5-card suit by agreement)
- **5–10 HCP**
- Good suit quality
- No interest in game opposite a passed partner

Distribution is more important than high-card points.

---

# Suit Quality

A typical suit contains:

- Two of the top three honors, or
- Three of the top five honors

Examples:

```
AKJxxx
```

```
KQJxxx
```

```
AQ10xxx
```

Poor suits should generally not be used for weak jump overcalls, especially when vulnerable.

# Strength Guidelines

| Vulnerability | Typical HCP |
|---------------|-------------|
| Not Vulnerable | 5–9 HCP |
| Equal | 6–10 HCP |
| Vulnerable | 7–10 HCP with better suit quality |

The more vulnerable you are, the stronger the suit should be.

---

# Partner's Responses

## Pass

Most common.

Partner has no game interest.

---

## Raise

Competitive.

Shows support.

Example:

```
(1♦)
2♠
Pass
3♠
```

Invitational or competitive.

---

## Game

Partner judges that game is likely despite the weak overcall.

---

## New Suit

Natural.

Usually forcing for one round.

Shows strong values.

---

## 2NT

Many partnerships use **2NT** as an artificial inquiry (similar to the Ogust Convention).

Asks about:

- Strength
- Suit quality

---

# Ogust Continuations (Optional)

If playing **Ogust**, responses to 2NT are:

| Rebid | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| 3♣ | Weak hand, poor suit |
| 3♦ | Weak hand, good suit |
| 3♥ | Good hand, poor suit |
| 3♠ | Good hand, good suit |
| 3NT | Maximum with excellent suit (optional style) |

Partnership agreement required.

# When NOT to Use

Avoid a Weak Jump Overcall with:

- Strong opening values.
- Two-suited hands (consider Michaels Cue Bid or Unusual 2NT).
- Poor six-card suits, especially when vulnerable.
- Four- or five-card suits without partnership agreement.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Agree on:

- Minimum suit length (6 or 7 cards).
- Minimum HCP.
- Whether five-card suits are ever acceptable.
- Whether 2NT is Ogust.
- Responses after interference.
- Vulnerability style.
- Whether jump overcalls at the three level require a seven-card suit.

---

# Advantages

- Highly disruptive to the opponents.
- Consumes valuable bidding space.
- Suggests an opening lead.
- Helps partner judge competitive actions.
- Easy to learn and widely played.

---

# Disadvantages

- May concede penalties if overused.
- Vulnerability requires discipline.
- Strong hands require different methods.

---

# Comparison

| Action | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| Simple Overcall | Opening values, good suit |
| Weak Jump Overcall | Weak hand, long suit |
| Michaels Cue Bid | Two-suited hand |
| Unusual 2NT | Two lower unbid suits |

---

# Common Partnership Style

Most expert partnerships play:

- **2-level jump overcall:** 6-card suit, 5–10 HCP.
- **3-level jump overcall:** Usually 7-card suit.
- **2NT inquiry:** Ogust.
- Better suits required when vulnerable.

# Memory Aid

**Weak Jump Overcall = "Preempt with a long suit."**

Remember:

- **Weak hand**
- **Long, good-quality suit**
- **Preemptive**
- **Not invitational**
- **2NT often asks about strength and suit quality (Ogust)**

The Weak Jump Overcall is one of the fundamental preemptive tools in modern bridge, combining obstruction with accurate description of a long suit while leaving stronger and two-suited hands to other conventions.
