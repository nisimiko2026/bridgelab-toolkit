# Michaels Cue Bid

## Bridge Insight

**The Michaels Cue Bid transforms a single overcall into a description of two suits. By showing shape rather than strength, it enables the partnership to compete aggressively while conserving bidding space.**

---

# Overview

The **Michaels Cue Bid** is a conventional overcall made by cue-bidding the opponent's opening suit. Instead of showing strength in the opponent's suit, the cue bid promises a two-suited hand with at least 5-5 distribution.

The convention allows the overcalling partnership to describe both long suits with a single bid, making competitive auctions more accurate and obstructive.

Michaels is one of the most widely played two-suited overcall conventions in modern bridge.

---

# Objectives

Michaels Cue Bid enables the partnership to:

- Show a two-suited hand immediately.
- Compete aggressively.
- Consume opponents' bidding space.
- Locate major-suit fits quickly.
- Differentiate two-suited hands from natural overcalls.

---

# When to Use

Typical auctions:

```text
(1♣)   2♣
```

```text
(1♦)   2♦
```

```text
(1♥)   2♥
```

```text
(1♠)   2♠
```

The cue bid is artificial.

---

# Requirements

Typical hand:

- At least 5-5 distribution.
- Opening strength or better (partnership dependent).
- Good playing strength.
- Offensive orientation.

Many partnerships permit vulnerable and non-vulnerable style differences.

---

# Convention Structure

## After a Minor Opening

```
(1♣) 2♣

or

(1♦) 2♦
```

Shows:

- Hearts
- Spades

Normally at least 5-5.

---

## After a Major Opening

```
(1♥) 2♥
```

Shows:

- Spades
- An unspecified minor

---

```
(1♠) 2♠
```

Shows:

- Hearts
- An unspecified minor

---

```
5-5
```

# Advancer's Rebids

Unlike asking conventions, Michaels places the next decision with **advancer**.

## After a Minor Opening

Since both majors are known, advancer should:

- Prefer the longer major.
- Bid game with sufficient strength.
- Invite with intermediate values.

---

## After a Major Opening

Because the minor is unknown:

### 2NT

The conventional inquiry.

Example:

```text
1♥      2♥

Pass     2NT
```

asks:

> Which minor?

Typical replies:

```text
3♣

↓

Clubs
```

```text
3♦

↓

Diamonds
```

Partnerships may include strength information in these responses.

---

## Simple Preference

With support for the known major:

```text
1♠      2♠

Pass     3♥
```

Shows heart support.

---

## Game Invitation

Advancer may:

- Raise.
- Bid Notrump.
- Cue bid.
- Explore slam.

depending on strength.

# Partnership Agreements

The partnership should agree:

- Minimum strength.
- Required distribution.
- Style at favorable vulnerability.
- Whether 2NT always asks for the minor.
- Continuations after interference.
- Cue-bid raises.

These agreements should appear on the convention card.

# Michaels versus Unusual 2NT

| Michaels | Unusual 2NT |
|-----------|-------------|
| Cue bid | 2NT overcall |
| Always includes at least one major | Usually shows both minors |
| Shows 5-5 distribution | Shows two lower unbid suits |
| Uses opponent's suit | Independent bid |

The two conventions are designed to complement each other.

# Expert Thinking

Experts evaluate Michaels primarily by **playing strength**, not High Card Points.

A hand such as:

```
♠ KQJ94
♥ AQJ83
♦ 4
♣ 73
```

is far stronger for Michaels than a balanced 15-count.

Distribution creates tricks.

Shape wins competitive auctions.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Using Michaels with only 5-4 shape.
- Confusing Michaels with a natural cue bid.
- Forgetting the unknown minor.
- Overvaluing balanced hands.
- Ignoring vulnerability.

---

# Best Practices

- Promise real two-suited distribution.
- Discuss minimum strength.
- Agree the 2NT inquiry.
- Practice competitive continuations.
- Record agreements on the convention card.

# Expert Tip

**Think of Michaels as a distribution convention, not a strength convention.**

Your shape is usually more important than your point count. Two long suits create offensive trick-taking potential and make Michaels an ideal weapon in competitive auctions. The goal is to identify the best fit quickly while making life difficult for the opponents.

---

# Memory Aid

**Michaels = "One Cue Bid, Two Suits."**

Remember:

- Cue-bid the opponent's opening suit.
- After a **minor opening**, you show **both majors**.
- After a **major opening**, you show the **other major and an unspecified minor**.
- Advancer uses **2NT** to identify the minor when necessary.
- Michaels is an offensive convention built on shape, making it one of the most effective competitive tools in modern bridge.
