# 2NT Inquiry after Multi 2♦

## Bridge Insight

**The 2NT Inquiry is the cornerstone response to the Multi 2♦ opening. It allows responder to uncover opener's actual hand type before committing the partnership to a contract.**

---

# Overview

The **2NT Inquiry** is an artificial, forcing response to a **Multi 2♦** opening. It asks opener to further describe the hand represented by the Multi opening.

Since a Multi 2♦ opening may represent several different hand types (depending on the partnership agreement), responder normally lacks enough information to judge the correct contract. The 2NT inquiry solves this problem by requesting additional details.

The exact responses vary among partnerships, but the convention is universally regarded as the principal inquiry over a Multi 2♦ opening.

---

# Objectives

The convention enables responder to:

- Determine opener's hand type.
- Distinguish between weak and strong variants.
- Identify opener's long suit.
- Evaluate game and slam prospects.
- Reach the correct strain and level.

---

# When to Use

Typical auction:

```
West      East

2♦        2NT
?
```

The 2NT response is artificial and forcing.

---

# Requirements

Responder usually has:

- Invitational or stronger values.
- Uncertainty regarding the best contract.
- Interest in game or slam.

With weak hands, responder normally prefers to pass or make a natural correction.

---

# What Does 2NT Ask?

The inquiry asks opener to clarify:

- Which hand type does the Multi opening represent?
- If weak, which major suit?
- If strong, what type of strong hand?
- Additional strength and distribution as agreed.

---

# Typical Responses

Because Multi systems differ, partnerships should define precise responses.

A common structure is:

| Rebid | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| 3♣ | Minimum weak hand |
| 3♦ | Maximum weak hand |
| 3♥ | Weak Two in Hearts |
| 3♠ | Weak Two in Spades |
| 3NT or higher | Strong variant |

Other partnerships instead use the cheapest available bid to identify the hand type before showing strength or suit.

---

# Advantages

The inquiry:

- Removes ambiguity.
- Helps locate the correct major.
- Distinguishes weak and strong meanings.
- Improves game and slam decisions.
- Forms the backbone of Multi auctions.

---

# Limitations

The inquiry:

- Requires detailed partnership agreements.
- Consumes bidding space.
- Gives information to the opponents.
- Has many different response structures.

---

# Partnership Agreements

The partnership should agree:

- Which hand types are included in the Multi 2♦ opening.
- Exact meanings of every response.
- Whether strength or suit is shown first.
- Continuations after the inquiry.

These agreements should be recorded on the convention card.

---

# System Variations

Common Multi styles include:

- Weak Two in either major only.
- Weak Two plus strong balanced.
- Weak Two plus strong one-suiters.
- Weak Two plus Acol Two openings.

The inquiry structure varies accordingly.

---

# Expert Thinking

Experts view the Multi opening as a concealed hand rather than a concealed suit.

Responder's first priority is therefore **identification**, not contract selection.

Only after discovering opener's hand type can the partnership accurately evaluate game, slam, or partscore.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Assuming all Multi partnerships use identical responses.
- Forgetting the agreed inquiry structure.
- Asking with weak hands.
- Bidding game before identifying opener's hand.
- Ignoring vulnerability.

---

# Best Practices

- Keep the response structure simple.
- Agree every continuation.
- Record the structure on the convention card.
- Review follow-up auctions regularly.
- Use the inquiry only when the answer will affect the final contract.
