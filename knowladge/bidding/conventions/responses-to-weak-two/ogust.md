# Ogust Convention

## Bridge Insight

**Ogust transforms the uncertainty of a Weak Two opening into a structured partnership conversation. Instead of guessing whether partner's preempt is sound, responder learns both the hand's strength and the quality of its long suit before deciding whether to stop, bid game, or investigate slam.**

---

# Overview

The **Ogust Convention** is an artificial inquiry used after partner opens a **Weak Two** (2♦, 2♥, or 2♠) and responder bids **2NT**.

The 2NT response asks opener to describe two independent characteristics:

- Overall hand strength.
- Quality of the long suit.

This information enables responder to evaluate whether the partnership should:

- Stop in partscore.
- Invite game.
- Bid game.
- Explore slam.

Ogust is one of the most widely played conventions over Weak Two openings.

---

# Objectives

Ogust enables the partnership to:

- Evaluate game prospects accurately.
- Distinguish minimum and maximum Weak Two openings.
- Evaluate suit quality.
- Improve game bidding.
- Avoid poor games while bidding thin but excellent games.

---

# When to Use

Typical auctions:

```text
2♥      2NT
```

```text
2♠      2NT
```

The **2NT response is artificial**.

---

# Requirements

### Opener

- Weak Two opening.
- Partnership agreement to play Ogust.

### Responder

Normally:

- Invitational or stronger values.
- Genuine game interest.

Weak responding hands usually pass.

Hands with obvious game values may simply bid game.

---

# Convention Structure

Ogust answers two questions simultaneously:

1. Is opener minimum or maximum?

2. Is opener's suit poor or good?

This creates four possible hand types.

### Standard Responses

| Response | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| 3♣ | Minimum • Poor Suit |
| 3♦ | Minimum • Good Suit |
| 3♥ | Maximum • Poor Suit |
| 3♠ | Maximum • Good Suit |

*(Many partnerships use different structures. See System Variations.)*

---

# Defining Strength

Typical agreement:

### Minimum

```
5–7 HCP
```

### Maximum

```
8–10 HCP
```

Playing strength and vulnerability may justify adjustments.

---

# Defining Suit Quality

A **good suit** typically contains:

- Two of the top three honors.

or

- Three of the top five honors.

Examples:

```
AKQxxx

AQJxxx

KQJxxx
```

Poor suit examples:

```
Qxxxxx

J109xxx
```

The exact definition is a partnership agreement.

# Continuations

Receiving the Ogust response is only the beginning of the auction.

Responder should combine:

- Opener's strength.
- Suit quality.
- Trump support.
- Controls.
- Distribution.
- Vulnerability.

The final contract should be based on **playing strength**, not simply HCP.

---

## Minimum • Poor Suit

```text
2♠      2NT

3♣
```

Responder usually:

- Passes.
- Signs off.
- Avoids optimistic game tries.

---

## Minimum • Good Suit

```text
2♥      2NT

3♦
```

Responder evaluates:

- Trump fit.
- Side controls.
- Distribution.

A close game may still be excellent.

---

## Maximum • Poor Suit

```text
2♠      2NT

3♥
```

Maximum values improve the hand, but poor suit quality reduces trick-taking potential.

Responder carefully evaluates:

- Outside winners.
- Suit texture.
- Whether 3NT is preferable.

---

## Maximum • Good Suit

```text
2♥      2NT

3♠
```

This is the most encouraging response.

Responder frequently:

- Bids game.
- Begins slam investigation.
- Chooses between Major Suit and Notrump.

---

## Slam Interest

Although uncommon, responder may continue with:

- Cue Bidding.
- Roman Key Card Blackwood.
- Kickback.
- Control Bidding.

provided sufficient controls and trump support exist.

---

# Partnership Agreements

The partnership should define:

- What constitutes a maximum hand.
- What qualifies as a good suit.
- Whether HCP or playing tricks are emphasized.
- Continuations after interference.
- Whether Feature Ask is also played.
- Whether Ogust is the only meaning of 2NT.

These agreements should appear on the convention card.

---

# Ogust versus Feature Ask

| Ogust | Feature Ask |
|--------|-------------|
| Shows strength | Shows outside feature |
| Shows suit quality | Shows outside Ace or King |
| Evaluates offensive potential | Evaluates controls |

Many partnerships play both conventions.

---

# Ogust versus 2NT Inquiry

| Ogust | Generic 2NT Inquiry |
|--------|---------------------|
| Complete convention | Artificial asking bid |
| Defines the responses | Depends on partnership agreement |

Ogust is one possible treatment of a 2NT inquiry.

---

# System Variations

Common treatments include:

- Standard Ogust.
- Reverse Ogust.
- Playing-strength Ogust.
- Modified Ogust.
- Ogust combined with Feature Ask.

Each partnership should record its chosen structure.

---

# Expert Thinking

Experts understand that **Weak Two openings are judged by offensive potential rather than High Card Points alone**.

A minimum hand with an outstanding suit may be more valuable than a maximum hand with poor suit quality.

Ogust allows responder to evaluate both factors simultaneously before committing the partnership.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Forgetting the response structure.
- Asking without genuine game interest.
- Ignoring suit quality.
- Treating every maximum as an automatic game.
- Forgetting partnership agreements.

---

# Best Practices

- Memorize the four responses.
- Define "good suit" precisely.
- Evaluate playing strength rather than HCP alone.
- Practice continuations after every response.
- Record agreements on the convention card.

# Expert Tip

**Ogust is an evaluation convention, not a point-count convention.**

Its greatest value lies in helping responder judge **playing strength**. A spectacular six-card suit can compensate for fewer HCP, while poor suit quality can make a maximum hand unsuitable for game. Always evaluate the whole hand—not just the numbers.

---

# Memory Aid

**Ogust = "How Good Is Your Weak Two?"**

Remember:

- Ask with **2NT** only when the answer may influence the final contract.
- Opener describes **both strength and suit quality**.
- The four responses identify every combination of minimum/maximum and poor/good suit.
- Use the information together with your own hand evaluation to decide whether to stop safely, bid game, or explore slam.
