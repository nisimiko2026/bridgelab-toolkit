# 2NT Inquiry over Weak Two

## Bridge Insight

**The 2NT Inquiry is not a convention by itself—it is an artificial asking bid whose meaning is determined by the partnership agreement. Its purpose is to help responder evaluate whether a Weak Two opening should remain a partscore, advance to game, or occasionally explore slam.**

---

# Overview

After partner opens a **Weak Two** (2♦, 2♥, or 2♠), responder may bid **2NT** as an **artificial inquiry**. The bid asks opener for additional descriptive information about the opening hand.

The exact information requested depends on the partnership agreement. Common treatments include:

- **Ogust** (strength and suit quality).
- **Feature Ask** (outside Ace or King).
- Other partnership-specific methods.

The 2NT Inquiry is therefore a **framework** rather than a single convention.

---

# Objectives

The inquiry allows responder to:

- Determine whether game is realistic.
- Evaluate opener's offensive potential.
- Distinguish between minimum and maximum Weak Two openings.
- Discover useful outside values.
- Select the best final contract.

---

# When to Use

Typical auction:

```
West      East

2♥        2NT
?
```

or

```
West      East

2♠        2NT
?
```

The bid is artificial and forcing for one round.

---

# Requirements

Responder usually holds:

- Invitational values or better.
- Interest in game.
- Occasionally slam interest.

With weak hands, responder normally passes.

With obvious game values, responder may bid game directly without asking.

---

# Common Partnership Treatments

## Method 1 – Ogust

The most popular agreement.

The inquiry asks opener to describe:

- Hand strength.
- Suit quality.

Example:

| Rebid | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| 3♣ | Minimum – Poor suit |
| 3♦ | Minimum – Good suit |
| 3♥ | Maximum – Poor suit |
| 3♠ | Maximum – Good suit |

---

## Method 2 – Feature Ask

The inquiry asks opener to show an outside feature.

Typical responses:

- Cheapest bid = No feature.
- Suit bid = Feature in that suit.
- Optional 3NT = Maximum with excellent playing strength.

---

## Method 3 – Partnership Variations

Some partnerships combine both methods.

Example:

```
2NT = Ogust

3♣ = Feature Ask
```

Others reverse these meanings.

The exact structure is entirely a partnership agreement.

---

# Complete Auction Example 1

```
West      East

2♠        2NT
3♠        4♠
```

Responder learns opener has a maximum hand with a good suit and bids game.

---

# Complete Auction Example 2

```
West      East

2♥        2NT
3♣        Pass
```

Responder learns opener has minimum values and no attractive game prospects.

---

# Complete Auction Example 3

```
West      East

2♦        2NT
3♥        3NT
```

Responder judges that the combined assets justify game.

---

# Advantages

The 2NT Inquiry:

- Improves game decisions.
- Prevents optimistic bidding.
- Helps identify productive Weak Two openings.
- Supports accurate partnership judgment.
- Fits naturally into most Weak Two structures.

---

# Limitations

The inquiry:

- Has no universal meaning.
- Requires detailed partnership agreements.
- Uses valuable bidding space.
- May disclose information to the opponents.

---

# Expert Thinking

Expert players rarely decide game based solely on High Card Points.

Instead they ask:

- Is opener's suit capable of producing tricks?
- Does opener have useful outside controls?
- Is the Weak Two minimum or maximum?

The 2NT Inquiry answers these practical questions.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Assuming every partnership plays Ogust.
- Forgetting the agreed response structure.
- Asking without genuine game interest.
- Ignoring vulnerability.
- Treating every maximum Weak Two as a game bid.

---

# Best Practices

- Agree before playing exactly what 2NT asks.
- Record the agreement on the convention card.
- Use the inquiry only when the answer may change the final contract.
- Combine the response with your own hand evaluation.

---

# System Variations

Common agreements include:

- Ogust.
- Feature Ask.
- Combined Ogust/Feature structures.
- Natural 2NT (rare).

The convention card should specify the chosen treatment.

# Expert Tip

**Do not memorize the response structure until you know what 2NT is asking.**

The same auction:

```
2♠ — 2NT
```

can mean completely different things depending on whether the partnership plays **Ogust**, **Feature Ask**, or another agreement. The inquiry is simply the asking bid—the convention defines the answers.
