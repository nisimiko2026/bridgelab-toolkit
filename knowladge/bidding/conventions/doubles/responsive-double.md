# Responsive Double

## Overview

A **Responsive Double** is an artificial double made after **partner has made a Takeout Double** and the opponents have raised their suit. It shows sufficient values to compete but no clear natural bid. Rather than being for penalty, the double asks partner to further describe the hand by choosing among the remaining unbid suits.

Responsive Doubles allow the partnership to compete constructively after the opponents have found a fit while preserving valuable bidding space.

Typical auction:

```
West   North   East   South
1♦     Double   2♦     Double*
```

The second double is a **Responsive Double**.

---

# Purpose

Responsive Doubles are used to:

- Compete after partner's Takeout Double.
- Show values without committing to one suit.
- Locate the best fit.
- Preserve bidding space.
- Distinguish constructive hands from penalty doubles.
- Continue describing the partnership's distribution.

---

# When to Use

A Responsive Double is available when:

1. An opponent opens.
2. Partner makes a Takeout Double.
3. The opponents raise their suit.
4. You double.

Typical auctions include:

```
1♦   Double
2♦   Double*
```

```
1♥   Double
2♥   Double*
```

```
1♠   Double
2♠   Double*
```

The convention generally applies only through an agreed level (commonly through **3♦** or **3♥**).

---

# Requirements

Typical requirements include:

- Approximately 8+ HCP.
- Interest in competing.
- No clear natural bid.
- Tolerance for the unbid suits.

Distribution is generally more important than raw high-card points.

---

# What the Double Shows

A Responsive Double generally promises:

- Values sufficient to compete.
- Interest in one or more unbid suits.
- No desire to defend.
- No suitable natural call.

It is **not** a penalty double.

---

# Partner's Rebids

After a Responsive Double, partner should:

- Bid the best unbid suit.
- Rebid notrump with an appropriate balanced hand.
- Cue bid with extra values.
- Pass only with a specific partnership agreement.

The doubler expects partner to continue describing the hand.

---

# Responder's Continuations

After partner's rebid, the doubler may:

- Pass.
- Invite game.
- Compete further.
- Bid game.
- Investigate slam (rare).

The decision depends upon:

- Combined strength.
- Trump fit.
- Distribution.
- Vulnerability.

---

# Responsive Double vs Takeout Double

| Takeout Double | Responsive Double |
|----------------|-------------------|
| First action | Second action |
| Asks partner to bid | Continues the search for the best fit |
| Shortness in opener's suit | Values after opponents raise |
| Usually opening strength | Usually constructive values |

---

# Responsive Double vs Negative Double

| Negative Double | Responsive Double |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| Made by responder | Made after partner's Takeout Double |
| After an overcall | After a raise |
| Shows unbid suits | Shows continued interest in unbid suits |
| Begins competition | Continues competition |

# Advantages

Responsive Doubles:

- Preserve bidding space.
- Differentiate constructive and penalty doubles.
- Improve competitive bidding.
- Locate the best fit.
- Allow flexible hand description.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Requires detailed partnership agreements.
- Varies between partnerships.
- Is unavailable once the auction becomes too high.
- May occasionally help the opponents judge the auction.

---

# Variations

Common partnership styles include:

- Responsive through 2♠.
- Responsive through 3♥.
- Responsive through 4♦.
- Four-card major style.
- Modern Responsive Doubles.

Every partnership should define its preferred methods.

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

Responsive Doubles occur only in competitive auctions. The convention allows the partnership to continue competing after the opponents have shown a fit.

The objective is to improve the search for the best contract while preserving flexibility.

---

## Opponent Competes Further

Example

```
1♦
Double
2♦
Double
3♦
?
```

Both partners should reevaluate:

- Combined strength.
- Trump fit.
- Playing tricks.
- Vulnerability.
- Law of Total Tricks.

Do not compete automatically.

---

## Cue Bids

After a Responsive Double, cue bids frequently show:

- Strong competitive hands.
- Game interest.
- Forcing values.
- Excellent support.

The exact meanings depend on partnership agreement.

---

## Penalty Conversion

Occasionally partner may pass the Responsive Double for penalty.

This should occur only when:

- Partnership agreements permit.
- Defensive strength is obvious.
- The opponents are unlikely to escape.

Such conversions are relatively uncommon.

---

## Balancing

If the opponents stop after a Responsive Double sequence, either partner may later have a balancing opportunity.

Example

```
1♥
Double
2♥
Double
Pass
Pass
?
```

Balancing decisions should consider:

- Distribution.
- Playing strength.
- Vulnerability.
- Partnership style.

---

## Partnership Agreements

Partners should define:

- Highest level for Responsive Doubles.
- Responses after further competition.
- Cue-bid meanings.
- Penalty conversions.
- Competitive raises.
- Balancing methods.

---

## General Principles

When making a Responsive Double:

- Continue searching for the best fit.
- Do not use the double for penalty.
- Compete only with sufficient values.
- Reevaluate after every competitive action.
- Let distribution guide your decisions.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Confusing Responsive and Negative Doubles

Negative Doubles occur after an overcall.

Responsive Doubles occur after **partner's Takeout Double** and the opponents' raise.

---

## Treating the Double as Penalty

Responsive Doubles are constructive.

---

## Doubling Without Enough Values

The convention promises genuine competitive strength.

---

## Ignoring Vulnerability

Competitive decisions should always take vulnerability into account.

---

## Forgetting Partnership Agreements

Different partnerships use different strength ranges and suit requirements.

---

# Tips

- Learn exactly when a Responsive Double is available.
- Think of the convention as extending partner's Takeout Double.
- Discuss the highest level through which it applies.
- Combine Responsive Doubles with Takeout and Negative Doubles for a complete competitive system.
- Practice competitive auctions where both sides have found a fit.

---

# Summary

The Responsive Double is a valuable competitive bidding convention used after partner has made a Takeout Double and the opponents have raised their suit. Rather than suggesting a penalty, it shows constructive values and continued interest in the unbid suits, allowing the partnership to compete accurately while preserving bidding space. Together with Takeout Doubles, Negative Doubles, and Support Doubles, Responsive Doubles form one of the foundations of modern competitive bidding.
