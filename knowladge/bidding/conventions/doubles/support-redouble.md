# Support Redouble

## Bridge Insight

**The Support Redouble allows opener to show exactly three-card support for responder's suit after the opponents make a takeout double. By distinguishing three-card support from four-card support, the partnership can judge the correct level and strain with far greater accuracy.**

---

# Overview

The **Support Redouble** is a conventional redouble used after:

- Opener makes a natural one-level suit opening.
- Responder bids a new suit.
- The next opponent doubles (typically a takeout double).

The redouble shows **exactly three-card support** for responder's suit.

With four-card or longer support, opener normally raises responder's suit directly.

---

# Objectives

Support Redouble enables the partnership to:

- Show exactly three-card support.
- Distinguish three-card support from four-card support.
- Improve competitive judgment.
- Locate 5-3 major-suit fits.
- Preserve bidding space.

---

# When to Use

Typical auction:

```text
West    North    East    South

1♣       Pass     1♠      Double
Redouble
```

The redouble shows:

- Exactly three Spades.
- Normal opening values.

---

Another example:

```text
1♦      Pass

1♥      Double

Redouble
```

Shows exactly three Hearts.

---

# Requirements

Opener normally has:

- Natural opening bid.
- Exactly three-card support for responder's suit.
- Opening strength.
- No immediate descriptive rebid that is more appropriate.

---

# Partnership Agreements

The partnership should define:

- Maximum level for Support Redouble.
- Whether it applies after all doubles.
- Minimum opening strength.
- Continuations after further competition.
- Whether the convention applies after passed-hand responses.

These agreements should appear on the convention card.

---

# Advantages

Support Redouble:

- Improves fit discovery.
- Distinguishes 5-3 from 5-4 fits.
- Preserves bidding space.
- Enhances competitive accuracy.
- Simplifies opener's rebids.

---

# Limitations

Support Redouble:

- Requires partnership agreement.
- Applies only in specific auctions.
- May reveal useful information to the opponents.
- Can be forgotten in fast competitive auctions.

---

# Expert Thinking

Modern bidding recognizes that distinguishing between **three-card and four-card support** significantly improves competitive decisions.

Without Support Redouble, responder often cannot determine whether the partnership has an eight-card fit or only a seven-card fit. This information frequently determines whether the partnership should compete further or defend.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Confusing Support Redouble with a penalty redouble.
- Forgetting that it promises **exactly three-card support**.
- Using it with four-card support.
- Playing different partnership agreements.
- Forgetting the agreed maximum level.

---

# Best Practices

- Play Support Double and Support Redouble together.
- Agree the maximum level.
- Always distinguish three-card and four-card support.
- Practice competitive auctions.
- Record agreements on the convention card.

# Expert Tip

**Support Redouble completes the Support Double convention.**

Together they create a consistent partnership style:

- **Support Double** after an **overcall**.
- **Support Redouble** after a **double**.

This allows opener to distinguish three-card support from four-card support, giving responder a much clearer picture of the partnership's fit.

---

# Memory Aid

**Support Redouble = "Double by Opponents, Three-Card Support by Opener."**

Remember:

- It is **not** a penalty redouble.
- It applies after the opponents **double responder's bid**.
- It promises **exactly three-card support**.
- Direct raises normally show **four or more cards**.
- Together with Support Double, it is one of the most useful modern competitive bidding agreements for accurately identifying major-suit fits.
