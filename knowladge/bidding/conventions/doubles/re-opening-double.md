# Reopening Double

## Overview

A **Reopening Double** is a conventional double made after the opponents' auction appears ready to end. It is used by the player in the **reopening seat** to keep the auction alive when partner has already passed.

Unlike a Penalty Double, a Reopening Double is primarily **constructive**. It asks partner to bid with appropriate values while still allowing partner to pass the double for penalty when holding a suitable defensive hand.

The convention is based on the assumption that partner may have passed because they lacked sufficient strength to act earlier, not because they lacked interest in competing.

Example:

```
West    North    East    South
1♦      Pass     Pass    Double*
```

The double reopens the auction.

---

# Purpose

A Reopening Double is used to:

- Prevent the auction from ending too early.
- Compete for part-score.
- Find an undiscovered fit.
- Show opening-equivalent values.
- Allow partner to choose between bidding and defending.
- Balance the auction.

---

# When to Use

A Reopening Double is available when:

- The opponents appear ready to buy the contract.
- Partner has passed.
- You are in the reopening seat.
- Your hand is unsuitable for a natural overcall.

Typical auctions include:

```
1♣
Pass
Pass
Double*
```

```
1♦
Pass
Pass
Double*
```

```
1♥
Pass
Pass
Double*
```

```
1♠
Pass
Pass
Double*
```

---

# Requirements

Typical requirements include:

- Opening values or equivalent playing strength.
- Shortness in the opponent's suit.
- Support for the unbid suits.
- Desire to compete.

Because partner has already passed, many partnerships allow slightly lighter values than for an immediate Takeout Double.

---

# Strength Requirements

Typical style:

### Immediate Takeout Double

Approximately:

- 12–16 HCP

---

### Reopening Double

Often:

- 10–14 HCP

with good distribution.

Many partnerships borrow approximately **3 points from partner**, assuming partner may have passed with a marginal opening hand.

---

# Distribution

Typical shapes include:

```
4-4-4-1
```

```
5-4-3-1
```

```
4-3-5-1
```

Shortness in opener's suit remains desirable.

---

# Partner's Responsibilities

After a Reopening Double, partner should evaluate:

- Defensive strength.
- Distribution.
- Suit length.
- Combined values.

Partner may:

- Bid naturally.
- Jump with extra strength.
- Pass for penalty.
- Cue bid with strong hands.

---

# Passing the Double

One of the strengths of the convention is that partner may convert the double into a **Penalty Double**.

Example:

```
1♠
Pass
Pass
Double
Pass
Pass
Pass
```

Partner's pass says:

> "I believe defending is our best result."

---

# Responses

## Minimum Hand

Bid the cheapest suitable suit.

---

## Invitational Hand

Jump in the chosen suit.

---

## Strong Hand

Cue bid or make another forcing action.

---

## Defensive Hand

Pass the double.

---

# Reopening Double vs Takeout Double

| Takeout Double | Reopening Double |
|----------------|------------------|
| Immediate action | After two passes |
| Full opening strength | Slightly lighter values acceptable |
| Partner has not passed | Partner has already passed |
| Constructive | Constructive with penalty option |

---

# Reopening Double vs Balancing

| Reopening Double | Balancing |
|------------------|-----------|
| Uses a double | May use any bid |
| Shows takeout values | General balancing action |
| May become penalty | May be natural or conventional |

# Advantages

Reopening Doubles:

- Keep the auction alive.
- Locate hidden fits.
- Prevent opponents from buying contracts cheaply.
- Permit penalty conversions.
- Improve competitive judgment.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Requires accurate judgment.
- Depends upon partnership agreements.
- May occasionally help the opponents.
- Can become expensive if misused.

---

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

Reopening Doubles frequently begin another round of competition. Both partners should continue describing their hands naturally while reassessing offensive and defensive prospects.

---

## Opponent Competes Further

Example

```
1♦
Pass
Pass
Double
2♦
?
```

The doubler and partner should evaluate:

- Combined strength.
- Trump fit.
- Playing tricks.
- Vulnerability.
- Law of Total Tricks.

Possible actions include:

- Pass.
- Compete.
- Cue bid.
- Double later for penalty.

---

## Cue Bids

After a Reopening Double, cue bids generally show:

- Strong hands.
- Game interest.
- Forcing values.
- Excellent support.

Exact meanings should be agreed beforehand.

---

## Penalty Conversion

One of the defining features of a Reopening Double is partner's ability to pass.

Partner should consider passing only when holding:

- Significant defensive strength.
- Length in the opponent's suit.
- Confidence that the contract will fail.

Example:

```
1♥
Pass
Pass
Double
Pass
Pass
Pass
```

This converts the reopening double into a Penalty Double.

---

## Balancing Auctions

Many reopening doubles occur in balancing situations.

Example

```
1♠
Pass
Pass
?
```

A double here combines:

- Balancing principles.
- Takeout principles.
- Reopening principles.

Understanding the relationship between these concepts is essential for sound competitive bidding.

---

## Partnership Agreements

Every partnership should define:

- Minimum strength.
- Distribution requirements.
- Responses.
- Penalty conversions.
- Cue-bid meanings.
- Balancing methods.
- Whether "borrowed values" are used.

---

## General Principles

When making a Reopening Double:

- Assume partner may have some hidden values.
- Be prepared to support every unbid suit.
- Do not reopen with purely defensive hands.
- Continue describing your hand naturally.
- Reevaluate after every competitive action.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Forgetting That Partner Already Passed

Partner's earlier pass affects the expected strength of both hands.

---

## Reopening Too Lightly

Although lighter values are permitted, the hand should still have constructive potential.

---

## Confusing Reopening and Penalty Doubles

A Reopening Double asks partner to act.

A Penalty Double asks partner to defend.

---

## Ignoring Distribution

Distribution remains more important than HCP.

---

## Never Passing the Double

Partner should remember that passing for penalty is one of the convention's greatest strengths.

---

# Tips

- Think of a Reopening Double as a delayed Takeout Double.
- Borrow a few points from partner—but not too many.
- Be prepared for partner to pass.
- Count playing tricks as well as HCP.
- Practice balancing and reopening auctions together.

---

# Summary

The Reopening Double is an essential competitive bidding convention that prevents the opponents from winning low-level contracts uncontested. Made after the auction appears ready to end, it shows constructive values, support for the unbid suits, and a willingness to compete, while allowing partner to convert the double into penalties when holding the appropriate defensive strength. Together with Takeout Doubles, Negative Doubles, Responsive Doubles, Support Doubles, and Maximal Doubles, it forms a cornerstone of modern competitive bidding.
