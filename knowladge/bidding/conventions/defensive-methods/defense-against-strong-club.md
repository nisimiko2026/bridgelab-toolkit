# Defense Against Strong Club

## Purpose

A **Strong Club** opening is an artificial **1♣** bid showing a strong hand, typically **16+ HCP** or **17+ HCP**, depending on the system.

Unlike a natural club opening, the bid reveals little about opener's distribution but promises significant strength.

A successful defense should:

- Enter the auction aggressively when appropriate.
- Describe one- and two-suited hands efficiently.
- Make it difficult for the opponents to exchange information.
- Preserve constructive bidding when holding values.

---

# Common Strong Club Systems

Examples include:

- Precision Club
- Blue Team Club
- MOSCITO
- Relay-based Strong Club systems

Although the opening structure differs, many defensive methods apply to all strong club systems.

---

# Defensive Approaches

Several methods are commonly played.

## Natural Defense

The simplest approach.

| Call | Meaning |
|------|---------|
|Double|Natural, showing clubs or general strength (partnership agreement)|
|1♦ / 1♥ / 1♠|Natural overcall|
|1NT|Balanced hand (range by agreement)|
|2-level bids|Natural|

Advantages:

- Easy to remember.
- Works well for occasional partnerships.

Disadvantages:

- Does not describe two-suited hands efficiently.

---

## CRASH

**CRASH** stands for:

> **Color, Rank, Shape**

The overcall describes two-suited hands according to shared characteristics:

- Same color
- Same rank
- Same shape

CRASH is one of the best-known expert defenses to Strong Club systems.

---

## Suction

**Suction** transfers the meaning of bids.

Each overcall shows:

- The next higher suit.
- Or a two-suited hand including the next higher suit.

The method creates bidding interference while making the opponents guess.

---

## CROC

A two-suited defense using artificial overcalls to identify combinations of suits.

---

## Mathe

A natural-style defense emphasizing:

- One-suited overcalls.
- Balanced hands.
- Strong takeout doubles.

---

## DONT-Style Defenses

Some partnerships adapt **DONT** principles to Strong Club auctions.

These methods usually emphasize:

- One-suited hands.
- Two-suited hands.
- Constructive competition.

---

# Natural Overcalls

Regardless of the defense chosen, partnerships often retain:

- Good one-suited overcalls.
- Balanced notrump overcalls.
- Penalty doubles against later contracts.

---

# Competitive Philosophy

Good defenses aim to:

- Disrupt relay sequences.
- Consume bidding space.
- Identify offensive hands quickly.
- Avoid helping the opponents with unnecessary information.

---

# Hand Evaluation

When competing:

Upgrade:

- Distributional hands.
- Two-suited hands.
- Intermediate honors.

Downgrade:

- Flat minimum hands.
- Poor suit quality.

---

# Advantages

- Interferes with relay auctions.
- Makes strong club systems less effective.
- Allows constructive competition.
- Adaptable to partnership style.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires partnership agreements.
- Many methods are memory intensive.
- Artificial defenses can occasionally misfire.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Agree on:

- Which defense is used.
- Meanings of doubles.
- Notrump ranges.
- Responses after interference.
- Vulnerability adjustments.
- Balancing-seat methods.

# Summary

There is **no single standard defense** to a Strong Club opening.

Partnerships typically choose one of four approaches:

- Natural defense
- CRASH
- Suction
- CROC (or another artificial defense)

The most important partnership decision is selecting **one consistent defensive structure** and using it in every auction against strong artificial club systems.
