# Texas Transfer

## Overview

**Texas Transfer** is an artificial response to a **1NT** or **2NT** opening that transfers opener into a major suit at the **game level**.

Unlike Jacoby Transfers, which begin at the two level and leave room for further exploration, Texas Transfers immediately commit the partnership to game while allowing the stronger notrump hand to become declarer.

Typical auctions:

```
1NT
4♦*
4♥
```

```
1NT
4♥*
4♠
```

```
2NT
4♦*
4♥
```

```
2NT
4♥*
4♠
```

where:

- **4♦** transfers to **4♥**
- **4♥** transfers to **4♠**

Both bids are artificial.

---

# Purpose

Texas Transfers are used to:

- Reach a major-suit game immediately.
- Keep the strong notrump hand hidden.
- Protect vulnerable honors from the opening lead.
- Simplify auctions with no slam interest.
- Reduce opportunities for opponents to exchange information.

---

# When to Use

Texas Transfers are normally used when responder has:

- A six-card or longer major suit.
- Sufficient strength for game.
- No interest in investigating game.
- No immediate slam ambitions.

The convention is commonly played after:

- 1NT opening
- 2NT opening
- Equivalent strong balanced sequences

---

# Requirements

Responder typically holds:

- Six or more cards in the target major.
- Game-going values.
- A hand that already knows game is the correct contract.

Some partnerships allow a strong five-card major, although six cards is the standard requirement.

---

# Typical Hand Types

Texas Transfers are well suited for:

- Six-card heart suit.
- Six-card spade suit.
- Weak notrump hands already committed to game.
- Balanced game-going hands with a long major.
- Distributional hands without slam interest.

---

# Transfer Structure

## 4♦

Artificial.

Requests opener to bid:

```
4♥
```

---

## 4♥

Artificial.

Requests opener to bid:

```
4♠
```

---

## Opener's Responsibility

Opener simply accepts the transfer.

Example

```
1NT
4♦*
4♥
```

No choice exists unless the partnership has special agreements.

---

# Continuations

Most Texas Transfer auctions end immediately.

```
1NT
4♦*
4♥
Pass
```

or

```
1NT
4♥*
4♠
Pass
```

---

# Slam Interest

With slam interest, many partnerships prefer:

- Jacoby Transfer
- Stayman
- Quantitative bidding

rather than Texas Transfers.

Some advanced partnerships incorporate Texas Transfer into slam sequences, but these methods require detailed partnership agreements.

---

# Texas Transfer Versus Jacoby Transfer

| Texas Transfer | Jacoby Transfer |
|----------------|-----------------|
| Begins at the four level | Begins at the two level |
| Commits to game | Leaves room below game |
| Usually denies slam interest | Frequently allows slam exploration |
| Faster auction | More descriptive auction |
| Fewer decisions | Greater flexibility |

---

# Advantages

Texas Transfers:

- Keep the strong hand hidden.
- Simplify bidding.
- Reach game efficiently.
- Reduce information available to defenders.
- Prevent unnecessary bidding.

---

# Limitations

Texas Transfers:

- Consume bidding space.
- Leave little room for slam investigation.
- Require responder to know game is correct.
- Are unsuitable for invitational hands.

---

# Variations

Popular partnership agreements include:

- Standard Texas Transfers.
- Texas with slam continuations.
- Texas followed by cue bidding.
- Texas after 2NT.
- Super Texas (less common).

Every partnership should agree on the exact continuations.

---

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

The sequences described above assume an uncontested auction. When the opponents interfere after a **1NT** or **2NT** opening, Texas Transfer may no longer be available in its standard form.

Every partnership should agree in advance how interference affects transfer bids.

---

## Opponent Overcalls

Example

```
1NT   (2♠)   ?
```

Responder's priorities remain:

- Reach the correct major-suit contract.
- Preserve game whenever appropriate.
- Continue describing the hand accurately.

Many partnerships choose one of two approaches:

### Systems On

Texas Transfers remain available whenever bidding space permits.

### Systems Off

After interference, transfers are abandoned and replaced by natural or competitive methods.

---

## Opponent Doubles

Example

```
1NT   (Double)   ?
```

Partnership agreements vary.

Typical treatments include:

- Systems On.
- Systems Off.
- Pass.
- Redouble.
- Escape methods.

Every partnership should define these actions before play.

---

## Interference After the Transfer Bid

Example

```
1NT
Pass
4♦*
(Double)
?
```

or

```
1NT
Pass
4♦*
4♠
?
```

Possible partnership treatments include:

- Complete the transfer.
- Double showing values.
- Pass forcing.
- Natural continuation.

The exact treatment depends upon partnership agreement.

---

## Lebensohl and Rubensohl

Some partnerships extend **Lebensohl** or **Rubensohl** principles to auctions where transfer methods are disrupted by interference.

These conventions distinguish:

- Weak hands.
- Invitational hands.
- Game-going hands.
- Hands with or without stoppers.

See the respective articles for complete treatments.

---

## Maintaining the Transfer

When practical, the partnership should continue to:

- Place the contract in the correct major.
- Keep the stronger hand as declarer.
- Avoid revealing unnecessary information.
- Preserve game and slam opportunities whenever possible.

---

## Partnership Agreements

Every partnership should discuss:

- Systems On versus Systems Off.
- Transfers after doubles.
- Transfers after overcalls.
- Continuations after interference.
- Slam continuations.
- Escape methods.

---

## General Principles

When opponents interfere:

- Preserve the transfer whenever practical.
- Continue describing the hand accurately.
- Protect the strong notrump hand.
- Follow partnership agreements rather than improvising.
- Reevaluate the auction after every competitive action.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Using Texas With Invitational Hands

Texas Transfers commit the partnership to game.

Use Jacoby Transfers for invitational auctions.

---

## Choosing Texas With Slam Interest

Texas often consumes too much bidding space for effective slam investigation.

---

## Forgetting the Transfer

Opener should automatically complete the transfer unless the partnership has agreed exceptions.

---

## Confusing Texas and Jacoby Transfers

Remember:

- Texas begins at the **four level**.
- Jacoby begins at the **two level**.

---

## Ignoring Partnership Agreements After Interference

Every partnership should know how competitive auctions affect transfer bids.

---

# Tips

- Use Texas only when you already know that game is the correct contract.
- Prefer Jacoby Transfers when further exploration may be required.
- Keep the strong notrump hand hidden whenever possible.
- Discuss competitive continuations before playing.
- Practice both uncontested and competitive transfer auctions.

---

# Summary

Texas Transfer is a straightforward and highly effective convention that enables partnerships to reach major-suit games immediately while keeping the strong notrump hand as declarer. It is particularly valuable when responder has a long major and no need for further investigation. Because interference can disrupt transfer sequences, successful partnerships establish clear agreements regarding Systems On, Systems Off, and competitive continuations.
