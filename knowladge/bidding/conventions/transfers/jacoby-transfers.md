# Jacoby Transfers

## Overview

**Jacoby Transfers** are artificial responses to a **1NT** or **2NT** opening that allow responder to show a **five-card or longer major suit** while requesting opener to bid that suit.

The convention keeps the stronger notrump hand as declarer, making the opening lead more difficult for the defenders while preserving bidding space for game and slam exploration.

Typical auctions are:

```
1NT
2♦*
2♥
```

and

```
1NT
2♥*
2♠
```

where:

- **2♦** transfers to **hearts**
- **2♥** transfers to **spades**

The transfer bids are completely artificial.

---

# Purpose

Jacoby Transfers help the partnership:

- Locate major-suit fits.
- Keep the strong notrump hand hidden.
- Preserve bidding space.
- Improve game and slam bidding.
- Differentiate invitational, game-going and slam-oriented hands.

---

# When to Use

Jacoby Transfers are normally used after:

- 1NT opening
- 2NT opening
- Equivalent strong balanced auctions

Responder should normally hold:

- Five or more cards in the major suit.
- Any strength from weak to slam-going, depending on the continuation.

---

# Requirements

## Suit Length

Normally:

- Five-card major
- Six-card major
- Longer major suit

Some partnerships occasionally transfer with four-card suits for tactical reasons, but this is uncommon.

---

## Strength

Transfers may be used with:

### Weak Hands

Planning to pass after the transfer.

---

### Invitational Hands

Planning to invite game.

---

### Game-Going Hands

Planning to bid game.

---

### Slam Hands

Planning to investigate slam.

# Transfer Structure

## 2♦

Artificial.

Requests opener to bid:

```
2♥
```

Shows hearts.

---

## 2♥

Artificial.

Requests opener to bid:

```
2♠
```

Shows spades.

# Opener's Responsibilities

Normally opener simply accepts the transfer.

Example

```

2♦*
2♥
```

or

```

```

Only special partnership agreements allow alternative actions.

```

Responder may continue with:

# Super Accept

Many partnerships play **Super Accepts**.

Instead of simply completing the transfer, opener may show:

- Maximum strength.
- Four-card support.
- Excellent trump support.

Example

```
1NT
2♦
3♥
```

The exact meanings vary considerably.

---

# Jacoby Transfer After 2NT

Transfers apply equally after a 2NT opening.

Example

```
2NT
3♦*
3♥
```

or

```
2NT
3♥*
3♠
```

The continuations follow the same principles.

---

# Jacoby Transfer Versus Stayman

| Jacoby Transfer | Stayman |
|-----------------|----------|
| Shows a five-card major | Asks about four-card majors |
| Keeps opener declarer | Searches for a 4-4 fit |
| Works with weak through slam hands | Usually invitational or stronger |
| Begins with a transfer | Begins with an inquiry |

---

# Advantages

Jacoby Transfers:

- Keep the strong hand hidden.
- Locate major fits.
- Preserve bidding space.
- Improve game selection.
- Improve slam investigation.
- Allow weak hands to escape from notrump.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Requires partnership agreement.
- Has several continuation structures.
- May be disrupted by interference.
- Requires accurate follow-up bidding.

---

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

The auctions described above assume an uncontested **1NT** or **2NT** opening. When the opponents interfere, transfer sequences may no longer be available in their normal form.

Every partnership should agree how interference affects transfer bids before play.

---

## Opponent Overcalls

Example

```
1NT   (2♣)   ?
```

Responder's priorities remain:

- Show a five-card or longer major.
- Keep opener as declarer whenever possible.
- Reach the correct game or part-score.
- Preserve slam opportunities.

Many partnerships adopt one of two approaches.

### Systems On

Transfers remain available whenever bidding space permits.

### Systems Off

Transfers are abandoned after interference and replaced by natural or competitive methods.

---

## Opponent Doubles

Example

```
1NT   (Double)   ?
```

Partnership agreements vary considerably.

Common treatments include:

- Systems On.
- Systems Off.
- Pass.
- Redouble.
- Escape methods.
- Natural bidding.

Every partnership should discuss these continuations before play.

---

## Interference After the Transfer Bid

Example

```
1NT
Pass
2♦*
(Double)
?
```

or

```
1NT
Pass
2♦*
2♠
?
```

Possible partnership agreements include:

- Complete the transfer whenever possible.
- Double showing values.
- Pass forcing.
- Natural continuation.
- Alternative transfer completion.

The exact treatment depends on partnership agreement.

---

## Lebensohl and Rubensohl

Many partnerships employ **Lebensohl** or **Rubensohl** after interference over a notrump opening.

These conventions help distinguish:

- Weak hands.
- Invitational hands.
- Game-going hands.
- Hands with or without a stopper.

Detailed methods are discussed in the corresponding articles.

---

## Maintaining the Transfer Principle

Even after interference, the partnership should attempt to:

- Keep the stronger hand as declarer.
- Reach the correct major-suit contract.
- Preserve constructive bidding whenever practical.
- Continue exchanging useful information.

---

## Partnership Agreements

Partners should discuss:

- Systems On versus Systems Off.
- Transfers after doubles.
- Transfers after overcalls.
- Super Accept after interference.
- Slam continuations.
- Escape methods.

---

## General Principles

When opponents interfere:

- Preserve transfers whenever practical.
- Continue describing the hand accurately.
- Keep the strong notrump hand hidden whenever possible.
- Follow partnership agreements rather than improvising.
- Reevaluate the combined strength after every competitive action.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Forgetting the Transfer

Opener should normally complete the transfer automatically.

---

## Using Stayman Instead

With a five-card major, a transfer is frequently superior to Stayman.

---

## Missing a Weak Transfer

Weak hands often benefit from escaping to a long major rather than remaining in notrump.

---

## Forgetting Super Accept Agreements

Only use Super Accept methods that have been agreed with partner.

---

## Ignoring Competitive Agreements

Transfers often change after doubles and overcalls.

Know your partnership methods before playing.

---

# Tips

- Think of the transfer bid as the beginning of the auction, not the end.
- Learn the continuations after the transfer is accepted.
- Use weak transfers to escape unsuitable notrump contracts.
- Discuss Super Accepts with your partner.
- Practice competitive auctions as well as uncontested ones.

---

# Summary

Jacoby Transfers are among the most effective conventions in modern bridge. By allowing responder to show a five-card or longer major while keeping the strong notrump hand as declarer, they improve part-score, game, and slam bidding. Their flexibility allows use with weak, invitational, game-going, and slam-oriented hands. Successful partnerships supplement the basic convention with clear agreements regarding Super Accepts, competitive auctions, and continuations after interference.
