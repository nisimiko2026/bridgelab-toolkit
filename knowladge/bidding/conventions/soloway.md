# Soloway Jump Shifts

## Purpose

Soloway Jump Shifts replace the traditional strong jump shift by giving responder a much more precisely defined hand. The convention allows partnerships to investigate slam while keeping the auction low enough for accurate exploration.

Instead of simply showing a very strong hand, the jump shift promises one of a small number of well-defined hand patterns.

## When It Applies

- Opener starts with a natural one-level suit opening.
- No interference.
- Responder makes a jump shift into a new suit.

Example:

```
West    North
1♦       2♠
```

The jump shift is artificial and game forcing.

---

# Basic Requirements

Most partnerships require approximately:

- 17–18 HCP (some play 15–18 or 17+)
- Excellent suit quality
- Slam interest
- One of three specific hand types

The exact point range is partnership agreement. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

# The Three Hand Types

## 1. Strong One-Suited Hand

Responder has:

- Excellent 6+ card suit
- Slam interest
- No support for opener

After opener's relay, responder rebids the jump-shift suit.

Example

```
1♣ – 2♥
2♠ (relay)
3♥
```

Meaning:

"I have a powerful heart suit."

---

## 2. Balanced Strong Hand

Responder has:

- Balanced distribution
- Usually 18–19 HCP
- Good five-card suit

After the relay responder bids notrump.

Example

```
1♦ – 2♠
2NT (relay)
3NT
```

Meaning:

"Balanced hand with strong values."

---

## 3. Support for Opener

Responder has

- Excellent own suit
- Good support for opener
- Slam interest

After the relay:

- Raise opener's suit without shortness
- Bid a new suit to show shortness (splinter-style)

Example

```
1♠ – 3♣
3♦ (relay)
4♠
```

Meaning:

Club suit plus spade support.

Example with shortness

```
1♥ – 2♠
2NT (relay)
3♣
```

Shows

- Heart support
- Spade suit
- Club singleton or void

---

# Opener's Responsibility

The jump shift asks opener to make the cheapest available relay.

Examples

```
1♣ – 2♦
2♥
```

```
1♦ – 2♠
2NT
```

The relay gives responder room to describe the exact hand type.

---

# Continuations

Responder's rebid | Meaning
-----------------|--------
Rebid jump-shift suit | Strong one-suiter
Bid NT | Balanced hand
Raise opener | Fit without shortness
New suit | Fit with shortness (cue/splinter)

# Advantages

- Very accurate slam investigation.
- Excellent differentiation of responder's hand.
- Keeps bidding space available.
- Stronger than classical jump shifts.
- Makes later cue bidding more descriptive.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires partnership agreement.
- Traditional strong jump shift meanings disappear.
- More memory than standard methods.
- Not widely played outside advanced partnerships.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Discuss:

- Exact point range (15–18, 17–18, or 17+)
- Whether opener always relays
- Minimum suit quality
- Whether new suits after relay are splinters or cue bids
- Treatment after interference

---

# Common Mistakes

- Making Soloway with an ordinary opening hand.
- Using weak jump shifts by mistake.
- Failing to relay as opener.
- Jump shifting with two unrelated suits.
- Forgetting that the convention promises slam interest.

# Summary

Soloway Jump Shifts are an advanced replacement for traditional strong jump shifts. By restricting the jump shift to three carefully defined hand patterns—strong one-suiters, balanced powerhouse hands, and hands with excellent support for opener—they provide much more precise slam investigation while preserving valuable bidding space. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}
