# Game Invitations

## Overview

A **game invitation** is a bid that asks partner to bid game only with a maximum hand or additional playing strength. Invitational bids occupy the middle ground between signing off in part-score and committing directly to game.

Successful invitational bidding depends on accurate hand evaluation by both partners. The player making the invitation expresses uncertainty about whether the partnership has sufficient combined strength for game, while partner decides whether to accept or decline based on the information already exchanged.

---

# Objectives

An invitational bid aims to:

- Reach game when the partnership has sufficient combined strength.
- Stop safely below game when it does not.
- Allow partner to evaluate maximum and minimum hands.
- Preserve bidding space for accurate decision-making.

---

# Invitational Strength

Although exact ranges vary by system, invitational hands typically contain:

- About **10–12 HCP** opposite a minimum opening.
- Good distribution or playing strength.
- Some uncertainty about whether game is correct.

Distribution, controls, and fit frequently influence the decision as much as high-card points.

---

# Accepting an Invitation

Partner should generally accept with:

- Maximum values for the previous bid.
- Extra playing strength.
- Good distribution.
- Excellent trump support.
- Useful controls.

Example:

A 14 HCP opening with a sixth trump and useful intermediates may accept an invitation that a flat 13 HCP hand would decline.

---

# Declining an Invitation

Partner should usually decline with:

- Minimum values.
- Poor distribution.
- Wasted values.
- Weak trump support.
- Limited offensive potential.

The goal is to avoid bidding marginal games with poor chances of success.

---

# Common Invitational Auctions

Invitational auctions appear throughout natural and conventional bidding.

Examples include:

- **1NT – 2NT**
- **1♥ – 3♥** (natural limit raise)
- **1♠ – 3♠**
- **1♣ – 2NT**
- **2NT – 4NT** (quantitative invitation to slam)

Many partnerships use conventional methods that replace or refine these natural invitations.

---

# Evaluating Invitations

When deciding whether to invite or accept an invitation, consider:

- High-card points
- Distribution
- Trump fit
- Controls
- Suit quality
- Playing tricks
- Vulnerability
- Partnership style

These factors often outweigh raw point count.

---

# Invitational Bids in Modern Systems

Modern bidding systems frequently replace natural invitations with more descriptive conventions.

Examples include:

- Limit Raises
- Bergen Raises
- Jacoby 2NT
- Quantitative 4NT
- Drury
- Two Over One Game Force

These methods improve accuracy while preserving bidding space.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Inviting With Clear Game Values

If game is certain, bid game rather than invite.

---

## Inviting Without Enough Strength

Marginal hands should usually sign off rather than issue optimistic invitations.

---

## Accepting Automatically

Partner should evaluate the actual hand rather than relying solely on HCP.

---

## Ignoring Distribution

A shapely maximum often justifies accepting an invitation that a flat hand would decline.

---

# Tips

- Think in terms of combined strength.
- Evaluate playing strength as well as HCP.
- Invite only when genuinely uncertain.
- Accept invitations with extra offensive values.
- Discuss invitational ranges with your partner.

---

# Summary

Game invitations are a fundamental part of constructive bidding. They allow partnerships to distinguish between minimum and maximum hands, improving the accuracy of game decisions. Successful invitational bidding relies on careful hand evaluation, partnership agreement, and an understanding that distribution and playing strength are often as important as high-card points.
