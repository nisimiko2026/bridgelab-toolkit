# Opener Evaluation

## Purpose

Before making a Game Try, opener must answer one fundamental question:

> **Is there a reasonable possibility that game will succeed if partner has useful values?**

Making unnecessary Game Tries wastes bidding space and may encourage partner to
overbid.

Failing to make a justified Game Try may miss excellent games.

This guide explains how opener should evaluate the hand before inviting game.

---

# General Principle

After partner raises opener's major suit,

the partnership has already established

- a trump fit,
- approximately known combined strength,
- the basic level of the auction.

The remaining question is

> **Are my extra values sufficient to invite game?**

---

# The Evaluation Process

1. Reassess your opening hand.
2. Count offensive—not defensive—strength.
3. Evaluate trump fit.
4. Evaluate controls.
5. Evaluate distribution.
6. Decide whether partner needs additional information.
7. Choose the correct Game Try.

---

# Step 1 – Re-evaluate Your Opening

The hand that was merely an opening bid may become much stronger after partner
shows support.

Ask

- Have my long suits improved?
- Has my distribution become valuable?
- Do I have extra playing strength?

---

# Step 2 – Minimum, Medium or Maximum?

A simple HCP count is not enough.

### Minimum

Typical examples

- 12–13 balanced
- Poor suit quality
- No distribution

Usually sign off.

---

### Medium

Typical examples

- Good 14
- Useful shape
- Excellent trump suit

Often consider a Game Try.

---

### Maximum

Typical examples

- 15–17 HCP
- Excellent controls
- Strong offensive hand

Usually make a Game Try or bid game directly.

---

# Step 3 – Offensive Strength

Offensive values are more important than defensive values.

Upgrade

- long suits,
- strong trumps,
- controls,
- distribution,
- intermediates.

Downgrade

- scattered queens,
- flat shape,
- defensive honors.

---

# Step 4 – Trump Suit

Ask

Is my trump suit capable of producing ten tricks?

Upgrade

- six-card suit,
- excellent suit texture,
- AKQ,
- good intermediates.

Poor trump quality argues against inviting.

---

# Step 5 – Controls

Upgrade

- Aces,
- Kings,
- singleton,
- void.

Controls frequently justify a Game Try.

---

# Step 6 – Distribution

Upgrade

- singleton,
- void,
- sixth card,
- seventh card,
- double fit.

Distribution often compensates for missing HCP.

---

# Step 7 – Source of Tricks

Can your hand create additional tricks?

Examples

- Long side suit
- Ruffing potential
- Strong trump suit
- Extra controls

If not,

game may be unrealistic.

---

# Step 8 – Playing Strength

Estimate

expected tricks,

not points.

Many successful Game Tries are made with

only 13–14 HCP

but exceptional playing strength.

---

# Choosing the Right Game Try

## Need Help in One Suit

↓

Help Suit Game Try.

---

## Long Side Suit

↓

Long Suit Game Try.

---

## Shortness

↓

Short Suit Game Try.

---

## Competition

↓

Maximal Game Try.

---

## Advanced Partnership

↓

Two-Way

or

Artificial Game Try.

---

# Upgrade Factors

Upgrade

✓ Sixth trump

✓ Fifth side suit

✓ Excellent intermediates

✓ Aces

✓ Kings

✓ Singleton

✓ Void

✓ Double fit

✓ Source of tricks

---

# Downgrade Factors

Downgrade

✓ Flat hand

✓ Weak trumps

✓ Unsupported Queens

✓ Defensive honors

✓ Broken suits

✓ No source of tricks

# Losing Trick Count

If the partnership uses LTC,

combine it with

- controls,
- trump quality,
- distribution.

Never rely on LTC alone.

---

# Vulnerability

Favorable vulnerability

↓

Stretch slightly.

---

Unfavorable vulnerability

↓

Require stronger playing strength.

---

# Matchpoints versus IMPs

## Matchpoints

Invite conservatively.

Avoid thin games.

---

## IMPs

Invite aggressively.

Thin games often produce positive swings.

---

# Partnership Style

Natural partnerships

typically use

- Help Suit,
- Long Suit,
- Maximal.

---

Advanced partnerships

may employ

- Two-Way,
- Artificial,
- Artificial 2NT.

---

# Common Errors

- Counting only HCP.
- Ignoring distribution.
- Overvaluing Queens.
- Forgetting trump quality.
- Making automatic invitations with 15 HCP.
- Failing to invite with strong distribution.

---

# Practical Checklist

Before making a Game Try ask yourself:

✓ Do I have extra playing strength?

✓ Can my hand produce extra tricks?

✓ Does partner need more information?

✓ Which Game Try best describes my hand?

✓ Would game be reasonable opposite a maximum raise?

If most answers are **Yes**, a Game Try is usually appropriate.

---

# Golden Rule

> **Invite because your hand plays better than its HCP—not because it has more HCP.**

Playing strength is the foundation of successful Game Tries.

---

# Decision Flow

```
Major fit established

↓

Extra playing strength?

↓

No

↓

Sign off

↓

Yes

↓

Competition?

↓

Yes

↓

Maximal Game Try

↓

No

↓

What feature best describes my hand?

↓

Weak suit
→ Help Suit

↓

Long side suit
→ Long Suit

↓

Shortness
→ Short Suit

↓

Either long suit or shortness
→ Two-Way

↓

Artificial partnership
→ Artificial or 2NT
```

# Relationship to Responder Evaluation

Opener and responder evaluate different questions.

**Opener asks**

> "Should I invite?"

**Responder asks**

> "Should I accept?"

Understanding both evaluations is essential for accurate Game Try bidding.

---

# Summary

Making a successful Game Try begins with accurate hand evaluation.

Opener should focus on

- offensive strength,
- controls,
- trump quality,
- distribution,
- source of tricks,

rather than High Card Points alone.

The purpose of the Game Try is not merely to invite game, but to communicate
the feature that will allow responder to judge whether the partnership's
combined assets justify bidding game.

Strong partnerships consistently make better Game Try decisions because they
evaluate **playing strength**, not just point count.
