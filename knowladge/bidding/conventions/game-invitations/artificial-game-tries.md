# Artificial Game Tries (AGT)

## Definition

An **Artificial Game Try (AGT)** is a partnership agreement in which one or more bids are assigned **conventional rather than natural meanings** to investigate whether a partnership should bid game after a major-suit fit has been established.

Unlike Help Suit, Long Suit, or Short Suit Game Tries, an Artificial Game Try does **not necessarily describe a specific suit**.

Instead, it asks partner to evaluate the hand according to an agreed structure.

Artificial Game Tries represent the most advanced family of game-invitation methods used in modern bridge.

---

# Basic Philosophy

Natural game tries answer questions such as

- Can my long suit produce tricks?
- Can your honors help my weak suit?
- Does my shortness fit your hand?

Artificial Game Tries ask a different question

> **"Please evaluate your hand using our agreed partnership methods."**

The emphasis shifts from describing shape to exchanging information efficiently.

---

# Purpose

Artificial Game Tries allow partnerships to

- exchange more information,
- improve game decisions,
- reduce guesswork,
- separate different hand types,
- integrate advanced hand evaluation,
- preserve bidding space.

---

# Why Artificial Methods Were Developed

Experts discovered that natural game tries have limitations.

A single natural bid can describe only one feature

- long suit,
- short suit,
- weakness.

Many hands contain several important features simultaneously.

Artificial methods provide greater flexibility.

---

# General Principle

Artificial Game Tries usually begin with

an agreed artificial bid

followed by

one or more descriptive continuations.

The initial bid typically says

> **"Partner, let's investigate game."**

Subsequent bidding determines

- hand type,
- distribution,
- controls,
- offensive potential,
- trump quality.

---

# Artificial versus Natural

Natural methods

describe

one feature immediately.

Artificial methods

first establish

an inquiry,

then exchange further information.

This usually produces greater accuracy.

---

# Typical Artificial Bids

Different partnerships may use

```
2NT
```

or

```
2♠
```

or

another agreed relay

to begin the investigation.

The exact treatment depends entirely upon partnership agreement.

There is **no universal standard**.

---

# Relay Philosophy

Artificial methods frequently employ

relay bidding.

Example

```
2NT
```

↓

Artificial inquiry

↓

Partner replies

↓

Further clarification

Each bid provides additional information.

---

# Information That May Be Exchanged

Artificial structures may describe

- long suits,
- short suits,
- balanced hands,
- controls,
- Losing Trick Count,
- trump quality,
- side-suit strength,
- offensive values,
- distribution.

Not every system includes all of these features.

---

# Typical Objectives

Artificial Game Tries seek to

- maximize descriptive bidding,
- preserve bidding space,
- improve responder's evaluation,
- avoid poor games,
- reach thin games,
- prepare for possible slam exploration.

---

# Suitable Auctions

Artificial Game Tries are generally used after

```
1♥
2♥
```

or

```
1♠
2♠
```

when

- game is uncertain,
- the partnership has agreed a trump fit,
- additional information would improve the decision.

---

Typical opener has

- good 14 HCP,
- 15–17 HCP,
- strong offensive values,
- distributional extras,
- uncertainty regarding game.

As with all game tries,

playing strength is more important than HCP.

Artificial Game Tries should generally **not** be used with

### Weak Competitive Hands

Most partnerships replace them with **Maximal Game Try** after significant interference.

---

# Advantages

Artificial Game Tries

- Convey more information.
- Use bidding space efficiently.
- Improve hand evaluation.
- Separate different hand types.
- Integrate naturally with relay methods.
- Reduce partnership guesswork.

---

# Disadvantages

The methods also have limitations.

- Require extensive partnership discussion.
- Increase memory requirements.
- Differ widely between partnerships.
- Can be difficult for infrequent partnerships.
- Often require detailed convention-card disclosure.

---

# Relationship to Other Game Tries

Artificial Game Tries represent the most advanced stage of game-invitation development.

Natural progression is typically


Rather than replacing the earlier methods,

Artificial Game Tries often incorporate their concepts into a more flexible framework.

---

# Historical Development

As tournament bridge evolved,

expert partnerships sought methods that

- exchanged more information,
- consumed less bidding space,
- distinguished more hand types.

This led to

- relay game tries,
- artificial inquiries,
- coded responses,
- integrated hand-evaluation systems.

Today,

many international partnerships employ some form of Artificial Game Try.

---

# Key Concepts Introduced

Artificial Game Tries are built upon

- relay bidding,
- partnership agreements,
- offensive hand evaluation,
- distribution,
- controls,
- Losing Trick Count,
- working values,
- modern bidding theory.

These ideas provide the foundation for the bidding structures, continuations, responder evaluation, and advanced partnership agreements described in the following sections.

# Advanced Philosophy

Artificial Game Tries represent the highest level of natural game-invitation
development before the partnership enters fully relay-based bidding systems.

Their guiding principle is

> **"Describe the hand as efficiently as possible before committing to game."**

Rather than immediately showing

- a long suit,
- shortness,
- or a weak suit,

the partnership first establishes an artificial framework and then exchanges
precisely the information needed for the final decision.

Each stage increases

# Summary

Artificial Game Tries are a family of advanced partnership treatments that use
artificial bids and relay structures to exchange information before deciding
whether to bid game.

Unlike natural game tries, they do not immediately describe a particular suit.

Instead, they establish a framework in which partners can communicate

- strength,
- distribution,
- controls,
- offensive potential,
- hand type,

using agreed artificial responses.

These methods provide greater accuracy and flexibility than natural game tries,
but they require significantly more partnership preparation, memory, and
documentation.

For expert partnerships, Artificial Game Tries represent one of the most
powerful approaches to game invitation in modern bridge.

---

# Key Points

- Artificial Game Tries are a **family of partnership treatments**, not a single convention.
- They replace immediate natural descriptions with structured information exchange.
- Most begin with an artificial inquiry, frequently using a relay.
- They allow partners to describe multiple hand characteristics before deciding on game.
- Playing strength, distribution, controls, and trump quality remain the basis for evaluation.
- There is no universally accepted Artificial Game Try structure.
- Clear partnership agreements and convention-card disclosure are essential.
- Most partnerships suspend Artificial Game Tries after significant interference and use Maximal Game Try.
- Artificial methods integrate naturally with advanced relay and slam-investigation systems.
