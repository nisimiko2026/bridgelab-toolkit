# Josephine

## Bridge Insight

**Josephine is a grand slam convention that asks partner about the quality of the agreed trump suit rather than about aces or key cards. It is used when the partnership already knows that all key controls are present and needs only to determine whether the trump suit is strong enough for a grand slam.**

---

# Overview

**Josephine** is a specialized slam convention used after the partnership has:

- Agreed a trump suit.
- Established sufficient controls for slam.
- Determined that the remaining question is the quality of the trump suit.

Unlike **Blackwood** or **Roman Key Card Blackwood**, Josephine is **not an ace-asking convention**.

Instead, it asks partner to evaluate the top honors in the agreed trump suit.

---

# Objectives

Josephine enables the partnership to:

- Investigate a grand slam.
- Evaluate the quality of the trump suit.
- Avoid unnecessary grand slams.
- Reach excellent grand slams when the trump suit is solid.

---

# When to Use

Josephine is normally initiated by:

```text
5NT
```

after the partnership has agreed a trump suit and completed its key-card investigation.

**Important:** In partnerships using Josephine, **5NT is not a king ask**. It specifically asks about the quality of the agreed trump suit.

---

# Requirements

Before using Josephine:

- A trump suit has been agreed.
- The partnership already knows enough about key cards and controls.
- The only remaining question is whether the trump suit is sufficiently strong for a grand slam.

---

# Convention Structure

### Asking Bid

```text
5NT
```

asks:

> "How good is your trump suit?"

---

### Typical Responses

The exact response structure varies by partnership.

A common treatment is:

| Holding in Trump Suit | Suggested Action |
|------------------------|------------------|
| AKQ | Bid 7 in the agreed trump suit |
| AK or AQ | Bid 6 in the agreed trump suit |
| Lesser holding | Sign off below grand slam |

Some partnerships use step responses rather than direct contract placement.

# Partnership Agreements

The partnership should define:

- Whether 5NT is Josephine or a king ask.
- Exact response structure.
- Whether Josephine applies only after RKCB.
- Continuations after interference.
- Applicability to minor suits.

These agreements should appear on the convention card.

---

# System Variations

### Classical Josephine

5NT asks about the quality of the agreed trump suit.

---

### Grand Slam Force

Many players use the terms **Josephine** and **Grand Slam Force** interchangeably.

---

### Modified Josephine

Some partnerships use relay responses or step responses instead of direct contract placement.

---

# Advantages

Josephine:

- Focuses on the one remaining unknown.
- Excellent for grand slam decisions.
- Simple to remember.
- Avoids unnecessary grand slams.

---

# Limitations

Josephine:

- Applies only after trump agreement.
- Requires previous control investigation.
- Not universally played.
- May conflict with partnerships using 5NT as a king ask.

---

---

# Expert Thinking

Josephine recognizes that, after confirming all key controls, **the deciding factor for a grand slam is often the trump suit itself**.

A trump suit headed by **AKQ** may make a grand slam excellent, while **AQxxx** may leave the partnership with unavoidable losers despite holding every key card.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Using Josephine before key-card investigation is complete.
- Confusing Josephine with a king ask.
- Forgetting the agreed response structure.
- Asking without an agreed trump suit.
- Assuming every partnership plays Josephine.

---

# Best Practices

- Agree whether 5NT is Josephine or a king ask.
- Use only after key controls are known.
- Keep response methods simple.
- Record the agreement on the convention card.
- Practice grand slam auctions.

# Expert Tip

**Josephine should only be used when all other important information is already known.**

If you still need to ask about aces, key cards, or controls, use Blackwood, RKCB, Kickback, or Redwood first. Josephine is a finishing tool for deciding whether the trump suit itself justifies bidding a grand slam.

---

# Memory Aid

**Josephine = "Show Me Your Trumps."**

Remember:

- It is a **grand slam** convention.
- It is **not** an ace-asking or key-card-asking method.
- The focus is entirely on the **quality of the agreed trump suit**.
- Many partnerships use **Josephine** and **Grand Slam Force** as synonymous terms.
- The exact responses should always be defined by partnership agreement.
