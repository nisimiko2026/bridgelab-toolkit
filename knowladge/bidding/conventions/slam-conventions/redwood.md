# Redwood

## Bridge Insight

**Redwood is a specialized Roman Key Card Blackwood treatment for minor-suit contracts. By replacing 4NT with a suit bid as the key-card ask, the partnership preserves valuable bidding space for investigating minor-suit slams without sacrificing natural bidding sequences.**

---

# Overview

**Redwood** is a variation of **Roman Key Card Blackwood (RKCB)** used **only after a minor suit (Clubs or Diamonds) has been agreed as trumps**.

Instead of using **4NT** as the key-card ask, Redwood uses:

- **4♦** to ask for key cards when **Clubs** are trumps.
- **4♥** to ask for key cards when **Diamonds** are trumps.

The response structure is identical to the partnership's agreed **RKCB** method (1430 or 3014).

---

# Objectives

Redwood enables the partnership to:

- Save bidding space after agreeing a minor suit.
- Investigate minor-suit slams more accurately.
- Preserve room for queen and king asks.
- Avoid premature five-level bidding.
- Integrate naturally with Roman Key Card Blackwood.

---

# When to Use

A minor suit has already been agreed.

### Clubs agreed

```text
1♣      3♣

4♦
```

4♦ is Redwood.

---

### Diamonds agreed

```text
1♦      3♦

4♥
```

4♥ is Redwood.

---

# Requirements

Before using Redwood:

- Clubs or Diamonds have been explicitly agreed as trumps.
- Partnership plays Redwood.
- Partnership has agreed RKCB responses.
- Slam investigation is appropriate.

---

# Convention Structure

## Asking Bids

| Trump Suit | Redwood Ask |
|------------|-------------|
| ♣ Clubs | 4♦ |
| ♦ Diamonds | 4♥ |

These bids replace **4NT** as the key-card ask.

---

## Responses

Redwood uses the partnership's standard RKCB response method.

### 1430 Example

| Response | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| First step | 1 or 4 key cards |
| Second step | 3 or 0 key cards |
| Third step | 2 without the trump queen |
| Fourth step | 2 with the trump queen |

Partnerships playing **3014** simply substitute their normal response structure.

# Continuations

After the response, opener may:

- Ask for the trump queen.
- Ask for specific kings.
- Cue bid remaining controls.
- Place the final contract.

These continuations follow the partnership's normal RKCB agreements.

---

# Partnership Agreements

The partnership should define:

- Whether Redwood is always used after minor-suit agreement.
- Whether responses follow 1430 or 3014.
- Queen-asking methods.
- King-asking methods.
- Treatment after interference.

These agreements should appear on the convention card.

# Advantages

Redwood:

- Saves bidding space.
- Improves minor-suit slam bidding.
- Preserves room for follow-up asks.
- Integrates seamlessly with RKCB.
- Easy to remember.

---

# Limitations

Redwood:

- Applies only to minor suits.
- Requires firm trump agreement.
- Requires partnership agreement.
- May conflict with natural control bids in some systems.

# Expert Thinking

Minor-suit slams often require more bidding space than major-suit slams because game already begins at the five level.

Redwood solves this problem by moving the key-card ask below 4NT, allowing the partnership to exchange more information before committing to the six level.

Many partnerships that prefer simple methods adopt Redwood rather than full Kickback because it improves minor-suit auctions without changing major-suit methods.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Using Redwood before a minor suit has been agreed.
- Confusing Redwood with Kickback.
- Forgetting the agreed RKCB response structure.
- Treating 4♦ or 4♥ as natural.
- Mixing Redwood and standard RKCB without agreement.

---

# Best Practices

- Use Redwood only after agreeing Clubs or Diamonds.
- Keep the same RKCB responses (1430 or 3014).
- Practice queen and king asks.
- Record Redwood on the convention card.
- Agree how interference is handled.

# Expert Tip

**Think of Redwood as "Minor-Suit Kickback."**

If your partnership uses Redwood, **only minor-suit slams change**. Major-suit auctions continue to use standard RKCB (or Kickback, if you also play it). This makes Redwood easier to adopt than full Kickback while capturing most of the bidding-space advantage in the auctions where it matters most.

---

# Memory Aid

**Redwood = "Red Suit Asks for Minor."**

Remember:

- **4♦** asks when **Clubs** are trumps.
- **4♥** asks when **Diamonds** are trumps.
- Responses are exactly the same as your normal **RKCB** method.
- Redwood is a specialized improvement for **minor-suit slam bidding**.
