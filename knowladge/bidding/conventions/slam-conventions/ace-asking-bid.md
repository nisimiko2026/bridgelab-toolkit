# Ace-Asking Bid (AAB)

## Purpose

An **Ace-Asking Bid (AAB)** is an artificial bid used during **slam investigation** to determine whether the partnership holds enough aces (or key cards) to bid a slam safely.

The partnership normally uses an Ace-Asking Bid only after:

- A fit has been established (or notrump is the intended strain)
- Game is certain
- The partnership is exploring slam

Ace-Asking methods are among the most widely played slam conventions in bridge.

---

# What Is an Ace-Asking Bid?

An Ace-Asking Bid asks partner to describe:

- The **number of aces**, or
- The **number of key cards**, or
- Occasionally the **location** of specific aces

The exact information depends on the convention being used.

---

# Typical Uses

Ace-Asking Bids help determine:

- Whether two quick losers exist.
- Whether a small slam is safe.
- Whether a grand slam should be considered.
- Whether additional investigation is required.

---

# Common Ace-Asking Conventions

## Blackwood

Asks for the number of **aces**.

Typical responses:

| Response | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
|5♣|0 or 4 aces|
|5♦|1 ace|
|5♥|2 aces|
|5♠|3 aces|

---

## Roman Key Card Blackwood (RKCB)

Asks for **five key cards**:

- Four aces
- The king of the agreed trump suit

Many versions also locate the trump queen.

---

## Exclusion Blackwood

Asks for key cards **excluding** the ace of the suit in which responder is void.

Used after a jump showing a void.

---

## Gerber

Uses **4♣** to ask for aces after a notrump auction.

---

# When to Use

Use an Ace-Asking Bid only when:

- The partnership already knows the trump suit (or intends to play notrump).
- Further information about aces or key cards will determine whether to bid slam.

Avoid using ace asks before agreeing the trump suit unless your partnership has explicit agreements.

---

# Advantages

- Quickly counts missing aces or key cards.
- Prevents poor slams with two immediate losers.
- Simple and widely understood.
- Compatible with many bidding systems.

---

# Disadvantages

- Does not identify where missing controls are located.
- Can recommend a poor slam when controls are poorly placed.
- May be inferior to cue bidding in some auctions.

# Summary

An **Ace-Asking Bid** is an artificial slam-investigation method that asks partner to disclose aces or key cards before committing to a slam. Modern partnerships usually employ **Roman Key Card Blackwood** rather than traditional Blackwood, while specialized methods such as **Gerber** and **Exclusion Blackwood** are used in particular auction types.
