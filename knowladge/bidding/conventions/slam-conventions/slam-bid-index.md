# Slam Bidding

> *Reaching excellent slams while avoiding poor ones.*

Slam bidding is one of the most rewarding—and demanding—aspects of Contract
Bridge. Successful slam auctions require partnerships to exchange precise
information about strength, controls, distribution, trump support, and honor
location while preserving enough bidding space to investigate safely.

This section of **BridgeLab** covers the principles, conventions, and expert
methods used to bid small and grand slams accurately.

---

# Introduction

A slam contract requires

- twelve tricks (Small Slam), or
- all thirteen tricks (Grand Slam).

Unlike game bidding, slam bidding depends less on raw high-card points and more
on

- controls,
- trump fit,
- distribution,
- honor location,
- partnership evaluation.

Successful slam bidding minimizes guesswork through structured information
exchange.

---

# Objectives

Slam bidding seeks to

- identify suitable slam opportunities,
- avoid poor slams,
- locate controls,
- evaluate trump quality,
- investigate key cards,
- determine honor location,
- maximize scoring potential.

# Fundamental Principles

Successful slam bidding depends upon

- sufficient controls,
- a reliable trump fit,
- efficient communication,
- accurate hand evaluation,
- partnership agreements,
- disciplined judgment.

---

# Hand Evaluation

Before investigating slam, evaluate

- High-card points
- Controls
- Distribution
- Trump support
- Suit quality
- Working honors
- Playing tricks
- Entry structure

Controls generally outweigh raw point count.

---

# Trump Fit

Most slams require

- an eight-card fit,
- solid trumps,
- adequate controls,
- sufficient entries.

Notrump slams require exceptional combined strength and communication.

---

# Control Evaluation

Expert partnerships often evaluate

- first-round controls,
- second-round controls,
- wasted values,
- duplication of honors,

before considering ace-asking conventions.

# Common Mistakes

- Asking for aces too early.
- Ignoring trump quality.
- Overvaluing unsupported honors.
- Confusing controls with points.
- Failing to evaluate distribution.
- Using conventions without complete agreements.

---

# Matchpoints versus IMPs

## Matchpoints

- Bid only high-percentage slams.
- Avoid speculative contracts.
- Overtricks may be significant.

---

## IMPs

- Bid sound slams aggressively.
- Thin slams are often worthwhile.
- Accurate control evaluation is essential.
