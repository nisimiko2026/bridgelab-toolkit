# Slam Trial Bids

## Definition

A **slam trial bid** is any bid whose primary purpose is to **investigate the possibility of a small slam (6-level) or grand slam (7-level)** after the partnership has already established a likely game contract.

Unlike game invitations, slam trial bids assume that **game is virtually certain**. The remaining question is whether the combined hands have sufficient values, controls, and fitting cards to justify bidding slam.

A slam trial bid is **not a single convention**. Rather, it is a broad class of bidding actions used in virtually every bidding system.

---

# Objectives

A slam trial bid aims to:

- Determine whether slam is practical.
- Exchange information beyond basic hand strength.
- Identify controls and losers.
- Evaluate the quality of the trump fit.
- Stop safely in game when slam is unsuitable.
- Reach good slams while avoiding poor ones.

---

# When Slam Exploration Begins

Slam investigation generally starts after:

- A trump fit has been agreed.
- Game is assured.
- Both partners have shown enough strength.

Examples:

```
1♠
3♠
```

or

```
1♥
2NT (Jacoby)
```

or

```
1♦
3♦
```

At this stage, bidding may continue for slam investigation rather than game invitation.

---

# Requirements for Slam Interest

A partnership typically considers slam when it has:

- Approximately 33+ combined HCP, or
- Exceptional distribution,
- Strong fitting values,
- Extra controls,
- Excellent trump support,
- Good playing strength.

Distribution and controls often outweigh raw point count.

---

# Types of Slam Trial Bids

Slam investigation can involve many different methods.

## Cue Bids

The most common form.

Partners show first- or second-round controls.

Example:

```
1♠
3♠
4♣
```

4♣ shows club control.

---

## Control Bids

Some partnerships distinguish:

- First-round controls (Ace or void)
- Second-round controls (King or singleton)

These bids help determine whether quick losers exist.

---

## Serious 3NT

After a major suit fit:

```
1♥
3♥
3NT
```

3NT may show serious slam interest.

---

## Non-Serious 3NT

The opposite agreement.

3NT denies serious slam interest while allowing partner to continue.

---

## Splinter Bids

A splinter both:

- Shows trump support.
- Shows shortness.
- Suggests slam interest.

Example:

```
1♠
4♣
```

Showing club shortness with spade support.

---

## Keycard Asking Bids

Examples include:

- Roman Keycard Blackwood
- Blackwood
- Kickback
- Redwood
- Minorwood

These determine whether essential keycards are present.

---

## Grand Slam Force

A specialized method for determining whether all top trumps are held before bidding seven.

---

## Last Train

A bid that says:

> "I still have slam interest but no suitable control bid."

Widely used by expert partnerships.

---

# Evaluation During Slam Investigation

Partners evaluate:

## Controls

Aces

Kings

Voids

Singletons

---

## Trump Quality

Solid trump suits greatly improve slam chances.

Example:

```
AKQxx
```

is much stronger than

```
QJxxx
```

---

## Distribution

Extra tricks often come from:

- Voids
- Singletons
- Long suits

rather than high-card points alone.

---

## Wasted Values

Example:

```
Partner:
♠AKQxx

You:
♠Jxx
```

The jack adds relatively little.

By contrast:

```
Partner:
♣AKQxx

You:
♣Jxx
```

may provide a useful extra trick.

---

## Working Honors

Honors that fit partner's hand are more valuable than duplicated honors.

---

# General Slam Investigation Sequence

A common progression is:

1. Agree trump.
2. Exchange cue bids.
3. Confirm controls.
4. Ask for keycards if appropriate.
5. Ask for trump queen if needed.
6. Ask for kings if appropriate.
7. Decide between:
   - Game
   - Small slam
   - Grand slam

---

# Example 1

```
South      North

1♠
3♠
4♣
4♦
4NT
5♥
6♠
```

Meaning

- Spades agreed.
- Cue bids.
- Keycard inquiry.
- Small slam reached.

# Example 2

```
1♥
2NT
3♣
3♦
3NT
4♣
4NT
5♦
6♥
```

Jacoby sequence leading to slam.

# Example 3

```
1♦
3♦
4♣
4♦
Pass
```

Partners investigate but stop safely in game.

Good slam bidding includes knowing **when not to bid slam**.

---

# When to Stop

Reasons to stop in game include:

- Missing two keycards.
- Missing trump queen.
- Two fast losers.
- Poor trump quality.
- Misfitting distribution.
- Lack of controls.

Excellent partnerships stop frequently.

---

# Common Agreements

Partnerships should define:

- Cue bidding style.
- First-round vs second-round controls.
- Serious 3NT.
- Last Train.
- Keycard responses.
- Queen asks.
- King asks.
- Grand slam methods.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Asking Too Early

Do not use Blackwood before:

- agreeing trump, or
- knowing whether slam is realistic.

---

## Counting Only HCP

Many slams succeed because of:

- distribution,
- controls,
- fit,

rather than point count.

---

## Ignoring Wasted Honors

Poor-fitting honors often contribute little.

---

## Using Blackwood Without Controls

Blackwood cannot compensate for poor control bidding.

A partnership may discover enough aces but still have two immediate losers.

---

## Forgetting Partnership Agreements

Many expert methods differ regarding:

- cue bidding,
- Serious 3NT,
- Last Train,
- Kickback,
- exclusion bids.

---

# Expert Principles

Experts generally:

- Agree trump early.
- Exchange controls efficiently.
- Avoid unnecessary keycard asks.
- Evaluate fitting values carefully.
- Stop below slam whenever evidence is insufficient.

The goal is not to bid every possible slam, but to bid **good slams consistently** while avoiding poor ones.

---

# Relationship to Other Conventions

Slam trial bidding frequently incorporates:

- Splinter Bids
- Cue Bids
- Control Bids
- Blackwood
- Roman Keycard Blackwood (RKCB)
- Kickback
- Redwood
- Minorwood
- Exclusion Blackwood
- Serious 3NT
- Non-Serious 3NT
- Last Train
- Grand Slam Force
- Josephine

These methods complement one another and are selected according to the partnership's agreed system.

---

# Key Points

- Slam trial bids investigate slam after game is essentially assured.
- They are a family of bidding methods rather than a single convention.
- Trump agreement usually precedes serious slam exploration.
- Controls, distribution, trump quality, and fitting values are often more important than high-card points.
- Good slam bidding requires both reaching excellent slams and stopping safely in game when the evidence is insufficient.
