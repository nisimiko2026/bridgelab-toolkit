# Roman Key Card Blackwood (RKCB)

## Overview

**Roman Key Card Blackwood (RKCB)** is the modern successor to the classic Blackwood convention. Instead of asking only for the four Aces, RKCB asks for the **five key cards**:

- ♠ Ace
- ♥ Ace
- ♦ Ace
- ♣ Ace
- King of the agreed trump suit

By including the trump King, RKCB provides a far more accurate picture of slam potential than standard Blackwood.

Today, RKCB is the **standard Ace-asking convention** used by expert partnerships worldwide.

---

# Purpose

RKCB is designed to:

- Count the five key cards.
- Determine whether slam is safe.
- Locate the trump Queen.
- Investigate grand slams.
- Improve slam accuracy.

---

# When to Use

RKCB is normally used when:

- A trump suit has been agreed.
- The partnership has already established game.
- Cue bidding is complete.
- Only the number of key cards remains unknown.

Example:

```
1♠
3♠
4NT*
```

The **4NT** bid is artificial.

---

# The Five Key Cards

The five key cards are:

- Ace of Clubs
- Ace of Diamonds
- Ace of Hearts
- Ace of Spades
- King of the agreed trump suit

Example:

If **Hearts** are trumps:

Key cards are:

- ♣A
- ♦A
- ♥A
- ♠A
- ♥K

---

# Two Popular Versions

Two response structures are widely played.

## RKCB 1430

The most popular modern version.

| Response | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| 5♣ | 1 or 4 Key Cards |
| 5♦ | 0 or 3 Key Cards |
| 5♥ | 2 Key Cards without the trump Queen |
| 5♠ | 2 Key Cards with the trump Queen |

---

## RKCB 3014

The original Roman structure.

| Response | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| 5♣ | 0 or 3 Key Cards |
| 5♦ | 1 or 4 Key Cards |
| 5♥ | 2 without the trump Queen |
| 5♠ | 2 with the trump Queen |

Today, **1430** is considerably more common.

---

# Why 1430?

The 1430 structure has two advantages:

- One key card is shown more economically.
- It leaves more room below the six-level for slam exploration.

For these reasons, it has become the preferred expert treatment.

# Asking for the Trump Queen

After learning the number of key cards, the captain may ask about the trump Queen.

Example:

```
4NT
5♦
5♥*
```

The cheapest available bid that is **not the agreed trump suit** asks:

> "Do you have the trump Queen?"

Typical responses:

- Lowest step = No.
- Return to trump = No.
- Cheapest new suit = Yes, without side King.
- Higher suit = Yes, showing a side King.

Several response methods exist.

---

# Showing Side Kings

After confirming the trump Queen, opener may also identify side Kings.

Typical structure:

| Response | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| Cheapest suit | Cheapest King |
| Next suit | Next King |
| 5NT | Two Kings |
| 6 of trumps | No side Kings |

Partnership agreement is essential.

---

# Asking for Kings

Some partnerships continue with:

```
5NT
```

asking:

> "How many useful Kings?"

Others use **5NT** as a grand slam invitation.

This must be agreed beforehand.

---

# Opener's Continuations

After responding to RKCB opener simply answers the inquiry.

Responder then decides whether to:

- Sign off.
- Bid small slam.
- Investigate a grand slam.
- Continue with control bidding.

---

# When NOT to Use RKCB

Avoid RKCB when:

- Trump has not been agreed.
- Side-suit controls remain uncertain.
- Distribution has not been clarified.
- Two fast losers are still possible.

RKCB is the **last step** of slam investigation—not the first.

---

# RKCB vs Blackwood

| RKCB | Blackwood |
|-------|-----------|
| Counts five key cards | Counts four Aces |
| Includes trump King | Ignores trump King |
| Asks about trump Queen | No Queen inquiry |
| More accurate | Simpler |
| Modern standard | Historical method |

---

# RKCB vs Gerber

| RKCB | Gerber |
|-------|---------|
| Uses 4NT | Uses 4♣ |
| Suit contracts | Notrump auctions |
| Five key cards | Four Aces |

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

When opponents interfere over RKCB, special agreements replace normal responses.

---

## DOPI

If the opponents interfere below the expected response:

```
4NT*
(5♣)
?
```

Typical meanings:

- Double = 0 or 3 Key Cards.
- Pass = 1 or 4 Key Cards.

---

## ROPI

If the opponents double:

```
4NT*
(Double)
?
```

Typical meanings:

- Redouble = 0 or 3 Key Cards.
- Pass = 1 or 4 Key Cards.

---

## DEPO

Some partnerships instead use:

- Double = Even.
- Pass = Odd.

This applies after high-level interference.

---

## Slam Investigation

After learning the key cards, continue evaluating:

- Trump quality.
- Side-suit controls.
- Distribution.
- Source of tricks.

Missing one key card does not always prevent slam.

Likewise, holding all five key cards does not guarantee success.

---

## Partnership Agreements

Partners should define:

- DOPI or DEPO.
- ROPI.
- Queen Ask.
- Side King responses.
- Void-showing methods.
- Kickback interaction.

---

## General Principles

When using RKCB:

- Agree trumps first.
- Exchange cue bids before asking.
- Count controls, not HCP.
- Remember that distribution still matters.
- Treat RKCB as the final confirmation.

---

# Advantages

RKCB:

- More accurate than Blackwood.
- Includes the trump King.
- Locates the trump Queen.
- Improves grand slam bidding.
- Standard expert treatment.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Requires detailed partnership agreements.
- Does not show voids.
- Does not replace cue bidding.
- Can become complicated after interference.

---

---

# Common Mistakes

## Using RKCB Before Agreeing Trumps

This is the most frequent error.

---

## Forgetting Which Version You Play

Confusing **1430** and **3014** is disastrous.

---

## Skipping Cue Bids

RKCB should usually follow control bidding—not replace it.

---

## Asking with Two Fast Losers

Key cards alone do not guarantee slam.

---

## Confusing Quantitative 4NT with RKCB

Quantitative 4NT invites slam.

RKCB asks for key cards.

---

# Tips

- Learn **1430** unless your partnership already plays **3014**.
- Always confirm the agreed trump suit before bidding 4NT.
- Practice Queen Ask responses thoroughly.
- Discuss DOPI and ROPI before tournament play.
- Think of RKCB as the **final confirmation** after cue bidding.

---

# Modern Usage

Roman Key Card Blackwood is the **worldwide standard** for Ace asking.

It is played in virtually every modern bidding system:

- Standard American
- 2/1 Game Force
- SAYC
- Acol
- Precision
- Polish Club

Most expert partnerships use **RKCB 1430** together with:

- Cue Bids
- Splinters
- Jacoby 2NT
- Kickback
- Exclusion Blackwood

---

# Summary

Roman Key Card Blackwood (RKCB) is the modern evolution of the Blackwood convention. By counting the **four Aces plus the King of the agreed trump suit**, and by allowing further inquiries about the **trump Queen** and **side Kings**, it provides a much more accurate evaluation of slam potential than classic Blackwood. Combined with Cue Bids, Splinters, Jacoby 2NT, and Control Bids, RKCB forms the cornerstone of modern slam bidding and is considered essential for advanced partnerships.
