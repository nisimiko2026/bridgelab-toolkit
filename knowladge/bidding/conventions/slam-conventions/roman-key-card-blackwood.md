## Roman Key Card Blackwood (RKCB)

## Bridge Insight

**Roman Key Card Blackwood (RKCB) extends the Blackwood concept by recognizing that the trump king is just as important as the four aces. Instead of asking for four aces, RKCB asks for the five key cards needed to make a slam safe.**

---

# Overview

**Roman Key Card Blackwood (RKCB)** is an artificial slam-investigation convention that asks partner for the number of **key cards** held.

The five key cards are:

- The four aces.
- The king of the agreed trump suit.

By including the trump king, RKCB provides a much more accurate evaluation of slam safety than classical Blackwood.

Today RKCB is the standard ace-asking convention for most tournament partnerships.

---

# Objectives

RKCB enables the partnership to:

- Count the five key cards.
- Avoid missing two key cards.
- Determine whether the trump queen is held.
- Improve both small slam and grand slam bidding.
- Replace classical Blackwood with a more accurate method.

---

# When to Use

Typical auction:

```text
1♠      3♠
4NT
```

or

```text
1♥      4♥
4NT
```

Before using RKCB the partnership should already have:

- Agreed a trump suit.
- Determined that slam is a realistic possibility.

---

# Requirements

Before bidding RKCB:

- Trump suit agreed.
- Serious slam interest.
- Partnership agreement on responses.
- Agreement whether 1430 or 3014 responses are used.

---

# Convention Structure

## The Five Key Cards

- ♠A
- ♥A
- ♦A
- ♣A
- King of the agreed trump suit

---

## Standard 1430 Responses

| Response | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| 5♣ | 1 or 4 key cards |
| 5♦ | 0 or 3 key cards |
| 5♥ | 2 key cards without the trump queen |
| 5♠ | 2 key cards with the trump queen |

*(Many partnerships instead use the 3014 structure. See System Variations.)*

---

# Complete Auction Examples

## Example 1 – Small Slam

```text
West      East

1♠        3♠
4NT       5♥
6♠
```

Responder learns that opener has two key cards but lacks the trump queen. Combined assets are still sufficient for slam.

---

## Example 2 – Stop in Game

```text
West      East

1♥        4♥
4NT       5♦
5♥
```

The partnership discovers too many key cards are missing and wisely stops in game.

---

## Example 3 – Grand Slam Investigation

```text
West      East

1♠        4♠
4NT       5♠
5NT
```

After confirming all necessary key cards and the trump queen, the partnership investigates a grand slam.

---

# Continuations

The key-card response rarely ends the auction.

## Missing Two Key Cards

Responder should normally sign off below slam.

---

## Missing One Key Card

Responder evaluates:

- Trump quality.
- Side controls.
- Distribution.
- Source of tricks.

A small slam is frequently correct.

---

## All Key Cards Present

Responder may continue with:

- Queen Ask.
- King Ask.
- Grand Slam Force.
- Cue Bidding.

---

# Queen Ask

After learning the number of key cards, responder may ask about the **trump queen**.

Typical method:

The cheapest available bid other than the trump suit asks:

> "Do you hold the trump queen?"

Typical responses:

- Cheapest step = No.
- Return to trump suit = No.
- Higher suit = Yes, with side king(s).

Exact methods vary by partnership.

---

# King Ask

After confirming:

- All five key cards.
- Trump queen.

Responder may bid:

```text
5NT
```

asking for kings.

Partnerships should agree the exact response structure.

# RKCB versus Blackwood

| Blackwood | RKCB |
|------------|------|
| Four aces | Five key cards |
| Ignores trump king | Includes trump king |
| Less accurate | More accurate |
| Historical standard | Modern standard |

# Expert Thinking

Expert partnerships do not use RKCB simply because they are interested in slam.

They ask only after:

- Establishing a trump fit.
- Exchanging control information.
- Evaluating distribution.

RKCB is the **confirmation step**, not the exploratory step.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Using RKCB without agreeing trumps.
- Forgetting whether the partnership plays 1430 or 3014.
- Asking when holding a void.
- Ignoring control bidding.
- Forgetting Queen Ask agreements.

---

# Best Practices

- Agree the response structure before playing.
- Learn Queen Ask continuations.
- Record all agreements on the convention card.
- Use cue bidding before RKCB.
- Practice common slam sequences.

# AI Summary

**Purpose**

Determine how many key cards partner holds before committing to slam.

**Artificial Bid**

```
4NT
```

**Key Cards**

- Four aces.
- Trump king.

**Continuation**

- Evaluate missing key cards.
- Ask about the trump queen.
- Ask about kings.
- Decide between game, small slam, or grand slam.

---

# Expert Tip

**RKCB should usually be the last question—not the first.**

Before asking about key cards, strong partnerships first establish:

- The trump suit.
- Controls.
- Distribution.
- Overall slam potential.

RKCB confirms whether the partnership has the essential controls needed to bid slam safely.

---

# Memory Aid

**RKCB = "Five Keys to Slam."**

Remember:

- Four aces plus the trump king are the five key cards.
- Use RKCB only after agreeing trumps.
- Learn whether your partnership plays **1430** or **3014**.
- Follow the key-card response with the appropriate Queen Ask or King Ask when necessary.
- RKCB is the modern standard because it evaluates the partnership's combined controls more accurately than classical Blackwood.
