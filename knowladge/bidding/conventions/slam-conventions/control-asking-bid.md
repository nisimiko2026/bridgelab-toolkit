# Control-Asking Bid (CAB)

## Purpose

A **Control-Asking Bid (CAB)** is an artificial bid used during **slam investigation** to determine partner's control holdings.

Unlike **Blackwood** or **Roman Key Card Blackwood**, which primarily count key cards, a Control-Asking Bid identifies **where** the partnership's controls are located.

The convention is most commonly found in:

- Precision Club
- Blue Team Club
- Symmetric Relay
- MOSCITO
- Other strong club systems

---

# What Is a Control?

A control is a holding that prevents the opponents from immediately taking two tricks in a suit.

Typical controls are:

- Ace
- King
- Singleton
- Void

Depending on partnership style, only first-round controls or both first- and second-round controls may be shown.

---

# When It Is Used

Control-Asking Bids normally occur after:

- A trump suit has been agreed.
- Game is certain.
- The partnership begins slam exploration.

Example:

```
1♣
1♦
1♥
3♥
4♣  (Control Ask)
```

The exact asking bid depends on the system.

---

# Common Response Methods

Several methods are used.

## Step Responses

Responder answers by steps.

Example:

| Step | Meaning |
|------|---------|
|1st Step|0–1 controls|
|2nd Step|2 controls|
|3rd Step|3 controls|
|4th Step|4 controls|

---

## Suit-by-Suit Controls

Some systems ask specifically about one suit.

Example:

```
4♦
```

asks:

"Do you control diamonds?"

Responses distinguish:

- First-round control
- Second-round control
- No control

---

## Italian Control Bidding

Rather than asking directly, partners naturally show:

- First-round control
- Then second-round control

This is cooperative rather than asking.

---

# Difference from Blackwood

| Blackwood | Control Ask |
|------------|-------------|
|Counts Aces or Key Cards|Identifies specific controls|
|Less descriptive|More descriptive|
|Simple|More memory-intensive|
|Widely played|Mostly expert systems|

---

# Advantages

- Extremely accurate slam investigation
- Locates weak suits
- Identifies missing controls
- Helps avoid poor slams
- Integrates well with relay systems

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires extensive partnership agreements
- Many published methods exist
- More memory-intensive than RKCB

---

# Partnership Agreements

Discuss:

- What constitutes a control.
- Step meanings.
- Whether Kings count.
- First- versus second-round controls.
- Suit-specific asks.
- Interaction with cue bidding.

---

# Variations

- Blue Team Control Ask
- Precision Control Ask
- Relay Control Ask
- Step Control Ask
- CAB after Symmetric Relay

Each uses different response structures.

# Summary

A **Control-Asking Bid** is an artificial slam-investigation tool that requests partner to describe specific control holdings rather than merely counting key cards. It provides more detailed information than Blackwood and is most commonly used in advanced relay and strong club systems.
