# Blackwood Convention

## Bridge Insight

**Blackwood is not a convention for deciding whether to bid slam—it is a convention for deciding whether a slam is safe after the partnership has already agreed that slam is a serious possibility.**

---

# Overview

The **Blackwood Convention** is an artificial ace-asking convention used after the partnership has established a fit or otherwise determined that slam is a realistic objective.

Responder bids **4NT** to ask partner how many aces they hold. The answer enables the partnership to judge whether important controls are missing before committing to a slam.

Although modern partnerships often prefer **Roman Key Card Blackwood (RKCB)**, classical Blackwood remains an important convention and the foundation upon which later ace-asking methods were developed.

---

# Objectives

Blackwood enables the partnership to:

- Count the partnership's aces.
- Avoid missing two aces in slam.
- Improve small slam bidding.
- Provide a simple and reliable ace inquiry.
- Form the basis for further slam investigation.

---

# When to Use

Typical auction:

```text
1♥      3♥
4NT
```

or

```text
1♠      4♠
4NT
```

The partnership should already know:

- The likely trump suit.
- That slam is under consideration.

Blackwood is **not** intended to determine whether a fit exists.

---

# Requirements

Before using Blackwood:

- A fit should normally be agreed.
- Slam should already be under consideration.
- Neither partner should have shown severe weakness.
- Partnership agreements should be understood.

---

# Convention Structure

## Basic Responses

| Response | Number of Aces |
|----------|----------------|
| 5♣ | 0 or 4 |
| 5♦ | 1 |
| 5♥ | 2 |
| 5♠ | 3 |

---

# Complete Auction Examples

## Example 1 – Small Slam

```text
West      East

1♠        3♠
4NT       5♦
6♠
```

Responder learns the partnership is missing only one ace and bids the small slam.

---

## Example 2 – Stop Below Slam

```text
West      East

1♥        4♥
4NT       5♥
5♥
```

The partnership is missing two aces and wisely avoids slam.

---

## Example 3 – Grand Slam Investigation

```text
West      East

1♠        4♠
4NT       5♣
5NT
```

After confirming all four aces are held, the partnership investigates a grand slam.

---

# Continuations

The ace response is only the beginning of the slam auction.

## Missing Two Aces

Responder normally signs off below slam.

Example:

```text
4NT
5♥
Pass
```

---

## Missing One Ace

Responder evaluates:

- Trump quality.
- Side kings.
- Distribution.
- Controls.

Small slam may still be excellent.

---

## All Aces Present

Responder may continue with:

- 5NT (King Ask)
- Grand Slam Force
- Cue Bidding
- Control Bidding

---

# King Ask

Once all aces are accounted for, responder may bid:

```text
5NT
```

asking for the number of kings.

Typical responses:

| Response | Kings |
|----------|-------|
| 6♣ | 0 or 4 |
| 6♦ | 1 |
| 6♥ | 2 |
| 6♠ | 3 |

(Some partnerships use different structures.)

---

# When NOT to Use Blackwood

Blackwood should generally be avoided when:

- No trump suit has been agreed.
- A void exists.
- Slam interest is uncertain.
- The partnership needs more information about controls.

In these situations, cue bidding or control bidding is often superior.

---

# Blackwood versus RKCB

| Blackwood | RKCB |
|-----------|------|
| Counts aces | Counts key cards |
| Ignores trump king | Includes trump king |
| Simpler | More accurate |
| Older method | Modern standard |

Most tournament partnerships now prefer RKCB.

---

# Partnership Agreements

The partnership should agree:

- When Blackwood is permitted.
- Whether 4NT is always Blackwood.
- How King Ask works.
- Whether 5NT asks for kings.
- How interference is handled.
- Whether DOPI/ROPI apply.

These agreements should appear on the convention card.

# Expert Thinking

Blackwood should answer **only one question**:

> "Are we missing two aces?"

It should **not** be used to discover whether slam is a good idea in the first place.

Expert partnerships investigate:

- Controls.
- Fit quality.
- Distribution.

before asking about aces.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Using Blackwood before agreeing trumps.
- Asking with a void.
- Confusing Blackwood with RKCB.
- Forgetting the response scale.
- Treating Blackwood as a slam invitation.

---

# Best Practices

- Agree trumps first.
- Use cue bidding before Blackwood.
- Learn RKCB after mastering Blackwood.
- Record partnership agreements.
- Review continuations regularly.

# AI Summary

**Purpose**

Determine the number of aces held by partner before committing to slam.

**Artificial Bid**

```text
4NT
```

**Responses**

- 5♣ = 0 or 4
- 5♦ = 1
- 5♥ = 2
- 5♠ = 3

**Continuation**

Evaluate missing aces.

If appropriate, continue with a King Ask or sign off.

---

# Expert Tip

**Blackwood comes late in the auction—not early.**

If you still need to discover whether a slam is good, Blackwood is premature. First establish:

- the trump suit,
- controls,
- overall strength,
- and partnership fit.

Only then ask for aces.

---

# Memory Aid

**Blackwood asks one simple question:**

> **"How many aces do you have?"**

Remember:

- Agree trumps first.
- Ask only when slam is already a realistic target.
- Use the responses to confirm safety—not to decide whether slam is desirable.
- Modern partnerships often replace Blackwood with RKCB, but understanding Blackwood remains essential because it is the foundation of all ace-asking conventions.
