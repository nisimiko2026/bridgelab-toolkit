# Cue Bidding (Control Bidding)

## Purpose

**Cue Bidding** (also called **Control Bidding**) is a slam-investigation convention used after a trump suit has been agreed. Instead of bidding naturally, each new suit bid shows a **control** (an Ace, King, singleton, or void, depending on partnership agreement).

Cue Bidding enables partners to:

- Determine whether slam is safe.
- Locate missing controls.
- Evaluate wasted values.
- Decide between game, small slam, or grand slam.

Cue Bidding is one of the most fundamental slam conventions and is commonly combined with **Roman Key Card Blackwood (RKCB)**.

---

# When It Applies

Cue Bidding begins after:

- A trump suit has been agreed.
- Both partners have sufficient strength to consider slam.

Example:

```
1♠
3♠
4♣
```

The bid of **4♣** is **not natural**.

It shows a **club control**.

---

# What Is a Control?

A control prevents the opponents from taking the first two tricks in a suit.

## First-Round Control

- Ace
- Void

---

## Second-Round Control

- King
- Singleton

Partnership agreement determines whether first- and second-round controls are shown together or separately.

---

# Two Common Styles

## American Cue Bidding

Shows:

- First- or second-round control.

No distinction is made.

Example:

```
4♦
```

Simply promises a diamond control.

---

## Italian Cue Bidding

Shows controls in order:

1. First-round controls (Ace or void).
2. Second-round controls (King or singleton).

This method provides more precise information and is widely used by expert partnerships.

---

# Basic Principles

A cue bid:

- Is not natural.
- Shows control in the bid suit.
- Promises interest in slam.
- Usually skips suits without controls.

---

# Standard Auction

```
1♥
3♥
4♣
```

Shows:

- Club control.
- Slam interest.

---

Responder:

```
4♦
```

Shows:

- Diamond control.

---

Opener:

```
4NT
```

Roman Key Card Blackwood.

---

# Typical Auction

```
1♠
3♠
4♣
4♦
4♥
4NT
```

Sequence:

- Club control.
- Diamond control.
- Heart control.
- Key-card inquiry.

---

# What Is NOT a Cue Bid?

A cue bid does **not** show:

- Length in the suit.
- Desire to play the suit.
- A stopper for notrump.
- A natural suit.

Its only purpose is to show a **control**.

---

# Opener's Responsibilities

After partner cue bids:

- Continue cue bidding if interested in slam.
- Sign off in game with minimum values.
- Use RKCB when enough information has been exchanged.

---

# Responder's Responsibilities

Continue by:

- Showing the next available control.
- Signing off if unsuitable.
- Using RKCB when appropriate.

# Typical Cue Bidding Order

Controls are generally shown:

```
Clubs
Diamonds
Hearts
Spades
```

using the cheapest available bid.

This conserves bidding space.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Agree on:

- American or Italian style.
- Whether singleton equals second-round control.
- Whether voids are always cue bid.
- Whether first-round controls are shown before second-round controls.
- Relationship with Last Train.
- Relationship with Serious 3NT.
- Whether cue bids can be bypassed.

---

# Advantages

- Excellent slam investigation.
- Locates missing controls.
- Conserves bidding space.
- Works naturally with RKCB.
- Standard expert treatment.

---

# Disadvantages

- Gives information to opponents.
- Requires detailed partnership agreements.
- Different expert styles exist.

---

# Relationship with Other Slam Conventions

Typical expert sequence:

1. Agree trump.
2. Cue bid controls.
3. Last Train (if needed).
4. Roman Key Card Blackwood.
5. Queen Ask.
6. Grand Slam investigation.

---

# Comparison

| Convention | Purpose |
|------------|---------|
| Splinter | Shows shortness immediately |
| Cue Bidding | Shows controls |
| Last Train | Slam interest without a control |
| Serious 3NT | Extra slam interest |
| RKCB | Counts key cards |
| DOPI/ROPI | Handles interference over RKCB |

---

# Common Partnership Style

Most expert partnerships play:

- Cheapest available cue.
- First-round controls before second-round controls (Italian style).
- No skipping of controls unless denied.
- RKCB after sufficient controls are identified.

---

# Memory Aid

**Cue Bidding = "I control this suit."**

Remember:

- **Trump suit must already be agreed.**
- **Cue bids are not natural.**
- **They show controls, not length.**
- **Cheapest available cue first.**
- **Usually followed by Roman Key Card Blackwood.**

Cue Bidding is one of the cornerstones of modern slam bidding. When combined with **Splinter Bids**, **Italian Cue Bidding**, **Last Train**, **Serious 3NT**, and **Roman Key Card Blackwood**, it provides a complete and highly accurate framework for exploring small and grand slams.
