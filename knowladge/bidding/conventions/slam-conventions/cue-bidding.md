# Cue Bidding

## Overview

**Cue Bidding** refers to a family of artificial bids in which a player bids a suit for a meaning other than a desire to play in that suit.

The term is used in **two completely different contexts**:

1. **Control Cue Bids** during slam investigation.
2. **Competitive Cue Bids** (such as Michaels Cue Bid or Western Cue Bid).

Because the meanings are unrelated, it is important to understand the auction before interpreting a cue bid.

In modern bridge, the term **Cue Bid** most commonly refers to **Control Cue Bidding**, used after a trump fit has been established.

---

# Purpose

Cue bidding allows partnerships to:

- Exchange information about controls.
- Investigate slam safely.
- Avoid losing two quick tricks.
- Locate missing controls.
- Explore grand slams.
- Describe powerful hands in competitive auctions.

---

# Types of Cue Bids

| Type | Purpose |
|-------|---------|
| Control Cue Bid | Shows first- or second-round control |
| Michaels Cue Bid | Shows a two-suited hand |
| Western Cue Bid | Asks for a stopper |
| Directional Cue Bid | Shows lead preference (rare) |
| Competitive Cue Bid | Strong hand or distribution |

This article focuses primarily on **Control Cue Bidding**.

---

# Control Cue Bidding

## Overview

Once the partnership has agreed on a trump suit and has game-going values, players begin cue bidding to show **controls**.

A control is:

- First-round control (Ace or void)
- Second-round control (King or singleton)

Example:

```
1♠
3♠
4♣*
```

The **4♣** bid is not natural.

It says:

> "I have club control."

---

# What Is a Control?

## First-Round Control

Prevents opponents from taking the first trick.

Examples:

- Ace
- Void

---

## Second-Round Control

Prevents opponents from taking the first two tricks.

Examples:

- King
- Singleton

---

# Standard Cue-Bidding Order

Cue bids are normally made:

From the **lowest available suit upward**.

Example

```
1♠
3♠
4♣*
4♦*
4♥*
```

Each bid shows control.

---

# Skipping a Suit

If a player skips a suit, that usually denies control.

Example

```
1♠
3♠
4♣*
4♥*
```

Responder has skipped diamonds.

This often suggests:

- No diamond control.

---

# Returning to Trump

After cue bidding sufficiently, either player may simply return to:

```
4♠
```

This usually says:

> "Enough information."

or

> "No slam interest."

---

# Example Auction

```
1♥
3♥
4♣*
4♦*
4NT*
```

Interpretation:

- Club control
- Diamond control
- Roman Key Card Blackwood

---

# Cue Bidding Before Blackwood

Modern expert practice is:

1. Agree trumps.
2. Exchange cue bids.
3. Use RKCB.

Never reverse this order.

---

# Why Cue Bid?

Holding all five key cards does not guarantee slam.

Example:

```
♠AKQ
♥AKQ
♦xx
♣xx
```

Missing two club losers defeats the slam immediately.

Cue bidding discovers this.

---

# First- and Second-Round Controls

Most partnerships treat:

| Holding | Control |
|----------|----------|
| Ace | First-round |
| Void | First-round |
| King | Second-round |
| Singleton | Second-round |

Some expert partnerships distinguish between them.

---

# Serious Slam Interest

Cue bidding often works together with:

- Serious 3NT
- Last Train
- Control Bids
- RKCB

These conventions help determine whether slam should be pursued.

---

# Cue Bidding After Splinter

Example

```
1♠
4♦*
```

Splinter.

Now cue bidding begins immediately.

---

# Competitive Cue Bids

The same term "cue bid" is also used in competitive auctions.

Examples include:

## Michaels Cue Bid

```
(1♦)
2♦*
```

Shows:

- Both majors.

---

## Western Cue Bid

```
1♦
Pass
2♦*
```

Asks:

> "Do you have a stopper?"

---

## Directional Cue Bid

Occasionally used to suggest an opening lead.

Rare in modern partnerships.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should discuss:

- First- and second-round controls.
- Whether singleton = control.
- Whether voids are shown immediately.
- Cue-bidding order.
- Serious 3NT.
- Last Train.
- RKCB style.

# Competitive Auctions

## Cue Bidding After Interference

Example

```
1♠
(2♦)
3♠
(4♦)
?
```

Cue bidding may still continue if partnership agreements allow.

---

## Showing Controls Under Pressure

When bidding space is limited:

- Show the cheapest available control.
- Skip unavailable controls.
- Continue naturally.

---

## Slam Investigation

Cue bidding is often **more important than RKCB**.

Knowing partner has:

- Club Ace
- Diamond King

may matter more than simply counting key cards.

---

## Void Showing

Some partnerships treat:

Void

as:

First-round control.

Others use:

Exclusion Blackwood.

Agreement is essential.

---

# Cue Bidding vs Control Bidding

These terms are often used interchangeably.

Some authors distinguish them:

| Cue Bid | Control Bid |
|----------|-------------|
| Any artificial cue | Specifically shows controls |

Modern literature generally treats them as synonyms.

---

# Cue Bidding vs Splinter

| Cue Bid | Splinter |
|----------|----------|
| Shows control | Shows fit + shortness |
| Occurs after fit | Often establishes fit |
| Multiple bids possible | Usually one jump |

---

# Cue Bidding vs Michaels

| Cue Bid | Michaels |
|----------|----------|
| Slam convention | Competitive convention |
| Shows controls | Shows two suits |

# Advantages

Cue bidding:

- Locates controls.
- Improves slam accuracy.
- Prevents poor slams.
- Excellent partnership communication.
- Standard expert treatment.

---

# Limitations

Cue bidding:

- Requires partnership discipline.
- Can consume bidding space.
- Has several styles.
- May reveal information to defenders.

---

# Variations

Common styles include:

- First-round controls only.
- First- and second-round controls.
- Italian Cue Bidding.
- American Cue Bidding.
- Up-the-line cue bidding.
- Serious 3NT style.
- Last Train style.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Starting RKCB Too Early

Cue bidding should usually come first.

---

## Forgetting the Order

Cue bids proceed:

Lowest suit first.

---

## Showing Unsupported Controls

Do not cue bid without genuine slam interest.

---

## Ignoring Skipped Suits

Skipping a suit usually denies control.

---

## Confusing Competitive Cue Bids

Remember:

Michaels cue bids have nothing to do with slam investigation.

---

# Tips

- Agree trumps before cue bidding.
- Bid controls from the lowest suit upward.
- Learn cue bidding before RKCB.
- Combine cue bids with Splinters and Serious 3NT.
- Remember that cue bidding exchanges **quality** of controls, while RKCB counts **quantity** of key cards.

---

# Modern Usage

Cue bidding is considered an essential component of modern slam bidding and is played in virtually every bidding system:

- Standard American
- 2/1 Game Force
- SAYC
- Precision
- Polish Club
- Acol

Expert partnerships almost always cue bid before using Roman Key Card Blackwood, allowing them to evaluate not only the number of key cards but also whether quick losers exist in any side suit.

---

# Summary

Cue Bidding is one of the most powerful communication tools in bridge. After a trump fit has been established, cue bids allow partners to exchange information about first- and second-round controls, making slam bidding significantly more accurate than relying solely on Ace-asking conventions. The term "cue bid" is also used for several competitive conventions such as Michaels and Western Cue Bids, but in modern bridge literature it most commonly refers to the systematic exchange of control information during slam investigation. Mastering cue bidding is an essential step toward expert-level slam bidding.
