# Ekren

## Bridge Insight

**The Ekren Convention is an artificial weak two-diamond opening that immediately describes a two-suited hand containing both major suits. By identifying both majors at the first opportunity, the partnership can quickly locate major-suit fits while making competitive bidding difficult for the opponents.**

---

# Overview

The **Ekren Convention** is an artificial **2♦ opening** that shows a weak hand with both major suits.

Unlike a natural Weak Two or the Multi 2♦ convention, Ekren immediately promises a two-suited hand, allowing responder to evaluate major-suit fits and game prospects with greater accuracy.

Several versions of Ekren are played worldwide. The exact strength requirements and distribution should be agreed by the partnership.

---

# Objectives

The Ekren Convention enables the partnership to:

- Show both major suits immediately.
- Compete aggressively with distributional hands.
- Locate major-suit fits quickly.
- Consume opponents' bidding space.
- Distinguish two-suited weak hands from natural Weak Twos.

---

# When to Use

Typical auction:

```text
2♦
```

Artificial.

Usually shows:

- Weak opening values.
- Both major suits.

---

# Requirements

Typical agreements:

### Strength

- Approximately 5–10 HCP.
- Good playing strength.

### Distribution

Most partnerships require one of:

- 5-5 in the majors.
- 5-4 in the majors.
- 5-5 or better.

The exact requirement is a partnership agreement.

---

# Opening Structure

Standard Ekren:

```text
2♦

↓

Both majors
```

Responder does not yet know:

- Which major is longer.
- Opener's exact strength.
- Suit quality.

These are clarified during the continuation.

Natural and constructive.

# Partnership Agreements

The partnership should define:

- Minimum HCP.
- Whether 5-4 is acceptable.
- Whether 5-5 is required.
- Meaning of the 2NT inquiry.
- Continuations after interference.
- Vulnerability style.

These agreements should appear on the convention card.

# System Variations

Common treatments include:

### Original Ekren

- 4-4 or better majors.
- Weak hand.

### Modern Ekren

- Usually 5-4 or 5-5 majors.
- More disciplined style.

### Extended Ekren

Some partnerships include additional strong hand types.

---

# Advantages

The Ekren Convention:

- Locates major fits quickly.
- Makes competitive bidding difficult.
- Uses bidding space efficiently.
- Describes distribution immediately.
- Applies pressure to the opponents.

---

# Limitations

Ekren:

- Requires detailed partnership agreements.
- Sacrifices the natural Weak Two in Diamonds.
- May complicate responder's decisions.
- Is subject to special defensive methods.

---

# Expert Thinking

Ekren values **distribution over point count**.

A weak hand with two five-card majors often has greater offensive potential than a balanced hand with significantly more high-card points.

By showing both majors immediately, opener allows responder to judge the partnership's fit rather than relying solely on HCP.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Opening with insufficient major length.
- Forgetting partnership agreements.
- Confusing Ekren with Multi 2♦.
- Using Ekren with unsuitable balanced hands.
- Overvaluing poor suit quality.

---

# Best Practices

- Promise genuine major-suit length.
- Agree the minimum distribution.
- Practice the 2NT inquiry.
- Discuss responses after interference.
- Record all agreements on the convention card.

# Expert Tip

**Ekren is a fit-finding convention, not simply a preempt.**

Its greatest strength lies in immediately revealing that the partnership has potential fits in both major suits. Responder should focus first on locating the best trump suit and only then evaluate whether the combined strength justifies game or slam.
