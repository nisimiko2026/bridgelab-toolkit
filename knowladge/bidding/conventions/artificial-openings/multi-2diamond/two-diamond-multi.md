# Multi 2♦

## Bridge Insight

**The Multi 2♦ convention is one of the most effective preemptive weapons in bridge. By concealing the identity of opener's hand, it forces the opponents to make difficult decisions while allowing the partnership to exchange accurate information later in the auction.**

---

# Overview

The **Multi 2♦** is an artificial opening bid that represents **more than one possible hand type**. Its most common meaning is:

- A weak two opening in either Hearts or Spades.

Many partnerships also include one or more strong hand types, depending on their system.

Unlike natural Weak Two openings, Multi 2♦ intentionally conceals opener's suit, making competitive bidding much more difficult for the opponents.

---

# Objectives

Multi 2♦ enables the partnership to:

- Preempt efficiently with either major.
- Conceal opener's long suit.
- Consume opponents' bidding space.
- Increase competitive pressure.
- Preserve strong artificial opening options in some systems.

---

# When to Use

Typical auction:

```text
2♦
```

Artificial.

Most commonly shows:

- Weak Two in Hearts.
- Weak Two in Spades.

Additional meanings depend upon partnership agreements.

---

# Requirements

### Typical Weak Option

Normally:

- Six-card major.
- Approximately 5–10 HCP.
- Good playing strength.

### Strong Options

Depending upon partnership style, Multi may also include:

- Strong balanced hands.
- Strong one-suiters.
- Acol Two openings.
- Game-forcing hands.

# Continuations

The opening bid intentionally conceals information.

Responder's primary task is to identify opener's hand type before selecting the final contract.

---

## Pass-or-Correct

The most common response:

```text
2♦

2♥
```

asks opener to:

- Pass with Hearts.
- Correct to Spades.

This enables weak hands to find the correct major with minimal bidding.

---

## 2NT Inquiry

With stronger values, responder may bid:

```text
2NT
```

asking opener to further describe the hand.

The exact responses depend on partnership agreements and are covered in:

**2NT Inquiry after Multi 2♦**

---

## Natural Responses

Some partnerships permit:

- Natural suit bids.
- Invitational raises.
- Direct game bids.

These should be defined on the convention card.

---

# Partnership Agreements

The partnership should define:

- Which hand types are included.
- Weak hand strength range.
- Minimum suit quality.
- Pass-or-Correct structure.
- 2NT inquiry responses.
- Continuations after interference.
- Responses to doubles.

These agreements should be recorded on the convention card.

---

# System Variations

Common Multi styles include:

- Weak Two in either major only.
- Weak Two plus Strong Balanced.
- Weak Two plus Strong Minor.
- Weak Two plus Acol Two.
- Modern Multi.

Each variation has its own response structure.

---

# Advantages

Multi 2♦:

- Conceals opener's suit.
- Makes competitive bidding difficult.
- Creates excellent preemptive pressure.
- Uses bidding space efficiently.
- Supports several system styles.

---

# Limitations

Multi 2♦:

- Requires extensive partnership agreements.
- More difficult to learn than natural Weak Twos.
- Often subject to special defense methods.
- Restricted or regulated in some bridge events.

---

# Expert Thinking

The strength of Multi 2♦ lies not in the weak hand itself but in **information concealment**.

After a natural Weak Two, the opponents immediately know:

- The suit.
- Approximate strength.
- Distribution.

After Multi:

They know almost none of these.

That uncertainty frequently creates incorrect competitive decisions.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Forgetting included hand types.
- Confusing Pass-or-Correct with a natural response.
- Misunderstanding the 2NT inquiry.
- Using unsuitable weak hands.
- Playing different response structures from partner.

---

# Best Practices

- Keep the structure simple.
- Memorize Pass-or-Correct auctions first.
- Learn the 2NT inquiry second.
- Record all agreements on the convention card.
- Practice competitive auctions.

**Opening**


---

# Expert Tip

**Think of Multi 2♦ as a concealed Weak Two, not as an artificial opening.**

The convention's greatest strength is uncertainty. By hiding the identity of opener's suit—and sometimes even the type of hand—the partnership forces the opponents to compete with incomplete information while preserving an efficient structure for its own continuations.

---

# Memory Aid

**Multi 2♦ = "One Opening, Many Possibilities."**

Remember:

- The opening is artificial.
- The most common meaning is a Weak Two in either major.
- Weak responders use **Pass-or-Correct** to locate the correct major.
- Strong responders use **2NT** or other agreed methods to identify opener's exact hand.
- Success with Multi 2♦ depends on disciplined partnership agreements and consistent continuations.
