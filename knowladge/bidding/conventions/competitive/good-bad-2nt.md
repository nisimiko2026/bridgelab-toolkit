# Good-Bad 2NT

## Purpose

**Good-Bad 2NT** is an artificial competitive bidding convention that allows a partnership to distinguish between:

- **Competitive ("Bad")** hands that simply want to compete safely.
- **Constructive ("Good")** hands with extra values.

Without the convention, both hand types often require identical three-level bids.

Good-Bad 2NT creates a clear distinction while preserving bidding space.

---

# Basic Principle

After interference, an artificial **2NT** says:

> "I have a competitive hand and want to compete, but I do **not** have extra values."

Partner is expected to bid the **cheapest available suit**.

The original bidder may then:

- Pass.
- Correct.
- Continue naturally.

Direct bidding at the three level, without using 2NT first, shows a **good**, constructive hand.

---

# When It Applies

Good-Bad 2NT is typically played after:

- Partner opens.
- Right-hand opponent competes.
- The partnership still has room for an artificial 2NT.

Example:

```text
West     North     East     South

1♥        Pass      2♥*      ?
```

(*Opponent's raise)

Responder:

```text
2NT
```

Artificial.

Partner bids:

```text
3♣
```

Responder may now bid:

```text
3♦
```

showing a merely competitive diamond hand.

# Typical Auction

```text
1♠
Pass
2♠
3♣
?
```

Options:

### Good Hand

```text
3♦
```

Constructive.

---

### Bad Hand

```text
2NT
Pass
3♣
Pass
3♦
```

Competitive only.

---

# Meaning of 2NT

The artificial 2NT:

- Is forcing for one round.
- Does **not** promise notrump values.
- Requests partner to bid the cheapest available suit.

Partner should not interpret 2NT as natural.

---

# Requirements

Typical "Bad" hand:

- Distributional.
- Competitive values.
- No game interest.

Typical "Good" hand:

- Extra high-card strength.
- Constructive intentions.
- Possible game interest.

Exact ranges vary by partnership.

---

# Advantages

- Distinguishes competitive and constructive actions.
- Preserves bidding space.
- Reduces guesswork.
- Widely used by tournament players.

---

# Disadvantages

- Sacrifices the natural 2NT bid.
- Requires partnership agreement.
- Less useful when no relay bid is available.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Discuss:

- Which auctions use Good-Bad 2NT.
- Whether 2NT is always artificial.
- Cheapest relay response.
- Treatment after further interference.
- Strength ranges for "good" and "bad" hands.

---

# Variations

Some partnerships extend Good-Bad 2NT to:

- Competitive raises.
- New-suit bids.
- Support auctions.
- Passed-hand situations.

Others limit it to specific auctions after simple overcalls.

# Summary

| Action | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
|Artificial 2NT, then bid suit|Competitive ("Bad") hand|
|Direct three-level bid|Constructive ("Good") hand|

**Primary idea:** **Good-Bad 2NT** uses an artificial **2NT relay** to distinguish **competitive hands** from **constructive hands** that would otherwise make the same three-level bid, allowing the partnership to compete more accurately after interference.
