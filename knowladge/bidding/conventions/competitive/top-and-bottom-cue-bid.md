# Top and Bottom Cue Bid

## Overview

**Top and Bottom Cue Bid (TBCB)** is an advanced competitive bidding convention
used over an opponent's natural suit opening. It employs cue bids to describe
either **strong one-suited hands** or **specific two-suited distributions**,
depending on the partnership agreement.

The convention was developed as an enhancement to standard cue-bid methods,
allowing partnerships to distinguish between different offensive hand types
while consuming minimal bidding space.

Although less common than **Michaels Cue Bids** or **Unusual Notrump**, Top and
Bottom Cue Bid is popular among experienced tournament partnerships seeking
greater descriptive precision.

---

# Objectives

The convention enables the partnership to

- distinguish one-suited and two-suited overcalls,
- identify offensive distribution,
- improve competitive accuracy,
- preserve bidding space,
- compete aggressively,
- investigate sacrifices and games more effectively.

---

# Background

Traditional cue bids often suffer from ambiguity.

Example

```
West     North
1♦       2♦*
```

The cue bid could mean

- Michaels,
- very strong hand,
- asking bid,
- or another artificial agreement.

Top and Bottom Cue Bid assigns a specific meaning to cue bids, reducing this
ambiguity.

---

# Basic Principle

The convention uses the opponent's suit as an artificial bid.

The exact meaning depends upon partnership style.

The most common treatment is

- **Cue bid of the opponent's suit** = the **highest-ranking and lowest-ranking
  unbid suits** ("top and bottom").

Example

```
1♦
2♦*
```

shows

- clubs (bottom),
- spades (top).

Thus responder immediately knows both suits.

---

# Why "Top and Bottom"?

After an opponent opens,

the remaining suits are ranked.

Example

Opponent opens

```
1♦
```

Remaining suits

```
♣
♥
♠
```

Top

```
♠
```

Bottom

```
♣
```

The cue bid therefore promises

```
♠ + ♣
```

---

Example

Opponent opens

```
1♥
```

Remaining suits

```
♣
♦
♠
```

Top

```
♠
```

Bottom

```
♣
```

Again,

Top + Bottom.

# Typical Meanings

| Opponent Opens | Cue Bid Shows |
|----------------|---------------|
| 1♣ | ♦ + ♠ |
| 1♦ | ♣ + ♠ |
| 1♥ | ♣ + ♠ |
| 1♠ | ♣ + ♥ |

The exact structure varies slightly between partnerships.

---

# Relationship to Michaels Cue Bid

Michaels uses the cue bid to show

- both majors,
- or a major and an unspecified minor.

Top and Bottom instead promises

- highest unbid suit,
- lowest unbid suit.

This removes ambiguity.

---

# Hand Requirements

Typical expert requirements

- 5-5 distribution or better,
- good suit quality,
- offensive playing strength,
- opening values or strong intermediate values,
- little defensive interest.

Some partnerships permit

- 5-4 favorable vulnerability.

---

# Suit Quality

At least one suit should normally contain

- two of the top three honors,
- or three of the top five honors.

Examples


# Modern Expert Treatments

There is **no universally accepted Top and Bottom Cue Bid structure**. The two
most common expert approaches are:

### Top-and-Bottom Only

The cue bid always promises

- highest unbid suit,
- lowest unbid suit.

This is the classic treatment.

---

### Top-and-Bottom within Ghestem

Many expert partnerships incorporate Top and Bottom Cue Bid as one component of
the **Ghestem** convention:

- Cue bid = Top and Bottom suits.
- 2NT = Lowest two suits.
- Jump overcall = Highest two suits.

Together, these three bids cover **all three possible two-suited combinations**
after an opponent's opening bid.

This is now the most common expert application.

---

# Expert Partnership Agreements

A partnership should explicitly define:

- Minimum distribution (5-5 or allow 5-4?)
- Minimum playing strength
- Vulnerability adjustments
- Responses with equal support
- Continuations after interference
- Whether the treatment is independent or part of **Ghestem**

---

# Historical Perspective

Top and Bottom Cue Bid emerged as an attempt to eliminate the ambiguity of
traditional Michaels Cue Bids by assigning one precise suit combination to the
cue bid. Although it never became as widespread as Michaels, it remains an
effective expert treatment, particularly when incorporated into comprehensive
two-suited overcall structures such as **Ghestem**.

---

# Summary

**Top and Bottom Cue Bid** is an advanced competitive bidding convention that
uses a cue bid of the opponents' suit to show the **highest-ranking and
lowest-ranking unbid suits**. It provides immediate knowledge of both suits,
improves competitive accuracy, and integrates naturally with **Ghestem** and
other expert two-suited overcall systems.

While less common than Michaels Cue Bids, it offers a highly logical and
precise method for describing two-suited hands and is well suited to advanced
partnerships seeking maximum descriptive efficiency.
