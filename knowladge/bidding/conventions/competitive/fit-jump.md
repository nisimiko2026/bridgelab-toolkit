# Jump Shift

## Overview

A **Jump Shift** is any bid in a **new suit at a higher-than-minimum level**,
bypassing one or more available bidding levels.

Unlike a simple shift, which introduces a new suit economically, a jump shift
consumes additional bidding space to convey extra information about

- strength,
- distribution,
- offensive potential,
- support,
- or partnership agreements.

Jump shifts are among the most variable bids in bridge. Their meaning depends
entirely upon

- who makes the bid,
- the auction,
- the bidding system,
- and partnership agreements.

Modern bridge employs several distinct types of jump shifts, including

- Strong Jump Shifts,
- Weak Jump Shifts,
- Soloway Jump Shifts,
- Fit Jump Shifts,
- Jump Shift Overcalls,
- Jump Shift Rebids.

---

# Objectives

Jump shifts enable the partnership to

- show exceptional strength,
- preempt opponents,
- describe two-suited hands,
- establish a game force,
- identify offensive distribution,
- improve slam investigation.

---

# Basic Principle

A jump shift skips one or more bidding levels while introducing a new suit.

Example

```
1♣
2♠
```

Normal shift.

```
1♣
3♠
```

Jump shift.

---

# Historical Perspective

Traditionally,

jump shifts showed

- very strong hands,
- powerful suits,
- game-forcing values.

Modern bidding has diversified these meanings.

Today,

different jump shifts serve entirely different purposes depending upon the
system.

---

# Types of Jump Shifts

Modern expert bridge recognizes several categories.

## Strong Jump Shift

Shows

- excellent suit,
- game-forcing values,
- slam interest.

Common in traditional Standard American.

---

## Soloway Jump Shift

Modern replacement for traditional strong jump shifts.

Shows

- game force,
- excellent suit,
- preserves bidding space.

Widely used in Two-over-One Game Force.

---

## Weak Jump Shift

Shows

- weak hand,
- long suit,
- preemptive values.

Designed to obstruct the opponents.

---

## Fit Jump Shift

Shows

- support for partner,
- long side suit,
- constructive values.

Used primarily in competitive auctions.

---

## Jump Shift Overcall

After an opponent opens,

a jump shift may show

- weak preemptive hand,
- intermediate hand,
- very strong hand,

depending upon partnership style.

---

## Jump Shift Rebid

Made by opener or responder after previously bidding.

Usually shows

- extra strength,
- distribution,
- excellent suit.

---

# Natural versus Artificial

## Natural Jump Shift

Shows

- genuine long suit,
- additional strength,
- natural distribution.

---

## Artificial Jump Shift

Shows

- fit,
- relay,
- controls,
- partnership agreement.

The suit itself may not be natural.

---

# Requirements

Requirements depend upon the treatment.

### Traditional Strong

- Opening values or more
- Excellent suit
- Game force

---

### Weak

- Approximately 4–8 HCP
- Six-card suit
- Good suit quality

---

### Soloway

- Game force
- Self-sustaining suit
- Excellent offensive strength

---

### Fit Jump

- Four-card support
- Five-card side suit
- Constructive values

---

# Suit Quality

Strong jump shifts require

- self-sustaining suit,
- excellent honors,
- strong intermediates.

Weak jump shifts require

- reasonable playing strength,
- good suit quality.

---

# Hand Evaluation

Modern experts evaluate jump shifts using

- playing tricks,
- Losing Trick Count,
- support points,
- controls,
- offensive potential,

rather than HCP alone.

# Opener's Responsibilities

After a jump shift opener should determine

- forcing status,
- strength,
- fit,
- controls,
- game prospects.

Continuation depends upon the partnership agreement.

---

# Responder's Responsibilities

Responder should

- clarify distribution,
- describe secondary suits,
- evaluate slam,
- avoid unnecessary jumps.

---

# Competitive Auctions

Jump shifts become particularly valuable after interference.

Example

```
1♦
(1♥)
3♣*
```

May be

- Fit Jump,
- Weak Jump,
- Natural,

depending upon agreements.

---

# Vulnerability

Weak jump shifts become more disciplined

- unfavorable vulnerability,

and more aggressive

- favorable vulnerability.

Constructive jump shifts change little.

---

# Modern Expert Philosophy

Experts no longer assign one universal meaning to jump shifts.

Instead,

different auctions use different specialized treatments.

# Jump Shift by Responder

Example

```
1♠
3♣
```

Meaning depends upon system.

Possible meanings

- Soloway,
- Weak Jump Shift,
- Natural strong,
- Fit Jump.

---

# Jump Shift by Passed Hand

Most partnerships modify meanings because

the passed hand has already denied an opening.

Common treatments include

- Passed-Hand Splinter,
- Passed-Hand Bergen,
- Passed-Hand Jacoby,
- Natural constructive jump.

---

# Jump Shift After Competition

Modern expert agreements frequently redefine jump shifts.

Common treatments include

- Fit Jump Shift,
- Weak Jump Shift,
- Mixed Raise substitute,
- Splinter.

Agreement required.

# Modern Expert Treatments

There is **no single "Jump Shift Convention."** Modern partnerships assign
different meanings to jump shifts depending on the auction.

Common expert styles include

### Traditional Strong Jump Shift

Natural.

Game forcing.

---

### Soloway Jump Shift

Strong one-suited game force.

Most common in expert natural systems.

---

### Weak Jump Shift

Preemptive.

Designed to consume bidding space.

---

### Fit Jump Shift

Constructive.

Shows support plus a second suit.

---

### Relay-Based Systems

Many relay systems eliminate natural jump shifts entirely, replacing them with
artificial relay sequences that describe strong hands more economically.

---

# Expert Recommendations

A modern expert partnership should define **every category of jump shift**
explicitly, including:

- Jump shifts by responder.
- Jump shifts by opener.
- Jump shift rebids.
- Jump shift overcalls.
- Passed-hand jump shifts.
- Competitive jump shifts.
- Fit Jump Shifts.
- Soloway Jump Shifts.
- Weak Jump Shifts.

Because jump shifts occur in many different contexts, they should not be
treated as a single convention but as a **family of specialized bidding tools**.

---

# Summary

A **Jump Shift** is any bid that introduces a new suit by jumping one or more
levels. While historically associated with strong one-suited hands, modern
bridge has developed a wide range of specialized jump-shift treatments,
including **Strong Jump Shifts**, **Weak Jump Shifts**, **Soloway Jump
Shifts**, **Fit Jump Shifts**, and various relay-based methods.

Expert partnerships define the meaning of each jump shift according to the
auction, enabling highly accurate hand description, effective competitive
bidding, and efficient game and slam investigation.
