# Michaels Cue Bid

## Overview

The **Michaels Cue Bid** is one of the most widely played two-suited overcall conventions in bridge. Instead of overcalling naturally, the overcaller bids the **opponent's suit**, showing a two-suited hand with at least **5-5 distribution**.

Michaels allows partnerships to describe powerful distributional hands immediately, making it easier to locate major-suit fits, compete for partscore, and occasionally reach game.

The convention is commonly paired with the **Unusual Notrump**, together providing an efficient method for showing nearly every two-suited hand.

---

# Purpose

Michaels Cue Bid is designed to:

- Show two-suited hands.
- Compete aggressively.
- Locate major-suit fits.
- Describe distribution immediately.
- Make life difficult for the opponents.
- Preserve bidding space.

---

# Basic Meaning

The meaning depends on the opponent's opening suit.

## Cue Bid of a Minor

After:

```
(1♣)
2♣*
```

or

```
(1♦)
2♦*
```

Shows:

- Hearts
- Spades

Usually:

- At least 5-5.

---

## Cue Bid of a Major

After:

```
(1♥)
2♥*
```

or

```
(1♠)
2♠*
```

Shows:

- The other major.
- An unspecified minor.

Usually:

- At least 5-5.

---

# Standard Structure

| Opponent Opens | Cue Bid Shows |
|----------------|---------------|
| 1♣ | Both majors |
| 1♦ | Both majors |
| 1♥ | Spades + a minor |
| 1♠ | Hearts + a minor |

---

# Strength Requirements

Michaels may be used with:

### Weak Style

- 6-11 HCP
- Good distribution

### Intermediate Style

- 8-14 HCP

### Strong Style

- Very strong two-suiters

Modern partnerships generally use Michaels with:

- Distributional hands.
- Offensive values.

---

# Suit Requirements

Typical requirements:

- At least 5-5.

Some partnerships allow:

- Excellent 5-4.

Most experts prefer genuine 5-5 shape.

---

# Why Use Michaels?

Without Michaels:

```
(1♦)
?
```

Holding:

```
♠ KQJ84
♥ AQ983
♦ 5
♣ J73
```

Natural bidding cannot describe both suits.

Michaels immediately shows:

- Both majors.
- Distribution.
- Competitive intentions.

---

# Responses by Advancer

Advancer evaluates:

- Fit.
- Strength.
- Vulnerability.
- Offensive potential.

Possible actions:

- Pass.
- Choose a suit.
- Invite game.
- Bid game.
- Explore slam.

---

# Responses After a Minor Cue Bid

Example

```
West   North
1♦     2♦*
Pass   2♥
```

Heart preference.

Responder may instead choose:

```
2♠
```

with better spade support.

---

# Responses After a Major Cue Bid

Example

```
West   North
1♥     2♥*
Pass   2NT*
```

The **2NT** bid is often an inquiry asking:

> "Which minor do you have?"

Typical responses:

| Response | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| 3♣ | Clubs |
| 3♦ | Diamonds |

# Strong Hands

Some partnerships use:

```
Cue Bid
Then Bid Again
```

to show:

- Extra values.
- Slam interest.

Others simply continue naturally.

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

Michaels is itself a competitive convention and is intended for immediate overcalls.

---

## Opponent Raises

Example

```
1♦
2♦*
3♦
?
```

Overcaller evaluates:

- Playing tricks.
- Vulnerability.
- Distribution.

Possible actions:

- Compete further.
- Bid game.
- Defend.

---

## Opponent Doubles

Example

```
1♣
2♣*
(Double)
?
```

Advancer normally:

- Chooses a suit.
- Redoubles with strength (partnership agreement).
- Makes natural bids.

---

## Balancing Position

Many partnerships also play Michaels in balancing seat.

Example

```
1♦
Pass
Pass
?
```

Cue bid retains the same meaning.

Some partnerships reduce minimum strength in balancing position.

---

## Slam Investigation

Although primarily a competitive convention, Michaels occasionally discovers excellent fits.

Slam exploration proceeds naturally using:

- Cue Bids.
- Control Bids.
- Roman Key Card Blackwood.

---

# Michaels vs Unusual Notrump

Together these conventions describe virtually every two-suited hand.

| Michaels | Unusual Notrump |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Cue bid | 2NT overcall |
| Uses opponent's suit | Uses 2NT |
| Usually majors or major + minor | Usually lowest two unbid suits |

The two conventions complement each other.

---

# Advantages

Michaels:

- Shows two suits immediately.
- Makes competitive bidding easier.
- Helps locate major fits.
- Excellent preemptive effect.
- Standard tournament treatment.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Gives information to the opponents.
- Requires partnership agreements.
- Usually promises 5-5 shape.
- Sometimes forces high-level decisions.

---

# Variations

Common partnership treatments include:

- Standard Michaels
- Mini-Michaels
- Strong Michaels
- Michaels with Weak NT
- Michaels with 5-4 shapes

Some partnerships also vary their style according to vulnerability.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Using Michaels with 5-3 Shape

The convention normally promises:

- At least 5-5.

---

## Forgetting the Meaning After Major Openings

Remember:

Minor opening →

**Both majors**

Major opening →

**Other major + unknown minor**

---

## Confusing Michaels with Cue Raises

Michaels is an **overcall convention**, not a raise.

---

## Forgetting the 2NT Inquiry

After a major cue bid, 2NT usually asks for the minor.

---

## Ignoring Vulnerability

Aggressive Michaels bids should consider vulnerability carefully.

---

# Tips

- Memorize the two basic rules:
  - **Minor cue = Both majors**
  - **Major cue = Other major + minor**
- Learn Michaels together with Unusual Notrump.
- Prefer genuine 5-5 distribution.
- Practice balancing-seat auctions.
- Agree exactly how the 2NT inquiry works.

---

# Modern Usage

Michaels Cue Bid is one of the world's most popular competitive conventions.

It is standard in:

- Standard American
- 2/1 Game Force
- SAYC
- Precision
- Acol
- Polish Club

Nearly every tournament partnership plays Michaels together with **Unusual Notrump**, forming the foundation of modern two-suited competitive bidding.

---

# Summary

The Michaels Cue Bid is a cornerstone of modern competitive bidding. By cue bidding an opponent's opening suit, the overcaller immediately shows a powerful two-suited hand, enabling the partnership to compete accurately while placing pressure on the opponents. Together with Unusual Notrump, Michaels provides a comprehensive system for describing distributional overcalls and is considered essential knowledge for tournament bridge players.
