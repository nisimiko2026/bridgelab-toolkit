# Conventions

> *Expanding the language of bridge beyond natural bidding.*

Conventions are the vocabulary that transforms natural bidding into a precise,
efficient system of partnership communication. By assigning agreed artificial
meanings to selected bids, conventions allow partnerships to exchange
information that would otherwise require several natural bids—or could not be
communicated at all.

The **BridgeLab Conventions** section documents the most widely played
conventions as well as many advanced and expert treatments used in modern
tournament bridge.

---

# Introduction

A convention is an **agreed artificial meaning** assigned to one or more bids.

Unlike natural bids, which generally describe the suit bid, conventions may
show

- strength,
- distribution,
- suit support,
- controls,
- slam interest,
- competitive intentions,
- defensive methods.

Some conventions have become nearly universal, while others are played only by
advanced partnerships.

---

# Objectives

Bridge conventions aim to

- improve bidding accuracy,
- exchange information efficiently,
- preserve bidding space,
- locate major-suit fits,
- investigate game and slam,
- compete effectively,
- interfere with the opponents,
- improve partnership understanding.

# Choosing Conventions

No partnership can successfully play every convention.

When selecting conventions, consider

- partnership experience,
- memory requirements,
- frequency of occurrence,
- tournament regulations,
- compatibility with your bidding system.

Many successful expert partnerships prefer a relatively small number of
well-understood conventions over a large collection of poorly remembered ones.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Many conventions require explicit agreements concerning

- strength ranges,
- suit quality,
- continuations,
- interference,
- forcing status,
- competitive actions,
- vulnerability adjustments.

BridgeLab identifies these partnership decisions throughout the encyclopedia.

# Standardization

Some conventions are essentially standardized.

Examples include

- Stayman
- Jacoby Transfers
- Blackwood
- Roman Key Card Blackwood

Others exist in multiple published versions.

Examples include

- Lebensohl
- CRASH
- Suction
- TWERB
- Woolsey
- Meckwell

Where no universal standard exists, BridgeLab presents the most widely accepted
treatment and discusses important expert variations.

# Expert Notes

Bridge conventions continue to evolve. New treatments appear regularly, while
older methods are refined or replaced. BridgeLab distinguishes between

- historically important conventions,
- modern mainstream methods,
- expert partnership treatments,
- system-specific agreements.

This ensures that readers understand not only **how** a convention works, but
also **where** and **why** it is used.

# Summary

The **Conventions** section is BridgeLab's comprehensive reference to modern
bridge bidding methods. Covering everything from universally played conventions
such as Stayman and Roman Key Card Blackwood to advanced expert treatments like
CRASH, Suction, and Kokish Relay, it provides structured, technically accurate,
and partnership-oriented guidance for every level of player.

Understanding conventions is not about memorizing hundreds of artificial bids,
but about selecting, understanding, and applying the methods that best suit
your partnership and style of play.
