# Splinter Convention

## Overview

The **Splinter Convention** is one of the most effective slam-investigation tools in bridge. A **double jump** by responder after partner opens a suit shows:

- Four-card (or longer) support for opener's suit.
- Game-forcing values.
- A singleton or void in the suit bid.

Unlike Jacoby 2NT, which first establishes a game force and later describes distribution, a Splinter immediately identifies shortness, allowing opener to evaluate wasted values and slam potential.

The convention is used primarily after major-suit openings but can also be adapted for minor-suit openings.

---

# Purpose

Splinter is designed to:

- Establish a game-forcing trump fit.
- Show singleton or void.
- Help opener evaluate honors opposite shortness.
- Improve slam bidding.
- Preserve bidding space for control bidding.

---

# When to Use

Most commonly after:

```
1♥
?
```

or

```
1♠
?
```

The auction should normally be uncontested.

---

# Requirements

Responder normally promises:

- Four-card or longer trump support.
- Game-forcing values.
- Singleton or void.
- Interest in slam.

Typical strength:

- 12-15 HCP with excellent distribution.

Playing strength is far more important than raw HCP.

---

# Standard Splinter Bids

## After 1♥

| Bid | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| 3♠ | Singleton/Void Spade |
| 4♣ | Singleton/Void Club |
| 4♦ | Singleton/Void Diamond |

There is **no splinter in hearts**, since hearts are the agreed trump suit.

---

## After 1♠

| Bid | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| 4♣ | Singleton/Void Club |
| 4♦ | Singleton/Void Diamond |
| 4♥ | Singleton/Void Heart |

---

## Example

Responder holds:

```
♠ K983
♥ AQJ7
♦ 5
♣ AK74
```

Auction:

```
1♥
4♦*
```

Shows:

- Four hearts
- Singleton diamond
- Game force

---

# Why Show Shortness?

Suppose opener holds:

```
♦ AKQ
```

Responder's singleton diamond means those honors lose much of their value.

Conversely, if opener has:

```
♣ AKQ
```

those honors become extremely valuable.

Splinter allows opener to judge slam accurately.

---

# Opener's Rebids

After a Splinter opener evaluates:

- Wasted values opposite shortness.
- Trump quality.
- Controls.
- Distribution.
- Overall playing strength.

Possible actions:

- Sign off in game.
- Cue bid.
- Control bid.
- Roman Key Card Blackwood.
- Grand Slam investigation.

---

# Evaluating Wasted Values

Good hand opposite a splinter:

```
♠ AQ
♥ AKJ85
♦ 764
♣ AQJ
```

Poor hand opposite a diamond splinter:

```
♠ AQ
♥ AKJ85
♦ AKQ
♣ 764
```

Three diamond honors lose considerable value opposite singleton diamonds.

---

# Responder's Continuations

After opener's rebid responder may:

- Cue bid.
- Show controls.
- Bid Roman Key Card Blackwood.
- Sign off.
- Investigate slam.

---

# Strength Requirements

Typical partnerships require:

- Four-card support.
- Game-forcing values.

Many experts prefer:

- Excellent controls.
- Shortness.
- Offensive hand.

rather than simply counting HCP.

---

# Splinter vs Jacoby 2NT

| Splinter | Jacoby 2NT |
|-----------|------------|
| Shows shortness immediately | Shows game force first |
| Distributional | Balanced or unbalanced |
| Excellent slam tool | Flexible descriptive tool |
| Less room to exchange information | Maximum bidding space |

---

# Splinter vs Swiss Raise

| Splinter | Swiss Raise |
|-----------|-------------|
| Singleton/void shown | No shortness shown |
| Distribution emphasized | General strength emphasized |
| Excellent for slam | Strong raise |

---

# Splinter vs Bergen Raises

| Splinter | Bergen Raises |
|-----------|---------------|
| Game forcing | Usually invitational or weaker |
| Shows singleton | No shortness shown |
| Slam interest | Primarily game interest |

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should discuss:

- Minimum strength.
- Four-card support requirement.
- Singleton versus void.
- Whether five-card support is acceptable.
- Responses after interference.
- Slam continuations.

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

Splinters are primarily designed for uncontested auctions. When opponents interfere, many partnerships replace Splinters with cue raises or other game-forcing methods.

---

## Opponent Overcalls

Example

```
1♥
(1♠)
?
```

Common treatments:

- Cue Raise.
- Jacoby 2NT.
- Natural raises.
- Splinter Off.

Some partnerships retain splinters when bidding space permits.

---

## Opponent Doubles

Example

```
1♠
(Double)
?
```

Many partnerships play:

- Jordan 2NT.
- Redouble.
- Natural raises.
- Splinter Off.

Others retain splinters at the four-level.

---

## Competition After a Splinter

Example

```
1♥
4♣*
(4♠)
?
```

Both partners should reevaluate:

- Controls.
- Offensive values.
- Vulnerability.
- Law of Total Tricks.

Possible actions:

- Double.
- Continue cue bidding.
- Bid slam.
- Sign off.

---

## Slam Investigation

Splinters frequently begin sophisticated slam auctions.

Typical continuations include:

- Cue Bids.
- First-round controls.
- Second-round controls.
- Roman Key Card Blackwood.
- Exclusion Blackwood (when appropriate).

---

## Partnership Agreements

Partners should define:

- Whether Splinter remains on after interference.
- Cue-bid priorities.
- Void versus singleton treatment.
- Exclusion Blackwood agreements.
- Serious 3NT.
- Control bidding.

---

## General Principles

When making a Splinter:

- Promise genuine game-forcing values.
- Promise four-card support.
- Show real shortness.
- Expect opener to evaluate wasted honors.
- Continue exchanging controls naturally.

---

# Advantages

Splinter:

- Immediately establishes the trump fit.
- Shows valuable distribution.
- Greatly improves slam judgment.
- Helps evaluate wasted honors.
- Fits naturally with cue bidding.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Gives information to the opponents.
- Consumes bidding space.
- Requires partnership agreement.
- Is less useful with balanced hands.

---

# Variations

Common treatments include:

- Standard Splinters.
- Mini-Splinters.
- Delayed Splinters.
- Splinters after 2/1 auctions.
- Splinters over minor openings.
- Exclusion Splinters.

Modern partnerships should agree on the exact structure.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Splintering Without Four Trumps

Most partnerships require:

- Four-card support.

---

## Splintering With Weak Hands

Splinter is **always game forcing**.

---

## Showing Shortness Without Offensive Values

Shortness alone does not justify a Splinter.

---

## Ignoring Wasted Honors

Opener's reevaluation is the entire purpose of the convention.

---

## Confusing Splinter with Swiss Raise

Swiss Raise shows strength.

Splinter shows **shortness**.

---

# Tips

- Think **Fit + Shortness + Game Force**.
- Use controls rather than HCP when evaluating slam.
- Practice opener's reevaluation carefully.
- Learn Splinter together with Cue Bids and Roman Key Card Blackwood.
- Discuss voids and singleton treatment before playing.

---

# Modern Usage

Splinter is one of the most widely played slam conventions in modern bridge.

It is standard in:

- Standard American
- 2/1 Game Force
- SAYC
- Acol (modern versions)
- Precision
- Polish Club

Because it immediately identifies shortness, many expert partnerships consider Splinter and Jacoby 2NT to be complementary rather than competing conventions.

---

# Summary

The Splinter Convention is one of bridge's most powerful slam-investigation tools. By making a double jump that promises **four-card trump support**, **game-forcing values**, and a **singleton or void**, responder immediately gives opener the information needed to reevaluate honors, controls, and overall slam potential. Used alongside Jacoby 2NT, Cue Bids, and Roman Key Card Blackwood, Splinter forms a cornerstone of modern major-suit slam bidding.
