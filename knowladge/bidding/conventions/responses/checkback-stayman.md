# Checkback Stayman

## Overview

**Checkback Stayman** is a conventional rebid used after opener has rebid **1NT** following a one-level suit opening. It allows responder to determine whether opener has:

- Three-card support for responder's major.
- An unshown four-card major.
- Extra strength for game.
- A suitable notrump contract.

It replaces guesswork with a structured inquiry and is widely played in Standard American, 2/1 Game Force, and many modern natural systems.

Typical auction:

```
1♣  Pass 1♠
Pass 1NT Pass
2♣*
```

The **2♣** bid is artificial.

---

# Purpose

Checkback Stayman helps the partnership:

- Discover an eight-card major-suit fit.
- Avoid missing a major-suit game.
- Differentiate invitational and game-forcing hands.
- Improve notrump decisions.
- Begin accurate game or slam investigation.

---

# When to Use

Checkback Stayman is available after opener rebids **1NT**.

Typical auctions include:

```
1♣ – 1♥
1NT – 2♣*
```

```
1♦ – 1♠
1NT – 2♣*
```

The convention is **not** used after a direct 1NT opening.

---

# Requirements

Responder usually holds:

- Invitational or stronger values.
- An unresolved major-suit question.
- Interest in game.

Typical strength:

- 11+ HCP (system dependent)

Some partnerships allow weaker invitational hands.

---

# Typical Hand Types

Responder may have:

- Five-card major.
- Four-card major.
- Balanced hand.
- Mild slam interest.

The key requirement is uncertainty regarding opener's support or shape.

---

# The 2♣ Checkback Bid

The artificial **2♣** asks opener:

- Do you have three-card support for my major?
- Do you have an unshown four-card major?
- Are you minimum or maximum?

It does **not** promise clubs.

---

# Opener's Responses

Partnership agreements vary, but common responses include:

## 2♦

Minimum hand.

No three-card support.

No unshown four-card major.

---

## 2♥

Three-card support for hearts.

or

Four hearts, depending on partnership agreement.

---

## 2♠

Three-card support for spades.

or

Four spades.

---

## 2NT

Maximum balanced hand without major support.

---

## Higher Bids

Show extras, controls or special distribution according to partnership agreement.

---

# Responder's Continuations

After opener replies, responder chooses among:

## Sign Off

With invitational values opposite a minimum.

---

## Invite Game

Continue if opener has shown useful extras.

---

## Bid Game

Once an eight-card fit or sufficient combined strength is identified.

---

## Slam Investigation

When opener's response reveals extra values.

# Advantages

Checkback Stayman:

- Locates hidden major fits.
- Improves game selection.
- Preserves bidding space.
- Distinguishes minimum and maximum hands.
- Reduces guesswork.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Requires partnership agreement.
- Consumes bidding space.
- May not be available after interference.
- Has several popular response structures.

---

# Variations

Popular treatments include:

- Simple Checkback Stayman
- Two-Way Checkback
- XYZ
- New Minor Forcing

Each differs in forcing status and response structure.

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should agree on:

- Minimum strength.

- Two-Way Checkback methods.
- Continuations after major support.
- Slam continuations.

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

The continuations described above assume an uncontested auction. When the opponents interfere after opener's **1NT rebid**, the partnership may no longer have sufficient bidding space to use standard Checkback Stayman methods.

Every partnership should agree in advance how interference affects the convention.

---

## Interference Before Checkback

Example

```
1♦
Pass
1♠
Pass
1NT
(2♣)
?
```

Responder's priorities remain:

- Search for a major-suit fit.
- Determine opener's support.
- Reach the best game.

Many partnerships abandon Checkback Stayman and switch to natural competitive methods.

Others retain the convention whenever bidding space permits.

---

## Interference After Checkback

Example

```
1♦
Pass
1♠
Pass
1NT
Pass
2♣
(2♥)
?
```

Opener should continue according to partnership agreement.

Possible approaches include:

- System On
- System Off
- Natural bidding
- Double showing values

Because methods differ considerably, partnership discussion is essential.

---

## Doubles

If the opponents double the artificial **2♣** inquiry:

```
1♦
Pass
1♠
Pass
1NT
Pass
2♣*
(Double)
?
```

Typical agreements include:

- Pass
- Redouble
- Continue normal responses
- Natural bidding

The meaning of each call should be discussed before play.

---

## Maintaining the Auction

Even after interference, both partners should continue trying to determine:

- Whether a major fit exists.
- Whether game is available.
- Whether notrump remains the best contract.

Competition should not prevent accurate hand description whenever practical.

---

## Partnership Agreements

Every partnership should discuss:

- Whether Checkback remains available after interference.
- System On versus System Off.
- Treatment after doubles.
- Treatment after overcalls.
- Whether forcing status changes.
- Continuations when bidding space is limited.

---

## General Principles

When opponents interfere during a Checkback Stayman auction:

- Preserve the search for a major fit whenever possible.
- Continue exchanging useful information.
- Avoid unnecessary guesswork.
- Follow partnership agreements rather than improvising.
- Reevaluate the partnership's combined strength after every bid.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Using Checkback Without Invitational Values

The convention is intended for constructive auctions.

---

## Forgetting Partnership Agreements

Response structures vary considerably.

---

## Ignoring a Major Fit

The primary objective is locating an eight-card major fit.

---

## Bypassing Game

Once sufficient combined strength is known, responder should place the contract.

---

## Forgetting the Convention After Interference

Every partnership should know in advance whether Checkback Stayman remains available after doubles or overcalls.

---

# Tips

- Learn the complete response structure, not just the 2♣ inquiry.
- Think about the partnership's combined hand.
- Search for major fits before committing to notrump.
- Discuss competitive continuations with your partner.
- Remember that different partnerships play different versions of Checkback Stayman.

---

# Summary

Checkback Stayman is one of the most useful modern conventions for auctions in which opener rebids **1NT** after a minor opening. By asking about three-card major support and unshown four-card majors, it enables partnerships to locate hidden fits, distinguish invitational from game-going hands, and choose the best contract. Clear partnership agreements regarding response structure and competitive auctions are essential for using the convention effectively.
