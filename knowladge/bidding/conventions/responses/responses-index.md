# Responses

> *The responder's first bid is the partnership's second opportunity to exchange information.*

After partner opens the bidding, responder assumes an equally important role in
describing the partnership assets. The response communicates strength,
distribution, suit preference, support, and future intentions while helping the
partnership determine the correct contract.

This section of **BridgeLab** covers natural responses, conventional responses,
forcing methods, raises, transfers, relay responses, and advanced partnership
agreements.

---

# Introduction

Responder's first bid should

- describe the hand,
- support partner when appropriate,
- introduce a new suit,
- establish forcing or non-forcing status,
- investigate game or slam,
- preserve bidding space,
- cooperate with partner's opening.

Every response contributes toward identifying

- the best strain,
- the best level,
- the declarer,
- the partnership fit.

---

# Objectives

Responses aim to

- exchange information efficiently,
- locate major-suit fits,
- identify notrump contracts,
- distinguish minimum and maximum hands,
- establish forcing sequences,
- prepare for later bidding,
- improve slam investigations.

# Fundamental Principles

Successful responses should

- accurately describe strength,
- preserve bidding space,
- support partner whenever appropriate,
- establish forcing status,
- anticipate later rebids,
- fit the partnership system.

---

# Hand Evaluation

Responder should evaluate

- High-card points,
- distribution,
- controls,
- fit,
- suit quality,
- playing strength,
- vulnerability,
- partnership style.

Modern methods emphasize offensive potential rather than point count alone.

---

# Forcing versus Non-Forcing

One of responder's first responsibilities is establishing whether the auction is

- Non-Forcing,
- Invitational,
- Game Forcing,
- Slam Investigative.

Understanding forcing status governs every subsequent bid.
