# 2NT Inquiry after Multi 2♦

## Overview

The **2NT Inquiry** is the principal artificial response to the **Multi 2♦**
opening. It requests opener to clarify the meaning of the Multi by describing

- hand type,
- strength,
- major suit,
- suit quality,
- and, in some partnerships, outside features.

Unlike the **2NT Inquiry over a natural Weak Two**, the inquiry after a Multi
2♦ first determines **which hand type opener holds**, since the Multi opening
may represent several different hands.

The convention is forcing for at least one round.

---

# Objectives

The 2NT Inquiry allows responder to

- identify opener's major suit,
- distinguish minimum from maximum weak hands,
- identify strong hand variants,
- evaluate game prospects,
- determine the correct strain,
- investigate slam when appropriate.

Responder becomes the **captain of the auction**.

---

# Multi 2♦ Review

A standard Multi 2♦ opening normally shows one of

- a Weak Two in hearts,
- a Weak Two in spades,
- a strong balanced hand (optional style),
- a strong one-suited hand,
- another agreed strong option.

The exact meanings depend on partnership agreement.

# General Principles

The inquiry asks opener to describe

- weak versus strong,
- hearts versus spades,
- suit quality,
- outside features,

depending upon the partnership structure.

---

# Standard Weak-Hand Structure

A common expert treatment is

| Rebid | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| 3♣ | Weak Two in hearts (minimum) |
| 3♦ | Weak Two in spades (minimum) |
| 3♥ | Weak Two in hearts (maximum or good suit) |
| 3♠ | Weak Two in spades (maximum or good suit) |
| 3NT | Strong variant |

Other structures are equally valid.

---

# Feature-Ask Structure

Many partnerships continue with Feature Ask principles.

Example

```

2NT*
3♥
```

Responder may continue

```
3NT
```

or

```
4♥
```

depending upon

- suit quality,
- controls,
- game prospects.

---

# Ogust-Style Structure

Some partnerships adapt **Ogust** to the Multi.

Responses distinguish

- minimum or maximum,
- poor or good suit,

after opener identifies the major.

---

# Strong Hand Responses

When the Multi includes strong hands,

opener first identifies

- the strong option,
- then continues naturally or by relay.

Example

```
2♦*
2NT*
3NT
```

Strong balanced hand.

or

```
2♦*
2NT*
4♣
```

Strong one-suited hand.

The exact continuation depends on partnership methods.

---

# Continuations

After opener's clarification,

responder may

- Pass,
- bid game,
- invite game,
- investigate slam,
- sign off in the correct major.

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should define

- all possible Multi meanings,
- exact inquiry responses,
- minimum versus maximum,
- suit-quality definitions,
- feature responses,
- continuations after interference.

Because Multi methods vary widely, detailed agreements are essential.

---

# Competitive Auctions

If opponents interfere,

partnerships should define

- whether 2NT remains artificial,
- replacement relays,
- doubles,
- cue bids,
- continuation methods.

Most expert partnerships preserve the inquiry whenever practical.

---

# Expert Style

Modern expert partnerships usually

- use 2NT as the primary inquiry,
- identify weak or strong hands first,
- distinguish suit quality,
- preserve bidding space for constructive auctions.

Some partnerships incorporate relay structures that reveal additional
information efficiently.

---

# Advantages

- Clarifies the ambiguous Multi opening.
- Distinguishes weak and strong hand types.
- Preserves bidding space.
- Improves game and slam decisions.
- Provides accurate hand evaluation.

---

# Disadvantages

- Requires extensive partnership agreements.
- Multiple expert structures exist.
- Continuations differ significantly between systems.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Assuming a Natural 2NT

The bid is almost always artificial.

## Forgetting Partnership Structure

Different Multi systems use different response tables.

Although related, the inquiry after Multi first identifies opener's hand type.

---

## Ignoring Strong Variants

If the Multi includes strong options,

the response structure must accommodate them.

---

## Continuing Without Clarification

Responder should usually wait for opener's description before committing to game.

# Summary

The **2NT Inquiry after a Multi 2♦ opening** is the cornerstone of modern Multi
continuations. Because the opening is intentionally ambiguous, responder first
uses the inquiry to determine **which hand type opener holds**, then evaluates
strength, suit quality, and game potential.

Expert partnerships customize the response structure to match their version of
the Multi, but the underlying principle remains the same: **clarify the opening
efficiently while preserving as much bidding space as possible for accurate game
and slam decisions.**
