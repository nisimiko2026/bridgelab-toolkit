# Puppet Stayman

## Overview

**Puppet Stayman** is an advanced extension of the Stayman convention used primarily after a **2NT opening** or equivalent strong balanced auctions. Unlike standard Stayman, which asks opener to show a four-card major, Puppet Stayman is designed to locate both **5-3** and **4-4 major-suit fits**, while preserving bidding space for game and slam investigation.

The convention is widely used in modern bridge because strong notrump openings frequently contain five-card majors, making it important to distinguish between different major-suit fits before committing to game.

Typical auctions include:

```
2NT
3♣*
```

or

```
2♣
2♦
2NT
3♣*
```

where **3♣** is artificial.

---

# Purpose

Puppet Stayman helps the partnership:

- Locate 5-3 major-suit fits.
- Locate 4-4 major-suit fits.
- Distinguish between different major holdings.
- Improve game selection.
- Preserve bidding space for slam investigation.
- Avoid inferior notrump contracts.

---

# When to Use

Puppet Stayman is normally used after:

- 2NT opening.
- Strong balanced hands shown later in the auction.
- Occasionally after very strong artificial openings.

It is generally **not** used after a normal 1NT opening unless specifically agreed.

---

# Requirements

Responder typically holds:

- Invitational or stronger values.
- Interest in a major-suit contract.
- Uncertainty regarding opener's major-suit holdings.

Many partnerships use Puppet Stayman with both game-going and slam-oriented hands.

---

# Typical Hand Types

Responder may hold:

- One four-card major.
- One five-card major.
- Both four-card majors.
- Balanced hands.
- Slam interest.

---

# The 3♣ Inquiry

The **3♣** response asks opener to describe major-suit holdings.

Unlike standard Stayman, the inquiry is broader than simply asking for a four-card major.

Typical questions include:

- Do you have a five-card major?
- Do you have a four-card major?
- Which major?
- Do you have both?

---

# Typical Response Structure

Response structures vary considerably.

One common treatment is:

## 3♦

No five-card major.

May contain one or both four-card majors.

---

## 3♥

Five hearts.

---

## 3♠

Five spades.

---

## 3NT

No four-card or five-card major.

Some partnerships use different meanings.

Every partnership should agree on the exact structure.

---

# Continuations

After opener's response, responder may:

## Bid Game

Select:

- 4♥
- 4♠
- 3NT

according to the discovered fit.

---

## Continue Describing Shape

Responder may clarify:

- Four-card major.
- Five-card major.
- Slam interest.

---

## Investigate Slam

If a suitable fit has been found, continue with:

- Cue Bidding
- Blackwood
- Roman Key Card Blackwood

---

# Major Suit Fits

Puppet Stayman excels at distinguishing:

## 5-3 Fits

Often superior to notrump.

---

## 4-4 Fits

Frequently the preferred game contract.

---

## No Fit

When no suitable fit exists, the partnership normally selects 3NT.

---

# Slam Investigation

Once a major fit has been located, Puppet Stayman naturally transitions into slam exploration.

Typical continuations include:

- Cue Bids.
- Roman Key Card Blackwood.
- Control Bids.
- Quantitative invitations.

---

# Advantages

Puppet Stayman:

- Locates hidden major fits.
- Distinguishes 5-3 from 4-4 fits.
- Improves game selection.
- Preserves bidding space.
- Enhances slam investigation.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Is more complex than standard Stayman.
- Requires detailed partnership agreements.
- Has numerous competing response structures.
- Is not universally standardized.

---

# Variations

Popular treatments include:

- Standard Puppet Stayman.
- Muppet Stayman.
- Five-card-major Puppet.
- Modified Puppet Stayman.

Each partnership should agree on the exact meanings of every response.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should discuss:

- Exact response structure.
- Whether opener may hold five-card majors.
- Continuations after every response.
- Invitational versus game-forcing hands.
- Slam continuations.
- Competitive auctions.

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

The sequences described above assume an uncontested auction. When the opponents interfere after a **2NT** opening or another strong balanced sequence, Puppet Stayman may no longer be available in its normal form.

Because bidding space is limited, partnership agreements become essential.

---

## Opponent Overcalls

Example

```
2NT   (3♦)   ?
```

Responder's priorities remain:

- Locate a major fit.
- Select the best game.
- Preserve slam opportunities.

Partnerships commonly choose one of two approaches:

### Systems On

Normal Puppet Stayman responses continue whenever bidding space permits.

### Systems Off

Artificial responses are abandoned in favor of natural competitive methods.

---

## Opponent Doubles

Example

```
2NT   (Double)   ?
```

Typical agreements include:

- Systems On.
- Systems Off.
- Redouble.
- Natural bidding.
- Escape methods.

Every partnership should define these continuations before play.

---

## Interference After the Inquiry

Example

```
2NT
Pass
3♣*
(3♦)
?
```

Opener should continue according to partnership agreement.

Possible methods include:

- Continue normal responses.
- Natural bidding.
- Double showing values.
- Pass forcing.

---

## Lebensohl and Rubensohl

Some partnerships extend **Lebensohl** or **Rubensohl** principles to auctions involving Puppet Stayman after interference.

These methods help distinguish:

- Invitational hands.
- Game-going hands.
- Slam interest.
- Hands with or without stoppers.

The exact treatment depends upon partnership agreement.

---

## Maintaining the Search

Even after interference, the partnership should continue trying to determine:

- Whether a major fit exists.
- Whether notrump remains superior.
- Whether slam should be explored.

Do not abandon constructive bidding simply because the opponents have entered the auction.

---

## Partnership Agreements

Every partnership should agree on:

- Systems On versus Systems Off.
- Responses after doubles.
- Responses after overcalls.
- Whether Puppet Stayman remains available.
- Slam continuations after interference.

---

## General Principles

When opponents interfere:

- Preserve the search for a major fit whenever practical.
- Continue exchanging useful information.
- Follow partnership agreements rather than improvising.
- Reevaluate the partnership's combined strength after every bid.
- Remember that the primary objective remains selecting the best game or slam.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Confusing Puppet Stayman with Standard Stayman

The two conventions ask different questions and have different response structures.

---

## Forgetting the Partnership Structure

Different partnerships assign different meanings to the responses.

---

## Missing a 5-3 Fit

One of Puppet Stayman's principal objectives is locating five-card major fits.

---

## Choosing Notrump Too Early

Always complete the inquiry before committing to 3NT.

---

## Forgetting Competitive Agreements

Every partnership should know how interference affects the convention.

---

# Tips

- Learn the complete response structure, not just the initial inquiry.
- Practice distinguishing 5-3 and 4-4 fits.
- Discuss competitive continuations before playing.
- Use Puppet Stayman to improve both game and slam bidding.
- Remember that different bridge systems use different versions of the convention.

---

# Summary

Puppet Stayman is an advanced notrump convention that extends the principles of Stayman to identify both 5-3 and 4-4 major-suit fits after a 2NT opening or equivalent strong balanced sequence. By preserving bidding space and providing a structured method for describing major-suit holdings, it significantly improves game and slam bidding. Because several response structures exist, successful use depends on thorough partnership agreements, especially regarding continuations after interference.
