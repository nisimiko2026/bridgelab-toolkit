# Support Double Convention

## Purpose

The **Support Double** is used by opener after responder introduces a new suit and the opponents interfere. It allows opener to distinguish between **exactly three-card support** and **four-card support** for responder's suit.

Without the convention, opener's simple raise could show either three or four trumps. Support Doubles improve competitive bidding and help the partnership locate the best contract.

---

# When It Applies

A Support Double applies when:

- Opener makes a natural one-level opening.
- Responder bids a new suit.
- Right-hand opponent overcalls or makes a competitive bid.
- Opener has **exactly three-card support** for responder's suit.

Typical auction:

```
South   West   North   East
1♦      Pass   1♠      2♣
?
```

If opener has exactly **three spades**, opener doubles.

---

# Basic Principle

| Opener's Action | Meaning |
|-----------------|---------|
| **Double** | Exactly **3-card support** for responder's suit |
| **Raise** | **4+ card support** |
| Other bid | No support or describes another feature |


- Exactly three spades

---

## Example 3

```
1♥   Pass

```

Shows:

- Four or more spades

No Support Double because opener has four-card support.

---

# Requirements

A Support Double requires:

- Exactly **three-card support** for responder's major or minor (depending on partnership agreement).
- Normal opening strength.
- No more descriptive natural rebid available.

Most partnerships play Support Doubles only through **2♥** or **2♠**.

---

# Opener's Rebids

## Double

Exactly three-card support.

No extra strength implied.

---

## Raise

Four or more-card support.

Natural.

---

## New Suit

Natural.

Shows another suit.

Denies three-card support.

---

## Notrump

Balanced hand.

Shows stopper(s) in the opponent's suit.

---

## Pass

Usually denies support and lacks a suitable action.

---

# Responder's Continuations

After a Support Double, responder reevaluates the combined fit.

### Minimum Hand

Return to responder's suit.

Example:

```
1♦   Pass
1♠   2♣
Double Pass
2♠
```

Competing with an eight-card fit.

---

### Invitational Hand

Invite game with:

- 3♠
- 2NT
- New suit

Depending on distribution.

---

### Game Force

Bid game or begin slam investigation.

# Support Redouble

If the opponents **Double** responder instead of bidding a suit, many partnerships play:

```
1♦
Pass
1♠
Double
Redouble
```

The **Redouble** shows:

- Exactly three-card support.

The direct raise still promises four-card support.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Agree on:

- Maximum level for Support Doubles (commonly through **2♥** or **2♠**).
- Whether the convention applies after all competitive bids.
- Whether it applies when responder bids a minor.
- Whether Support Redoubles are also played.
- Whether opener may have extra strength.
- Continuations after further interference.

---

# Advantages

- Distinguishes 3-card and 4-card support.
- Helps locate 8-card and 9-card fits.
- Improves competitive bidding accuracy.
- Makes game decisions easier.
- One of the most widely played modern conventions.

---

# Disadvantages

- Uses the Double for an artificial purpose.
- Requires partnership agreement.
- Exact scope (through which level) varies among partnerships.

---

# Comparison

| Opener's Action | Meaning |
|-----------------|---------|
| Double | Exactly 3-card support |
| Raise | 4+ card support |
| New Suit | Natural, no 3-card support |
| Notrump | Balanced with stopper |

---

# Common Partnership Style

Most expert partnerships play:

- Support Doubles through **2♥**.
- Support Redoubles after an opponent's takeout double.
- Double = exactly **3-card support**.
- Raise = **4+ card support**.

Some partnerships extend the convention through **2♠** or even **3♣**.

# Memory Aid

**Support Double = "I have exactly three."**

Remember:

- **Double = 3-card support**
- **Raise = 4 or more-card support**
- **Support Redouble = 3-card support after an opponent's double**

The convention allows opener to define the trump fit immediately, giving responder a much clearer picture of the partnership's combined distribution.
