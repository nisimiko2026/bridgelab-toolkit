# Namyats Convention

## Overview

**Namyats** is an artificial opening convention that transfers the meaning of strong four-level major openings to the minor suits:

- **4♣** = Strong opening with hearts
- **4♦** = Strong opening with spades

This frees the natural **4♥** and **4♠** openings to remain purely preemptive while allowing strong major-suit hands to begin the auction below game.

The convention was developed by **Stayman** spelled backwards ("Namyats").

---

# Purpose

Namyats enables partnerships to:

- Distinguish strong and weak four-level major openings.
- Preserve bidding space for game and slam investigation.
- Keep natural 4♥ and 4♠ openings available as preempts.
- Describe powerful one-suited major hands accurately.

---

# When to Use

Namyats is used only as an opening bid.

Typical auctions are:

```
4♣
```

Artificial.

Shows a strong heart hand.

---

```
4♦
```

Artificial.

Shows a strong spade hand.

---

Natural four-level openings become:

```
4♥
```

Weak preempt.

---

```
4♠
```

Weak preempt.

---

# Requirements

Typical requirements are:

- Approximately opening strength or better (15-18+ HCP or equivalent playing strength).
- Eight or more playing tricks.
- A strong seven-card or longer major suit.
- Little interest in opening at the one level because of the hand's offensive nature.

Some partnerships evaluate primarily by playing tricks rather than HCP.

---

# Typical Hand Types

Examples include:

- Seven-card heart suit with excellent quality.
- Seven-card spade suit with excellent quality.
- Powerful distributional hands.
- Strong one-suited hands with limited defensive values.

---

# Bid Structure

## 4♣

Artificial.

Shows:

- Strong hearts.
- Usually seven or more hearts.
- Strong playing strength.

---

## 4♦

Artificial.

Shows:

- Strong spades.
- Usually seven or more spades.
- Strong playing strength.

---

## 4♥

Natural weak preempt.

---

## 4♠

Natural weak preempt.

---

# Responder's Actions

Responder first evaluates:

- Trump support.
- Controls.
- Slam potential.
- Defensive values.

Possible actions include:

- Sign off in game.
- Cue bid.
- Blackwood.
- Roman Key Card Blackwood.
- Control bidding.

---

## Minimum Hand

Responder normally accepts game.

Example

```
4♣
4♥
```

---

## Invitational Hands

Rare.

Game has effectively been reached.

---

## Slam Interest

Responder begins cue bidding or another agreed slam investigation.

---

# Opener Rebids

After responder's action, opener continues describing:

- Controls.
- Shortness.
- Extra playing strength.
- Suit quality.

Opener generally cooperates with slam investigation.

---

# Responder Rebids

Responder reevaluates:

- Combined controls.
- Trump quality.
- Distribution.
- Outside controls.

Responder decides whether to:

- Sign off.
- Continue cue bidding.
- Use Roman Key Card Blackwood.
- Bid slam.

---

# Slam Investigation

Namyats was designed partly to preserve space for slam bidding.

Common methods include:

- Cue Bidding.
- Blackwood.
- Roman Key Card Blackwood.
- Control Bids.

---

# Advantages

Namyats:

- Distinguishes strong and weak four-level openings.
- Preserves bidding room.
- Improves slam bidding.
- Makes natural four-level openings more effective preempts.
- Clearly defines hand type.

---

# Limitations

The convention:

- Gives up natural 4♣ and 4♦ openings.
- Requires detailed partnership agreements.
- Is relatively uncommon.
- May help opponents with the opening lead.

---

# Variations

Popular variations include:

- Standard Namyats.
- Modified Namyats.
- Namyats with transfer responses.
- Different strength requirements.

Every partnership should agree on its methods.

---

# Partnership Agreements

Partners should discuss:

- Minimum strength.
- Suit quality.
- Playing trick requirements.
- Responses.
- Cue bidding.
- Slam continuations.
- Competitive auctions.

# Competitive Auctions

## Overview

The auctions described above assume an uncontested opening. Because a Namyats opening already consumes considerable bidding space, opponents frequently interfere in an attempt to disrupt slam investigation.

Despite the interference, the partnership should continue exchanging information whenever practical.

---

## Opponent Overcalls

Example

```
4♣*   (4♠)   ?
```

Responder should evaluate:

- Trump support.
- Controls.
- Slam interest.
- Offensive potential.

Possible actions include:

- Pass.
- Double (partnership agreement).
- Cue bid.
- Blackwood (when available).

Competition should not automatically discourage slam investigation.

---

## Opponent Doubles

Example

```
4♣*   (Double)   ?
```

Typical agreements include:

- Pass.
- Redouble showing strength.
- Natural continuations.
- System On approach.

Every partnership should define these treatments before play.

---

## Competition After the Relay

Example

```
4♣*
4♥
(4♠)
?
```

Both partners should continue evaluating:

- Combined controls.
- Trump quality.
- Offensive potential.
- Whether slam remains practical.

Competition may force quicker decisions because bidding space is limited.

---

## Slam Investigation Under Pressure

Even after interference, partnerships should continue using agreed methods whenever possible.

Typical tools include:

- Cue Bids.
- Roman Key Card Blackwood.
- Control Bids.

If bidding space becomes too limited, practical judgment often replaces complete investigation.

---

## Partnership Agreements

Partnerships should agree on:

- Responses after doubles.
- Responses after overcalls.
- Whether relay responses remain unchanged.
- Slam continuations.
- Competitive doubles.
- Pass forcing situations.

---

## General Principles

When opponents interfere after a Namyats opening:

- Continue describing the hand accurately.
- Preserve slam investigation whenever practical.
- Do not compete automatically.
- Reevaluate controls after every bid.
- Follow partnership agreements rather than improvising.

---

# Common Mistakes

## Opening With Insufficient Playing Strength

Namyats promises a powerful offensive hand.

---

## Using Weak Suits

The major suit should be self-sufficient.

---

## Forgetting That 4♥ and 4♠ Become Weak

Once Namyats is adopted:

- 4♥ is preemptive.
- 4♠ is preemptive.

Strong major hands must begin with:

- 4♣
- 4♦

---

## Missing Slam Opportunities

One of Namyats' principal advantages is preserving room below game.

Use that space wisely.

---

## Ignoring Competitive Agreements

High-level auctions leave little room for recovery.

Discuss interference before playing the convention.

---

# Tips

- Evaluate playing tricks rather than HCP alone.
- Use Namyats only with genuine one-suited powerhouses.
- Keep natural 4♥ and 4♠ available for weak preempts.
- Learn complete slam continuations.
- Practice competitive auctions as carefully as uncontested ones.

---

# Summary

Namyats is an advanced convention that distinguishes strong and weak four-level major openings by transferring strong heart hands to **4♣** and strong spade hands to **4♦**. This preserves natural four-level major openings as preempts while creating valuable bidding space for constructive game and slam investigation. Successful use of Namyats depends on disciplined hand evaluation, clear partnership agreements, and well-defined continuations after both uncontested and competitive auctions.
