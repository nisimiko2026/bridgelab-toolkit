# Optional Double

## Bridge Insight

**The Optional Double combines offensive and defensive possibilities into a single call. Rather than insisting on defending or demanding partner bid, the doubler expresses a hand suitable for either action and leaves the final decision to partner.**

---

# Overview

An **Optional Double** is a double that gives partner the choice of:

- Passing for penalty, or
- Bidding another contract.

Unlike:

- **Penalty Double**, which commits the partnership to defending.
- **Takeout Double**, which requests partner to bid.

An Optional Double expresses a hand that is comfortable with **either outcome**.

The convention is commonly used after:

- Weak preemptive openings.
- Competitive auctions.
- High-level contracts.
- Certain balancing situations.

---

# Objectives

The Optional Double enables the partnership to:

- Compete without overcommitting.
- Penalize overbidding when appropriate.
- Allow partner to judge the best action.
- Balance offensive and defensive values.
- Increase flexibility in competitive auctions.

---

# When to Use

Typical auctions include:

```text
West      North

3♠        Double
```

or

```text
West      North

4♥        Double
```

The double says:

> "If defending is best, pass."

> "If bidding is better, choose a bid."

---

# Requirements

Typical hand characteristics:

- Opening values or better.
- Defensive strength.
- Shortness in opener's suit.
- Tolerance for unbid suits.
- No clear preference between defending and declaring.

---

# Convention Structure

The Optional Double communicates:

- Defensive potential.
- Offensive potential.
- Flexibility.

Partner becomes captain.

---

# Partner's Responsibilities

Partner evaluates:

- Defensive tricks.
- Offensive strength.
- Fit.
- Vulnerability.
- Form of scoring.

Partner then chooses between:

### Pass

Convert the double into penalty.

---

### Bid

Remove to the best contract.

# Hand Evaluation

Optional Double requires a balance between:

Offensive assets:

- Shape.
- Fit potential.
- Distribution.

Defensive assets:

- Quick tricks.
- Trump holding.
- Honor strength.

Neither aspect should dominate completely.

- Continuations after further competition.

# Advantages

Optional Double:

- Extremely flexible.
- Allows partner to judge the final action.
- Balances offense and defense.
- Difficult for opponents to judge.

---

# Limitations

Optional Double:

- Requires experienced partnership judgment.
- Misjudgment can be expensive.
- Not universally played.
- Easily confused with Penalty or Takeout Doubles.

# Expert Thinking

An Optional Double recognizes that some hands contain **both offensive and defensive potential**.

Rather than forcing one action, the doubler transfers the final decision to partner, who often has a clearer picture of the combined assets.

The convention works best when both partners understand vulnerability, scoring, and the likely number of offensive and defensive tricks.

---

# Common Mistakes

- Treating the double as automatic penalty.
- Automatically removing without evaluating defense.
- Using Optional Double with purely offensive hands.
- Confusing it with Takeout Double.
- Ignoring vulnerability.

---

# Best Practices

- Agree exactly which auctions use Optional Double.
- Evaluate offense and defense equally.
- Trust partner's judgment.
- Practice competitive examples.
- Record agreements on the convention card.

# Expert Tip

**The Optional Double is a partnership agreement, not a universal bridge law.**

Different systems assign different meanings to doubles in competitive auctions. Before using Optional Doubles, partners should agree **exactly which auctions are optional** and which remain takeout or penalty. Without explicit agreement, misunderstandings are common.

---

# Memory Aid

**Optional Double = "Your Choice, Partner."**

Remember:

- It is neither a pure takeout nor a pure penalty double.
- It shows a hand suitable for **either defending or competing**.
- Partner becomes captain and decides whether to pass or bid.
- The convention is most common after preempts and in competitive auctions.
- Clear partnership agreements are essential because its use varies significantly between systems.
